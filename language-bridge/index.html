Language Bridge

Grade-level mathematics with the language barrier removed — not the math. Every support here changes what a student has to read and write. None of them change what a student has to understand.

Language Bridge is not a separate program. It is the language-access layer that already runs through the curriculum, gathered into one place so a student, a teacher, or a family can find it.

See the scaffold ladder
The shared scaffold ladder

Five rungs, one question

Any substantial written response in the curriculum can be offered at five support levels. The prompt and the learning target are authored once and shown unchanged at every rung, so a page cannot quietly ask a supported student an easier mathematical question.

  1. 4

    Vocabulary bank

    The words are provided. The student builds every sentence.

    ratio · equivalent · multiply · factor · table

  2. 3

    Sentence starter

    The opening is given. The student finishes the thought.

    “These ratios are equivalent because…”

  3. 2

    Complete sentence frame

    The sentence shape is given. The student supplies the mathematics.

    “4:6 and 6:9 are equivalent because I multiplied ___ by ___ to get ___.”

  4. 1

    Paragraph frame

    A multi-sentence structure to complete, for a full explanation.

    “First, I looked at ___. Then I multiplied ___ by ___. This shows the ratios are equivalent because ___.”

  5. 0

    Independent response

    The student's own words and own structure. This is the destination.

    A blank box.

Why the numbering runs downward

Rung 0 is independence. A student moving down the ladder over a unit is the progress signal the system watches for. Support use is recorded as information for a teacher — it never lowers a score and is never treated as a weakness.

For students

Supports you can turn on

Choose what helps. You never have to explain why, and nothing about a diagnosis, an IEP, or a disability is stored anywhere in this system.

  • Vocabulary Hub Bilingual math vocabulary with audio, images, and examples in context.
  • ESOL Practice Language practice built around the mathematics students are actually doing.
  • Reading & Writing Support Reading and writing scaffolds that carry across math and literacy.
  • ESOL Study Guide A study guide that pairs math content with the language it needs.
  • Vocabulary Scrambler Quick vocabulary practice for the words in the current unit.
  • ACCESS Practice Practice with the task types students meet on the ACCESS assessment.
For families

Home-language support

Families reach the same material through Family Connections. There is no separate family login and no second parent portal — one family surface, reached the way families already reach it.

  • Family Connections Unit-by-unit explanations, home activities, and what to ask about tonight.
  • Family Hub The family front door for the whole curriculum.
  • Family Letters Printable letters that explain a unit in plain language.
For teachers

Planning with language objectives

Every lesson in the canonical curriculum registry carries a language objective alongside its content objective, so lesson pages, teacher views, family summaries, and project rubrics all draw from the same statement instead of drifting apart.

  • Learning Supports Manager Assign and review the supports a student has available.
  • Tiered Differentiation Builder Build tiered tasks that keep the same mathematical target.
  • Curriculum Hub Language objectives appear on every lesson in the full directory.
Being precise

What this is and is not

  • No WIDA endorsement or certification is claimed. Specific features have been mapped against WIDA descriptors; that mapping is a design reference, not an accreditation.
  • Spanish is currently the only home language with translated vocabulary and family copy. Other languages fall back to English text with read-aloud and visual supports.
  • Translations here are teacher-authored and have not had an external translation review. A native-speaker review is listed as outstanding manual work.
  • Read-aloud uses the browser's built-in speech synthesis. Voice quality and language coverage vary by device.
