The Mystery Files

The Lantern in the Stacks

Students describe what a witness observed and separate that observation from a guess about who moved the lantern.

Share a direct link

  • Grades 3–5, guided
  • Grades 6–8, developing, speaking focus
  • Grades 6–8, guided writing, independent speaking

WIDA alignment for practice

Aligned for classroom practice to the WIDA English Language Development Standards Framework, 2020 Edition. This is not an official WIDA score, ACCESS score, or proficiency level.

  • Standard: Language for Language Arts
  • Key Language Use: inform
  • Discourse: A short investigative report that states observations before any inference.
  • Sentence: Sentences that describe a thing and then give a reason with because or so.
  • Word and phrase: observe, evidence, inference, witness, detail, report.

Teaching notes

About 18 minutes. Recommended grades 3-5. Content area: language-arts.

  • Describe a scene with concrete details.
  • Distinguish an observation from an inference.

Guided pathway shows frames and a word bank. Developing pathway hides frames until the student opens them. Independent pathway adds a second source and no frames.

Likely language demands

  • Students may treat a guess as if it were seen.
  • Because-clauses may restate the claim instead of adding a detail.

What independent performance looks like

Independent performance is a recording or a revision made with the transcript off and the frames unopened, using a detail from the note.

Follow-up

  • Five-minute speaking practice on describing a picture, then Mystery Files adventure 2.
