Your student has a Unit 3 Test coming up. This homework reviews all of it.

Unit 3 is about ratios and rates: comparing two amounts, finding the amount for one (a unit rate), using tables and graphs to find equivalent ratios, and converting between units like feet and yards. Every question in this review practices a skill that is on the test. The numbers and stories are new, so this is real practice, not a copy of the test.

The part that feels hardest is the part worth practicing together before test day. The My Results tab lists exactly which parts still need work.

Part 1Ratios & tape diagramsA ratio compares two amounts, and order matters. 20 : 28 simplifies to 5 : 7 — "for every 5 of the first, there are 7 of the second."
Part 2Unit rates & unit pricesA unit rate is the amount for exactly 1. $5.85 for 3 pounds is $5.85 ÷ 3 = $1.95 per pound. The lower unit price is the better buy.
Part 3Ratio tables & graphsEquivalent ratios fill a table and line up in a straight line on a graph. Each point is an ordered pair, like (2, 8).
Parts 4 & 5Rate problems & converting unitsFind the unit rate, then use it. To convert, use a known rate like 3 feet per yard: feet to yards means fewer, bigger units, so divide.
👀 What to listen for
  • "Which amount is named first?" The order of a ratio is part of the answer.
  • Equivalent ratios multiply both parts by the same number. Adding the same number to both parts is the most common mistake.
  • "Per" means "for each one." A unit rate always has a 1 underneath.
  • A bigger package is not automatically the better buy. Compare unit prices.
  • Changing to a bigger unit gives a smaller number (1,800 feet = 600 yards).
🤝 How to help
  • Let your student set up the problem first, before you say anything.
  • Ask "How did you get that?" and "Does that answer make sense?"
  • If they are stuck, press the Hint button instead of giving the answer.
  • You do not need to know the math. Listening to the explanation is the whole job.
  • Two short sessions before the test work better than one long one.
💬 Sentence starters

The ratio ___ : ___ means for every ___, there are ___.

The unit rate is ___ per 1 ___.

I multiplied both parts by ___, so ___.

These rates are / are not equivalent because ___.

___ is the better buy because its unit price is lower.

Try this together · 5 minutes
Find a unit price and a unit rate at home.

Pick up any package that shows a price and an amount (a bag of rice, a box of granola bars, a bottle of juice). Ask your student: what does one pound, one bar, or one ounce cost? Then pick something you do every day — walking to the bus, reading a few pages — and ask: how long does it take for one? That is a unit rate.

Part 1 · Ratios & Tape Diagrams

Write ratios in the right order, simplify them, explain what they mean, and use a tape diagram to find a missing amount.

6.RP.A.1 · 5 questions
Remember how
A garden has 12 tomato plants and 18 pepper plants. What is the ratio of tomato plants to pepper plants in simplest form?
Write it in order
12 tomato18 pepper
÷ 6
Divide both by the GCF, 6
23
What it means: for every 2 tomato plants, there are 3 pepper plants. Tape diagram trick: if the ratio is 2 : 3 and there are 40 tomato plants, the 2 tomato boxes hold 40, so each box is 40 ÷ 2 = 20, and pepper is 3 × 20 = 60.

Part 2 · Unit Rates & Unit Prices

A unit rate is the amount for exactly 1. Divide the top by the bottom so the bottom becomes 1. Compare unit prices to find the better buy.

6.RP.A.2 · 4 questions
Remember how
A printer prints 168 pages in 8 minutes. What is the unit rate?
Set up the rate
168 pages8 minutes
÷ 8
Make the bottom 1
21 pages1 minute
21 pages per minute. For money, the cost goes on top: $6.40 for 4 pounds is $6.40 ÷ 4 = $1.60 per pound. The lower unit price is the better buy, no matter which bag is bigger.

Part 3 · Ratio Tables & Graphs

Fill in ratio tables by multiplying both rows by the same number, plot equivalent ratios on a graph, and use ordered pairs to compare.

6.RP.A.3a · 5 questions
Remember how
A recipe uses 3 cups of rice for every 2 cups of beans. Complete the table.
Worked example: rice and beans in a 3 to 2 ratio
Rice (cups) 3 6 9 12
Beans (cups) 2 4 6 8
9 rice is 3 × 3, so beans is 2 × 3 = 6. Each column is an ordered pair (rice, beans): (3, 2), (6, 4), (9, 6), (12, 8). Plotted on a graph, the points line up in a straight line that would pass through (0, 0).

Part 4 · Unit Rate Problems

Find the unit rate first, then use it to answer the question. Two rates are equivalent when they have the same unit rate.

6.RP.A.3b · 3 questions
Remember how
Rosa walks 2 miles in 36 minutes. At this rate, how many miles does she walk in 54 minutes?
Unit rate · 36 ÷ 2
18 minutesper mile
→
Use it · 54 ÷ 18
3 milesin 54 minutes
Equivalent or not? $30 for 5 hours and $48 for 8 hours: $30 ÷ 5 = $6 per hour and $48 ÷ 8 = $6 per hour. Same unit rate, so the rates are equivalent.

Part 5 · Converting Units

Use a conversion rate like a unit rate. Going to a bigger unit, the number gets smaller (divide). Going to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger (multiply).

6.RP.A.3d · 4 questions
Reference sheet
Use these conversions, just like the test's reference sheet.
1 foot = 12 inches
1 yard = 3 feet
1 mile = 5,280 feet
1 pound = 16 ounces
1 kilogram ≈ 2.2 pounds
1 gallon = 4 quarts
Example: 2,400 feet to yards. Yards are bigger, so there will be fewer of them: 2,400 ÷ 3 = 800 yards. And 40 kilograms to pounds: pounds are smaller, so there will be more of them: 40 × 2.2 = 88 pounds.
