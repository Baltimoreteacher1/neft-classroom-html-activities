# Paper + digital Language Passport — design (draft for Joel's approval)

Status: **draft. Nothing in sections 3–5 is built.** Sections 1–2 are built in this worktree and uncommitted.

## Intent

Students and families use the ACCESS Practice Lab online. Teachers want something physical they can stamp,
that families can check off at home, and that kids enjoy. The paper passport is the shared record between
school and home, because online progress is saved per device (localStorage) and students do not bring the
phone or tablet they use at home to school.

## Goals

- A paper passport teachers stamp and families check off, tied to the lab's existing stamps and badge names.
- A travel theme (countries, trips, postcards) that makes practice feel like a journey.
- Family practice that follows a guided, ordered route chosen by the student's need.
- Everything works with no accounts, no uploads and no new tracking of children.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- Stamps never claim mastery, a score or a language level (the lab already says this). No paper or screen text may imply one.
- No leaderboards, timers, or competition between children. Class-wide goals only, if any.
- No languages beyond English and Spanish.
- No student photo, recording or name leaves the device unless the student chooses "Keep for my teacher".
- No change to existing practice, scoring, or the ACCESS1/ACCESST1 codes.

## 3. Country routes (proposed)

- **12 countries = 4 skills x 3 levels.** Each country is one route; its stops are activity numbers.
- Grades 3–5 have 12 activities per level: one stop each. Grades 6–8 have 24–28: one stop per two activities, the last stop takes the remainder.
- Finishing a route earns a bought country sticker for the booklet.
- _My call:_ the paper passport shows **country names, never the level letters** A/B/C. A teacher key maps country to skill and level, so families do not see tiers.
- The skill pages become route pages: per skill, three country rows with a strip of small stop boxes and a sticker circle.
- Digital: My Passport shows the same countries and stop counts from saved results (read-only view of existing data).

## 4. Family Trip (proposed)

- A trip is **one skill, six stops, one 10-minute session per week**, led by a grown-up. Stops unlock in order.
- A stop = Look (talk prompt, any language) / Do (1–2 activities) / Say (child tells one sentence; grown-up checks the box).
- The last stop is a "souvenir": the child's best sentence or recording, saved with "Keep for my teacher".
- **Data (verified in code):** the planner (`src/family/plan.js`, `buildPracticePlan`) already orders unfinished, then retries, then new work, and builds `/play?ids=…` playlists. A stop is the planner's next set for the trip's skill, frozen when the stop starts. New state is small: `{ skill, level, stopsDone, startedOn }` in `prefs.family`.
- **Caveat (verified):** the skill graph (`src/lang/skill-graph.js`) is flat, with no prerequisite order. "Sequential" means the authored activity order inside a level plus the planner's priorities, not a words-to-paragraph ladder. Whether authored order runs easy to hard is unchecked.
- **Need:** suggest the skill with the least practice evidence (the "suggested next domain" My Passport already computes), with a teacher override through the existing `?skill=…&level=…` link.
- Paper: Family Check-In becomes a Trip Log (two trips per page, a route line of six stop boxes each; a star sticker per checked stop; a trip stamp at the end).
- Progress is per device like everything else; the paper box is the authoritative record.

## 5. Postcards with the camera (proposed, later)

- After a stop, the student photographs **an object or place, not a person**, and says or writes 1–2 sentences about it (a picture-description task). The lab adds a city name, date and stamp frame, making an album postcard.
- Stored on-device only (IndexedDB, same pattern as kept audio), deletable from My Passport, 30-day expiry, excluded from transfer codes unless the student chooses to share.
- Re-saving through a canvas drops location metadata. Rear camera by default.
- Fallback: choose a lab picture or draw one, so no camera is never a barrier. Offer "download postcard" because Safari can evict site data after about a week unused.
- **Needs review before building:** school device policy on cameras and the lab's privacy document (`docs/access-practice-lab/privacy-and-ai-governance.md`), since photos of children are student records.
