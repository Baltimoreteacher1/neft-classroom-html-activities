# Community Math Studio Evaluation Plan

Version: 1.0 · 2026-07-14

## Evaluation questions

1. Do students improve in grade-level mathematical modeling and reasoning from baseline to transfer?
2. Do multilingual learners demonstrate growth in explaining, arguing, and discussing mathematics without reduced mathematical demand?
3. Which supports expand participation, revision, and successful transfer?
4. Can teachers outside the design team implement the model with acceptable time, adaptation, and support?
5. Which learner groups experience weaker access, completion, revision, or outcomes, and what design changes address those gaps?

## Design

Use a staged mixed-method implementation study. Do not claim causality from a single classroom pre/post comparison.

### Stage 1: classroom feasibility

- Two flagship projects in one or more classrooms.
- Common baseline and transfer tasks.
- Moderated rubric scoring.
- Student work progression and experience survey.
- Teacher implementation log and interview.
- Primary goal: identify workflow, rubric, support, and measurement failures.

### Stage 2: external replication

- At least three classrooms or schools in meaningfully different contexts.
- Same core measures and minimum implementation expectations.
- Fidelity/adaptation record.
- Double-scored sample from every site.
- Primary goal: determine transferability and support burden.

### Stage 3: comparative effectiveness

- Use a reasonable comparison or historical benchmark when feasible and ethical.
- Predefine the analysis, exclusions, subgroup reporting, and missing-data treatment.
- Measure delayed or unfamiliar transfer rather than interface completion alone.
- Primary goal: estimate likely impact and uncertainty, not manufacture a winning result.

## Measures

| Construct | Measure | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Content understanding | Short standards-aligned performance task | Baseline and transfer |
| Modeling | Common analytic rubric: formulation, assumptions, representation, calculation, validation, limitations, revision, communication | Draft, final, transfer |
| Mathematical language | Separate rubric: discourse organization, sentence relationships, word/phrase precision, representation-language connection | Draft and final |
| Critique and revision | Peer-feedback quality plus revision memo | Mid-project |
| Agency | Choice record, reflection, and brief student survey | During and after |
| Opportunity to learn | Participation, support use, completion, critique, revision, and transfer access | Throughout |
| Experience | Student and teacher survey/interview, including negative cases | After project |
| Implementation | Time, fidelity, adaptations, failures, and support requests | Every session |
| Authentic contribution | Audience feedback and evidence that feedback affected a model or decision | Defense/follow-up |

## Shared modeling rubric dimensions

Use four performance levels with annotated anchors.

1. **Problem formulation:** identifies the decision, quantities, constraints, and success criteria.
2. **Assumptions:** states reasonable assumptions, units, unknowns, and consequences.
3. **Representations:** constructs and connects at least two useful representations or strategies.
4. **Accuracy:** calculates correctly and interprets quantities in context.
5. **Validation:** tests reasonableness using appropriate evidence or cases.
6. **Tradeoffs and limitations:** compares options and acknowledges where a model may fail.
7. **Revision:** makes a substantive mathematical change in response to evidence or critique.
8. **Communication:** defends the decision with claim, evidence, reasoning, and response to questions.
9. **Transfer:** adapts the underlying relationship to a materially changed case.

## Language-development rubric

Language evidence supplements rather than replaces the mathematics score.

- **Discourse:** organizes an explanation or argument for an audience.
- **Sentence:** expresses causal, conditional, comparative, and qualifying relationships.
- **Word/phrase:** uses quantities, units, mathematical terms, and referents precisely enough to communicate meaning.
- **Multimodality:** connects language with symbols, tables, diagrams, graphs, manipulatives, or prototypes.
- **Interaction:** asks for clarification, revoices, compares, challenges with evidence, and revises.

Do not score accent, speaking speed, or English conventions as mathematical understanding.

## Scoring quality

- Create at least three annotated anchor papers per performance level.
- Train scorers on the construct and common misconceptions.
- Double-score at least 20% of the sample at every site.
- Report exact agreement and an appropriate reliability statistic when the sample permits.
- Resolve rubric ambiguity by revising descriptors, not by silently forcing agreement.
- Keep versioned copies of rubrics and anchors.

## Analysis and reporting

- Report sample size, dates, dosage, completion, missing evidence, and exclusions.
- Show baseline, final, and transfer distributions—not only averages.
- Report change and effect magnitude with uncertainty when defensible.
- Review outcomes and opportunity-to-learn measures across locally relevant learner groups.
- Suppress interpretations based on very small groups.
- Include representative strong, developing, and contradictory student work.
- Publish null and negative findings and the resulting design changes.
- Distinguish usage/completion telemetry from demonstrated learning.
