# The Language Page — usability study protocol

**Status:** a protocol, written 2026-11-25. **No sessions have been run.** Run it before any claim about ease of use.
Needs real participants and consent (H-09).

## Participants (aim for 5 per persona; stop a persona when no new problems appear)
| Persona | Needs to be able to |
| --- | --- |
| Grade 1–2 learner with a caregiver nearby | Start a story, hear it, tap an answer, find the way back |
| Grade 3–5 newcomer (months in the US) | Choose a task, pick a support level, finish, see feedback |
| Grade 6–8 learner | Write a claim-evidence paragraph, get feedback, revise |
| ESOL teacher | Find a task for a group, see who needed support, plan tomorrow |
| Classroom (content) teacher | Understand what the lab does and set up a class in under 10 minutes |
| Spanish-speaking caregiver | Understand what their child practised and do the at-home task |

## Setup
Real device each participant uses (phone, Chromebook, tablet); screen and audio recording only with consent; a facilitator
and a silent note-taker; no coaching during tasks; sessions of 30 minutes (learners) and 45 minutes (adults).

## Tasks (adapt per persona; each has a success criterion written before the session)
1. From the home page, get to the right place for you without help.
2. Do one practice task from start to finish, choosing a support level.
3. Say what you would do next after finishing.
4. (Teachers) Open the teacher hub, read the evidence for a class, and choose a next task.
5. (Caregivers) Switch the language to Spanish and find what your child practised.
6. Find how to delete everything saved on this device (privacy page).

## Think-aloud script (read aloud; Spanish version for Spanish-speaking participants)
> "We are testing the website, not you. There are no wrong answers. Please say out loud what you are looking at, what you
> are trying to do and what you expect to happen. If you get stuck, tell me what you would do next. I may not answer
> questions during the tasks, but I will answer everything at the end. You can stop at any time."
>
> "Estamos probando el sitio web, no a usted. No hay respuestas incorrectas. Diga en voz alta lo que ve, lo que quiere
> hacer y lo que espera que pase. Si se atasca, diga qué haría. Puede que no responda preguntas durante las tareas, pero
> responderé todo al final. Puede parar cuando quiera."

## Measures
- **Task success** (yes / yes with help / no), time on task, errors and hesitations, and where they happened.
- **System Usability Scale (SUS)**, 10 items, 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree (adults and Grade 6–8):
  1 I think I would like to use this system frequently. 2 I found it unnecessarily complex. 3 I thought it was easy to use.
  4 I think I would need support from a technical person to use it. 5 I found the functions well integrated.
  6 I thought there was too much inconsistency. 7 I imagine most people would learn it very quickly. 8 I found it very
  cumbersome to use. 9 I felt very confident using it. 10 I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going.
  Score: odd items minus 1, even items 5 minus the answer, sum, times 2.5.
- **UMUX-Lite**, 2 items (capabilities; ease), 7-point scale.
- For young learners: a three-face scale and one open question ("What was hard? What was fun?"), read aloud.

## Severity rating (per problem)
0 not a problem · 1 cosmetic · 2 minor, workaround found · 3 major, task nearly failed · 4 blocker, task failed.
Fix severity 3–4 before the next round; log everything with the route, device and persona.
