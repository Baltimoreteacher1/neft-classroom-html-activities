# The Language Page: art direction and content system

## Editorial promise

The site should feel like a welcoming, carefully edited learning library. A family should see a short practice choice and plain language help; a student should see inviting places to learn; a teacher should see classroom tasks and shareable resources. Every screen should answer three questions in order: **Where am I? What can I do here? What happens when I open this?**

## Direction explored

| Direction | Strength | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning field guide | Warm paper, precise headings, illustrated learning scenes | Use for role hubs and content collections |
| Game arcade | Energy and bold art | Reserve for individual games; too busy for finding resources |
| Institutional portal | Dense information and efficient scanning | Use its clear metadata and filters, without its visual coldness |

The chosen direction extends the existing Fraunces and Atkinson Hyperlegible pair and the site's warm paper, harbor navy, teal, and gold. It keeps artwork purposeful: a picture should tell a learner what kind of activity follows. A single gold accent identifies the suggested starting action; teal denotes interaction and progress. Domain colors may label skills, but do not compete with the primary action.

## Page grammar

1. **Orientation:** audience, page title, one sentence explaining what the page offers.
2. **Start:** one primary action, with time and grade when known.
3. **Browse:** grouped resources labeled by user task, not by internal product name.
4. **Details:** skill, expected time, format, support, and who can use it, in the same order on every resource card.
5. **Recovery:** obvious Back, change grade, help, and print or offline paths where applicable.

The family, student, and teacher hubs now apply this hierarchy. The Resource Finder remains the complete catalog, while curated hub choices provide a short path into it. For every new activity, its title and summary should say what the learner will actually do. Avoid claims about test scores or official WIDA status.

## Visual specification

- **Type:** Fraunces for titles and section headings; Atkinson Hyperlegible for directions, controls, metadata, and long text. Keep body copy at 16 px or larger. Use sentence case.
- **Color:** warm off-white `#f8f3ea` as the dominant page; paper `#fffcf5` for content; navy `#123a5c` for headings and actions; teal `#0f766e` for focus and progress; gold `#e2a72e` for one featured action. Keep existing semantic success, error, and domain colors.
- **Spacing:** 8 px rhythm, with 24–40 px between major sections. Let collections breathe while keeping task choices visible on the first screen.
- **Shape:** existing 16–30 px radius, thin borders, restrained shadows. The hero uses two background layers to create depth without placing text on an illustration.
- **Art:** use the repository's original scene library. Favor a specific action or setting over a decorative mascot. Each image needs an editorial brief: what concept it teaches, where it appears, and whether it conveys information. Decorative art gets empty alt text; instructional art gets a concise description.
- **Motion:** only a small hover lift for cards and clear state feedback. Honor reduced motion. Never make animation necessary for understanding.

## Production sequence for the remaining library

1. **Inventory:** use the existing content index and Resource Finder metadata to find pages missing grade, skill, time, format, or learner support. Fix source records, then regenerate derived indexes.
2. **Edit:** review each activity's title, directions, example, feedback, and printable. Check that one activity leads to one clear learning move and that the language matches the grade band.
3. **Design:** apply the page grammar to activity families through their shared templates. Keep student work and teacher keys separate. Use a small set of scene compositions, not one-off graphics on every card.
4. **Proof:** review each template at phone, Chromebook, and desktop widths; keyboard and screen-reader order; contrast, focus, zoom, and print. Sample content from every grade band and skill.

This is a template-led upgrade. It avoids hand-editing hundreds of generated pages and makes later content improvements consistent across the site.
