The Ink Inventory of 1748

Students will argue to the print-shop apprentices how what the inventory actually counts changes the next action, using a cited line rather than a general impression.

Grades 3-5 · guided

Reading

The Ink Inventory of 1748 is on the table for the print-shop apprentices. The inventory lists 40 pounds of lampblack ink and 12 pounds of vermilion on 3 March 1748. The record keeps that line beside the time, the tool, and the people who must act.

A later margin note says vermilion was scarce that spring. The inventory itself does not say why. Apprentices have to keep the count separate from the guess.

Writing

Write to the print-shop apprentices about The Ink Inventory of 1748. Tell them what the inventory actually counts. Use because or so, and cite this line in your own sentence: The inventory lists 40 pounds of lampblack ink and 12 pounds of vermilion on 3 March 1748.

I am writing to the print-shop apprentices because ___.

The record says ___, so ___.

  • The audience is named.
  • A line from the source is cited.
  • because or so links the reason to the action.
