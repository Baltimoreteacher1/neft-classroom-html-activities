Teacher Studio

From a standard to a reviewed, classroom-ready resource — with the review steps built into the path instead of left to memory.

Every tool below already existed and already worked. Teacher Studio does not rebuild them or move them onto one screen; it puts them in order and adds the checkpoints that keep generated material from reaching students unreviewed.

The guided workflow

Ten steps, four review gates

Steps 5 through 8 are the reason this workflow exists. They are not optional and they are not automated away.

1 · Choose a unit, lesson, or standard

Start from the canonical curriculum registry rather than a blank prompt. A generator constrained to real curriculum data cannot invent a unit title or a standard that contradicts the rest of the site.

  • Curriculum HubTen units, the full lesson directory, and every standard.
  • Standards Shift StudioWork between the 2025 Maryland codes and the older CCSS codes.

2 · Choose what you need

Lesson plan, cards, study pack, worksheet, unit plan, or a Canvas package.

3 · Generate or locate it

Use the tool that already does this job. Nothing here is new.

  • Lesson Plan GeneratorA full plan from a lesson or standard.
  • Math Card BuilderTask cards, sorts, and practice sets.
  • Study Pack MakerA revision pack for a unit or a standard.
  • Unit Plan BuilderPlan a whole unit's sequence and resources.
  • Resource FinderFind what already exists before making something new.
  • Do Now GeneratorA warm-up matched to the lesson's standard.

4 · Mathematical validation

Check the mathematics itself: are the numbers right, do the models match the standard, is the difficulty where the lesson expects it? This is a teacher's judgment supported by the tools, not replaced by them.

5 · Answer-key validation

Every worked answer is checked against the problem as printed. The repository's answer-key checks run over the project pages; a hand-built resource needs the same pass before it is duplicated.

6 · Accessibility review

Readable at 200% zoom, keyboard-operable if interactive, labelled form controls, adequate contrast, and a print layout that works. A resource that fails here is not finished.

7 · ESOL-support review

Does it carry a language objective? Is the vocabulary previewed? Is a writing scaffold available for any substantial written response — without changing the mathematical target?

Language Bridge holds the scaffold ladder this step checks against.

8 · Review and edit

Generated instructional content is never published automatically. A teacher reads it, edits it, and decides. This is the difference between a tool and an unattended content pipeline.

9 · Export or publish

  • Canvas StudioPackage a resource for the LMS.
  • SCORM BuilderBuild a SCORM package for an assignable activity.
  • Canvas SetupWire the course up before the first import.

10 · Register the resource

Record the finished resource against its lesson in the curriculum registry. This is the step that keeps the site and the registry from drifting apart — and it is why a new resource cannot introduce a second, conflicting version of a unit title or a standard.

Registering writes to repository data, so it completes on a maintainer workstation rather than in the browser. See docs/award-portfolio/maintainer-checklist.md.

Security posture

What is deliberately not here

The published site does not expose, on any route:

  • Shell or script execution, package-manager controls, or process controls.
  • Terminal output, server logs, or environment values.
  • Development-server controls, or a connection status that implies one.
  • A student roster, student names, or resume codes without the teacher-key check.

The repository's build, audit, and packaging scripts are workstation commands. The Math Command Center now shows its workspace overview on the public site and enables the local-server integration only when the page is actually served from a developer machine. Roster data is reachable through the gradebook and its existing teacher key.
