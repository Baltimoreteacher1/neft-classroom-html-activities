Core test

Does AI remove a barrier while students still do the target thinking?

Access support removes a barrier while preserving the learning target. Overreliance removes the learning demand itself.

OSAMR quick reference

OSAMR at a glance

The goal is not the highest level. The goal is the right level for the learning target.

S

Substitute

AI substitutes one access support. Students still do the target thinking.

A

Augment

AI adds helpful support. The task becomes clearer without being solved.

M

Modify

AI meaningfully changes the experience. The learning target stays the same.

R

Redefine

AI makes a previously inaccessible experience possible. Rigor is preserved.

!

Overreliance

AI performs the target thinking. The learning demand is removed.

Evidence tokens. Use these five labels to keep discussion grounded: 1. Barrier — what blocked access? 2. Thinking — what did students still do? 3. AI Action — what did AI provide? 4. Red Flag — what would cross the line? 5. Fix-It — how could support improve?

Model Case

Watch how the decision is made.

Grade 6 Math · Proportional relationships · Directions rewritten in plain language

Go to case rooms

The case

Sixth-grade students decide whether a table shows a proportional relationship. Several multilingual learners understand the math, but the directions are dense and the word ratio is unfamiliar. The teacher uses AI to rewrite the directions in plain language and add a short vocabulary note. Students still calculate ratios, decide whether they are equal, and explain their answer.

Teacher AI input

Rewrite these directions in plain language. Add one short note for “ratio.” Do not solve, name the operation, or tell students if the table is proportional.

The analysis

Barrier removed

Dense directions and unfamiliar vocabulary made it hard to start.

Thinking preserved

Students still calculate ratios, decide proportional or not, and explain why.

OSAMR level

A — Augment. AI makes the task clearer without solving it.

Evidence

AI does not find the constant ratio.

Red flag

AI gives the answer or tells students which operation to use.

Model takeaway: the support changes access to the task, not the math thinking.

Multi-Step Problem Entry

Grade 4 Math · Getting-started barrier · AI built an entry scaffold

Case rooms Gallery board
Case brief
LOW / MEDIUM RISK

1. Case story

A fourth-grade math teacher teaches multi-digit multiplication with: “A school orders 24 boxes of crayons. Each box has 16 crayons. How many crayons are there in all?” Several students with IEPs struggle to know where to start. AI creates a problem-entry scaffold and sentence frame. Students set up and solve independently.

2. Teacher AI input

“Create a three-step entry scaffold: circle the numbers, underline the question, and draw a quick sketch. Add this frame: “I need to find ___ because ___.” Do not name the operation or solve.”

Learning target: Choose an operation, set up the problem, solve, and explain the reasoning.
Barrier clue: Students struggle to start and organize information.
Student thinking: Students still choose a strategy, set up the problem, compute, and explain.

Group 6 Case Room · 9–12 Science

Data Summarized for CER

Grade 9–12 Science · Large-dataset barrier · AI chose the evidence

Case rooms Gallery board
Case brief
HIGH RISK

1. Case story

Students analyze a local air-quality dataset to write a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph. The teacher uses AI to summarize the dataset into three key findings. Students use those AI findings as evidence. They write their own claim and reasoning, but they do not examine the full dataset or choose evidence themselves.

2. Teacher AI input

“Summarize this local air-quality dataset into three key findings students can use as evidence in CER paragraphs.”

Learning target: Analyze data, select evidence, and write a claim with supporting reasoning.
Barrier clue: The full dataset may be too large or dense to analyze efficiently.
Student thinking: AI removes too much target thinking by choosing the evidence.
