Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Use the formula A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h to find the area of each trapezoid.

    TrapezoidBase 1Base 2HeightArea
    Shape A4 ft6 ft3 ft
    Shape B8 ft12 ft5 ft

    Hint Each row lists Base 1, Base 2, and Height — the Area column is the only blank to fill.

    Why: The trapezoid formula adds the two bases, multiplies by the height, and then takes half: A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h. Shape A gives ½ × (4 + 6) × 3 = ½ × 30 = 15 sq ft, and Shape B gives ½ × (8 + 12) × 5 = ½ × 100 = 50 sq ft.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the area of a trapezoid with bases 6 cm and 10 cm, and height 4 cm?

    1. A24 sq cm
    2. B32 sq cm
    3. C40 sq cm
    4. D64 sq cm

    Hint A trapezoid has two bases (6 cm and 10 cm) plus a height (4 cm) — all three belong in the formula.

    Answer: B — 32 sq cm

    Why: A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h = ½ × (6 + 10) × 4 = ½ × 16 × 4 = 32 sq cm.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Area of Trapezoids is forgetting the ½ in A = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h. After adding the bases and multiplying by the height, students stop there instead of taking half — for example, with bases 6 ft and 10 ft and a height of 4 ft, they compute (6 + 10) × 4 = 64 sq ft instead of taking half to get the correct 32 sq ft. That unhalved number is really the area of the parallelogram made from two trapezoids, not the area of one trapezoid. Before you submit, always ask: "Did I take half of my last answer?"

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Missing Half

    1. 1Read the blueprintA trapezoid-shaped patio stone has b1 = 4 ft, b2 = 8 ft, and h = 6 ft.
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h
    3. 3Add the bases, then multiply by height(4 + 8) × 6 = 12 × 6 = 72
    4. 4State the areaA = 72 sq ft

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Compare the last step to the formula A = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h — what operation is missing?

    Mistake: Step 4. The work found (4 + 8) × 6 = 72 and stopped there, never applying the ½ from the formula. That 72 is the area of the parallelogram you get from two copies of this trapezoid, so one trapezoid is half of it: A = ½ × 72 = 36 sq ft.

    Correct work: The student forgot to multiply by ½, so their answer is double the true area. The correct area is A = ½ × (4 + 8) × 6 = ½ × 72 = 36 sq ft, not 72 sq ft.

  4. 4Mark and label each value on the number line.

    Find the missing height: b₁=6, b₂=10, A=48

    Answer: height at 6

    Why: Substituting into A = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h gives 48 = ½ × (6 + 10) × h. Since ½ × 16 = 8, the equation becomes 48 = 8h, so h = 48 ÷ 8 = 6 and the point belongs at 6 on the number line.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Using the Wrong Height

    1. 1Read the blueprintA trapezoid window has b1 = 7 ft and b2 = 13 ft. The slanted side measures 10 ft, and the straight-up height between the bases is 6 ft.
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × (7 + 13) × 10
    4. 4CalculateA = ½ × 20 × 10 = 100 sq ft

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The 10 ft slanted side was substituted for the height, but the height of a trapezoid is the straight-up perpendicular distance between the two bases, which is 6 ft here. Using the true height gives A = ½ × (7 + 13) × 6 = ½ × 20 × 6 = 60 sq ft; the slant is always longer, which is why 100 sq ft came out too big.

    Correct work: The student used the slanted side (10 ft) as the height instead of the true perpendicular height (6 ft). The height in the trapezoid formula must be measured straight up between the two bases, not along the slanted side. The correct area is A = ½ × (7 + 13) × 6 = ½ × 20 × 6 = 60 sq ft, not 100 sq ft.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Plot the trapezoid and calculate its area.

    Answer: A, B, C, D

    Why: Plotting A(0,0), B(10,0), C(7,6), and D(3,6) forms a trapezoid whose bottom base runs 10 units, whose top base from x = 3 to x = 7 runs 4 units, and whose height is the 6-unit vertical gap between them. So A = ½ × (10 + 4) × 6 = ½ × 14 × 6 = 42 square units.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Area Error

    1. 1Identify the bases and heightb₁ = 10 m, b₂ = 6 m, h = 7 m
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × (10 + 6) × 7
    4. 4CalculateA = (10 + 6) × 7 = 112 sq m

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The ½ appears in the formula and in the substitution, then disappears in the final calculation. Keeping it gives A = ½ × 16 × 7 = ½ × 112 = 56 sq m, which is exactly half of the 112 that was reported.

    Correct work: The student forgot to multiply by ½. A = ½ × 16 × 7 = ½ × 112 = 56 sq m.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A trapezoid and a parallelogram both have a height of 8 ft. The trapezoid has bases of 5 ft and 11 ft. The parallelogram has a base of 7 ft. Which shape has the greater area? Explain your reasoning using the formulas.

    Look for: trapezoid, parallelogram, formula, compare, area, height, base

    Why: The trapezoid's area is ½ × (5 + 11) × 8 = ½ × 16 × 8 = 64 sq ft and the parallelogram's is 7 × 8 = 56 sq ft, so the trapezoid is greater by 8 sq ft. Since both shapes share the height of 8 ft, the comparison really comes down to the trapezoid's average base, ½ × (5 + 11) = 8 ft, against the parallelogram's base of 7 ft.
