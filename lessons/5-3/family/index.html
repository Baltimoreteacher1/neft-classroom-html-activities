Unit 5, Lesson 3: Determine the Area of Trapezoids6.GR.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A trapezoid is a four-sided shape with one pair of parallel sides. The two parallel sides are called the bases, and they have different lengths.

Key idea: A trapezoid has two parallel sides called bases. To find its area, add the two bases, multiply by the height, then take half. Bases 6 ft and 10 ft with height 4 ft: (6 + 10) × 4 ÷ 2 = 32 square feet.
Idea clave: Un trapecio tiene dos lados paralelos llamados bases. Para hallar su área, suma las dos bases, multiplica por la altura y luego toma la mitad. Bases de 6 pies y 10 pies con altura de 4 pies: (6 + 10) × 4 ÷ 2 = 32 pies cuadrados.

Example

  1. My trapezoid window has a top base of 4 feet, a bottom base of 8 feet, and a height of 5 feet.
  2. I write the formula: A = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h.
  3. I add the bases first: 4 + 8 = 12.
  4. I multiply by the height: 12 × 5 = 60.
  5. I take half: ½ × 60 = 30. So the area is 30 square feet.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Area of Trapezoids is forgetting the ½ in A = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h. After adding the bases and multiplying by the height, students stop there instead of taking half — for example, with bases 6 ft and 10 ft and a height of 4 ft, they compute (6 + 10) × 4 = 64 sq ft instead of taking half to get the correct 32 sq ft. That unhalved number is really the area of the parallelogram made from two trapezoids, not the area of one trapezoid. Before you submit, always ask: "Did I take half of my last answer?"
