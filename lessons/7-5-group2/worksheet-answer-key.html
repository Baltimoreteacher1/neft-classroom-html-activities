Learning target I can plot and identify points on the coordinate plane using ordered pairs, explain what each part stands for, and build one for a situation I have not seen before.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Plot these points to reveal a hidden shape: (1, 1), (1, 5), (6, 5), (6, 1)

    Answer: A, B, C, D

    Why: Each point starts at the origin, moves right the x amount, then up the y amount: A(1, 1), B(1, 5), C(6, 5), D(6, 1). A and B share x = 1, so B sits straight above A, and B and C share y = 5, so they line up across. Connected in order, the four points form a rectangle 5 units wide and 4 units tall.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each point to its quadrant or axis.

    1. A(3, 2)
    2. B(-4, 5)
    3. C(-2, -6)
    4. D(7, -1)
    5. E(0, 3)
    6. F(5, 0)
    • AQuadrant I
    • BQuadrant II
    • CQuadrant III
    • DQuadrant IV
    • Ey-axis
    • Fx-axis

    Why: The signs of the two coordinates place each point. (3, 2) is (+, +) in Quadrant I; (-4, 5) is (-, +) in Quadrant II; (-2, -6) is (-, -) in Quadrant III; and (7, -1) is (+, -) in Quadrant IV. A coordinate of 0 puts the point on an axis instead of inside a quadrant, so (0, 3) is on the y-axis and (5, 0) is on the x-axis.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Spot 2 — The map marks the Lookout Tower at (4, 9). What does the 4 tell you to do from the origin?

    1. AMove 4 units to the right along the x-axis
    2. BMove 4 units up along the y-axis
    3. CMove 4 units to the left
    4. DStay on the y-axis at 4

    Answer: A — Move 4 units to the right along the x-axis

    Why: The first number in (x, y) is the x-coordinate, so 4 means move 4 units to the right along the x-axis before moving up.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph on the Coordinate Plane is swapping the order of the coordinates — moving up/down first instead of right/left first. For example, when plotting (5, 2), students often move up 5 and right 2, landing on the wrong point (2, 5) instead of (5, 2). Remember: the x-coordinate (first number) always tells you how far to move right or left along the x-axis; the y-coordinate (second number) tells you how far up or down along the y-axis.

    Spot 3 — Vega writes: 'The Coconut Grove is 3 across and 8 up.' Which point on the map is the Coconut Grove?

    1. A(0, 8)
    2. B(3, 8)
    3. C(3, 3)
    4. D(8, 3)

    Answer: B — (3, 8)

    Why: Across (3) is the x-coordinate and up (8) is the y-coordinate, so the Coconut Grove is at (3, 8).

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph on the Coordinate Plane is swapping the order of the coordinates — moving up/down first instead of right/left first. For example, when plotting (5, 2), students often move up 5 and right 2, landing on the wrong point (2, 5) instead of (5, 2). Remember: the x-coordinate (first number) always tells you how far to move right or left along the x-axis; the y-coordinate (second number) tells you how far up or down along the y-axis.

  2. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which ordered pair names a point 3 units right and 7 units up from the origin?

    1. A(0, 7)
    2. B(3, 7)
    3. C(3, 3)
    4. D(7, 3)

    Answer: B — (3, 7)

    Why: The first coordinate is horizontal (right 3), the second is vertical (up 7). So (3, 7).

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph on the Coordinate Plane is swapping the order of the coordinates — moving up/down first instead of right/left first. For example, when plotting (5, 2), students often move up 5 and right 2, landing on the wrong point (2, 5) instead of (5, 2). Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 2 Extension — Captain Vega's Misread Coordinate

    1. 1ClueThe Pirate Flag is at (3, 6).
    2. 2Read the x-coordinateMove 3 units to the right along the x-axis.
    3. 3Read the y-coordinateMove 1 unit up along the y-axis.
    4. 4Plot the pointPlace the flag where (3, 1) would go.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. The x move is right: 3 units across. The mistake is the y move — the second number in (3, 6) is 6, not 1, so the flag should rise 6 units after the 3 right. Placing it at (3, 1) puts the Pirate Flag five units too low.

    Correct work: The x move is correct (3 right), but the y-coordinate is 6, not 1. The navigator should move 6 units up, placing the flag at (3, 6) — not (3, 1).
