Where we left off

Our goal: I can draw polygons from their vertex coordinates on the coordinate plane, and use coordinates to find side lengths and solve perimeter and area problems, explain what each part stands for, and build one for a situation I have not seen before.

The big idea: When two vertices share a coordinate, the side between them is the absolute difference of the coordinates that differ — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me find one side

  1. (-4.5, 3) and (5, 3) share y = 3, so the side between them is horizontal.
  2. Its length is the distance between the x-coordinates: |5 − (−4.5)| = 9.5 units.
  3. (5, 3) and (5, -3.75) share x = 5, so that side is vertical: |3 − (−3.75)| = 6.75 units.

  1. 2

    Think it throughThe two known vertices of the park are 3 units apart, and each grid unit represents 100 feet. What is the park's width in feet?

    1. A3 feet
    2. B30 feet
    3. C103 feet
    4. D300 feet

    Why is that the answer?

    Multiply the unit distance by the scale: 3 units × 100 feet per unit = 300 feet. Leaving off the scale (3 feet) or adding instead of multiplying (103 feet) ignores what the scale means.

  2. 3

    Think it throughThe park's area must be 240,000 sq ft and its width is 300 ft. What is its length?

    1. A720 feet
    2. B800 feet
    3. C80,000 feet
    4. D240,300 feet

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    Length = area ÷ width = 240,000 ÷ 300 = 800 feet. Adding the width to the area (240,300) confuses two different operations — area and length combine here by division, not addition.

  3. 4

    Think it throughThe park's length is 800 feet, and each grid unit represents 100 feet. How many units below the known vertices do the new corners sit?

    1. A0.8 units
    2. B8 units
    3. C80 units
    4. D800 units

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    Convert feet back to units by dividing by the scale: 800 ÷ 100 = 8 units. Dividing by 10 instead of 100 gives 80, and skipping the conversion entirely leaves the answer in feet (800), not map units.

  4. 5

    Back to the modelThe vertices (-4.5, 3) and (5, 3) share y = 3, so the side between them is horizontal, with length |5 − (−4.5)| = 9.5. Why is that side's length found by comparing the x-coordinates instead of the y-coordinates?

    When two vertices share the same y-coordinate, the segment between them is horizontal, so its length comes from the DIFFERENCE in the coordinate that changes — the x-coordinates.

Explain the thinking

  1. 6

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 7

    Say moreYou sorted pairs of vertices into 'subtract coordinates' versus 'slanted.' How did you decide that (0, 0) and (4, 3) belonged in the slanted group?

    Coordinates can be subtracted directly only when two vertices share an x-value or a y-value; if BOTH coordinates differ, the segment is slanted and this lesson's subtraction method doesn't apply.

  1. 9

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — A rectangle has vertices (-1, 4), (5, 4), (5, 1), (-1, 1). What is its perimeter?

    1. A6 units
    2. B9 units
    3. C18 units
    4. D18 square units

    Explain your choice.

    Width |5 − (−1)| = 6, height |4 − 1| = 3. Perimeter = 2(6) + 2(3) = 18 units.

  2. 10

    From Lesson 7.6Explain your thinking — What is the distance between the points (3, -2) and (3, 5)?

    1. A3 units
    2. B5 units
    3. C7 units
    4. D9 units

    How do you know?

    Both points share x = 3, so this is a vertical distance. |5 - (-2)| = |5 + 2| = 7 units.
