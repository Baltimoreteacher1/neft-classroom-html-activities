Learning target With my small group, I can graph the values from a ratio table as points on the coordinate plane — one step at a time, with support.

Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Points from a ratio table are plotted at (1, 4), (2, 8), and (3, 12). What is the ratio of y to x for each point?

    1. A1:4
    2. B2:8
    3. C3:4
    4. D4:1

    Hint The question asks y to x — the up-value compared to the across-value, in that order.

    Answer: D — 4:1

    Why: For each point: 4÷1 = 4, 8÷2 = 4, 12÷3 = 4. The ratio of y to x is always 4:1.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph Ratio Tables is writing the ordered pair in the wrong order — putting the second table value first instead of matching (x, y) to the table's column order. For example, if a recipe table shows 2 cups of sugar for every 4 cups of flour, a student might plot (4, 2) instead of (2, 4), landing the point in a completely different spot on the grid. Before you plot, check: which quantity goes on the x-axis, and does your ordered pair start with that number?

    Two students graph ratio tables. Student A plots (1,2), (2,4), (3,6). Student B plots (1,3), (2,6), (3,9). Whose line is steeper?

    1. AStudent A
    2. BSame steepness
    3. CCannot tell
    4. DStudent B

    Hint Steepness comes from how fast y climbs each time x grows by 1 — not from who has bigger points overall.

    Answer: D — Student B

    Why: Student A's ratio is 1:2 (y goes up 2 for each 1 in x). Student B's ratio is 1:3 (y goes up 3 for each 1 in x). A higher rate of change means a steeper line, so Student B's is steeper.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Recipe: 1 cup berries to 2 scoops yogurt. With cups on x and scoops on y, which pair shows 3 cups?

    1. A(3, 6)
    2. B(3, 5)
    3. C(3, 3)
    4. D(6, 3)

    Hint On this graph the x-coordinate always stands for cups and the y-coordinate always stands for scoops.

    Answer: A — (3, 6)

    Why: Cups (x) come first, scoops (y) second. 3 cups need 3 × 2 = 6 scoops, so the point is (3, 6).

  4. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    On the smoothie graph, every point follows the rule scoops = 2 × cups. Which equation describes the pattern between x (cups) and y (scoops)?

    1. Ay = 2x
    2. By = x + 2
    3. Cy = x ÷ 2
    4. Dx = 2y

    Hint An equation is just the rule of the ratio table rewritten with letters.

    Answer: A — y = 2x

    Why: Each cup needs 2 scoops, so y (scoops) is always 2 times x (cups): y = 2x. For 4 cups, y = 2 × 4 = 8.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 1 — Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the ratioA recipe uses 1 cup of sugar for every 2 cups of flour. Plot the point for 3 cups of sugar.
    2. 2Find the flour3 cups of sugar means 3 + 2 = 5 cups of flour.
    3. 3Write the ordered pair(3, 5)

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Look at Step 2. The student added 2 to the sugar instead of multiplying by 2.

    Mistake: Step 2. The mistake is adding instead of multiplying: the student turned 3 cups of sugar into 3 + 2 = 5 cups of flour. A ratio of 1 sugar to 2 flour means flour is always 2 times the sugar, so 3 cups of sugar needs 2 × 3 = 6 cups of flour and the point is (3, 6).

    Correct work: You should MULTIPLY, not add. The rule is 2 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar, so flour = 2 × sugar. For 3 cups of sugar, flour = 2 × 3 = 6, so the ordered pair is (3, 6), not (3, 5).

  2. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    On Level — Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the ratioA recipe uses 4 cups of water for every 1 cup of rice (rule: y = 4x). A student is plotting points for the line.
    2. 2List the points(1, 4), (2, 8), (3, 12)
    3. 3Add another pointThe student also plots (4, 14) and says it belongs on the same line.
    4. 4ConclusionAll four points form a straight line.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Check the point (4, 14) against the rule y = 4x. Does 14 equal 4 × 4?

    Mistake: Step 3. The first three points all follow y = 4x, but the extra point (4, 14) does not: when x = 4 the rule requires y = 4 × 4 = 16. Since (4, 14) breaks the rule, it falls below the line instead of on it, and the correct fourth point is (4, 16).

    Correct work: The point (4, 14) is off the line. Using the rule y = 4x, when x = 4 the y-value must be 4 × 4 = 16, so the correct point is (4, 16). Because (4, 14) does not satisfy y = 4x, it does not land on the line through the other points.
