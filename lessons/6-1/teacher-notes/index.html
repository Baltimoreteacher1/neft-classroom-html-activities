Unit 6, Lesson 1: Division Expressions with Fractions and Whole Numbers6.NOS.1

Objectives

Warm-up

Let's try 2 ÷ 1/3. The bar model counted 6 thirds inside 2 wholes — now we get there with the steps.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Dividing Whole Numbers & Unit Fractions. 1. Put a 1 under the whole number: 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3/1 ÷ 1/4. 2. Keep, Change, Flip: 3/1 ÷ 1/4 = 3/1 × 4/1. 3. Multiply across: 3/1 × 4/1 = 12/1 = 12

I do:

  1. I want to cut a 3-foot strip of tape into 1/4-foot pieces, so I write 3 ÷ 1/4.
  2. The 3 is a whole number, so I put a 1 under it. Now both numbers are fractions: 3/1 ÷ 1/4.
  3. Keep, Change, Flip. I keep 3/1, I change ÷ to ×, and I flip 1/4 to 4/1. That gives 3/1 × 4/1.
  4. I multiply across: 3 × 4 = 12 on top and 1 × 1 = 1 on the bottom, so 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3/1 × 4/1 = 12 pieces.

You do: Now try 1/2 ÷ 4. This time the whole number is the second number instead of the first. Step 1 does not change: put a 1 under the whole number. The 4 becomes 4/1, so you have 1/2 ÷ 4/1. Step 2: Keep, Change, Flip. Keep 1/2, change ÷ to ×, flip 4/1 to 1/4. Multiply across. You should get 1/8. Notice you used the same three steps as before — only the whole number moved.

Common misconception

A common mistake in Interpret Division of Fractions is assuming that dividing always makes the answer smaller. For 4 ÷ 1/3, students reason 'dividing shrinks the number' and compute 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.3 instead of asking how many 1/3-foot pieces fit into 4 feet (each whole foot holds 3 thirds, so 4 × 3 = 12). Before you submit, check: if the divisor is a fraction smaller than 1, the quotient must be BIGGER than the dividend, not smaller.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: Division of Fractions, Reciprocal, Quotient, Dividend, Divisor, Unit fraction (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

What does 5 ÷ 1/4 mean, and what is the quotient?

  • How many 1/4-size pieces fit into 5; quotient is 20 ✓ correct
  • 5 groups of 1/4; quotient is 5/4
  • 1/4 of 5; quotient is 5/4
  • 5 minus 1/4; quotient is 4 3/4

5 ÷ 1/4 means 'how many 1/4-size pieces fit into 5.' Put a 1 under the 5, then Keep, Change, Flip: 5/1 × 4/1 = 20.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.
