Warm-up

Let's try 2 ÷ 1/3. The bar model counted 6 thirds inside 2 wholes — now let's see why.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Dividing Whole Numbers & Fractions. 1. Name the question: 3 ÷ 1/4 asks how many 1/4s fit in 3, and 1/2 ÷ 4 asks how much is in each of 4 equal shares. 2. Use the model: each whole holds 4 fourths, so 3 wholes hold 3 × 4 = 12 fourths, and 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12. 3. Check by multiplying: 12 × 1/4 = 3. Shortcut: dividing by 1/4 gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal, 4

I do:

  1. I want to cut a 3-foot strip of tape into 1/4-foot pieces, so I write 3 ÷ 1/4. It asks: how many 1/4-foot pieces fit in 3 feet?
  2. Each foot holds 4 quarter-foot pieces, so 3 feet hold 3 × 4 = 12 pieces.
  3. That is why the shortcut works: dividing by 1/4 gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal, 4/1. So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3/1 × 4/1 = 12. (Some people call this Keep, Change, Flip.)
  4. Check by multiplying: 12 pieces × 1/4 foot = 12/4 = 3 feet. So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12 pieces.
  5. One more move to know: mixed number → improper fraction: multiply the bottom by the whole number, add the top, keep the same bottom. 3 1/2 → (2 × 3) + 1 = 7 → 7/2.

You do: Now try 1/2 ÷ 4. This one asks how much is in ONE group: half a pan of brownies is shared equally by 4 people. Cut the half into 4 equal parts. Each part is 1/8 of the whole pan, because 8 of those parts would fill the whole pan. The shortcut agrees: dividing by 4 is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, 1/4, so 1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8. Check by multiplying: 4 × 1/8 = 4/8 = 1/2. One more: the top number does not have to be a 1. For 3/4 ÷ 2, share 3/4 into 2 equal parts: 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8, and 2 × 3/8 = 6/8 = 3/4 checks it.

Common misconception

A common mistake in Interpret Division of Fractions is assuming that dividing always makes the answer smaller. For 4 ÷ 1/3, students reason 'dividing shrinks the number' and compute 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.3 instead of asking how many 1/3-foot pieces fit into 4 feet (each whole foot holds 3 thirds, so 4 × 3 = 12). Before you submit, check: if the divisor is a fraction smaller than 1, the quotient must be BIGGER than the dividend, not smaller.

Language & learning supports

Exit ticket

What does 5 ÷ 1/4 mean, and what is the quotient?

  • How many 1/4-size pieces fit into 5; quotient is 20 ✓ correct
  • 1/4 of 5; quotient is 5/4
  • 5 groups of 1/4; quotient is 5/4
  • 5 minus 1/4; quotient is 4 3/4

5 ÷ 1/4 means 'how many 1/4-size pieces fit into 5.' Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal: 5/1 × 4/1 = 20. Check by multiplying: 20 × 1/4 = 20/4 = 5.

Reteach
