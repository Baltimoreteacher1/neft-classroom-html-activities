Lesson 6-1: Division Expressions with Fractions and Whole Numbers

Question 1Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: D

What is the value of 6 ÷ 1/5?

  • A1/30
  • B6/5
  • C11
  • D30

Put a 1 under the whole number so it becomes 6/1. Keep 6/1, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/5 to its reciprocal 5/1. Multiply across: 6/1 × 5/1 = 30/1 = 30.

  • A: That is the reciprocal of the answer. Keep the 6 on top: 6/1 times 5/1.
  • B: That multiplies 6 by 1/5. Division flips the divisor first: 6/1 times 5/1.
  • C: That adds 6 and 5. Keep, change, flip, and then multiply.

Part B — correct answer: B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

  • AKeep 1/5, change ÷ to ×, and flip 6/1 to 1/6: 1/5 × 1/6 = 1/30.
  • BWrite 6 as 6/1. Keep 6/1, change ÷ to ×, flip 1/5 to 5/1, then multiply across: 6/1 × 5/1 = 30.
  • CWrite 6 as 6/1 and multiply straight across without flipping: 6/1 × 1/5 = 6/5.
  • DAdd the whole number and the denominator of the fraction: 6 + 5 = 11.

Keep, change, flip works only after the whole number is written as a fraction over 1, and only the second fraction gets flipped. That gives 6/1 × 5/1 = 30.

Question 2Select all that apply — correct: A, B, C, E

Select ALL statements that are true about the expression 4 ÷ 1/3.

  • AThe whole number 4 can be written as the fraction 4/1.
  • BKeep, change, flip rewrites the expression as 4/1 × 3/1.
  • CThe reciprocal of 1/3 is 3/1.
  • DThe quotient is 4/3.
  • EThe quotient is 12.
  • FThe quotient must be less than 4 because the expression uses division.

Write 4 as 4/1, keep it, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/3 to 3/1. Then 4/1 × 3/1 = 12/1 = 12. The quotient 4/3 comes from multiplying by 1/3 instead of dividing, and dividing by a number smaller than 1 makes the quotient larger, not smaller.
