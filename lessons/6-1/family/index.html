Unit 6, Lesson 1: Division Expressions with Fractions and Whole Numbers

Standard 6.NOS.1

What students are learning Qué aprenden

Key idea: Dividing by a fraction asks how many of those pieces fit. 3 ÷ 1/4 means: how many quarter-foot pieces fit in 3 feet? Each foot has 4 quarters, so 3 × 4 = 12. Check: 12 quarters make 3 feet.Idea clave: Dividir entre una fracción pregunta cuántas de esas piezas caben. 3 ÷ 1/4 significa: ¿cuántas piezas de un cuarto de pie caben en 3 pies? Cada pie tiene 4 cuartos, así que 3 × 4 = 12. Comprueba: 12 cuartos forman 3 pies.

How we did it in class today Cómo lo hicimos hoy en clase

  1. I want to cut a 3-foot strip of tape into 1/4-foot pieces, so I write 3 ÷ 1/4. It asks: how many 1/4-foot pieces fit in 3 feet?
  2. Each foot holds 4 quarter-foot pieces, so 3 feet hold 3 × 4 = 12 pieces.
  3. That is why the shortcut works: dividing by 1/4 gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal, 4/1. So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3/1 × 4/1 = 12. (Some people call this Keep, Change, Flip.)
  4. Check by multiplying: 12 pieces × 1/4 foot = 12/4 = 3 feet. So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12 pieces.
  5. One more move to know: mixed number → improper fraction: multiply the bottom by the whole number, add the top, keep the same bottom. 3 1/2 → (2 × 3) + 1 = 7 → 7/2.

If they get stuck... Si se traban...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Interpret Division of Fractions is assuming that dividing always makes the answer smaller. For 4 ÷ 1/3, students reason 'dividing shrinks the number' and compute 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.3 instead of asking how many 1/3-foot pieces fit into 4 feet (each whole foot holds 3 thirds, so 4 × 3 = 12). Before you submit, check: if the divisor is a fraction smaller than 1, the quotient must be BIGGER than the dividend, not smaller.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
Pregunte: «¿Puedes mostrarme cómo lo hiciste? ¿Nos saltamos algún paso?» Hablar de las matemáticas en voz alta ayuda mucho.
