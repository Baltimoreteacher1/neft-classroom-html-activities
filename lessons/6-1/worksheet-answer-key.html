Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning

    What does 3 ÷ 1/4 mean?

    1. AHow many 1/4-size pieces fit into 3
    2. B1/4 of 3
    3. C3 groups of 1/4
    4. D3 minus 1/4

    Hint Re-read the expression: 3 is the total amount and 1/4 is the size of one piece. What question could that setup be asking?

    Answer: A — How many 1/4-size pieces fit into 3

    Why: 3 ÷ 1/4 asks how many 1/4-size pieces fit into 3. To find that, put a 1 under the 3 and use Keep, Change, Flip: 3/1 × 4/1 = 12.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Interpret Division of Fractions is assuming that dividing always makes the answer smaller. For 4 ÷ 1/3, students reason 'dividing shrinks the number' and compute 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.3 instead of asking how many 1/3-foot pieces fit into 4 feet (each whole foot holds 3 thirds, so 4 × 3 = 12). Before you submit, check: if the divisor is a fraction smaller than 1, the quotient must be BIGGER than the dividend, not smaller.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Complete the pattern: as the unit fraction divisor gets smaller, what happens to the quotient?

    ExpressionQuotientPattern
    2 ÷ 1/2
    2 ÷ 1/3
    2 ÷ 1/4
    2 ÷ 1/6
    2 ÷ 1/10

    Why: The dividend stays at 2 while the divisor shrinks, so the quotients climb: 2/1 × 2/1 = 4, 2/1 × 3/1 = 6, 2/1 × 4/1 = 8, 2/1 × 6/1 = 12, and 2/1 × 10/1 = 20. Each quotient is 2 times the denominator, because flipping 1/b turns it into b. The pattern column names the rule: a smaller divisor produces a larger quotient.

  2. 2Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Complete the pattern: the same 3/4 shared among more and more people.

    ExpressionQuotientPattern
    3/4 ÷ 2
    3/4 ÷ 3
    3/4 ÷ 4
    3/4 ÷ 6

    Why: Each row writes the whole number over 1 and flips it: 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8, 3/4 × 1/3 = 3/12 = 1/4, 3/4 × 1/4 = 3/16, and 3/4 × 1/6 = 3/24 = 1/8. The dividend never moves, so sharing among more people always leaves each person less. Compare this with dividing 3/4 by a unit fraction, where the quotient grows instead.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 3Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Interpretation Error

    1. 1ProblemInterpret 4 ÷ 1/5
    2. 2Student says4 ÷ 1/5 means 1/5 of 4
    3. 3Student's answer4/5

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The "Student says" step multiplies without flipping: "1/5 of 4" means 1/5 × 4, which is a different question. Keep, Change, Flip flips the divisor, not the dividend: 4 ÷ 1/5 = 4/1 × 5/1 = 20. The answer 4/5 is smaller than the dividend, which cannot happen when you divide by a number less than 1.

    Correct work: 4 ÷ 1/5 is not "1/5 of 4." Put a 1 under the 4 and flip the divisor: 4/1 × 5/1 = 20.

  2. 4Answer in complete sentences.

    Dividing 6 by 1/2 gives 12, but dividing 6 by 2 gives 3. How can dividing by a smaller number (1/2) give a bigger answer than dividing by a larger number (2)? Explain using a real-world example.

    Look for: smaller pieces, more fit, halves, groups, 12, 3

    Why: The two expressions ask different questions, and the flip shows why. 6 ÷ 2 is 6/1 × 1/2 = 3, because flipping 2/1 gives a factor smaller than 1. 6 ÷ 1/2 is 6/1 × 2/1 = 12, because flipping 1/2 gives a factor larger than 1. Dividing by a number less than 1 always makes the quotient grow.

  3. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake — When the Numerator Is Not 1

    1. 1ProblemA 3/4-pound bag of powder is split equally into 2 kits. Find 3/4 ÷ 2.
    2. 2Student writesThe numerator is not a 1, so I will divide straight across: 3 ÷ 2 on top and 4 ÷ 2 on the bottom.
    3. 3Student's answer1.5/2 of a pound

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. In the "Student writes" step the student invented a new rule for a non-unit numerator and divided straight across, which leaves a fraction inside a fraction. The rule never changed: write the whole number over 1, then Keep, Change, Flip. 3/4 ÷ 2/1 = 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8 pound — and 3/8 is exactly half of 3/4, which is what splitting into 2 kits should give.

    Correct work: A numerator that is not 1 changes nothing. Write the whole number over 1 and Keep, Change, Flip: 3/4 ÷ 2/1 = 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8 pound.
