  2. 2

    (Lesson 6.1) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1ProblemHow many 1/5-cup scoops fill a 6-cup container? Find 6 ÷ 1/5.
    2. 2Student writesI will flip the first number instead of the divisor: 6 ÷ 1/5 = 1/6 × 5 = 5/6 scoop.
    3. 3Student's answer5/6 of a scoop

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the DIVISOR (1/5), not the dividend. The reciprocal of 1/5 is 5, so 6 ÷ 1/5 = 6 × 5 = 30 scoops. In the "Student writes" step the reciprocal was taken of the dividend 6 instead of the divisor 1/5. Only the divisor gets flipped: the reciprocal of 1/5 is 5, so 6 ÷ 1/5 = 6 × 5 = 30 scoops. That matches the story too — each cup holds 5 one-fifth scoops, and there are 6 cups.

  4. 4

    (Lesson 6.2) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem2/3 ÷ 1/2
    2. 2Change ÷ to × (but forgot to flip)2/3 × 1/2
    3. 3Multiply2/6
    4. 4Simplify1/3

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student changed ÷ to × but forgot to flip the divisor. Keep 2/3, change ÷ to ×, and flip the divisor 1/2 to its reciprocal 2/1: 2/3 × 2/1 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. The correct quotient is 4/3 (1 1/3), not 1/3. The step that changed ÷ to × left the divisor as 1/2 instead of flipping it, so the problem turned into multiplication of the original fractions. Keep, Change, Flip turns the divisor into 2/1, giving 2/3 × 2/1 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. That size makes sense, because 1/2 is smaller than 2/3, so more than one copy fits.

    (Lesson 6.3) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Expression2⁴
    2. 2Student's thinking2 × 4
    3. 3Calculate= 8

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 2⁴ does NOT mean 2 × 4. The exponent 4 tells you to use the base 2 as a factor 4 times: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16. So 2⁴ = 16, not 8. The "Student's thinking" step turns 2⁴ into 2 × 4. The exponent 4 is a count, not a factor: it says to use the base 2 four times, giving 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. Doubling step by step gives 2, 4, 8, 16, so 2⁴ = 16, not 8.

  2. 2

    (Lesson 6.2) Find the Division Error

    1. 1Problem2/5 ÷ 3/4
    2. 2Keep, Change, Flip2/5 × 3/4
    3. 3Multiply6/20
    4. 4Simplify3/10

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student forgot to flip. 2/5 × 4/3 (flip the divisor 3/4 to get 4/3). Then 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15. The Keep, Change, Flip step swapped the operation but left the divisor as 3/4, so no fraction was actually flipped. The reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3, so the problem is 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15, not 3/10.

  3. 3

    (Lesson 6.3) Find the Exponent Error

    1. 1Expression2³
    2. 2Student's thinking2 × 3
    3. 3Calculate= 6

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2, NOT 2 × 3. The exponent tells us to multiply the base by itself 3 times: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. The "Student's thinking" step reads 2³ as 2 × 3, which is multiplication by the exponent rather than repeated multiplication of the base. Written correctly, 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2, so 2 × 2 = 4 and 4 × 2 = 8. The value is 8, not 6.
