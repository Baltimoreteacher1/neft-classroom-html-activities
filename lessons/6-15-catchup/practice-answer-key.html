Where we left off

Our goal: I can show I am caught up on Lessons 6.4–6.15 by using each lesson's big idea in mixed practice.

The big idea: 6.4: To evaluate an expression, do grouping symbols first, then powers, then multiply and divide, then add and subtract, left to right. • 6.5: Use a letter for the unknown amount and turn the words into operations. To evaluate, substitute a number for the letter and use the order of operations. • 6.6: Expressions are equivalent when they match for every value of x. Combine like terms to show it, and test a number to check. • 6.7: Use the GCF to split amounts into equal groups. Use the LCM to find when repeating events line up again. • 6.8: The Commutative Property changes the order, and the Associative Property changes the grouping. Neither one changes a sum or a product. • 6.9: Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, and the answer tells how many pieces fit. • 6.10: To divide mixed numbers, change them to improper fractions, then multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. • 6.11: The total is the dividend and the size of one group is the divisor, so total ÷ group size tells how many groups. • 6.12: To find the LCM, list the multiples of each number and pick the smallest number that is in both lists. • 6.13: Keep splitting a number into factors until every branch is prime, then write the number as the product of those primes. • 6.14: To use the distributive property, multiply the outside number by every term inside the parentheses, then add the products. • 6.15: Like terms have the same variable, so add their coefficients and keep the variable the same.

Model to copy — One worked example per lesson

  1. Evaluate 2 × 15 + 4 × 20 + 8 × 2³.
  2. Find the power first: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
  3. Multiply, left to right: 30 + 80 + 64
  4. Add, left to right: 30 + 80 + 64 = 174
  5. The value is 174.
  6. Tickets cost $12 each, plus a $50 fee one time. Write an expression for t tickets. Then find the cost of 25 tickets.
  7. Write the cost for t tickets: 12t
  8. Add the fee one time: 12t + 50
  9. Replace t with 25. Evaluate: 12 × 25 + 50 = 300 + 50 = 350
  10. The expression is 12t + 50. For 25 tickets, the cost is $350.
  11. Are 3x + 2x + 10 and 5x + 10 equivalent?
  12. Combine the like terms 3x and 2x: 3x + 2x + 10 → 5x + 10
  13. Check by testing x = 4 in both: 12 + 8 + 10 = 30; 20 + 10 = 30
  14. Yes. They are equivalent.
  15. Find the GCF of 48 and 36.
  16. List the factors of each number: 48: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48; 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
  17. Circle the factors in both lists: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
  18. Choose the greatest one: GCF = 12
  19. The GCF of 48 and 36 is 12.
  20. Show that 3 + 5 + 2 and 5 + 3 + 2 give the same total. Name the property.
  21. Add in the first order: 3 + 5 + 2 = 10
  22. Add in the new order: 5 + 3 + 2 = 10
  23. Name what changed: order → Commutative Property
  24. Both equal 10. This is the Commutative Property.
  25. Agent Park has 4 pounds of powder. Each bag holds 1/2 pound. How many bags can he fill?
  26. Keep 4, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/2: 4 ÷ 1/2 = 4 × 2/1
  27. Multiply: 4 × 2 = 8
  28. He can fill 8 bags.
  29. Agent Chen has a route that is 31/2 miles long. She cuts it into 1/4-mile parts. How many parts are there?
  30. Change 31/2 to an improper fraction: 3 × 2 + 1 = 7 → 31/2 = 7/2
  31. Keep, change, flip. Then multiply: 7/2 × 4/1 = 28/2 = 14
  32. 31/2 ÷ 1/4 = 14. The route has 14 parts.
  33. Agent Torres has 2/3 gallon of dust. Each test uses 1/6 gallon. How many tests can she run?
  34. Write total ÷ size of one test: 2/3 ÷ 1/6
  35. Keep, change, flip. Then multiply: 2/3 × 6/1 = 12/3 = 4
  36. Check by multiplying back: 4 × 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3
  37. She can run 4 tests.
  38. Find the least common multiple (LCM) of 4 and 6.
  39. List the multiples of each number: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24; 6, 12, 18, 24
  40. Circle the first number in both lists: 12
  41. The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.
  42. Find the prime factorization of 60.
  43. Split 60 into two factors: 60 = 6 × 10
  44. Split each factor until all are prime: 6 = 2 × 3 → 10 = 2 × 5
  45. Write 60 as a product of primes: 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5
  46. 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5, or 2² × 3 × 5.
  47. Each bundle has a $15 ticket and a $5 snack. Use the distributive property to find 3(15 + 5).
  48. Multiply 3 by each term inside: 3 × 15 + 3 × 5
  49. Multiply, then add: 45 + 15 = 60
  50. 3(15 + 5) = 60. The 3 bundles cost $60.
  51. Simplify 5x + 2 + 3x + 4.
  52. Find the like terms: 5x and 3x → 2 and 4
  53. Add the coefficients of the x terms: 5 + 3 = 8 → 8x
  54. Add the constants: 2 + 4 = 6
  55. The simplified expression is 8x + 6.

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Simplify Algebraic Expressions is sweeping a constant into the variable terms — for example, simplifying 6x + 4 + 2x as 12x by adding all three numbers (6 + 4 + 2 = 12) instead of recognizing that 4 has no variable and cannot combine with 6x and 2x. The correct simplified expression is 8x + 4 (combine 6x + 2x = 8x, and let the 4 stay on its own). Before you submit, check that every term you combined has the exact same variable part.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.
