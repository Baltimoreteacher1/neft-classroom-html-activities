  1. 1

    A number line shows a closed circle at 8 and is shaded to the left. Which inequality does this represent?

    1. Ax ≤ 8
    2. Bx < 8
    3. Cx ≥ 8
    4. Dx > 8

    Closed circle means the value is included (≤ or ≥). Shaded left means less than or equal to: x ≤ 8.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph Inequalities is treating ≥ and ≤ the same as > and < and drawing the wrong circle. For example, students often graph x ≥ 6 with an OPEN circle (as if it were x > 6), which wrongly excludes 6 even though 6 itself makes x ≥ 6 true. A second common mistake is choosing the right circle but shading the wrong direction — for x < 9 students sometimes shade right instead of left. Always check the symbol twice: once for the circle (does it have an 'or equal to' line?) and once for the shading direction (does the symbol point toward larger or smaller numbers?).

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1ReadThe case file needs fewer than 9 clues, so c < 9. Graph it.
    2. 2Circle choiceOpen circle at 9
    3. 3Shade directionShade right
    4. 4Final graphOpen circle at 9, shaded right

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 3. Correct work: The circle choice was correct — c < 9 does not include 9, so it needs an open circle. But 'less than' means smaller values, so the shading should go LEFT toward smaller numbers, not right. Final graph: open circle at 9, shaded left. The open circle at 9 is correct because c < 9 leaves 9 out, but the shade direction step goes the wrong way. 'Fewer than 9 clues' means values smaller than 9, so the shading runs left, giving an open circle at 9 shaded left.

    Find the Graphing Error

    1. 1Inequalityx > 6
    2. 2Circle typeClosed circle at 6
    3. 3Shade directionShade right
    4. 4GraphClosed circle at 6, shaded right

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: x > 6 uses a strict inequality (no 'equal to'), so the circle should be OPEN, not closed. Open circle at 6, shaded right. The shading right matches x > 6, but the circle type step used a closed circle. Since > is strict, 6 > 6 is false and 6 is not a solution, so the graph needs an open circle at 6 shaded right.

    Look for: open, closed, true, false, included, not included, boundary.
