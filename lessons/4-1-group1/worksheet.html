WordWhat it meansExample
PercentSpanish: Porcentaje A ratio that compares a number to 100. The word means “per hundred,” and the symbol is %. 40% = 40 out of every 100
Per hundredSpanish: Por cada cien The meaning hidden inside the word percent: for every group of 100. It is why every percent can be written as a fraction over 100. 7% means 7 per hundred
Decimal gridSpanish: Cuadrícula decimal A 10-by-10 grid of 100 squares. Shading squares shows a percent directly, one square per percent. 62 shaded squares = 62%
Part-to-wholeSpanish: Parte a todo A comparison of one part against the whole amount. In a percent, the whole is always 100%. shaded squares to all squares
Greater than 100%Spanish: Mayor que 100 % More than one whole. A percent above 100 means the amount is larger than the thing it is being compared to. 150% of the gray kitten = 1.5 times its weight
Percents Greater Than 100% and Less Than 1%Spanish: Porcentajes mayores que 100% y menores que 1% Percents can describe amounts greater than one whole or smaller than one hundredth of a whole. 150% = 1.5 and 0.5% = 0.005

How it worksWorked exampleturning a ratio into a percent

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

In a survey, 18 out of every 50 sixth graders pack their lunch. What percent pack their lunch?

  1. Write the ratio: 18 out of 50.
  2. Ask: what times 50 makes 100? 50 × 2 = 100
  3. Multiply BOTH terms by 2: 18 out of 50 → 36 out of 100
  4. 36 out of 100 means 36%.

Answer: 36% of the sixth graders pack their lunch.

Now you trySame steps, your turn

The steps are the same as the worked example. The numbers are yours. Do the work.

In the same survey, 3 out of every 10 students ride a scooter. What percent ride a scooter?

  1. Write the ratio: out of
  2. What times 10 makes one hundred?
  3. Multiply BOTH terms: out of one hundred
  4. Write the percent: %
Answer:percent of students who ride a scooter

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake with percent is scaling only one term of the ratio: reading 7 out of 10 as 7%. A percent compares to 100, so BOTH terms must be multiplied by the same factor — 10 × 10 = 100, so 7 × 10 = 70, giving 70%. Reading it as 7% describes seats that are nearly empty when they are nearly full. A second mistake is treating a percent as an amount: 150% of a 200-point goal is 1.5 × 200 = 300 points, not 150 points.

  2. 2Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    2. 2Leo ran 2 times his goal. What do you multiply 10 laps by?
    3. 3Multiply 100% by 2 too. What percent of his goal did he run?
    4. 4Is 200% more or less than one whole?

  3. 3Complete each step, then write the final answer.
