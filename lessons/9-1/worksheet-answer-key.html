Lesson 9.1Explore Relationships Between Two Variables

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can recognize when two quantities change together, and identify which one is the independent variable and which is the dependent variable.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each quantity: independent or dependent?

    Groups: Independent variable (input) Dependent variable (output)
    • Independent variable (input)Hours of sunlight a tray of seedlings gets
    • Independent variable (input)Months of gym membership
    • Independent variable (input)Minutes spent jogging
    • Dependent variable (output)Height the seedlings grow
    • Dependent variable (output)Total cost of the membership
    • Dependent variable (output)Calories burned on the jog

    Hint Use the sentence check: 'The ___ depends on the ___.' The first blank is dependent.

    Why: Each pair passes the sentence check: the height depends on the sunlight, the cost depends on the months, and the Calories depend on the minutes. The chosen quantity — sunlight, months, minutes — is independent; the quantity that responds is dependent.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Akela chooses how many hours of sunlight each tray gets. Which is the INDEPENDENT variable?

    1. APlant height
    2. BHours of sunlight
    3. CNumber of trays
    4. DNumber of seeds

    Hint Which quantity did Akela get to decide?

    Answer: B — Hours of sunlight

    Why: Akela chooses the sunlight, so sunlight is subject to choice — that makes it independent.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    In Akela's experiment, which is the DEPENDENT variable?

    1. AHours of sunlight
    2. BThe kind of seed
    3. CThe size of the tray
    4. DPlant height

    Hint Which quantity RESPONDED to the other?

    Answer: D — Plant height

    Why: Height responds to how much sunlight the tray received, so height is the dependent variable.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Claire burned 123 Calories on a 30-minute jog. Which quantity is subject to choice?

    1. ACalories she burns
    2. BHer heart rate
    3. CMinutes she jogs
    4. DThe weather

    Hint What does Claire decide before she starts?

    Answer: C — Minutes she jogs

    Why: Claire decides how long to jog. The Calories follow from that decision.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which sentence is written in the correct order?

    1. AMinutes jogged depends on Calories burned.
    2. BCalories burned depends on Calories burned.
    3. CNeither quantity depends on the other.
    4. DCalories burned depends on minutes jogged.

    Hint Say both versions out loud. Which one is true?

    Answer: D — Calories burned depends on minutes jogged.

    Why: The dependent quantity comes first in the sentence '___ depends on ___ .' Calories respond to time, so Calories depend on minutes.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  5. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A tray that got 2 hours of sun grew to 4 cm. A tray that got 6 hours grew to 12 cm. As sunlight increases, plant height…

    1. Adecreases
    2. Bstays the same
    3. Cincreases
    4. Dcannot be determined

    Hint Compare the two trays.

    Answer: C — increases

    Why: 2 hours gave 4 cm and 6 hours gave 12 cm, so more sunlight went with more height.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  6. 7Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    You buy tickets at $45 each. Which quantity depends on the other?

    1. ATotal cost depends on number of tickets
    2. BNumber of tickets depends on total cost
    3. CThe price per ticket depends on the total cost
    4. DNothing depends on anything here

    Hint Which number do you pick at the counter?

    Answer: A — Total cost depends on number of tickets

    Why: You choose how many tickets to buy; the total cost is the result of that choice.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

Learning target I can recognize when two quantities change together, and identify which one is the independent variable and which is the dependent variable.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Claire burns Calories at a steady rate: 30 minutes burns 123 Calories. Complete the table.

    Minutes jogged (independent)ThinkingCalories burned (dependent)
    30
    60
    90

    Why: Because the rate is steady, doubling the minutes doubles the Calories: 60 minutes burns 246, and 90 minutes burns 369. The table shows dependence in action — every value in the Calories column is produced by a value in the minutes column.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A gym charges $24.95 per month. Which pair correctly names the variables?

    1. AIndependent: months; Dependent: total cost
    2. BIndependent: total cost; Dependent: months
    3. CIndependent: $24.95; Dependent: months
    4. DIndependent: months; Dependent: $24.95

    Answer: A — Independent: months; Dependent: total cost

    Why: You choose how many months to stay a member. The total cost responds to that.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Claire jogs 30 minutes and burns 123 Calories. At the same rate, how many Calories would a 90-minute jog burn?

    1. A246
    2. B300
    3. C369
    4. D410

    Answer: C — 369

    Why: 30 minutes burned 123 Calories, and 90 is 3 times 30, so the Calories are 3 times 123, which is 369.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which of these pairs is NOT a dependent relationship?

    1. ANumber of pizzas sold and weekly cost of cheese
    2. BNumber of pizzas sold and number of employees the shop has
    3. CHours jogged and Calories burned
    4. DTickets bought and total cost

    Answer: B — Number of pizzas sold and number of employees the shop has

    Why: The shop has 6 employees whether it sells 5 pizzas or 500. That count does not respond to pizza sales.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A plant grows 2 cm for every hour of daily sunlight. Which table row is correct?

    1. A4 hours to 8 cm
    2. B4 hours to 6 cm
    3. C4 hours to 2 cm
    4. D4 hours to 4 cm

    Answer: A — 4 hours to 8 cm

    Why: At 2 cm per hour, 4 hours of sunlight gives 4 times 2, which is 8 cm.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  5. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which question would you ask to find the DEPENDENT variable?

    1. AWhich quantity responds when the other one changes?
    2. BWhich quantity is larger?
    3. CWhich quantity has units?
    4. DWhich quantity comes first alphabetically?

    Answer: A — Which quantity responds when the other one changes?

    Why: The dependent variable is defined by its response to the independent variable — not by size, units, or name.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  6. 7Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Claire decides to walk instead of jog for the same 30 minutes. What happens to the two variables?

    1. ACalories burned becomes the independent variable
    2. BNeither is a variable any more
    3. CTime stays the independent variable; Calories burned still depends on it
    4. DTime becomes the dependent variable

    Answer: C — Time stays the independent variable; Calories burned still depends on it

    Why: Changing the activity changes how many Calories each minute burns, but time is still the quantity she chooses.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  7. 8Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    If Claire runs exactly 3 miles and records how long it takes, which is the independent variable now?

    1. ATime it takes
    2. BHer speed
    3. CDistance (3 miles)
    4. DCalories burned

    Answer: C — Distance (3 miles)

    Why: This time she fixes the distance by choice and the time is whatever results, so distance is independent and time is dependent.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Two quantities are related. Which statement is ALWAYS true?

    1. AThe independent variable is always the larger number
    2. BThe independent variable is subject to choice
    3. CThe dependent variable is always measured in dollars
    4. DThe independent variable is always listed first in a table

    Answer: B — The independent variable is subject to choice

    Why: Being subject to choice is the definition. Size, units, and table position are not part of it.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each scenario to its (independent → dependent) pair.

    1. AGym membership
    2. BSeedling experiment
    3. CJogging
    4. DBuying movie tickets
    • Amonths → total cost
    • Bhours of sunlight → plant height
    • Cminutes → Calories burned
    • Dtickets → total price

    Why: In every pair the left side is chosen and the right side responds: you choose the months, tickets, minutes, or sunlight hours, and the cost, price, Calories, or height follows. Writing relationships as input → output is the foundation for the tables, graphs, and equations of the next three lessons.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Give an example where the SAME quantity can be independent in one problem and dependent in another.

    1. ATime is always independent
    2. BTime: chosen when you pick how long to jog, but measured as a result when you run a set distance
    3. CTime is always dependent
    4. DA quantity can never switch roles

    Answer: B — Time: chosen when you pick how long to jog, but measured as a result when you run a set distance

    Why: Claire's two problems show exactly this: minutes is a choice in one and a result in the other.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  2. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The pizza shop spends $1.15 on cheese per pizza. Which weekly total is correct if it makes 200 pizzas?

    1. A$115
    2. B$201.15
    3. C$230
    4. D$1,150

    Answer: C — $230

    Why: 200 pizzas at $1.15 each is 200 times 1.15, which is $230.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  3. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which relationship is NOT a case of one quantity depending on the other?

    1. AHours studied and quiz score
    2. BMinutes jogged and Calories burned
    3. CPizzas made and cheese used
    4. DA student's height and their house number

    Answer: D — A student's height and their house number

    Why: A house number is assigned by an address, not by how tall someone is — the two are unrelated.

    Watch for: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

  4. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Write the relationship as a sentence: a printer prints 12 pages per minute.

    1. AMinutes running depends on pages printed
    2. B12 depends on the number of pages
    3. CPages printed depends on minutes running
    4. DThe printer depends on the pages

    Answer: C — Pages printed depends on minutes running

    Why: You choose how long to let it run; the number of pages is the result of that choice.
