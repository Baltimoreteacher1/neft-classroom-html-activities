Lesson 9-2: Analyze Graphs of Relationships Between Two Variables

Question 1Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: B

A stadium shuttle carries fans from the parking lot. The table pairs the number of trips with the total fans carried: 2 trips with 96 fans, 5 trips with 240 fans, and 8 trips with 384 fans. On a graph with trips on the horizontal axis and fans on the vertical axis, which ordered pair belongs to 3 trips?

  • A(3, 51)
  • B(3, 144)
  • C(3, 48)
  • D(144, 3)

Every row of the table is 48 times the number of trips: 96 ÷ 2 = 48, 240 ÷ 5 = 48, and 384 ÷ 8 = 48. The independent variable, trips, is written first, and 3 × 48 = 144, so the ordered pair is (3, 144).

  • A: 51 is not three times the rate. Every row runs 48 fans per trip, so three trips carry three times 48.
  • C: 48 is the number of fans for ONE trip. Three trips carry three times that.
  • D: The coordinates are swapped. Trips is the horizontal coordinate and fans the vertical.

Part B — correct answer: B

Which explanation justifies the ordered pair chosen in Part A?

  • AThe larger number always comes first in an ordered pair, so the fans are written before the trips.
  • BThe shuttle carries 48 fans on each trip, and trips come first in the ordered pair, so 3 trips pair with 3 × 48 = 144 fans.
  • CThe rate of 48 fans is what gets plotted, so 3 trips pair with 48 fans.
  • DThe trips and the fans are added, so 3 trips pair with 48 + 3 = 51 fans.

The ordered pair always lists the independent variable first, so trips come before fans. The relationship is a rate of 48 fans per trip, so the second coordinate is 3 × 48 = 144. Reversing the coordinates gives (144, 3), which reads as 144 trips carrying 3 fans.

Question 2Written response · 2 points

Type II Reasoning: Reversed Coordinates

Ramona is graphing sales at the stadium pretzel stand. Pretzels cost $6 each, and her table shows 1 pretzel with $6, 2 pretzels with $12, and 3 pretzels with $18. She labels the horizontal axis 'number of pretzels' and the vertical axis 'total cost'. For 3 pretzels she plots the point (18, 3).

Explain Ramona's misconception and give the correct ordered pair for 3 pretzels.

Model answer: Ramona reversed the coordinates. Her horizontal axis is the number of pretzels and her vertical axis is the total cost, so the first coordinate has to be pretzels and the second has to be dollars. Three pretzels cost 3 × 6 = 18 dollars, so the point is (3, 18). The point she plotted, (18, 3), reads as 18 pretzels costing 3 dollars, which does not match any row of her table.

2 pointsStudent explains that the independent variable, the number of pretzels, is written first in an ordered pair and belongs on the horizontal axis, and gives the point (3, 18).
1 pointStudent gives the point (3, 18) but does not explain that the independent variable is written first.
0 pointsStudent agrees with Ramona or gives an unrelated response.
