How we did it in class today

  1. Each ticket costs $45, so the total cost is 45 times the number of tickets: 1 ticket is $45, 2 tickets are $90, 3 tickets are $135.
  2. The number of tickets is the independent variable, so it goes first in each ordered pair: (1, 45), (2, 90), (3, 135).
  3. I label one axis 'Number of tickets' and the other 'Total cost ($)', then plot each pair as a point.
  4. The points rise to the right: as the number of tickets increases, the total cost increases.

Try this together

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

Movie tickets cost $12 each. A graph shows the number of tickets on the x-axis and the total cost in dollars on the y-axis. What does the point (4, 48) represent?

  1. 48 tickets cost a total of $4
  2. 4 tickets cost a total of $48
  3. 4 tickets cost $48 each
  4. The movie lasts 48 minutes
Check the answer

The answer is: 4 tickets cost a total of $48

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: Reversing the coordinates of a point — writing (cost, tickets) instead of (tickets, cost). The independent variable always comes first in the ordered pair.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
