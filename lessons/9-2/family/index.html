Unit 9, Lesson 2: Analyze Graphs of Relationships Between Two Variables6.AT.11

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A table shows a relationship with numbers. A graph shows the same relationship with points. Reading either one tells you how the dependent variable responds to the independent variable.

Key idea: A table and a graph can show the same relationship. Each row of the table, like (2, 10), becomes a point on the graph: go 2 across and 10 up. The independent variable goes on the bottom (across) axis, and the dependent variable goes on the side (up) axis.
Idea clave: Una tabla y una gráfica pueden mostrar la misma relación. Cada fila de la tabla, como (2, 10), se vuelve un punto en la gráfica: avanza 2 hacia la derecha y sube 10. La variable independiente va en el eje de abajo (horizontal), y la variable dependiente va en el eje del lado (vertical).

How we did it in class today

  1. Each ticket costs $45, so the total cost is 45 times the number of tickets: 1 ticket is $45, 2 tickets are $90, 3 tickets are $135.
  2. The number of tickets is the independent variable, so it goes first in each ordered pair: (1, 45), (2, 90), (3, 135).
  3. I label one axis 'Number of tickets' and the other 'Total cost ($)', then plot each pair as a point.
  4. The points rise to the right: as the number of tickets increases, the total cost increases.

Try this together

  1. Movie tickets cost $12 each. A graph shows the number of tickets on the x-axis and the total cost in dollars on the y-axis. What does the point (4, 48) represent?

    2. 4 tickets cost a total of $48
    3. 4 tickets cost $48 each

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: Reversing the coordinates of a point — writing (cost, tickets) instead of (tickets, cost). The independent variable always comes first in the ordered pair.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"

How families can help at home
