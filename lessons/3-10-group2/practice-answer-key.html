Where we left off

Our goal: I can convert measurement units using ratios and conversion factors, explain why the method works, and use it on a problem I have not seen before.

The big idea: Multiply when changing to a smaller unit, and divide when changing to a bigger unit — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. The cabinet is 5 feet tall. I want to know how many inches that is.
  2. My conversion factor is 1 foot = 12 inches.
  3. Inches are smaller than feet, so I multiply: 5 × 12 = 60.
  4. So 5 feet = 60 inches.

    Think it throughHow many inches tall is the 7-foot cabinet?

    1. A84
    2. B82
    3. C70
    4. D19

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    7 × 12 = 84 inches. Your friend's 82 is close but not correct.

  3. 7

    Think it throughWill the cabinet fit through the 80-inch doorway?

    1. ANo, because 84 > 80
    2. BYes, because 84 < 80
    3. CYes, because 7 feet is small
    4. DYou cannot tell without the width

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    Compare the two heights. The cabinet is 84 inches. The doorway is 80 inches. 84 is taller than 80, so it will not fit. Your friend's conclusion was right even though the conversion was off by 2.

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • Renaming a measurement in a different unit without changing the amount is to ___ it. → Convert Measurement Units
  • A ratio equal to 1 that relates two units, like 12 inches / 1 foot, is a ___. → Conversion Factor
  • A standard amount used to measure, like a foot or a gram, is a ___. → Unit

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake on this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Convert Measurement Units is applying the conversion factor in the wrong direction — for example, converting 4 feet to inches by dividing (4 ÷ 12 = 0.33 inches) instead of multiplying (4 × 12 = 48 inches), or converting 80 ounces to pounds by multiplying (80 × 16 = 1,280 pounds) instead of dividing (80 ÷ 16 = 5 pounds). Before you answer, ask: am I changing to a SMALLER unit (there will be MORE of them, so multiply) or a BIGGER unit (there will be FEWER of them, so divide)?

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 10

    Say moreHow did you use a conversion factor like '1 ft = 12 in' to fill in the ratio table, and how did you decide whether to multiply or divide?

    I multiply when going to a smaller unit and divide when going to a bigger unit.

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — Which is the correct conversion of 5 yards to inches?

    1. A180 inches
    2. B60 inches
    3. C15 inches
    4. D540 inches

    Explain your choice.

    5 yards × 3 = 15 feet. 15 feet × 12 = 180 inches. Or: 5 × 36 = 180 inches.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 3.9Explain your thinking — A store sells 5 notebooks for $8. At this rate, how much would 15 notebooks cost?

    1. A$24
    2. B$18
    3. C$20
    4. D$40

    How do you know?

    15 is 3 times 5, so the cost is 3 × $8 = $24. The ratio 5:8 scales to 15:24.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 3.9Why must both ingredients be multiplied by the same factor?

    1. ASo the ratio stays 2:3 and the smoothie tastes the same
    2. BBecause 2 and 3 are both small numbers
    3. CBecause addition would be harder
    4. DSo the total is a whole number

    How do you know?

    An equivalent ratio comes from scaling both parts equally. 10:15 simplifies back to 2:3; changing only one part would change the flavor.
