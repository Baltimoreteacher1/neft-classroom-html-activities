Learning target I can use everything Pre-Unit 1 taught — estimating with reasoning, fractions of a whole number, decimal operations, volume of a rectangular prism, and pattern rules — and explain which one a problem is asking for.

  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A jar is full of marbles — too many to count one at a time. You count one small handful: about 10 marbles. About 8 handfuls fill the jar. About how many marbles are in the jar?

    1. AAbout 80 marbles
    2. BAbout 18 marbles
    3. CAbout 2 marbles
    4. DAbout 800 marbles

    Hint What can you actually count here — the whole jar, or one handful?

    Answer: A — About 80 marbles

    Why: Estimating means finding a group you CAN count, then asking how many of those groups there are. 8 handfuls of about 10 is 8 x 10 = 80.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Three fourths of the 20 chairs in the room are taken. How many chairs are taken?

    1. A15 chairs
    2. B5 chairs
    3. C23 chairs
    4. D80 chairs

    Hint 'Of' a number, with a fraction in front of it, means multiply.

    Answer: A — 15 chairs

    Why: Three fourths OF a number means multiply: 3/4 x 20. One fourth of 20 is 5, so three fourths is 3 x 5 = 15 chairs.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A notebook costs $1.25. You buy 4 notebooks. What is the total cost?

    1. A$5.00
    2. B$4.25
    3. C$5.25
    4. D$0.31

    Hint Are you buying one notebook or four? Four equal prices is four equal groups.

    Answer: A — $5.00

    Why: Four notebooks at the same price is 4 groups of $1.25, so multiply: 1.25 x 4 = 5.00. Four quarters make a dollar, so four $1.25 notebooks make $5.00 exactly.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Three tickets cost $7.50 altogether. All three tickets cost the same. What does ONE ticket cost?

    1. A$2.50
    2. B$4.50
    3. C$22.50
    4. D$2.05

    Answer: A — $2.50

    Why: Equal shares means divide: 7.50 divided by 3 = 2.50. Check it back the other way — 2.50 x 3 = 7.50.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A Ferris wheel has 20 cars, and about 4 people fit in each car. About how many people can ride at one time?

    1. AAbout 80 people
    2. BAbout 24 people
    3. CAbout 5 people
    4. DAbout 16 people

    Answer: A — About 80 people

    Why: This is the estimate from Lesson 1-1. Count what you can (20 cars), estimate the rest (about 4 riders each), then multiply: 20 x 4 = 80. An estimate is a reasoned answer that is close enough to be useful.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Two thirds of the 18 students in a group finished early. How many students finished early?

    1. A12 students
    2. B6 students
    3. C27 students
    4. D9 students

    Answer: A — 12 students

    Why: Two thirds OF 18 means 2/3 x 18. One third of 18 is 6, so two thirds is 2 x 6 = 12 students.

Apply Day · Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can use everything Pre-Unit 1 taught — estimating with reasoning, fractions of a whole number, decimal operations, volume of a rectangular prism, and pattern rules — and explain which one a problem is asking for.

  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A large cereal box is 8 by 3 by 10 inches and a jumbo box is 10 by 4 by 10 inches. Three large boxes cost the same as two jumbo boxes. Which choice gives MORE cereal, and by how much?

    1. ATwo jumbo boxes, by 80 cubic inches
    2. BThree large boxes, by 80 cubic inches
    3. CThey give exactly the same amount
    4. DTwo jumbo boxes, by 160 cubic inches

    Answer: A — Two jumbo boxes, by 80 cubic inches

    Why: One large box is 8 x 3 x 10 = 240, so three hold 240 x 3 = 720 cubic inches. One jumbo box is 10 x 4 x 10 = 400, so two hold 400 x 2 = 800 cubic inches. 800 - 720 = 80, so the jumbo boxes give 80 cubic inches more for the same price.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A change jar holds pennies, nickels, dimes and quarters worth $53.50 in all. Why does the number of PENNIES have to be a multiple of 5?

    1. ABecause nickels, dimes and quarters all end in 0 or 5 cents, so only the pennies can make the total end in 0 — and it takes a multiple of 5 pennies to do that
    2. BBecause $53.50 divided by 5 is a whole number
    3. CBecause there are 5 pennies in a nickel
    4. DBecause every jar of coins has a multiple of 5 pennies

    Answer: A — Because nickels, dimes and quarters all end in 0 or 5 cents, so only the pennies can make the total end in 0 — and it takes a multiple of 5 pennies to do that

    Why: This is the generalization from Lesson 1-5. Every nickel, dime and quarter contributes a number of cents ending in 0 or 5. The total, $53.50, ends in 0. So the pennies must contribute an amount ending in 0 or 5 as well — and pennies are worth 1 cent each, so their COUNT has to be a multiple of 5.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A recipe needs 0.75 of a 40-ounce bag of flour. How many ounces does the recipe need?

    1. A30 ounces
    2. B40.75 ounces
    3. C53 ounces
    4. D3 ounces

    Answer: A — 30 ounces

    Why: 0.75 is the same as three fourths, so this is 3/4 of 40. One fourth of 40 is 10, so three fourths is 3 x 10 = 30 ounces. Decimals and fractions are two ways of writing the same amount.
