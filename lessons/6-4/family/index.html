What students are learning

A numerical expression is a math phrase made of numbers and operations, with no equal sign. Writing one for a whole situation keeps every part visible; evaluating it means finding its single value, following the order of operations.

Key idea: To find the value of a math expression, follow the order: parentheses first, then exponents, then multiply or divide, then add or subtract. In 2 × 15 + 8 × 2³, first 2³ = 8, then 30 + 64 = 94.
Idea clave: Para hallar el valor de una expresión matemática, sigue el orden: primero los paréntesis, luego los exponentes, luego multiplicar o dividir, y por último sumar o restar. En 2 × 15 + 8 × 2³, primero 2³ = 8, luego 30 + 64 = 94.

Example

  1. Sofia buys 2 member tickets at $15, 4 non-member tickets at $20, and 8 student tickets at $2 each. I write one expression for each ticket type: 2 × 15, 4 × 20, and 8 × 2.
  2. The whole order is the three costs added together: 2 × 15 + 4 × 20 + 8 × 2.
  3. Now I evaluate. There are no grouping symbols and no powers yet, so multiplication comes first, left to right: 30 + 80 + 16.
  4. Then I add: 30 + 80 + 16 = 126. The tickets cost $126.

Common mistake

A common mistake with powers is multiplying the base by the exponent: reading 3² as 3 × 2 = 6 instead of 3 × 3 = 9. A second is evaluating strictly left to right, so 3 + 4 × 2 becomes 14 instead of 11. The order of operations exists so one expression has exactly one value: grouping symbols, then powers, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right.
