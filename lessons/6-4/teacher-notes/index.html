Unit 6, Lesson 4: Write and Evaluate Numerical Expressions with Exponents6.AT.6c

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can write numerical expressions from a real situation and evaluate them using the order of operations, including powers.

Language: I can explain my steps using the words expression, evaluate, order of operations, base, and exponent.

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below before the lesson.

Warm-up

Suppose the student tickets cost 2³ dollars each instead of $2. The expression becomes 2 × 15 + 4 × 20 + 8 × 2³.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Order of Operations (PEMDAS). 1. Grouping Symbols: Parentheses, brackets, fraction bars inner to outer. 2. Exponents: Evaluate all powers. 3. Multiply & Divide left to right; Add & Subtract left to right

I do:

  1. Sofia buys 2 member tickets at $15, 4 non-member tickets at $20, and 8 student tickets at $2 each. I write one expression for each ticket type: 2 × 15, 4 × 20, and 8 × 2.
  2. The whole order is the three costs added together: 2 × 15 + 4 × 20 + 8 × 2.
  3. Now I evaluate. There are no grouping symbols and no powers yet, so multiplication comes first, left to right: 30 + 80 + 16.
  4. Then I add: 30 + 80 + 16 = 126. The tickets cost $126.

You do: Seven friends split a bill. The pizzas cost 3² dollars each and they order 4 of them, plus a $6 delivery fee. Write ONE numerical expression for the total bill. Evaluate it: which do you handle first, the power or the multiplication? Then divide the total among the 7 friends, and say what each step meant.

Common misconception

A common mistake with powers is multiplying the base by the exponent: reading 3² as 3 × 2 = 6 instead of 3 × 3 = 9. A second is evaluating strictly left to right, so 3 + 4 × 2 becomes 14 instead of 11. The order of operations exists so one expression has exactly one value: grouping symbols, then powers, then × and ÷ left to right, then + and − left to right.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: Write and Evaluate Numerical Expressions with Exponents, Numerical expression, Evaluate, Order of operations, Power, Base, Exponent (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Evaluate 2 × 15 + 8 × 2³.

  • 94 ✓ correct
  • 124
  • 736
  • 150

Powers first: 2³ = 8. Then multiply left to right: 2 × 15 = 30 and 8 × 8 = 64. Then add: 30 + 64 = 94.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.
