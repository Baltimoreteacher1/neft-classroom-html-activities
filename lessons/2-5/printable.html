The league analyst is studying three different data sets from the season: (1) Player heights across all teams, (2) Number of 3-pointers per game by each player, and (3) Years of experience for all coaches. Each data set has a different shape when graphed. Your job is to describe each shape and explain what it tells us about the data.

What shape is the data?

The shape of a data distribution describes how the values are spread out. Data can be symmetric (the same on both sides), skewed (bunched on one side with a long tail on the other), or have clusters and gaps.

Key idea: Symmetric data mirrors around the center; skewed data has a long tail, and the skew is named for the direction the tail points.

Watch me

  • Example 1, bar heights from left to right: 2, 5, 8, 5, 2. The bars rise to a peak in the middle and fall evenly on both sides.
  • Both sides look like mirror images, so this shape is symmetric.
  • Example 2, bar heights: 9, 6, 3, 1. The tallest bars are on the left and the bars get shorter going right.
  • The long tail points to the right, so this shape is skewed right. I name the skew after the tail, not the peak.

Let's try together

  • New bar heights: 1, 3, 6, 9. Where are the tallest bars, and which way does the tail point?
  • Since the tail points left, is this symmetric, skewed right, or skewed left?
  • Now picture heights 2, 6, 6, 2. Do both sides match? What shape is that?

Now it's your turn

  • Next, you will match each sports data description to its shape: symmetric, skewed right, or skewed left.
  • Look for the long tail to decide the direction of the skew.
