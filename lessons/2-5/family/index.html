Read this in your language · Lea esto en su idioma

The range tells how spread out all of the data is: range = greatest number − least number. The interquartile range (IQR) tells how spread out the middle half of the data is: IQR = Q3 − Q1.

  • Range and Interquartile Range — Two numbers that describe spread: the range covers all the data, and the interquartile range covers only the middle half.
  • Range — The distance from the least value to the greatest value, found by subtracting: greatest − least.
  • Quartile — One of the three values that cut an ordered data set into four equal-sized groups.
  • Interquartile range — The distance across the middle half of the data, found by subtracting the first quartile from the third: Q3 − Q1.
  • Variability — How much the values in a data set differ from one another — whether they sit close together or far apart.
  • Measure of variation — A single number that describes the spread of a whole data set. The range and the IQR are both measures of variation.
  • Extreme value — The least or greatest value in a data set, far from the rest.

How we did it in class today

  1. Here are 20 history test scores in order: 68, 70, 73, 74, 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 86, 88, 88, 90, 90, 93, 94. Ordering them first is what makes every step after this possible.
  2. Range: I subtract the least value from the greatest. 94 − 68 = 26. The scores span 26 points from end to end.
  3. Now the quartiles. The median splits the 20 scores into two halves of 10. The median is halfway between the 10th and 11th values: (83 + 84) ÷ 2 = 83.5.
  4. Q1 is the median of the lower 10 values: halfway between 75 and 76, so Q1 = 75.5. Q3 is the median of the upper 10: halfway between 88 and 88, so Q3 = 88.
  5. Interquartile range: IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 88 − 75.5 = 12.5. The middle half of the students' scores differed by only 12.5 points, even though the full range was 26.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake with Range and Interquartile Range is adding the two quartiles instead of subtracting them — writing IQR = Q3 + Q1. With Q1 = 42 and Q3 = 58 that gives 100, which is larger than the data set's entire range of 20. Every measure of variation is a DIFFERENCE, and the IQR can never exceed the range, because the middle half is part of the whole span. If your IQR comes out bigger than your range, you added when you should have subtracted.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
