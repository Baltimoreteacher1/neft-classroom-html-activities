Unit 1, Lesson 1: Math is MineMPP.3

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can describe the ways we are all doers of math, and compare my math story with a classmate's.

Language: I can tell my math story using the words strength, challenge, and I use math when ___ .

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below with ESOL students.

Warm-up

78.5 = 70 + 8.5.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Everyone uses math daily. We do it differently, and sharing how we do it makes all of us better at it.

I do:

  1. I count the cars, then estimate how many riders fit in one car.
  2. I do not count every person — an estimate is a reasoned answer that is close enough to be useful.
  3. About 78.5 people can ride at one time, so I would say “about 80 people.”

You do: Name three ways you used math this week outside of school. Then tell a classmate one math strength you already have.

Common misconception

Believing that 'doing math' only counts when it happens on paper in a classroom.

ESOL / SPED supports

  • Pre-teach: doer of math, math story, strength, decompose, estimate (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Which statement best shows what this lesson means by “we are all doers of math”?

  • Everyone uses mathematical thinking daily, in different ways, and sharing those ways helps us grow ✓ correct
  • Everyone gets the same score if they try hard enough
  • Everyone must solve problems the same way to be correct
  • Only the fastest students are really doing math

The summary makes three claims: everyone does math, we do it differently, and sharing our strengths helps all of us.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

What we believe about ourselves, about others, and about what we are learning shapes us. Your classmate has a math story too, and it is not the same as yours.
