🚀 Launch

You have been doing math your whole life — at home, in school, and in your neighborhood — often without calling it math. This year starts by naming what you already do.

We are all doers of math

Before any new mathematics, the book asks a different question: where does math already live in your day?

Key idea: Everyone uses math daily. We do it differently, and sharing how we do it makes all of us better at it.

Watch me estimate the Ferris wheel

  • I count the cars, then estimate how many riders fit in one car.
  • I do not count every person — an estimate is a reasoned answer that is close enough to be useful.
  • About 78.5 people can ride at one time, so I would say “about 80 people.”

Let's decompose 78.5 together

  • 78.5 = 70 + 8.5.
  • 78.5 = 78 + 0.5.
  • 78.5 = 80 − 1.5.
  • Every one of those is the same number wearing different clothes. Find two more.

Now you try

  • Name three ways you used math this week outside of school.
  • Then tell a classmate one math strength you already have.
