Unit 3, Lesson 1: Understand Ratios6.AT.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A ratio compares two amounts. It tells how much of one thing there is for another thing.

Key idea: Understanding Ratios. Formula: a : b | a to b | a/b. 1. A ratio compares two quantities; order of quantities matters. 2. Part-to-Part compares two distinct subgroups (e.g. boys to girls). 3. Part-to-Whole compares one subgroup to the total (e.g. boys to class)

Key words

  • Understand Ratios — Use a ratio to compare two quantities by division.
    Español: Comprender razones — Usar una razón para comparar dos cantidades mediante la división.
  • Ratio — A way to compare two amounts, like 3 to 2.
    Español: Razón — Una manera de comparar dos cantidades, como 3 a 2.
  • Comparison — Looking at two or more amounts to see how they are related.
    Español: Comparación — Mirar dos o más cantidades para ver cómo se relacionan.
  • Part-to-part — A ratio comparing one part of a group to another part.
    Español: Parte a parte — Una razón que compara una parte de un grupo con otra parte.
  • Part-to-whole — A ratio comparing one part to the whole group.
    Español: Parte a todo — Una razón que compara una parte con todo el grupo.
  • Colon notation — Writing a ratio with two dots between the numbers, like 3:2.
    Español: Notación con dos puntos — Escribir una razón con dos puntos entre los números, como 3:2.
  • Tape diagram — A bar cut into equal parts that shows how two amounts in a ratio compare, and how they make up the whole.
    Español: Diagrama de cintas — Una barra dividida en partes iguales que muestra cómo se comparan dos cantidades de una razón y cómo forman el total.
  • Double number line — Two number lines lined up so matching tick marks show the two quantities in a ratio at the same time.
    Español: Recta numérica doble — Dos rectas numéricas alineadas para que las marcas correspondientes muestren las dos cantidades de una razón al mismo tiempo.

Example

  1. Chef Reyes uses 3 cups of apple juice for every 2 cups of sparkling water.
  2. I am comparing two amounts: apple juice and sparkling water.
  3. I write apple juice to sparkling water, in that order, as 3 to 2, or 3:2.
  4. I can also draw the ratio. On a tape diagram I give apple juice 3 equal parts and sparkling water 2 equal parts, so one batch is 5 parts in all.
  5. Those same 5 parts show a second ratio: apple juice to the whole fruit drink is 3 to 5.
  6. On a double number line I line the two amounts up: 5 cups of fruit drink pairs with 3 cups of apple juice, and 10 cups of fruit drink pairs with 6 cups of apple juice.

Practice at home

Common mistake

A common mistake in Understand Ratios is writing the ratio in the wrong order: when asked for the ratio of apple juice to sparkling water in a recipe that uses 3 cups of apple juice for every 2 cups of sparkling water, some students write 2:3 (sparkling water to apple juice) because they list the ingredients in whatever order comes to mind rather than the order the question asks for. The two ratios describe different drinks — 3:2 means 3 cups of juice for every 2 cups of water, while 2:3 would mean only 2 cups of juice for every 3 cups of water. Always match the order of your ratio to the order named in the question: "apple juice to sparkling water" means the apple juice number comes first, giving 3:2.

How families can help at home

  • Ask your student to teach you the example above in their own words.
  • Have them say why each step works, not just the answer.
