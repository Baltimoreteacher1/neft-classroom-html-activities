Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    How many moves does a one-disc tower take? A two-disc tower?

    1. A1 move and 3 moves
    2. B1 move and 2 moves
    3. C1 move and 4 moves
    4. D2 moves and 4 moves

    Answer: A — 1 move and 3 moves

    Why: You can solve a one-disc tower in 1 step and a two-disc tower in 3 steps.

  2. 2Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    (Lesson 10.1) Find the volume of a new prism with dimensions 8 by 3 by 6.

    1. 1Base area: 8 × 3 = ___.24
    2. 2Volume: 24 × 6 = ___.144
    3. 3Label the answer in ___ units.cubic
    Final answer:144

    Why: Base area: 8 × 3 = 24. Volume: 24 × 6 = 144. Label the answer in cubic units.

  3. 3Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Evaluate the new power 10^3.

    1. 1Write the base as repeated factors: ___.10 × 10 × 10
    2. 2Multiply the factors: ___.1000
    Final answer:1000

    Why: Write the base as repeated factors: 10 × 10 × 10. Multiply the factors: 1000.

  4. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the minimum number of steps to solve a FIVE-disc Tower of Hanoi puzzle?

    1. A15 steps
    2. B30 steps
    3. C31 steps
    4. D35 steps

    Answer: C — 31 steps

    Why: A four-disc tower takes 15 steps, so a five-disc tower takes 15 × 2 + 1 = 31 steps.

  5. 5Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Evaluate the new power 11^3.

    1. 1Write the base as repeated factors: ___.11 × 11 × 11
    2. 2Multiply the factors: ___.1331
    Final answer:1331

    Why: Write the base as repeated factors: 11 × 11 × 11. Multiply the factors: 1331.

  6. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the fewest number of moves needed to solve a three-disc Tower of Hanoi puzzle?

    1. A3 moves
    2. B6 moves
    3. C7 moves
    4. D9 moves

    Answer: C — 7 moves

    Why: It takes at least 7 moves to solve a three-disc Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

  7. 7Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which move is against the rules of the Tower of Hanoi?

    1. AMoving one disc at a time
    2. BMoving the top disc from a stack
    3. CPlacing a larger disc on top of a smaller disc
    4. DPlacing a smaller disc on top of a larger disc

    Answer: C — Placing a larger disc on top of a smaller disc

    Why: A larger disc can never be on top of a smaller disc — that is the rule that makes the puzzle a puzzle.

  8. 8Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What will the solved puzzle look like?

    1. AThe discs in the same order — smallest on top to largest on bottom — but on a different rod
    2. BThe discs in reverse order, largest on top
    3. CThe discs spread out with one disc on each rod
    4. DThe discs back on the starting rod in any order

    Answer: A — The discs in the same order — smallest on top to largest on bottom — but on a different rod

    Why: The goal is to rebuild the whole stack, smallest to largest, on a different rod.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 9Answer in complete sentences.

    The 15 Puzzle is a sliding puzzle with 15 tiles in a frame 4 tiles high by 4 tiles wide. You arrange the tiles from 1 to 15 by sliding one tile at a time into the open space. Sometimes it is solvable and sometimes it is not. How would you make an argument about whether a particular 15 Puzzle is solvable, using what this lesson taught about being playful?

    Sample answer: I would play the way we played the Tower of Hanoi: start with small cases and look for patterns. The frame is 4 × 4 = 16 spaces holding 15 tiles, so there is exactly one open space to slide into. I would try to solve it, keep track of which arrangements I can reach, and look for a pattern or relationship in the tile positions that separates the solvable starting setups from the impossible ones. If every attempt at a certain arrangement fails the same way, that repeated pattern becomes my evidence for arguing it is unsolvable.
