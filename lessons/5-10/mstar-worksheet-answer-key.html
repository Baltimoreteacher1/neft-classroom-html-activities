Lesson 5-10: Volume of Rectangular Prisms

Grade 6 Mathematics · Standard 6.GR.2

Question 1Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: C

A container measures 1½ feet long, 2 feet wide, and 3 feet tall. What is its volume?

  • A6 cubic feet
  • B6½ cubic feet
  • C9 cubic feet
  • D18 cubic feet

1½ = 3/2. V = 3/2 × 2 × 3 = 3 × 3 = 9 cubic feet.

  • A: The 1½ was rounded down to 1. Use 3/2 for the length: 3/2 times 2 times 3.
  • B: The three measurements were added. Volume multiplies length, width and height.
  • D: That is twice the correct volume, as if the length were 3 feet rather than 1½.

Part B — correct answer: C

Why is a mixed number converted to an improper fraction before multiplying edge lengths?

  • ABecause improper fractions are always larger.
  • BBecause volume cannot be a fraction.
  • CSo the whole-number part and the fraction part are both multiplied, not just one of them.
  • DBecause mixed numbers cannot be multiplied at all.

Multiplying only the whole-number part drops the fractional part of the edge. Writing 1½ as 3/2 keeps the entire length in the product.

Question 2Written response · 2 points

Type II Reasoning: Dropping the Fraction

Omar found the volume of a box measuring 2½ ft by 4 ft by 2 ft. He wrote: 'V = 2 × 4 × 2 = 16 cubic feet' and said the half 'was too small to matter.'

Explain Omar's misconception and give the correct volume.

Model answer: V = 5/2 × 4 × 2 = 20 cubic feet. The half foot is a real part of the edge; dropping it undercounts the volume by 4 cubic feet.

2 pointsStudent converts 2½ to 5/2, computes 5/2 × 4 × 2 = 20 cubic feet, and explains that dropping the half discards a real part of the length.
1 pointStudent gives 20 cubic feet without explaining the conversion.
0 pointsStudent agrees with Omar or gives an unrelated response.
