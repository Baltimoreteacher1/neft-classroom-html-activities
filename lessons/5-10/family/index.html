Unit 5, Lesson 10: Volume of Rectangular Prisms6.GR.2

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

Volume is the space inside a rectangular prism. There are two ways to write the same rule. You can multiply all three edges, V = length × width × height. Or you can find the base area first, B = length × width, and then stack that base up the height: V = B × h. Both give the same number, and both work when an edge is a fraction or a decimal.

Key idea: Volume is the space inside a box. Multiply length × width × height, even when an edge is a fraction. A box 2 ft long, 1½ ft wide, and 1 ft tall holds 2 × 1½ × 1 = 3 cubic feet.
Idea clave: El volumen es el espacio dentro de una caja. Multiplica largo × ancho × alto, aunque una arista sea una fracción. Una caja de 2 pies de largo, 1½ pies de ancho y 1 pie de alto contiene 2 × 1½ × 1 = 3 pies cúbicos.

How we did it in class today

  1. My capsule is 2 ft long, 1.5 ft wide, and 1 ft tall. I write V = length × width × height.
  2. I put in the numbers: V = 2 × 1.5 × 1.
  3. First I multiply 2 × 1.5 = 3.
  4. Then 3 × 1 = 3.
  5. So the volume is 3 ft³. A fractional edge like 1.5 did not change my steps at all.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Volume of Rectangular Prisms is rounding a fractional edge length to the nearest whole number before multiplying, instead of using the exact fraction or decimal. For a box that is 2 ft × 1.5 ft × 1 ft, a student might round 1.5 down to 1 and compute 2 × 1 × 1 = 2 ft³, but the correct volume uses the exact edge length: 2 × 1.5 × 1 = 3 ft³ — a full cubic foot larger. Before you submit, ask: 'Did I multiply using the exact fractional or decimal edge length, or did I round it off first?'

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
