A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

When you divide a fraction by a fraction, you keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction (the divisor) to its reciprocal. Then multiply and simplify.

Key idea: Dividing fractions asks how many of one piece fit in another. 3/4 ÷ 1/8: write 3/4 as 6/8, and 6 eighths hold 6 pieces of 1/8. So the answer is 6. Check: 6 × 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.
Idea clave: Dividir fracciones pregunta cuántas piezas de un tamaño caben en otra cantidad. 3/4 ÷ 1/8: escribe 3/4 como 6/8, y 6 octavos tienen 6 piezas de 1/8. Así que la respuesta es 6. Comprueba: 6 × 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.

Example

  1. Agent Rivera has 3/4 pound and each portion is 1/8 pound. I want 3/4 ÷ 1/8.
  2. It asks how many 1/8-pound portions fit in 3/4 pound. I rename 3/4 in eighths: 3/4 = 6/8.
  3. 6 eighths ÷ 1 eighth = 6, the same way 6 apples ÷ 1 apple = 6. That is the common-denominator method.
  4. The reciprocal shortcut gives the same answer: 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6, because each pound holds 8 eighths. Check: 6 × 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4. She can make 6 portions.
  5. One more move to know: mixed number → improper fraction: multiply the bottom by the whole number, add the top, keep the same bottom. 3 1/2 → (2 × 3) + 1 = 7 → 7/2.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Divide Fractions is flipping the wrong fraction: students flip the dividend (the first fraction) instead of the divisor (the second fraction). For example, solving 3/4 ÷ 5/8 by flipping 3/4 to 4/3 and keeping 5/8 gives 4/3 × 5/8 = 20/24 = 5/6 — but only the divisor gets flipped. Keep 3/4, change ÷ to ×, and flip only the divisor 5/8 to its reciprocal 8/5: 3/4 × 8/5 = 24/20 = 6/5. Before you submit, check that the fraction you kept (the dividend) is still facing the same way it started.
