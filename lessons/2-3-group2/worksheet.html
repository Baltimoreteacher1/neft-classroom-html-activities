Answer Key

Name: Date:
  1. 1

    A recipe needs 2/3 cup of sugar. You only have a 1/6-cup scoop. How many scoops do you need?

    1. A4 scoops
    2. B3 scoops
    3. C6 scoops
    4. D2 scoops

    2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/3 × 6/1 = 12/3 = 4 scoops.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Fractions is flipping the wrong fraction: students flip the dividend (the first fraction) instead of the divisor (the second fraction). For example, solving 3/4 ÷ 5/8 by flipping 3/4 to 4/3 and keeping 5/8 gives 4/3 × 5/8 = 20/24 = 5/6 — but only the divisor gets flipped. Keep 3/4, change ÷ to ×, and flip only the divisor 5/8 to its reciprocal 8/5: 3/4 × 8/5 = 24/20 = 6/5. Before you submit, check that the fraction you kept (the dividend) is still facing the same way it started.

  2. 2

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem2/3 ÷ 1/2
    2. 2Change ÷ to × (but forgot to flip)2/3 × 1/2
    3. 3Multiply2/6
    4. 4Simplify1/3

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student changed ÷ to × but forgot to flip the divisor. Keep 2/3, change ÷ to ×, and flip the divisor 1/2 to its reciprocal 2/1: 2/3 × 2/1 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. The correct quotient is 4/3 (1 1/3), not 1/3.

  3. 3

    Find the Division Error

    1. 1Problem2/5 ÷ 3/4
    2. 2Keep, Change, Flip2/5 × 3/4
    3. 3Multiply6/20
    4. 4Simplify3/10

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student forgot to flip. 2/5 × 4/3 (flip the divisor 3/4 to get 4/3). Then 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15.

  4. 4

    Is 3/4 ÷ 1/2 the same as 1/2 ÷ 3/4? Solve both and explain why or why not. What does this tell you about the order in division?

    Look for: not the same, 1 1/2, 2/3, order matters, not commutative.
