How it worksWorked examplereading the meaning of a point

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A graph shows crayons in boxes (x = boxes, y = crayons). The line passes through (1, 9).

  1. Find the unit rate point (1, r). r = 9, so 1 box holds 9 crayons.
  2. Read x FIRST, then y — never flip them.
  3. Interpret another point. (4, 36) means 4 boxes hold 36 crayons.
  4. Predict with the unit rate. 7 boxes: 7 × 9 = 63 → (7, 63)

Answer: (1, 9) is the unit rate: 1 box holds 9 crayons.

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake in Graph Ratio Tables is writing the ordered pair in the wrong order — putting the second table value first instead of matching (x, y) to the table's column order. For example, if a recipe table shows 2 cups of sugar for every 4 cups of flour, a student might plot (4, 2) instead of (2, 4), landing the point in a completely different spot on the grid. Before you plot, check: which quantity goes on the x-axis, and does your ordered pair start with that number?
