What is 2,485 ÷ 5?

  • A487
  • B496
  • C497
  • D507

5 × 497 = 2,485. Running the cycle: 24 ÷ 5 → 4 with 4 left, 48 ÷ 5 → 9 with 3 left, 35 ÷ 5 → 7 with 0 left, so the quotient is 497 and the remainder is 0.

Scoring notes and distractor rationales below each item.

Question 7Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: C

Use the long division algorithm to find 936 ÷ 4.

  • A209
  • B232
  • C234
  • D243

9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1; bring down 3 to make 13. 13 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 1; bring down 6 to make 16. 16 ÷ 4 = 4. The quotient is 234.

  • A: A digit was skipped. After 9 divided by 4 leaves remainder 1, bring the 3 down to make 13, which gives the tens digit.
  • B: The last step is 16 divided by 4, which is 4. Check the final digit after bringing down the 6.
  • D: The tens and ones digits are swapped. Work left to right: 2, then 3, then 4.

Part B — correct answer: B

In the D-M-S-B cycle, what does the SUBTRACT step tell you?

  • AHow many times the divisor fits into the whole dividend.
  • BHow much of the current value is left over before the next digit is brought down.
  • CWhether the quotient will be even.
  • DThe final remainder of the entire problem.

Subtracting the product from the current partial dividend leaves the remainder at that place, which the next digit is appended to.

Question 8Written response · 2 points

Type II Reasoning: The Dropped Zero

Andre computed 618 ÷ 3. He wrote: '6 ÷ 3 = 2, then 1 ÷ 3 does not go so I skip it, then 8 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 2. My answer is 22 remainder 2.'

Explain Andre's misconception and give the correct quotient.

Model answer: 618 ÷ 3 = 206. When 1 ÷ 3 does not go, the quotient gets a 0 in the tens place and the 1 stays to make 18 with the next digit; 18 ÷ 3 = 6. Skipping the place entirely loses the value of that digit.

2 pointsStudent explains that when a digit is too small the quotient gets a 0 in that place and the digit is carried to the next step (1 becomes 18), and gives 206.
1 pointStudent gives 206 without explaining the placeholder zero.
0 pointsStudent agrees with Andre or gives an unrelated response.

Part C — Show Your Thinking

Model answer and scoring guidance.

Question 9Written response · 2 points

The station must load 3,360 ration packs equally into 14 storage pods. Solve it with long division. Name each step — divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — and check your answer by multiplying.

Model answer: Long division on 3,360 ÷ 14 runs the cycle three times: 33 ÷ 14 → 2 (2 × 14 = 28, leaving 5), bring down the 6 → 56 ÷ 14 → 4 (4 × 14 = 56, leaving 0), bring down the 0 → 0 ÷ 14 → 0, so a 0 must be written in the ones place. The quotient is 240 ration packs per pod, and 14 × 240 = 3,360 proves none were left over.

Award 2 points for a complete explanation with correct mathematics, 1 point for a correct answer with thin reasoning, 0 for an unrelated response.
