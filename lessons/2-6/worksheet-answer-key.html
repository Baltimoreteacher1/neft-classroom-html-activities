    What is 936 ÷ 12?

    1. A78
    2. B87
    3. C68
    4. D76

    12 × 78 = 936. You can verify: 12 × 70 = 840, 12 × 8 = 96, 840 + 96 = 936.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Multi-Digit Numbers is skipping a placeholder zero in the quotient when a digit doesn't divide evenly, which shifts every digit after it out of place. For example, dividing 3,216 ÷ 3: 3 ÷ 3 = 1, but the next digit 2 doesn't divide by 3, so a 0 must be written before continuing — bring down the 1 to make 21 ÷ 3 = 7, then bring down 6 ÷ 3 = 2, giving the correct quotient 1,072. A student who skips writing that 0 and jumps straight to 21 ÷ 3 = 7 ends up with 172 instead of 1,072 — an answer off by a factor of ten. Always check: does a digit in your quotient go missing every time you 'can't divide'? If so, you dropped a placeholder zero.

    Spot the Mistake in 1,134 ÷ 9

    1. 1ProblemDivide 1,134 ÷ 9 with long division. Dividend = 1,134, divisor = 9.
    2. 2Divide, Multiply, Subtract9 does not fit into 1, so start with 11. 11 ÷ 9 → 1. 1 × 9 = 9, and 11 − 9 = 2
    3. 3Bring down, repeatBring down the 3 to make 23. 23 ÷ 9 → 2. 2 × 9 = 18, and 23 − 18 = 5
    4. 4Final answerQuotient = 12, remainder = 5

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The student stopped one cycle early: the 4 at the end of 1,134 was never brought down. Bring it down next to the 5 to make 54, then DIVIDE 54 ÷ 9 → 6, MULTIPLY 6 × 9 = 54, SUBTRACT 54 − 54 = 0. The quotient is 126 with remainder 0, and 9 × 126 = 1,134 checks it. Every cycle shown is correct, but the work stops too soon: the final 4 of 1,134 was never brought down. Bringing it down makes 54, and 54 ÷ 9 → 6 with 54 − 54 = 0, so the quotient is 126 and the remainder is 0 — confirmed by 9 × 126 = 1,134.

    Spot the Mistake in 2,058 ÷ 14

    1. 1ProblemDivide 2,058 ÷ 14 with long division. Dividend = 2,058, divisor = 14.
    2. 2Divide, Multiply, Subtract14 does not fit into 2, so start with 20. 20 ÷ 14 → 1. 1 × 14 = 14, and 20 − 14 = 6
    3. 3Bring down, repeatBring down the 5 to make 65. 65 ÷ 14 → 4. 4 × 14 = 56, and 65 − 56 = 9
    4. 4Bring down, repeatBring down the 8 to make 98. 98 ÷ 14 → 8. 8 × 14 = 112, and 98 − 112 = −14
    5. 5Final answerQuotient = 148

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The DIVIDE step in the last cycle chose a digit that is too big: 8 × 14 = 112 is more than the 98 you have to subtract from, and you can never subtract more than the working number. The right digit is 7, since 7 × 14 = 98 and 98 − 98 = 0. The quotient is 147 with remainder 0, and 14 × 147 = 2,058 rebuilds the dividend. The last DIVIDE step is where it breaks: the student wrote 8, but 8 × 14 = 112 is larger than the 98 they had to subtract from, so the difference came out negative. The largest digit that fits is 7, since 7 × 14 = 98 leaves 0, so the quotient is 147 and 14 × 147 = 2,058 checks out.

    6. 6Final answerQuotient = 9 with 196 left over

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The SUBTRACT step is wrong: 153 − 144 = 9, not 19. Long division has a built-in warning here — the difference after you subtract must always be SMALLER than the divisor, and 19 is bigger than 16. With the correct difference of 9, bring down the 6 to make 96, then DIVIDE 96 ÷ 16 → 6 and SUBTRACT 96 − 96 = 0. The quotient is 96 with remainder 0, and 16 × 96 = 1,536. Step 3 is the break: 153 − 144 = 9, not 19. Long division has a built-in warning here — if the difference after SUBTRACT is as big as the divisor, another whole group still fits. With the correct difference of 9, bringing down the 6 gives 96, and 96 ÷ 16 → 6 with nothing left, so the quotient is 96 and 16 × 96 = 1,536 confirms it.
