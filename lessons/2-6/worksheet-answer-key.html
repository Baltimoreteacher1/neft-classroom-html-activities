    ProblemDivideMultiply & SubtractBring DownQuotient
    384 ÷ 8
    672 ÷ 6

    Hint Read the row left to right — it is one trip around the cycle: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.

    Why: Long division builds the quotient one digit at a time. In 384 ÷ 8 the first working number is 38: DIVIDE 38 ÷ 8 → 4, MULTIPLY 4 × 8 = 32, SUBTRACT 38 − 32 = 6, BRING DOWN the 4 to make 64, and 64 ÷ 8 → 8 with nothing left — so the quotient is 48. The second row runs the same cycle three times: 6 ÷ 6 → 1, then 7 ÷ 6 → 1 with 1 left, then 12 ÷ 6 → 2, giving 112.

Part 2Practice the skillshow every step
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 936 ÷ 12?

    1. A68
    2. B76
    3. C78
    4. D87

    Hint Estimate first: 12 × 70 = 840 and 12 × 80 = 960, so the quotient must land between 70 and 80.

    Answer: C — 78

    Why: 12 × 78 = 936. You can verify: 12 × 70 = 840, 12 × 8 = 96, 840 + 96 = 936.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Multi-Digit Numbers is skipping a placeholder zero in the quotient when a digit doesn't divide evenly, which shifts every digit after it out of place. For example, dividing 3,216 ÷ 3: 3 ÷ 3 = 1, but the next digit 2 doesn't divide by 3, so a 0 must be written before continuing — bring down the 1 to make 21 ÷ 3 = 7, then bring down 6 ÷ 3 = 2, giving the correct quotient 1,072. A student who skips writing that 0 and jumps straight to 21 ÷ 3 = 7 ends up with 172 instead of 1,072 — an answer off by a factor of ten. Always check: does a digit in your quotient go missing every time you 'can't divide'? If so, you dropped a placeholder zero.

    Spot the Mistake in 1,134 ÷ 9

    1. 1ProblemDivide 1,134 ÷ 9 with long division. Dividend = 1,134, divisor = 9.
    2. 2Divide, Multiply, Subtract9 does not fit into 1, so start with 11. 11 ÷ 9 → 1. 1 × 9 = 9, and 11 − 9 = 2
    3. 3Bring down, repeatBring down the 3 to make 23. 23 ÷ 9 → 2. 2 × 9 = 18, and 23 − 18 = 5
    4. 4Final answerQuotient = 12, remainder = 5

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Count the digits of 1,134 that have been used so far. Is one of them still waiting to be brought down?

    Mistake: Step 4. Every cycle shown is correct, but the work stops too soon: the final 4 of 1,134 was never brought down. Bringing it down makes 54, and 54 ÷ 9 → 6 with 54 − 54 = 0, so the quotient is 126 and the remainder is 0 — confirmed by 9 × 126 = 1,134.

    Correct work: The student stopped one cycle early: the 4 at the end of 1,134 was never brought down. Bring it down next to the 5 to make 54, then DIVIDE 54 ÷ 9 → 6, MULTIPLY 6 × 9 = 54, SUBTRACT 54 − 54 = 0. The quotient is 126 with remainder 0, and 9 × 126 = 1,134 checks it.

  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake in 2,058 ÷ 14

    1. 1ProblemDivide 2,058 ÷ 14 with long division. Dividend = 2,058, divisor = 14.
    2. 2Divide, Multiply, Subtract14 does not fit into 2, so start with 20. 20 ÷ 14 → 1. 1 × 14 = 14, and 20 − 14 = 6
    3. 3Bring down, repeatBring down the 5 to make 65. 65 ÷ 14 → 4. 4 × 14 = 56, and 65 − 56 = 9
    4. 4Bring down, repeatBring down the 8 to make 98. 98 ÷ 14 → 8. 8 × 14 = 112, and 98 − 112 = −14
    5. 5Final answerQuotient = 148

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The last DIVIDE step is where it breaks: the student wrote 8, but 8 × 14 = 112 is larger than the 98 they had to subtract from, so the difference came out negative. The largest digit that fits is 7, since 7 × 14 = 98 leaves 0, so the quotient is 147 and 14 × 147 = 2,058 checks out.

    Correct work: The DIVIDE step in the last cycle chose a digit that is too big: 8 × 14 = 112 is more than the 98 you have to subtract from, and you can never subtract more than the working number. The right digit is 7, since 7 × 14 = 98 and 98 − 98 = 0. The quotient is 147 with remainder 0, and 14 × 147 = 2,058 rebuilds the dividend.

With the correct difference of 9, bringing down the 6 gives 96, and 96 ÷ 16 → 6 with nothing left, so the quotient is 96 and 16 × 96 = 1,536 confirms it.
