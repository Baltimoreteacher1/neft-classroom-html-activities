📚 Vocabulary

WordWhat it meansExample
Percent of a Number
Porcentaje de un número		A part of a number found by multiplying the number by the percent written as a decimal.
Una parte de un número que se halla multiplicando el número por el porcentaje escrito como decimal.		0.30 × 80 = 24 — The percent was written as a decimal before multiplying.
Percent
Porcentaje		A way to compare a number to 100, shown with the % sign.
Una manera de comparar un número con 100, con el signo %.		9 out of 100 students walk to school → 9% — The comparison is already out of 100.
Base
Base		The whole amount you start with — the number right after the word “of.”
La cantidad total con la que empiezas: el número que va después de la palabra “de”.		The 80 in “30% of 80” — It is the whole amount, right after the word “of”.
Part
Parte		How much of the base (the whole) you get. It is your answer.
Cuánto obtienes de la base (el total). Es tu respuesta.		The 24 in “30% of 80 is 24” — It is the piece of the base the percent describes.
Equation
Ecuación		A number sentence with an equal sign. The percent one is: part = percent × base.
Una oración numérica con signo de igual. La de porcentaje es: parte = porcentaje × base.		0.25 × n = 30 — Percent times whole equals part, with the unknown whole as n.
Discount
Descuento		Money taken off the first price to make it cheaper.
Dinero que se quita del precio original para que sea más barato.		$20 off a $80 jacket — Money taken off the original price.
Double number line
Recta numérica doble		Two number lines that line up percents with amounts.
Dos rectas numéricas que alinean porcentajes con cantidades.		A line from 0 to 300 students over a line from 0% to 100% — Each amount lines up with its percent.
Equivalent fraction
Fracción equivalente		A fraction with the same value written with different numbers.
Una fracción con el mismo valor escrita con números diferentes.		16/20 and 80/100 — Both the top and bottom were multiplied by 5.

🚀 Launch

It's Discount Day at the arcade. Different games are offering percent-off deals on ticket prices. The claw machine is 25% off its usual 60-ticket price, and the racing game is 40% off 80 tickets. You need to calculate the discounts to figure out how many tickets each game actually costs!

How do I find the percent of a number?

A percent is a piece of a whole. The whole is called the base — it is the number right after the word “of,” and “of” means multiply. Your answer is the piece you get, called the part.

Key idea: Finding the Percent of a Number. Formula: Part = Percent × Whole. 1. Identify the percent and the whole (base amount). 2. Convert the percent to a decimal or simplified fraction. 3. Multiply the decimal/fraction by the whole to calculate the part

Watch me

GIVEN DATA / MODEL
  • The claw machine costs 60 tickets, and it is 25% off. I need 25% of 60.
  • Step 1 — find the base (the whole): the number after “of” is 60.
  • Step 2 — the percent as a decimal: 25% means 25 out of 100, so 25 ÷ 100 = 0.25.
  • Step 3 — multiply: 0.25 × 60 = 15.
  • The discount is 15 tickets. Check: 15 is about a quarter of 60, so that makes sense.
