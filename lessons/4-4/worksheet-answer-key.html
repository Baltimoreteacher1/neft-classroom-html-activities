    Find each percent of a number using the formula: Part = Percent × Base.

    PercentBasePart
    25%6015
    40%80
    15%200

    Hint The finished row shows the recipe: 25% of 60 became 0.25 × 60 = 15. Your rows use the same formula.

    Why: Part = Percent × Base works once the percent is written as a decimal, exactly as the finished row shows with 0.25 × 60 = 15. So 40% of 80 is 0.40 × 80 = 32, and 15% of 200 is 0.15 × 200 = 30.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 20% of 150?

    1. A20
    2. B30
    3. C35
    4. D75

    Hint '20% of 150' means you want a PART of 150 — the answer should be smaller than 150.

    Answer: B — 30

    Why: 20% = 0.20. Multiply: 0.20 × 150 = 30.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Always divide the percent by 100 to get the decimal, then multiply that decimal by the base.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 50% of 84?

    1. A34
    2. B42
    3. C50
    4. D168

    Hint 50% is a friendly percent — it means exactly one-half of the number.

    Answer: B — 42

    Why: 50% = 0.50. Multiply: 0.50 × 84 = 42. (Or simply divide by 2: 84 ÷ 2 = 42.)

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Always divide the percent by 100 to get the decimal, then multiply that decimal by the base.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 10% of 320?

    1. A3.2
    2. B32
    3. C64
    4. D320

    Hint 10% means one-tenth — there's a shortcut that needs no multiplication at all.

    Answer: B — 32

    Why: 10% = 0.10. Multiply: 0.10 × 320 = 32. (Or move the decimal one place left.)

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Always divide the percent by 100 to get the decimal, then multiply that decimal by the base.

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What percent of 50 is 15?

    1. A15%
    2. B30%
    3. C35%
    4. D50%

    Hint This question is backwards from the others: you have the part (15) and the whole (50), and you need the PERCENT.

    Answer: B — 30%

    Why: 15/50 → 50 × 2 = 100, so 15 × 2 = 30. That makes 15/50 = 30/100 = 30%.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Always divide the percent by 100 to get the decimal, then multiply that decimal by the base.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Find 30% of 8030% of 80
    2. 2Multiply by the percent number30 × 80 = 2,400
    3. 3Final answer30% of 80 = 2,400

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint A percent is not a whole number — change 30% to a decimal before you multiply.

    Mistake: Step 2. The student multiplied by 30 instead of by 30%. Percent means per hundred, so 30% = 30 ÷ 100 = 0.30, and 0.30 × 80 = 24. An answer of 2,400 should also look wrong right away, since a part of 80 can never be larger than 80.

    Correct work: First change the percent to a decimal: 30% = 30 ÷ 100 = 0.30. Then multiply by the base: 0.30 × 80 = 24. So 30% of 80 = 24, not 2,400. The student multiplied by 30 instead of 0.30.

    Complete each row to find the percent of a number.

    PercentBasePart
    10%250
    75%120
    35%200
    8%50

    Why: Each row is percent × base with the percent written as a decimal: 0.10 × 250 = 25, 0.75 × 120 = 90, 0.35 × 200 = 70, and 0.08 × 50 = 4. Every part comes out smaller than its base because each percent is under 100%.

  2. 2Use the model to solve. Show your work.

    200 ÷ 4 = 50. Each 25% section = 50 points. So 25% of 200 = 50.

    Why: The bar cuts the 200 points into 4 equal sections, and 4 sections of 25% make 100%, so one section is 200 ÷ 4 = 50 points. That single section is exactly 25% of 200, so 25% of 200 = 50.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    An arcade prize costs 240 tickets. If you have a coupon for 15% off, how many tickets do you save?

    1. A15 tickets
    2. B24 tickets
    3. C36 tickets
    4. D204 tickets

    Answer: C — 36 tickets

    Why: 15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36 tickets saved.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Always divide the percent by 100 to get the decimal, then multiply that decimal by the base.

  2. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A student scored 72 out of 90 on a test. What percent did they score?

    1. A72%
    2. B75%
    3. C80%
    4. D90%

    Answer: C — 80%

    Why: 72/90 simplifies to 8/10, and 10 × 10 = 100, so 8 × 10 = 80. That makes 72/90 = 80/100 = 80%.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Find the Percent of a Number is multiplying by the percent number itself instead of its decimal form — for example, finding 30% of 80 by computing 30 × 80 = 2,400 instead of 0.30 × 80 = 24. Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Find 15% of 6015% of 60
    2. 2Change the percent to a decimal15% = 0.15
    3. 3Multiply by the wrong number0.15 × 15 = 2.25
    4. 4Final answer15% of 60 = 2.25

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. Converting 15% to 0.15 was right, but then the student multiplied 0.15 by 15, reusing the percent number instead of the base. The word 'of' points to the base, so it is 0.15 × 60 = 9.

    Correct work: The base is 60, not 15. The word "of" tells you the base: 15% OF 60. Multiply by the base: 0.15 × 60 = 9. So 15% of 60 = 9, not 2.25. The student multiplied by the percent number instead of the base.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    A video game character starts with 500 health points. Complete the table to find how many health points are lost or gained.

    EventPercent ChangeCalculationHealth Points After
    Hit by enemyLose 20%500 × 0.20 = 100 lost400
    Health potionGain 50% of current
    Poison trapLose 10% of current

    Why: Each percent applies to the health the character has at that moment, not to the original 500. After the hit, health is 400, so the potion gives 400 × 0.50 = 200 gained for 600 health, and the poison then takes 600 × 0.10 = 60 lost, leaving 540.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Percent Error

    1. 1Find 30% of 9030% of 90
    2. 2Convert percent to decimal30% = 3.0
    3. 3Multiply3.0 × 90 = 270
    4. 4Final answer30% of 90 = 270

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The conversion step is where it goes wrong: the student moved the decimal only one place and wrote 30% = 3.0. A percent is divided by 100, which moves the decimal two places, so 30% = 0.30 and 0.30 × 90 = 27.

    Correct work: 30% = 0.30 (divide by 100, not by 10). 0.30 × 90 = 27. The correct answer is 27.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A class has 32 students. 25% are in the math club, 50% are in the reading club, and 12.5% are in both. How many students are in each group? Can the total of all three groups be more than 32? Explain why or why not.

    Look for: 8, 16, 4, overlap, can, counted twice, both

    Why: Taking each percent of the same 32 students gives 25% = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 in math club, 50% = 32 ÷ 2 = 16 in reading club, and 12.5% = 32 ÷ 8 = 4 in both. Adding 8 + 16 + 4 = 28 is misleading, because the 4 students in both are already inside the 8 and the 16 — counting them a third time overlaps, and the number of different students in at least one club is 8 + 16 − 4 = 20.
