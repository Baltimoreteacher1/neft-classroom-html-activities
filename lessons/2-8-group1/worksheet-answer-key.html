    Find the mean of: 4, 6, 8, 10

    1. A7
    2. B28
    3. C8
    4. D6

    Add the values: 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 = 28. There are 4 values, so the mean is 28 ÷ 4 = 7.

    Watch for: The most common mistake with the mean is adding correctly and then dividing by the wrong number — or not dividing at all. Students report the total (for 8, 10, 6, 12 they say the mean is 36) or they divide by a count that does not match the data, such as dividing a 4-value total by 5, or skipping a zero when counting the values. Before you finish, do two things: count the values in the data set out loud, and check that your mean lands between the smallest and largest value. A mean of 36 for data that never goes above 12 is impossible.

    1. 1

      The Mean Mistake (forgot to divide)

      1. 1Data setPoints scored in 4 games: 8, 10, 6, 12
      2. 2GoalFind the MEAN number of points per game.
      3. 3Add the values8 + 10 + 6 + 12 = 36
      4. 4AnswerMean = 36 points

      Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

      The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: Adding is only half of finding a mean. After the total (36) you must DIVIDE by how many values there are: 36 ÷ 4 = 9. The mean is 9 points per game, not 36. The sum 8 + 10 + 6 + 12 = 36 is correct, but the answer stops there. Dividing by the 4 values gives 36 ÷ 4 = 9. A mean of 36 is also impossible here, because no single game reached 36 points — a mean must fall between the smallest and largest value.

      If every one of the 10 games hit the target of 15 points, the total would be 15 × 10 = 150 points. That is the total she needs for a mean of 15.

    5. 5

      The Wrong Divisor

      1. 1Data setPoints in 4 games: 6, 11, 9, 14
      2. 2GoalFind the MEAN number of points per game.
      3. 3Add the values6 + 11 + 9 + 14 = 40
      4. 4DivideThe student divides by 5: 40 ÷ 5 = 8, so the mean is 8.

      Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

      The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The total of 40 is correct, but the divisor is not. There are 4 games in this data set, not 5, so the mean is 40 ÷ 4 = 10 points per game. Adding gives 40, which is correct. The error is the divisor: the mean divides by how many values the data set actually has, and this set lists four games. Dividing by 5 shares the points among a game that was never played, so the mean comes out too small.
