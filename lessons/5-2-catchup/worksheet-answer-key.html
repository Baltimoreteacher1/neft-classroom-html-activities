    (Lesson 5.1) What is the area of a parallelogram with base 10 cm and height 7 cm?

    1. A70 sq cm
    2. B34 sq cm
    3. C17 sq cm
    4. D70 cm

    A = b × h = 10 × 7 = 70 square centimeters.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Area of Trapezoids is forgetting the ½ in A = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h. After adding the bases and multiplying by the height, students stop there instead of taking half — for example, with bases 6 ft and 10 ft and a height of 4 ft, they compute (6 + 10) × 4 = 64 sq ft instead of taking half to get the correct 32 sq ft. That unhalved number is really the area of the parallelogram made from two trapezoids, not the area of one trapezoid. Before you submit, always ask: "Did I take half of my last answer?"

    1. 1Read the parallelogrambase = 9 cm, slanted side = 10 cm, height = 6 cm
    2. 2Write the formulaA = base × height
    3. 3Substitute the valuesA = 9 × 10
    4. 4Calculate the areaA = 90 sq cm

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 3. Correct work: The height is 6 cm, not the slanted side of 10 cm. Substitute the perpendicular height: A = 9 × 6 = 54 sq cm. Only the straight-up height belongs in A = base × height. The substitution step uses 10 cm, which is the slanted side, instead of the 6 cm height listed in the first step. Only the perpendicular height belongs in A = base × height, so A = 9 × 6 = 54 sq cm.

    1. 1Identify base and heightbase = 12 ft, height = 7 ft
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × base × height
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × 12 × 7
    4. 4CalculateA = (½ × 12) × (½ × 7) = 6 × 3.5 = 21 sq ft

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: There is only ONE ½ in the formula, not two. The student halved both the base and the height. Multiply base × height first, then take half just once: 12 × 7 = 84, then ½ × 84 = 42 sq ft. There is only ONE ½ in A = ½ × base × height, but the calculation halved the base and the height separately. Multiply first, then halve a single time: 12 × 7 = 84, and ½ × 84 = 42 sq ft. Halving twice cuts the area to a quarter of the rectangle, which is why 21 came out as exactly half of the true answer.

    (Lesson 5.3) Using the Wrong Height

    1. 1Read the blueprintA trapezoid window has b1 = 7 ft and b2 = 13 ft. The slanted side measures 10 ft, and the straight-up height between the bases is 6 ft.
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × (b1 + b2) × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × (7 + 13) × 10
    4. 4CalculateA = ½ × 20 × 10 = 100 sq ft

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The student used the slanted side (10 ft) as the height instead of the true perpendicular height (6 ft). The height in the trapezoid formula must be measured straight up between the two bases, not along the slanted side. The correct area is A = ½ × (7 + 13) × 6 = ½ × 20 × 6 = 60 sq ft, not 100 sq ft. The 10 ft slanted side was substituted for the height, but the height of a trapezoid is the straight-up perpendicular distance between the two bases, which is 6 ft here. Using the true height gives A = ½ × (7 + 13) × 6 = ½ × 20 × 6 = 60 sq ft; the slant is always longer, which is why 100 sq ft came out too big.

    (Lesson 5.1) Find the Area Error

    1. 1Identify base and heightb = 11 m, slant side = 8 m, h = 6 m
    2. 2Write the formulaA = b × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = 11 × 8
    4. 4CalculateA = 88 sq m

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 3. Correct work: The height is 6 m, not the slant side of 8 m. A = 11 × 6 = 66 sq m. Always use the perpendicular height, not the slanted side. The Substitute step plugs in the 8 m slant side for h, but the first step already labeled the height as 6 m. The slant side is longer than the true straight-up distance, so the correct area is A = 11 × 6 = 66 sq m.

  2. 2

    (Lesson 5.2) Find the Area Error

    1. 1Identify base and heightb = 14 m, h = 9 m
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × b × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × 14 × 9
    4. 4CalculateA = ½ × 23 = 11.5 sq m

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: 14 × 9 = 126, then ½ × 126 = 63 sq m. The student added 14 + 9 = 23 instead of multiplying 14 × 9 = 126. The setup A = ½ × 14 × 9 is written correctly, but the calculation added 14 + 9 = 23 instead of multiplying. Base and height must be multiplied: 14 × 9 = 126, and then ½ × 126 = 63 sq m.

  3. 3

    (Lesson 5.3) Find the Area Error

    1. 1Identify the bases and heightb₁ = 10 m, b₂ = 6 m, h = 7 m
    2. 2Write the formulaA = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h
    3. 3Substitute valuesA = ½ × (10 + 6) × 7
    4. 4CalculateA = (10 + 6) × 7 = 112 sq m

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The student forgot to multiply by ½. A = ½ × 16 × 7 = ½ × 112 = 56 sq m. The ½ appears in the formula and in the substitution, then disappears in the final calculation. Keeping it gives A = ½ × 16 × 7 = ½ × 112 = 56 sq m, which is exactly half of the 112 that was reported.
