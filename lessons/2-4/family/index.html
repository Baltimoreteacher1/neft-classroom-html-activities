A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A box plot is a simple picture that shows how a set of data is spread out, using just five key numbers: the lowest value, the first quartile (Q1), the median (middle), the third quartile (Q3), and the highest value.

Key idea: A box plot shows how data is spread out using five numbers: the lowest, the first quartile (Q1), the median, the third quartile (Q3), and the highest. The box goes from Q1 to Q3 and holds the middle half of the data; its length is Q3 − Q1. Lines called whiskers reach from the box out to the lowest and highest numbers.
Idea clave: Un diagrama de caja muestra cómo se reparten los datos usando cinco números: el menor, el primer cuartil (Q1), la mediana, el tercer cuartil (Q3) y el mayor. La caja va de Q1 a Q3 y contiene la mitad central de los datos; su largo es Q3 − Q1. Unas líneas llamadas bigotes van desde la caja hasta el número menor y el número mayor.

How we did it in class today

  1. My data, in order: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 18, 22, 24, 28. There are 11 numbers.
  2. The middle number is the 6th one, 14, so the median = 14.
  3. The lower half is 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. Its middle is 8, so Q1 = 8.
  4. The upper half is 15, 18, 22, 24, 28. Its middle is 22, so Q3 = 22.
  5. The lowest is 4 and the highest is 28. I draw a box from 8 to 22, a line at 14, and whiskers out to 4 and 28.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Display Data: Box Plots is treating a quartile as just one data point instead of the median of a half — for the lower half 4, 8, 10, 14, some students pick 10 (the last value) as Q1 instead of finding the median of that half: Q1 = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9. A second common slip is finding IQR by adding the quartiles instead of subtracting, writing IQR = Q3 + Q1 (like 30 + 18 = 48) instead of IQR = Q3 − Q1 (30 − 18 = 12) — always order the data first, then subtract Q1 from Q3 to find the spread of the middle 50%.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
