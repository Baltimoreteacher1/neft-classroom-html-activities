Learning target I can make and read a box plot to summarize a data set.

  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The values 4, 6, 9, 12 and 19 are already in order. What is the median?

    1. A6
    2. B9
    3. C10
    4. D12

    Answer: B — 9

    Why: With five values in order, the median is the third one: 9.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Display Data: Box Plots is treating a quartile as just one data point instead of the median of a half — for the lower half 4, 8, 10, 14, some students pick 10 (the last value) as Q1 instead of finding the median of that half: Q1 = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9. A second common slip is finding IQR by adding the quartiles instead of subtracting, writing IQR = Q3 + Q1 (like 30 + 18 = 48) instead of IQR = Q3 − Q1 (30 − 18 = 12) — always order the data first, then subtract Q1 from Q3 to find the spread of the middle 50%.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each team's ordered scores to its five-number summary (min, Q1, median, Q3, max).

    1. AHawks: 5, 8, 11, 15, 18, 21, 27
    2. BComets: 10, 13, 16, 20, 24, 28, 33
    3. CJets: 2, 6, 9, 14, 19, 23, 30
    4. DSuns: 4, 7, 10, 16, 22, 25, 31
    • AMin 5, Q1 8, Median 15, Q3 21, Max 27
    • BMin 10, Q1 13, Median 20, Q3 28, Max 33
    • CMin 2, Q1 6, Median 14, Q3 23, Max 30
    • DMin 4, Q1 7, Median 16, Q3 25, Max 31

    Why: Each team recorded 7 ordered scores, so the summary is the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 6th, and 7th values: the median is the 4th score and each quartile is the middle of a three-score half. That gives Hawks 5, 8, 15, 21, 27; Comets 10, 13, 20, 28, 33; Jets 2, 6, 14, 23, 30; and Suns 4, 7, 16, 25, 31.

  2. 2Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Each card describes a part of the stadium box plot. Sort each card under the matching box plot part.

    Groups: Part of the five-number summary Measure of spread
    • Part of the five-number summaryThe minimum — the lowest score
    • Part of the five-number summaryQ1 — the median of the lower half
    • Part of the five-number summaryThe median — the middle score
    • Part of the five-number summaryQ3 — the median of the upper half
    • Part of the five-number summaryThe maximum — the highest score
    • Measure of spreadIQR — Q3 minus Q1
    • Measure of spreadRange — maximum minus minimum

    Why: The five-number summary names five positions in the data — minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum — and each one is a single value you could mark on the number line. IQR = Q3 − Q1 and range = maximum − minimum are different: each is a subtraction that reports a distance, which is why they belong under measures of spread.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Two basketball teams' scoring data are displayed as box plots. Team A: Min=40, Q1=52, Median=60, Q3=68, Max=75. Team B: Min=35, Q1=55, Median=61, Q3=65, Max=80. A fan says Team B is better because they scored 80 points once. Is the fan right that Team B is better because of the 80-point game?

    1. AYes — 80 is the highest score in the data
    2. BNo — Team A's maximum is higher
    3. CYes — the maximum always decides
    4. DNo — one high game is a single extreme value, not typical performance

    Answer: D — No — one high game is a single extreme value, not typical performance

    Why: The 80 is the tip of a whisker — one game. Team B does look more consistent, but for a better reason: its middle 50% (55–65) is both higher and tighter than Team A's (52–68).

    Watch for: A common mistake in Display Data: Box Plots is treating a quartile as just one data point instead of the median of a half — for the lower half 4, 8, 10, 14, some students pick 10 (the last value) as Q1 instead of finding the median of that half: Q1 = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9. A second common slip is finding IQR by adding the quartiles instead of subtracting, writing IQR = Q3 + Q1 (like 30 + 18 = 48) instead of IQR = Q3 − Q1 (30 − 18 = 12) — always order the data first, then subtract Q1 from Q3 to find the spread of the middle 50%.

    The Hawks' points per quarter are 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12. Resting on the scoreboard, what are the minimum, median, and maximum?

    1. AMinimum 0, median 7, maximum 12
    2. BMinimum 2, median 7, maximum 12
    3. CMinimum 2, median 5, maximum 12
    4. DMinimum 2, median 8, maximum 10

    Answer: B — Minimum 2, median 7, maximum 12

    Why: The data is already in order. The smallest value is 2 and the largest is 12. There are 7 values, so the median is the 4th value, which is 7.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Display Data: Box Plots is treating a quartile as just one data point instead of the median of a half — for the lower half 4, 8, 10, 14, some students pick 10 (the last value) as Q1 instead of finding the median of that half: Q1 = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9.   3. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The Comets scored 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26, 30, 34 across 8 games. After ordering, what are Q1 and Q3?

    1. AQ1 = 12, Q3 = 28
    2. BQ1 = 10, Q3 = 30
    3. CQ1 = 14, Q3 = 26
    4. DQ1 = 20, Q3 = 34

    Answer: A — Q1 = 12, Q3 = 28

    Why: With 8 values the median splits the data into a lower half (6, 10, 14, 18) and an upper half (22, 26, 30, 34). Q1 is the median of the lower half: (10 + 14) ÷ 2 = 12. Q3 is the median of the upper half: (26 + 30) ÷ 2 = 28.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Display Data: Box Plots is treating a quartile as just one data point instead of the median of a half — for the lower half 4, 8, 10, 14, some students pick 10 (the last value) as Q1 instead of finding the median of that half: Q1 = (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = 9.     A box plot for the Rockets shows Min = 8, Q1 = 15, Median = 22, Q3 = 30, Max = 38. The middle 50% of their scores falls between which two values?

    1. ABetween 8 and 38
    2. BBetween 8 and 22
    3. CBetween 15 and 30
    4. DBetween 22 and 38

    Answer: C — Between 15 and 30

    Why: The box of a box plot stretches from Q1 to Q3, and that box always holds the middle 50% of the data. Here the box goes from Q1 = 15 to Q3 = 30.
