Where we left off

Our goal: I can recognize when two quantities change together, and identify which one is the independent variable and which is the dependent variable, explain how I can tell, and judge a case I have not seen before.

The big idea: The independent variable is subject to choice. The dependent variable changes in response to it — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me sort Akela's experiment

  1. The two quantities are hours of sunlight per day and average plant height after 3 weeks.
  2. Akela CHOSE where to put each tray, so she chose the sunlight. Sunlight is subject to choice — that makes it the independent variable.
  3. She did not choose how tall the plants got. Height responded. That makes height the dependent variable.
  4. Say it as a sentence: plant height depends on sunlight.

    Warm restartA ticket costs $8. Which equation represents "8 tickets cost $56"?

    1. A8t = 56
    2. Bt + 8 = 56
    3. Ct − 8 = 56
    4. Dt ÷ 8 = 56

    How do you know?

    Each of the 8 tickets costs t, so 8 × t = 56. The total is built by multiplying, not by adding.

    x is multiplied by 4, so divide both sides by 4: 32 ÷ 4 = 8.

Answer, then say how you know — the reason is the part that counts.

  1. 5

    Think it throughThe number of pizzas made each week determines other costs at the shop. Which variable is the independent variable in this relationship?

    1. Athe cost of cheese used
    2. Bthe number of pizzas made
    3. Cthe number of employees
    4. Dthe number of toppings allowed

    Why is that the answer?

    The number of pizzas is independent because it is the value that determines the other costs. Cost of cheese is dependent because it changes as a result of pizzas made; the number of employees and toppings allowed are fixed limits, not quantities that change with pizza count.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhich two quantities have a dependent relationship in this scenario?

    1. Anumber of employees and number of pizzas made
    2. Bcost of cheese and number of pizzas made
    3. Cnumber of toppings allowed and cost of sauce
    4. Dnumber of employees and number of toppings allowed

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    The cost of cheese rises as more pizzas are made ($1.15 per pizza), so it depends on the number of pizzas. The number of employees (6) and the topping limit (3) are fixed values that stay the same no matter how many pizzas are made, so they are not part of a dependent relationship here.

  3. 7

    Think it throughThe shop makes 20 pizzas in a week. Which of these does NOT depend on the number of pizzas sold?

    1. Athe total cost of cheese
    2. Bthe number of employees
    3. Cthe total cost of sauce
    4. Dthe total spent on cheese and sauce combined

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    Staff count (6 employees) is fixed and stays the same regardless of pizza volume. Cheese cost, sauce cost, and their combined total all scale up as more pizzas are made, so those three DO depend on the number of pizzas.

  4. 8

    Back to the modelAkela put her three bean trays in three different spots, so each got a different amount of sunlight. After three weeks she measured the height of the plants. Which of those two quantities did Akela choose, and which one responded?

    Akela chose where to place each tray, which set the hours of sunlight; the plant height was measured afterward, in response to the sunlight.

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • The quantity that is subject to choice is the ___ ___. → independent variable
  • The quantity that changes in response to the other is the ___ ___. → dependent variable
  • Say it in order: the responding quantity ___ ___ the chosen one. → depends on
  • Something that can be counted or measured is a ___. → quantity

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Naming the variables backward — calling the responding quantity 'independent' because it is the one the question asks about.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — A dog walker charges by the hour. Which sentence is correct?

    1. AThe total charge depends on the number of hours, so hours is the independent variable.
    2. BThe number of hours depends on the total charge, so the charge is the independent variable.
    3. CBoth quantities are independent variables.
    4. DNeither quantity depends on the other.

    Explain your choice.

    The walker chooses how many hours to work; the money that results responds to that choice. Hours is subject to choice, so hours is independent and the total charge is dependent.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 8.7Explain your thinking — A box of donuts has some donuts. After giving away 7, there are fewer than 5 left. Which inequality represents the starting number of donuts d?

    1. Ad − 7 < 5
    2. Bd + 7 < 5
    3. Cd − 7 > 5
    4. Dd − 7 = 5

    How do you know?

    Starting with d donuts and giving away 7 leaves fewer than 5: d − 7 < 5. Solve: d < 12. ✓

  3. 14

    From Lesson 8.7Why is one an equation and the other an inequality?

    1. ABecause equations are for money and inequalities are for counting
    2. BBecause 4 is smaller than 320
    3. CThere is no real difference
    4. DThe flashlights cost exactly $60, but the budget is a limit she must stay under

    How do you know?

    'Exactly' points to one value, so it is an equation. A budget — 'at most' — allows a whole range of values, so it is an inequality.
