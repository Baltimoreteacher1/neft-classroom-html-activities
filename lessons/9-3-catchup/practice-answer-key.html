  1. 5

    Think it throughIn Diamond's situation, what should be the independent variable in both equations?

    1. Athe total cost
    2. Bthe price per song
    3. Cthe type of service (purchase or stream)
    4. Dthe number of songs

    Why is that the answer?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Total cost depends on how many songs Diamond buys or streams, so the number of songs is independent and total cost is dependent. Price per song is a fixed rate (a constant in the equation, not a variable), and the service type determines WHICH equation to use rather than being a variable inside one.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhy does Diamond need TWO separate equations instead of one equation for both options?

    1. APurchasing and streaming likely follow different cost rules (such as a per-song price vs. a flat rate), so each relationship between songs and cost is different
    2. BThe number of songs is different for each option
    3. COne equation can only have one variable
    4. DStreaming has no relationship between songs and cost

    How do you know?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Purchasing and streaming follow different cost rules, so the relationship between number of songs and total cost differs between them, requiring separate equations. The independent variable (number of songs) is the same idea in both equations, equations regularly have two variables, and streaming still relates cost to how much is used.

  3. 7

    Think it throughOnce Diamond has both equations, how does she use them to decide which option is better?

    1. AAdd the two equations together to find one combined cost
    2. BPick whichever equation has the smaller numbers written in it, without solving anything
    3. CSubstitute the same number of songs into both equations and compare the two total costs
    4. DGraph only one equation and assume the other follows the same line

    Explain your thinking.

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Substituting the same number of songs into both equations lets Diamond compare the actual total costs for that amount of listening. Adding the equations doesn't answer a comparison question, comparing raw coefficients ignores how they combine with the number of songs, and assuming one graph represents both ignores that purchasing and streaming are different relationships.

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Writing the equation with addition — c = 24.95 + m — instead of multiplication. The fee is charged EVERY month, so the total is the rate times the number of months; always test the equation against a row of the table.

  1. 10

    Show what you know(Catch-up check, from Lesson 9.2) On the football ticket graph, what does the point (3, 135) represent?

    1. A3 tickets cost a total of $135
    2. B3 tickets cost $135 each
    3. C135 tickets cost a total of $3
    4. DThe game lasts 135 minutes

    Explain your choice.

    I know it is ___ because ___ .

    The independent variable comes first in an ordered pair. Here that is the number of tickets, so 3 is the tickets. Then find the cost: 3 × $45 = $135. The point pairs 3 tickets with a total cost of $135.

  2. 11

    From 8.4–8.7 Catch-Up(Catch-up check, from Lesson 8.5) How would you graph the inequality x ≥ 9?

    1. AClosed circle at 9, shaded right
    2. BOpen circle at 9, shaded right
    3. CClosed circle at 9, shaded left
    4. DOpen circle at 9, shaded left

    x ≥ 9 means 9 is included (closed circle) and values are 9 or greater (shaded right).

  3. 12

    From 8.4–8.7 Catch-UpWhy is one an equation and the other an inequality?

    1. ABecause equations are for money and inequalities are for counting
    2. BBecause 4 is smaller than 320
    3. CThere is no real difference
    4. DThe flashlights cost exactly $60, but the budget is a limit she must stay under

    'Exactly' points to one value, so it is an equation. A budget — 'at most' — allows a whole range of values, so it is an inequality.
