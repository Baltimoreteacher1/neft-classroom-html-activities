    Using prime factors, 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 and 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3. What is their GCF?

    1. A12, from the shared 2 × 2 × 3
    2. B144, from multiplying all the primes
    3. C6, from one 2 and one 3
    4. D2, the only prime they share

    Take only the primes both numbers have: two 2s and one 3. Multiplying them gives 2 × 2 × 3 = 12, matching the answer from the factor lists.

    Find the error

    1. 1ProblemAyasha has painting every 8 weeks and pottery every 6 weeks. When do both fall in the same week?
    2. 2Step 1Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8
    3. 3Step 2Factors of 6: 1, 2, 3, 6
    4. 4Step 3The greatest common factor is 2, so both classes meet in week 2.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: Step 1 starts down the wrong road: the classes REPEAT, so the weeks they land on are multiples, not factors. List multiples of 8 (8, 16, 24 …) and of 6 (6, 12, 18, 24 …); the first shared week is 24. The answer is also unreasonable on its face: a class that meets every 8 weeks cannot possibly happen in week 2. Checking the answer against the story catches the wrong tool immediately.

  4. 4

    Sample: This is a least-common-multiple problem: the pack sizes repeat, and he needs the first count both can reach exactly. Multiples of 10: 10, 20, 30, 40 …; multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40 …. The first number in both lists is 40, so the LCM of 10 and 8 is 40. That means 4 packs of hot dogs (4 × 10 = 40) and 5 packs of buns (5 × 8 = 40), giving 40 of each with nothing left over. It is a multiple problem rather than a factor one because nothing is being split into groups — he is counting packs forward until the two totals match. He will have 40 of each for 24 guests, which is more than enough; buying fewer packs would leave hot dogs or buns unmatched.
