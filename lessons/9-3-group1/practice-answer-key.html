Where we left off

Our goal: With my small group, I can write an equation from a table or a graph to model the relationship between the dependent and independent variables — one step at a time, with support.

The big idea: When the total is the rate times the amount, write it as an equation: dependent variable = rate × independent variable.

Model to copy — Watch me write Miguel's equation

  1. The quantities are the number of months of membership and the total cost. The total cost depends on the number of months.
  2. In the table, each month adds $24.95: 1 month is $24.95, 2 months are $49.90, 3 months are $74.85.
  3. The pattern in words: the total cost is $24.95 times the number of months.
  4. Define the variables — let m = number of months and c = total cost in dollars — and write the equation: c = 24.95m.
  5. Now ANY number of months works: for 10 months, c = 24.95 × 10 = 249.50.

  1. 5

    Think it throughIn Diamond's situation, what should be the independent variable in both equations?

    1. Athe number of songs
    2. Bthe total cost
    3. Cthe price per song
    4. Dthe type of service (purchase or stream)

    Why is that the answer?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Total cost depends on how many songs Diamond buys or streams, so the number of songs is independent and total cost is dependent. Price per song is a fixed rate (a constant in the equation, not a variable), and the service type determines WHICH equation to use rather than being a variable inside one.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhy does Diamond need TWO separate equations instead of one equation for both options?

    1. APurchasing and streaming likely follow different cost rules (such as a per-song price vs. a flat rate), so each relationship between songs and cost is different
    2. BThe number of songs is different for each option
    3. COne equation can only have one variable
    4. DStreaming has no relationship between songs and cost

    How do you know?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Purchasing and streaming follow different cost rules, so the relationship between number of songs and total cost differs between them, requiring separate equations. The independent variable (number of songs) is the same idea in both equations, equations regularly have two variables, and streaming still relates cost to how much is used.

  3. 7

    Think it throughOnce Diamond has both equations, how does she use them to decide which option is better?

    1. ASubstitute the same number of songs into both equations and compare the two total costs
    2. BAdd the two equations together to find one combined cost
    3. CPick whichever equation has the smaller numbers written in it, without solving anything
    4. DGraph only one equation and assume the other follows the same line

    Explain your thinking.

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Substituting the same number of songs into both equations lets Diamond compare the actual total costs for that amount of listening. Adding the equations doesn't answer a comparison question, comparing raw coefficients ignores how they combine with the number of songs, and assuming one graph represents both ignores that purchasing and streaming are different relationships.

  4. 8

    Back to the modelRecreation World charges $24.95 per month, and the table shows the cost growing by exactly $24.95 every row. Why is the equation c = 24.95m correct, but c = 24.95 + m would be wrong?

    c = 24.95m is correct because each month ___ another $24.95.

    The fee is charged EVERY month, so the total must be the rate multiplied by the number of months, not added to it once.

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake on this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Writing the equation with addition — c = 24.95 + m — instead of multiplication. The fee is charged EVERY month, so the total is the rate times the number of months; always test the equation against a row of the table.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    Testing a fact against an equation means substituting the known value and checking whether the equation produces the matching total.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 9.2Quick check — you've got this: On the football ticket graph, what does the point (3, 135) represent?

    1. A3 tickets cost a total of $135
    2. B135 tickets cost a total of $3
    3. C3 tickets cost $135 each
    4. DThe game lasts 135 minutes

    The independent variable comes first in an ordered pair. Here that is the number of tickets, so 3 is the tickets. Then find the cost: 3 × $45 = $135. The point pairs 3 tickets with a total cost of $135.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 9.2A point on the graph is located at (4, x). Based on the interest rate, what value of x makes this point correct?

    1. A$105
    2. B$78.75
    3. C$855
    4. D$131.25

    After 4 years Nakai has earned 4 × $26.25 = $105 in interest. $78.75 is only 3 years' worth of interest, $855 confuses interest earned with the total account balance (principal plus interest), and $131.25 is 5 years' worth.
