Unit 7, Lesson 2: Represent Rational Numbers and Their Opposites on the Number Line6.NOS.6

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A rational number can be written as a ratio of two integers with a nonzero denominator. Integers and terminating or repeating decimals are rational. A rational number that is not an integer lies between two neighboring integers.

Key idea: Every fraction and decimal has its own exact spot on the number line, just like whole numbers. For example, -2.5 is halfway between -2 and -3. Negative numbers go to the left of 0, and positive numbers go to the right.
Idea clave: Cada fracción y cada decimal tiene su propio lugar exacto en la recta numérica, igual que los números enteros. Por ejemplo, -2.5 está a la mitad entre -2 y -3. Los números negativos van a la izquierda del 0 y los positivos a la derecha.

How we did it in class today

  1. I will plot -2.5 on the number line.
  2. First, the integers it falls between: -2.5 is between -2 and -3.
  3. The .5 means halfway, so I split the space between -2 and -3 in half.
  4. I place my dot exactly halfway between -2 and -3. That is -2.5.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Rational Numbers on the Number Line is treating the numerator and denominator of a negative fraction as two separate integers instead of one value between two integers — for example, plotting -3/4 by going to -3 and then counting 4 more units to the left, landing (incorrectly) at -7, instead of recognizing that -3/4 = -0.75, which sits between 0 and -1. Before you submit an answer, find the two integers your value falls between, then split that space into equal parts.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
