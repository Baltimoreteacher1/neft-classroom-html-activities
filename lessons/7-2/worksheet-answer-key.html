    💡 Solution Rationale: -1.5 is between -1 and -2 because it is 0.5 units to the left of -1 (or 0.5 units to the right of -2).

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Rational Numbers on the Number Line is treating the numerator and denominator of a negative fraction as two separate whole numbers instead of one value between two integers — for example, plotting -3/4 by going to -3 and then counting 4 more units to the left, landing (incorrectly) at -7, instead of recognizing that -3/4 = -0.75, which sits between 0 and -1.   6. 6 ERROR ANALYSIS

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting 1.5

    1. 1Problem:Plot 1.5 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinking:1.5 has a 1 and a 5, so I will plot it between 1 and 5.
    3. 3Student answer:Point plotted at about 3, halfway between 1 and 5.

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: 1.5 does NOT mean between 1 and 5. The .5 is one half, so 1.5 is halfway between 1 and 2. I find the two whole numbers it falls between (1 and 2), split that space in half, and place the dot at the middle. The point belongs at 1.5, not 3.. Misconception: The "Student thinking" step reads 1.5 as two separate whole numbers, 1 and 5. The digit after the decimal point names a part of one unit, not a second endpoint: .5 is one half. So 1.5 sits between the whole numbers 1 and 2, exactly halfway, not near 3.

  5. 5 ERROR ANALYSIS

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting -2.25

    1. 1Problem:Plot -2.25 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinking:It is negative, so I go past -2. The .25 is one quarter, so I count toward 0 (to the right) by a quarter unit and land between -2 and -1.
    3. 3Student answer:Point plotted at -1.75, between -2 and -1.

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: Going more negative means moving LEFT, not right. -2.25 is between -2 and -3 (it is a quarter of a unit to the LEFT of -2), not between -2 and -1. I split the space from -2 to -3 into 4 equal parts and place the dot at the first mark past -2, at -2.25.. Misconception: The "Student thinking" step moves right from −2, back toward zero, which makes the number less negative instead of more. The extra .25 has to be added to the negative direction, so −2.25 sits a quarter of a unit to the left of −2, between −2 and −3. Splitting that space into 4 equal parts and marking the first one past −2 puts the dot in the right place.

    Find the Plotting Error

    1. 1Problem:Plot -3/4 on the number line
    2. 2Student thinking:-3/4 has a 3 and a 4, so I go to -3 on the number line and then count 4 more to the left
    3. 3Student answer:Point plotted at -7

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: -3/4 means negative three-fourths, which is 3/4 of the way from 0 toward -1. It should be plotted between 0 and -1, not at -7. The student treated the numerator and denominator as separate whole numbers.. Misconception: The "Student thinking" step splits −3/4 into the whole numbers 3 and 4 instead of reading it as one value. The bar in a fraction means division, so −3/4 is three fourths of a single unit taken in the negative direction: it belongs between 0 and −1, three quarters of the way to −1. A fraction whose numerator is smaller than its denominator can never reach even one whole unit, let alone −7.
  2. 2 OPEN RESPONSE

    Name three different rational numbers between -1 and 0. Write each as both a fraction and a decimal. Explain how you know they are between -1 and 0.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Key Terms to Look For: fraction, decimal, negative, between, number line, greater than -1, less than 0 Explanation: Any negative number greater than −1 works, so pick friendly fractions and convert them: −1/2 = −0.5, −1/4 = −0.25, and −3/10 = −0.3. Each is negative, so it sits left of 0, but each has an absolute value less than 1, so it never reaches −1. On the number line all three land inside the single unit between −1 and 0.
