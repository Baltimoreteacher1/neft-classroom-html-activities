Learning target I can place rational numbers, including fractions and decimals, on a number line.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each fraction to its decimal equivalent.

    1. A1/2
    2. B-3/4
    3. C1 1/4
    4. D-2 1/2
    5. E3/10
    6. F-1/5
    • A0.5
    • B-0.75
    • C1.25
    • D-2.5
    • E0.3
    • F-0.2

    Hint Each fraction equals a decimal. Halves, quarters, fifths, and tenths all have decimal partners you know.

    Why: Dividing the numerator by the denominator converts each fraction, and the sign never changes: 1/2 = 0.5, −3/4 = −0.75, 3/10 = 0.3, and −1/5 = −0.2. For the mixed numbers, keep the whole part and convert only the fraction, so 1 1/4 = 1.25 and −2 1/2 = −2.5. Matching the signs first splits the cards into two smaller groups before you compute.

  2. 2Mark and label each value on the number line.

    Plot these rational numbers: -1, 0.5, -0.5, 1, -1.5

    Hint The tick marks count by 0.5 — each hop is a HALF, not a whole unit.

    Answer: ? at -1; ? at 0.5; ? at -0.5; ? at 1; ? at -1.5

    Why: The ticks step by 0.5, so each hop is half a unit. Place −1 and 1 on their whole-number ticks, then 0.5 one tick right of 0 and −0.5 one tick left of 0. For −1.5, go to −1 and take one more half-step left, landing between −1 and −2. Negative numbers move left of zero and positive numbers move right, so the two halves mirror each other across 0.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which point is located between -1 and -2 on the number line?

    1. A-2.5
    2. B-1.5
    3. C-0.5
    4. D1.5

    Hint 'Between -1 and -2' is on the NEGATIVE side, in the gap from -1 to -2.

    Answer: B — -1.5

    Why: -1.5 is between -1 and -2 because it is 0.5 units to the left of -1 (or 0.5 units to the right of -2).

    Watch for: A common mistake in Rational Numbers on the Number Line is treating the numerator and denominator of a negative fraction as two separate whole numbers instead of one value between two integers — for example, plotting -3/4 by going to -3 and then counting 4 more units to the left, landing (incorrectly) at -7, instead of recognizing that -3/4 = -0.75, which sits between 0 and -1. Before you submit an answer, find the two whole numbers your value falls between, then split that space into equal parts.

  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting 1.5

    1. 1ProblemPlot 1.5 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinking1.5 has a 1 and a 5, so I will plot it between 1 and 5.
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at about 3, halfway between 1 and 5.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint What does the .5 in 1.5 mean — is it a separate whole number or a part of one?

    Mistake: Step 2. The "Student thinking" step reads 1.5 as two separate whole numbers, 1 and 5. The digit after the decimal point names a part of one unit, not a second endpoint: .5 is one half. So 1.5 sits between the whole numbers 1 and 2, exactly halfway, not near 3.

    Correct work: 1.5 does NOT mean between 1 and 5. The .5 is one half, so 1.5 is halfway between 1 and 2. I find the two whole numbers it falls between (1 and 2), split that space in half, and place the dot at the middle. The point belongs at 1.5, not 3.

    Answer: A at -3.5; B at -0.25; C at 2.75; D at -2; E at 1.5

    Why: Convert every value to a decimal first: −1/4 = −0.25 and 1 1/2 = 1.5. Then A goes half a step left of −3 at −3.5, D lands right on the −2 tick, E lands on the 1.5 tick, C sits at 2.75 — three quarters of the way from 2 to 3, just past the 2.5 tick — and B sits at −0.25, only a quarter of a unit left of zero.

    FractionDecimalBetween Which Two Integers?
    3/4

    Why: Convert each value, then name the integers on either side of it. 3/4 = 0.75, between 0 and 1. −7/2 = −3.5, between −4 and −3, since more negative means farther left. 0.6 is six tenths, which is 3/5, between 0 and 1. −1.25 is −1 1/4, between −2 and −1.     • -2.5

    • -1/4
    • 3
    • -1.5

    Why: Convert the fractions so everything is in the same form: −1/4 = −0.25 and 1/2 = 0.5. Reading left to right on a number line gives −2.5, −1.5, −1/4, 1/2, 0.75, 3. Among the negatives, −2.5 is farthest from zero so it is the least — the opposite of how the numbers would order if they were positive.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 7Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting -2.25

    1. 1ProblemPlot -2.25 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinkingIt is negative, so I go past -2. The .25 is one quarter, so I count toward 0 (to the right) by a quarter unit and land between -2 and -1.
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at -1.75, between -2 and -1.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The "Student thinking" step moves right from −2, back toward zero, which makes the number less negative instead of more. The extra .25 has to be added to the negative direction, so −2.25 sits a quarter of a unit to the left of −2, between −2 and −3. Splitting that space into 4 equal parts and marking the first one past −2 puts the dot in the right place.

    Correct work: Going more negative means moving LEFT, not right. -2.25 is between -2 and -3 (it is a quarter of a unit to the LEFT of -2), not between -2 and -1. I split the space from -2 to -3 into 4 equal parts and place the dot at the first mark past -2, at -2.25.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Plotting Error

    1. 1ProblemPlot -3/4 on the number line
    2. 2Student thinking-3/4 has a 3 and a 4, so I go to -3 on the number line and then count 4 more to the left
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at -7

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The "Student thinking" step splits −3/4 into the whole numbers 3 and 4 instead of reading it as one value. The bar in a fraction means division, so −3/4 is three fourths of a single unit taken in the negative direction: it belongs between 0 and −1, three quarters of the way to −1. A fraction whose numerator is smaller than its denominator can never reach even one whole unit, let alone −7.

    Correct work: -3/4 means negative three-fourths, which is 3/4 of the way from 0 toward -1. It should be plotted between 0 and -1, not at -7. The student treated the numerator and denominator as separate whole numbers.

  2. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Name three different rational numbers between -1 and 0. Write each as both a fraction and a decimal. Explain how you know they are between -1 and 0.

    Look for: fraction, decimal, negative, between, number line, greater than -1, less than 0

    Why: Any negative number greater than −1 works, so pick friendly fractions and convert them: −1/2 = −0.5, −1/4 = −0.25, and −3/10 = −0.3. Each is negative, so it sits left of 0, but each has an absolute value less than 1, so it never reaches −1. On the number line all three land inside the single unit between −1 and 0.
