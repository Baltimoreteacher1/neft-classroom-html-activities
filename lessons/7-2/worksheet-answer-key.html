  6. 6

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting 1.5

    1. 1ProblemPlot 1.5 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinking1.5 has a 1 and a 5, so I will plot it between 1 and 5.
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at about 3, halfway between 1 and 5.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 1.5 does NOT mean between 1 and 5. The .5 is one half, so 1.5 is halfway between 1 and 2. I find the two whole numbers it falls between (1 and 2), split that space in half, and place the dot at the middle. The point belongs at 1.5, not 3. The "Student thinking" step reads 1.5 as two separate whole numbers, 1 and 5. The digit after the decimal point names a part of one unit, not a second endpoint: .5 is one half. So 1.5 sits between the whole numbers 1 and 2, exactly halfway, not near 3.

    Spot the Mistake: Plotting -2.25

    1. 1ProblemPlot -2.25 on the number line.
    2. 2Student thinkingIt is negative, so I go past -2. The .25 is one quarter, so I count toward 0 (to the right) by a quarter unit and land between -2 and -1.
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at -1.75, between -2 and -1.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: Going more negative means moving LEFT, not right. -2.25 is between -2 and -3 (it is a quarter of a unit to the LEFT of -2), not between -2 and -1. I split the space from -2 to -3 into 4 equal parts and place the dot at the first mark past -2, at -2.25. The "Student thinking" step moves right from −2, back toward zero, which makes the number less negative instead of more. The extra .25 has to be added to the negative direction, so −2.25 sits a quarter of a unit to the left of −2, between −2 and −3. Splitting that space into 4 equal parts and marking the first one past −2 puts the dot in the right place.

    Find the Plotting Error

    1. 1ProblemPlot -3/4 on the number line
    2. 2Student thinking-3/4 has a 3 and a 4, so I go to -3 on the number line and then count 4 more to the left
    3. 3Student answerPoint plotted at -7

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: -3/4 means negative three-fourths, which is 3/4 of the way from 0 toward -1. It should be plotted between 0 and -1, not at -7. The student treated the numerator and denominator as separate whole numbers. The "Student thinking" step splits −3/4 into the whole numbers 3 and 4 instead of reading it as one value. The bar in a fraction means division, so −3/4 is three fourths of a single unit taken in the negative direction: it belongs between 0 and −1, three quarters of the way to −1. A fraction whose numerator is smaller than its denominator can never reach even one whole unit, let alone −7.
