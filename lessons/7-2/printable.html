How do you place rational numbers on a number line?

A rational number can be written as a ratio of two integers with a nonzero denominator. Integers and terminating or repeating decimals are rational. A rational number that is not an integer lies between two neighboring integers.

Key idea: Rational Numbers on the Number Line. 1. Find the two consecutive integers the rational number falls between. 2. Divide the segment between integers into equal parts matching the denominator. 3. Negative numbers move to the left of zero; positive numbers move to the right

Watch me

  • I will plot -2.5 on the number line.
  • First, the integers it falls between: -2.5 is between -2 and -3.
  • The .5 means halfway, so I split the space between -2 and -3 in half.
  • I place my dot exactly halfway between -2 and -3. That is -2.5.

Let's try together

  • Now let's plot 0.75. Which two integers is it between? It is between 0 and 1.
  • The decimal 0.75 equals the fraction 3/4. The denominator 4 splits one unit into four equal parts; the numerator 3 counts three parts.
  • So we split 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts and place the dot at the 3rd mark.
