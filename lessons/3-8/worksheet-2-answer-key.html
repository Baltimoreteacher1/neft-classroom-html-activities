  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    About how many pounds is 10 kilograms? (1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb)

    1. Aabout 2.2 pounds
    2. Babout 4.5 pounds
    3. Cabout 12.2 pounds
    4. Dabout 22 pounds

    Answer: D — about 22 pounds

    Why: Each kilogram is about 2.2 pounds, so 10 × 2.2 = 22 pounds.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Solve Problems with Unit Rates is comparing the total prices instead of the cost per item — the deal with the smaller total can still cost more per unit. Always reduce each option to its unit rate before you decide, then pick the lower one.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    For each prize item, find the unit rate of both deals and sort each card into Better Buy or Worse Buy.

    Groups: Better Buy (lower unit rate) Worse Buy (higher unit rate)
    • Better Buy (lower unit rate)Snack packs: 4 for $5.00 ($1.25 each)
    • Worse Buy (higher unit rate)Snack packs: 6 for $9.00 ($1.50 each)
    • Better Buy (lower unit rate)Stickers: 25 for $8.75 ($0.35 each)
    • Worse Buy (higher unit rate)Stickers: 10 for $4.00 ($0.40 each)
    • Better Buy (lower unit rate)Tokens: 250 for $20.00 ($0.08 each)
    • Worse Buy (higher unit rate)Tokens: 100 for $9.00 ($0.09 each)

    Why: Within each prize, the better buy is the deal with the lower cost per item: $1.25 beats $1.50 for snack packs, $0.35 beats $0.40 for stickers, and $0.08 beats $0.09 for tokens. The winning deal is not always the bigger bundle — the 4-pack of snacks beats the 6-pack.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each prize deal to its unit price (price ÷ quantity).

    1. AGlow bracelets: 5 for $4.50
    2. BBouncy balls: 8 for $6.00
    3. CMini erasers: 3 for $2.40
    4. DTemporary tattoos: 10 for $3.50
    • A$0.90 each
    • B$0.75 each
    • C$0.80 each
    • D$0.35 each

    Why: Each match is price ÷ quantity: $4.50 ÷ 5 = $0.90, $6.00 ÷ 8 = $0.75, $2.40 ÷ 3 = $0.80, and $3.50 ÷ 10 = $0.35. The deal with the largest total, the $6.00 bouncy balls, is not the priciest per item, which is exactly why unit prices are what you compare.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The arcade needs 600 prize tickets printed. Printer A prints 150 tickets in 5 minutes. Printer B prints 200 tickets in 8 minutes. The manager wants to use the faster printer. Which printer is faster, and how long for 600 tickets?

    1. APrinter A, 20 minutes
    2. BPrinter B, 20 minutes
    3. CPrinter B, 24 minutes
    4. DPrinter A, 24 minutes

    Answer: A — Printer A, 20 minutes

    Why: Printer A is faster at 30 per minute, and 600 ÷ 30 = 20 minutes. Printer B would take 600 ÷ 25 = 24 minutes.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Solve Problems with Unit Rates is comparing the total prices instead of the cost per item — the deal with the smaller total can still cost more per unit. Always reduce each option to its unit rate before you decide, then pick the lower one.

  2. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The snack bar compares two popcorn deals: 12 oz for $3.00 or 20 oz for $4.00. Which is the better buy?

    1. A12 oz for $3.00 — $0.25 per oz
    2. BThey cost the same per ounce
    3. CNot enough information
    4. D20 oz for $4.00 — $0.20 per oz

    Answer: D — 20 oz for $4.00 — $0.20 per oz

    Why: 12 oz: $3.00 ÷ 12 = $0.25 per oz. 20 oz: $4.00 ÷ 20 = $0.20 per oz. $0.20 < $0.25, so 20 oz for $4.00 is the better buy.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Solve Problems with Unit Rates is comparing the total prices instead of the cost per item — the deal with the smaller total can still cost more per unit. Always reduce each option to its unit rate before you decide, then pick the lower one.
