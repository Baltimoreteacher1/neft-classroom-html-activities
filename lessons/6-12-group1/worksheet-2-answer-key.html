Lesson 6.126.12 Small Group · Group 1

Group 1 · Set B · Answer KeySecond form · new problems, same idea

Learning target With my small group, I can find the least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers by listing or comparing their multiples — one step at a time, with support.

Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Two buses leave the station at 8:00 AM. Bus A returns every 15 minutes and Bus B returns every 20 minutes. When is the next time both buses are at the station together?

    1. A8:30 AM (30 minutes later)
    2. B8:40 AM (40 minutes later)
    3. C9:00 AM (60 minutes later)
    4. D9:20 AM (80 minutes later)

    Hint Both buses start together at 8:00 — they meet again after a number of minutes that both 15 and 20 divide evenly.

    Answer: C — 9:00 AM (60 minutes later)

    Why: LCM(15, 20) = 60 minutes. Both buses return together 60 minutes after 8:00 AM, at 9:00 AM.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Least Common Multiple is multiplying the two numbers together instead of listing multiples to find the smallest shared one. For example, for 4 and 6, a student calculates 4 × 6 = 24 and calls that the LCM, but the actual least common multiple is 12 (multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12; multiples of 6: 6, 12 — 12 is the first number that appears in both lists). This shortcut only works when the two numbers share no common factors (like 4 and 9, where the LCM really is 36 = 4 × 9); for numbers like 4 and 6 that share a factor of 2, multiplying overshoots the true LCM. Always list multiples of both numbers and pick the first one that appears in both lists.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    One machine runs every 4 min, another every 6 min. They just ran together. In how many minutes do they run together again?

    1. A6
    2. B10
    3. C12
    4. D24

    Hint They line up again at a time that is a multiple of 4 and also a multiple of 6 — that is the least common multiple.

    Answer: C — 12

    Why: Skip count: multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12; multiples of 6 are 6, 12. The first shared multiple is LCM(4, 6) = 12 minutes.

    One ship docks every 8 min, another every 12 min. They just docked together. In how many minutes do they dock together again?

    4. D48

    Hint Both ships dock together at a time that is a multiple of 8 and also a multiple of 12.

    Answer: C — 24

    Why: LCM(8, 12) = 24. Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24; multiples of 12: 12, 24. The first shared multiple is 24 minutes.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot Riley's Mistake — LCM of 4 and 6

    1. 1ProblemFind the LCM of 4 and 6.
    2. 2Multiples of 44, 8, 12, 16
    3. 3Multiples of 66, 12, 18
    4. 4Choose the answerRiley added the two numbers: 4 + 6 = 10, so LCM = 10.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Riley added 4 + 6 to get 10. Check: is 10 in both lists of multiples?

    Mistake: Step 4. In the last step Riley added 4 + 6 = 10, but adding does not produce a common multiple — 10 is not even in either list. The LCM is the first number that appears in BOTH lists, and 12 shows up in 4, 8, 12 and in 6, 12, so the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

    Correct work: You do NOT add the numbers. The LCM is the first number that appears in BOTH multiple lists. 12 is in the multiples of 4 (4, 8, 12) and in the multiples of 6 (6, 12), so the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

  2. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot Maya's Mistake — LCM of 6 and 9

    1. 1ProblemFind the LCM of 6 and 9.
    2. 2Multiples of 66, 12, 18, 24
    3. 3Multiples of 99, 18, 27
    4. 4Choose the answerMaya saw that 9 is bigger than 6, so she wrote LCM = 9.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Maya picked 9 because it is the larger number. Is 9 a multiple of 6? Skip count: 6, 12, 18 — there is no 9.

    Mistake: Step 4. In the last step Maya chose 9 just because it is the larger number, but 9 never appears in the multiples of 6 (6, 12, 18, 24). The LCM has to be in both lists, and the first number in both is 18, so the LCM of 6 and 9 is 18.

    Correct work: The LCM is not just the larger number. Check: 9 is in the multiples of 9, but it is NOT in the multiples of 6 (6, 12, 18...). The first number in BOTH lists is 18, so the LCM of 6 and 9 is 18.

    Level 2 Extension — Crew Member's Schedule Sync Mistake

    1. 1ProblemThe water recycler cycles every 8 minutes and the heat exchanger cycles every 12 minutes. They just ran together. When do they next sync?
    2. 2List multiples of 88, 16, 24, 32
    3. 3List multiples of 1212, 24, 36
    4. 4Pick the number to useThe greatest factor that 8 and 12 share is 4.
    5. 5AnswerThey next sync in 4 minutes.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint A scavenger-hunt schedule asks WHEN two repeating events meet again — that is an LCM problem, not a GCF problem.

    Mistake: Step 4. Step 4 switched to the greatest common FACTOR (4) instead of the least common MULTIPLE. A question about when two repeating cycles line up again calls for the LCM, and the first number in both lists — 8, 16, 24, 32 and 12, 24, 36 — is 24, so the two systems next sync in 24 minutes.

    Correct work: Step 4 should find the LEAST COMMON MULTIPLE, not the greatest common FACTOR. 'When do repeating events line up?' calls for the LCM. The first multiple in both lists is 24, so they next sync in 24 minutes.
