Group 1 · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target With my small group, I can find the least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers by listing or comparing their multiples — one step at a time, with support.

Start here pageNow you try — model solution

  1. What are the multiples of 5? (5, 10, 15.) What is the first number in both lists? (15.)
  2. So the LCM of 3 and 5 is 15.
  3. Sentence frame — say it with me: "My first step is ___ , because the problem asks for ___ ."
Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Mark and label each value on the number line.

    Find the LCM of 5 and 6 on the number line

    Hint Read the labels: some marks are multiples of 5 and some are multiples of 6. The LCM is the first spot that belongs to BOTH.

    Answer: mult of 5 at 5; mult of 6 at 6; mult of 5 at 10; mult of 6 at 12; mult of 5 at 15; mult of 6 at 18; mult of 5 at 20; mult of 6 at 24; mult of 5 at 25; BOTH — LCM! at 30

    Why: The multiples of 5 land on 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and the multiples of 6 land on 6, 12, 18, 24, 30. The first tick mark that belongs to both lists is 30, so the LCM of 5 and 6 is 30. Because 5 and 6 share no factor larger than 1, the two lists do not collide until 5 × 6.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each pair of numbers to their LCM.

    1. ALCM(2, 5)
    2. BLCM(4, 10)
    3. CLCM(3, 8)
    4. DLCM(6, 9)
    5. ELCM(5, 7)
    6. FLCM(4, 6)
    • A10
    • B20
    • C24
    • D18
    • E35
    • F12

    Hint Each match must be a multiple of BOTH numbers in its pair. Check divisibility both ways before you connect.

    Why: Each answer is the smallest number that both numbers in the pair divide evenly. LCM(5, 7) = 35 and LCM(3, 8) = 24 equal the products because those pairs share no factor bigger than 1, while LCM(4, 6) = 12 and LCM(6, 9) = 18 are smaller than the products because those pairs share a factor.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the LCM of 3 and 5?

    1. A3
    2. B5
    3. C15
    4. D30

    Hint LCM is about MULTIPLES (counting UP by each number), not factors. You want the smallest number BOTH lists reach.

    Answer: C — 15

    Why: Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, ... Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, ... The smallest common multiple is 15.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Least Common Multiple is multiplying the two numbers together instead of listing multiples to find the smallest shared one. For example, for 4 and 6, a student calculates 4 × 6 = 24 and calls that the LCM, but the actual least common multiple is 12 (multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12; multiples of 6: 6, 12 — 12 is the first number that appears in both lists). This shortcut only works when the two numbers share no common factors (like 4 and 9, where the LCM really is 36 = 4 × 9); for numbers like 4 and 6 that share a factor of 2, multiplying overshoots the true LCM. Always list multiples of both numbers and pick the first one that appears in both lists.

  4. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the LCM of 2 and 7?

    1. A2
    2. B7
    3. C9
    4. D14

    Hint Notice that 2 and 7 are both prime — they share no common factor. That makes this a special, faster case.

    Answer: D — 14

    Why: Since 2 and 7 share no common factors (they are relatively prime), LCM = 2 × 7 = 14.
