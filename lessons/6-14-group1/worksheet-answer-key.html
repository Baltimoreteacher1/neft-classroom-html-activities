  5. 5

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Expression4(n + 3)
    2. 2Distribute4 × n + 3
    3. 3Simplify4n + 3

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 4(n + 3) = 4 × n + 4 × 3 = 4n + 12. The student multiplied the 4 by n but forgot to multiply the 4 by the 3. The factor outside must reach BOTH terms inside the parentheses. The 4 reached the n but never reached the 3, so the 3 was copied down untouched. The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac, which means 4(n + 3) = 4 × n + 4 × 3 = 4n + 12, not 4n + 3.

  3. 3

    Level 2 Extension — The Spotlight Only Hit One Term

    1. 1Light cue3(x + 4)
    2. 2Distribute3 × x + 4
    3. 3Simplify3x + 4

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 3(x + 4) = 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12. The factor 3 is like a spotlight that must shine on EVERY term inside the parentheses, not just the first one. Picture the area model: a 3-by-x panel (3x) plus a 3-by-4 panel (12). The 3 shone on the x but skipped the 4. Distributing to every term gives 3(x + 4) = 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12. An area model shows why: a 3-by-x panel is 3x and a 3-by-4 panel is 12, and the whole rectangle is both panels together.

  4. 4

    Find the Distributive Error

    1. 1Expression5(2x + 4)
    2. 2Distribute5 × 2x + 4
    3. 3Simplify10x + 4

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 5(2x + 4) = 5 × 2x + 5 × 4 = 10x + 20. You must distribute the 5 to BOTH terms inside the parentheses, not just the first one. The 5 multiplied only the first term inside. Distributing to both gives 5(2x + 4) = 5 × 2x + 5 × 4 = 10x + 20. Testing with x = 1 confirms it: the original 5(2 + 4) = 30 and 10(1) + 20 = 30, while the student's 10x + 4 would give only 14.
