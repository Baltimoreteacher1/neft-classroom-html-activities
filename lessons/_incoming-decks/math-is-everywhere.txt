[slide 5] Chefs at Work
[slide 5] Chefs are found in restaurant kitchens overseeing food preparation and service. They supervise a team of cooks preparing everything from appetizers and soups to desserts.
[slide 5] How do chefs use math in their work?
[slide 6] Chefs plan menus and daily specials, ensure food quality, and organize the duties of the kitchen staff.
[slide 6] What are some other tasks that chefs might do in their restaurants?
[slide 6] Reveal:
[slide 7] Reveal: 
create recipes
set prices
oversee kitchen staff
prepare food
order ingredients
manage inventory
[slide 8] Here are some tasks that chefs do:
[slide 8] They plan menus for the restaurant and often create recipes for their dishes.​
They set the price for each dish the restaurant offers.​
They order the ingredients for their dishes.​
They determine the number of staff needed to serve restaurant customers.​
They make sure meals are cooked properly and look appetizing.​

[slide 12] When chefs plan menus, they use math to:
determine the number of items on the menu.

determine the number of side dishes for each entrée on the menu.

[slide 18] Everyone Can Grow Vegetables
[slide 18] Growing vegetables is an important business and a very popular hobby. Local farmers grow vegetables to sell to local restaurants. Community gardens can provide fresh vegetables for residents. Even apartment residents in cities can grow vegetables in planter gardens on their rooftops or balconies.
[slide 18] How can math help you plan a vegetable garden?
[slide 19] When planting a vegetable garden, gardeners consider how much space they have, what types of vegetables to plant, and what type of soil to use.
What other things might gardeners need to consider when planting a vegetable garden?
[slide 20] Reveal: 
the amount of sun available at the location and the amount of sun needed for the plants
how to protect the plants from animals and insects
the planter in which the plants will be planted
the amount of soil needed for the planters
what tools they will need
when to plant different vegetables
[slide 21] To plant a vegetable garden, gardeners consider:
[slide 21] the amount of sun available at the location and the amount of sun needed for the plants.​
the planter in which the plants will be planted.​
the amount of soil needed for the planters.​
the amount of space needed between plants to give them room to grow.​
the amount of water needed.​
[slide 22] Many hobbies include applications of math.
[slide 22] How might gardeners use math?
[slide 23] Reveal: 
to measure seed depth
to calculate the volume of the planter
to calculate the area of the planter
to measure the distance between seeds
[slide 25] Gardeners use math to:
calculate the volume of the planter to determine the amount of soil needed.
calculate the area of the planter to determine how many plants to plant.

[slide 28] Summarize: Math is Everywhere
[slide 28] Some people think that math is just a school subject with little relevance to our lives outside of the classroom. But math is everywhere!
People use math every day in their professions and their hobbies. It is also a good tool for solving problems around your house.
[slide 29] Apply: Brushing Your Teeth
[slide 29] For good hygiene, every day you should brush your teeth twice a day.

Did you know, that leaving the water running while brushing your teeth wastes an average of four gallons versus only turning the water on to rinse?
[slide 29] Question: How much water would you save each year if every person in your house ran water only to rinse? How does your savings compare to a household of 3, 4, 5, and 8 people?
