[slide 5] Tower of Hanoi
[slide 5] The Tower of Hanoi is a puzzle that was invented in 1883. It consists of three rods and a stack of discs arranged with the smallest on top to largest on the bottom of one rod. The number of discs can vary, but the number of rods does not.
[slide 6] The objective of the puzzle is to move the discs, one at a time, from one rod to another. The rules are: 
You can move only one disc at a time.
You can move only the top disc from a stack.
You can only place a disc on top of a larger disc. A larger disc can never be on top of a smaller disc.
[slide 6] What is the fewest number of moves to solve a three-disc Tower of Hanoi puzzle?

[slide 31] Summarize: Math is Playful
[slide 31] We play with math using our imagination to come up with possible solutions to problems or to solve puzzles.
Playing with math allows us to think differently by:
looking for patterns or relationships that help us think logically to solve a puzzle.
imaging different ways to visualize the problem.
[slide 32] Apply: The 15 Puzzle
[slide 32] The 15 Puzzle is a sliding puzzle with 15 tiles in a frame that is 4 tiles high by 4 tiles wide. The object of the puzzle is to arrange the tiles in order from 1 to 15. This is done by sliding one tile at a time to the open space. Sometimes the 15 Puzzle is solvable and sometimes it is unsolvable.
[slide 32] Question: Try solving this 15 Puzzle. Then make an argument for whether it is a solvable or unsolvable puzzle.
