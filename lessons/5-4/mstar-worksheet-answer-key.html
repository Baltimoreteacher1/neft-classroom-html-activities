Lesson 5-4: Apply Area Concepts to Solve Problems

Grade 6 Mathematics · Standard 6.GR.1

Part A — Skills Check

One point each.

Question 1Selected response — correct: C

An L-shaped room is made of two rectangles: 10 ft × 6 ft and 4 ft × 3 ft. What is the total area?

  • A12 sq ft
  • B60 sq ft
  • C72 sq ft
  • D72 ft

Area 1 = 10 × 6 = 60 sq ft. Area 2 = 4 × 3 = 12 sq ft. Total = 60 + 12 = 72 sq ft.

Question 2Selected response — correct: B

A rectangular patio is 15 ft × 10 ft with a 5 ft × 4 ft rectangular flower bed cut out. What is the remaining area?

  • A20 sq ft
  • B130 sq ft
  • C150 sq ft
  • D170 sq ft

Patio = 15 × 10 = 150 sq ft. Cutout = 5 × 4 = 20 sq ft. Remaining = 150 − 20 = 130 sq ft.

Question 3Selected response — correct: D

Find the area of an L-shape made of a 5×3 rectangle and a 2×4 rectangle.

  • A8 sq units
  • B15 sq units
  • C20 sq units
  • D23 sq units

5 × 3 = 15. 2 × 4 = 8. Total = 15 + 8 = 23 sq units.

Question 4Selected response — correct: D

When should you subtract areas instead of adding them to find the area of a composite figure?

  • AWhen the shapes overlap
  • BWhen one shape is larger than the other
  • CAlways subtract — never add
  • DWhen a piece is cut out or removed from a larger shape

Subtract when a piece is removed from a larger shape (like a window cut from a wall). Add when two shapes are joined together (like an L-shape).

Question 5Selected response — correct: C

A 7 ft by 5 ft rectangle has a triangle (base 7 ft, height 4 ft) on top. Add the two areas. What is the total area?

  • A14 sq ft
  • B35 sq ft
  • C49 sq ft
  • D63 sq ft

Rectangle = 7 x 5 = 35 sq ft. Triangle = 1/2 x 7 x 4 = 14 sq ft. The pieces are joined, so add: 35 + 14 = 49 sq ft.

Question 6Selected response — correct: D

Plan 2 — L-shaped lobby: decompose the L into two rectangles, one 9 ft by 6 ft and one 7 ft by 3 ft, then ADD. What is the total floor area?

  • A21 sq ft
  • B54 sq ft
  • C63 sq ft
  • D75 sq ft

First rectangle = 9 x 6 = 54 sq ft. Second rectangle = 7 x 3 = 21 sq ft. Joined L-shape, so add: 54 + 21 = 75 sq ft.

Part B — Test-Format Questions

Scoring notes and distractor rationales below each item.

Question 7Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: D

An L-shaped room splits into two rectangles: one is 12 feet by 8 feet, the other is 6 feet by 5 feet. What is the total floor area?

  • A30 square feet
  • B62 square feet
  • C96 square feet
  • D126 square feet

12 × 8 = 96 and 6 × 5 = 30. The total is 96 + 30 = 126 square feet.

  • A: That is only the second rectangle. Add the first one, 12 times 8.
  • B: That totals the side lengths rather than the areas. Multiply each rectangle's sides, then add the two products.
  • C: That is only the first rectangle. Add the second one, 6 times 5.

Part B — correct answer: A

Why is decomposing a composite figure into rectangles a valid strategy?

  • AThe areas of the non-overlapping pieces add to the area of the whole figure.
  • BRectangles are the only shapes with area.
  • CAny shape can be turned into one large rectangle.
  • DThe perimeter stays the same after decomposing.

Area is additive: if the pieces do not overlap and cover the figure completely, their areas sum to the total.

Question 8Written response · 2 points

Type II Reasoning: Counting a Region Twice

Nia split a composite figure into a 10 by 4 rectangle and a triangle with base 10 and height 4. She added 40 + 20 = 60 square units — but the triangle sat ON TOP of part of the rectangle she had already counted.

Explain the error in Nia's method and state what she must check before adding areas.

Model answer: Decomposition only works when the pieces do not overlap. Nia counted the shared region twice, so 60 square units is too large. She must verify the pieces cover the figure exactly once before adding.

2 pointsStudent explains that decomposed pieces must not overlap, identifies that the overlapping region was counted twice, and states the total is therefore too large.
1 pointStudent says the answer is wrong but does not explain the overlap.
0 pointsStudent agrees with Nia or gives an unrelated response.

Part C — Show Your Thinking

Model answer and scoring guidance.

Question 9Written response · 2 points

A T-shaped room can be decomposed in two different ways. Show both ways and prove they give the same total area. Use a T-shape where the top is 14 ft × 4 ft and the stem is 6 ft × 10 ft.

Model answer: Way 1 splits the T into its two named rectangles: 14 × 4 = 56 plus 6 × 10 = 60, a total of 116 sq ft. Way 2 wraps the T in a 14 ft × 14 ft rectangle worth 196 sq ft and subtracts the two empty 4 ft × 10 ft corners, 196 − 80 = 116 sq ft — the same region is being measured, so the decomposition you choose cannot change the area.

Award 2 points for a complete explanation with correct mathematics, 1 point for a correct answer with thin reasoning, 0 for an unrelated response.
