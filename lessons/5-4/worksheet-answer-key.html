    Sort each composite figure by whether you ADD or SUBTRACT the areas to find the total.

    Groups: Add the areas Subtract the areas

    • Subtract the areasWall with a window cut out
    • Add the areasT-shaped stage (two rectangles joined)
    • Subtract the areasFloor with a trapdoor removed
    • Subtract the areasU-shaped pool (rectangle with center removed)

    Hint Look for action words on each card: 'joined' versus 'cut out' or 'removed'.

    Why: When pieces are joined, the figure is bigger than either piece, so you add: the L-shaped hallway, the T-shaped stage, and the plus-sign shape all go under Add the areas. When something is cut out or removed, the figure is smaller than the original rectangle, so the window in the wall, the trapdoor in the floor, and the removed center of the U-shaped pool all go under Subtract the areas.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    An L-shaped room is made of two rectangles: 10 ft × 6 ft and 4 ft × 3 ft. What is the total area?

    1. A12 sq ft
    2. B60 sq ft
    3. C72 sq ft
    4. D72 ft

    Hint The L-shape is already split into two rectangles for you: 10 × 6 and 4 × 3.

    Answer: C — 72 sq ft

    Why: Area 1 = 10 × 6 = 60 sq ft. Area 2 = 4 × 3 = 12 sq ft. Total = 60 + 12 = 72 sq ft.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Area of Composite Figures is adding every piece even when a piece has been cut out of the figure. For a 10 ft by 8 ft rectangle with a 3 ft by 2 ft rectangular notch cut from one corner, students compute the full rectangle 10 × 8 = 80 sq ft and stop, or they add the notch instead of removing it (80 + 6 = 86 sq ft) instead of subtracting it: 80 − (3 × 2) = 80 − 6 = 74 sq ft. Before submitting, check whether each piece is part of the figure (add it) or missing from the figure (subtract it).

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    When should you subtract areas instead of adding them to find the area of a composite figure?

    1. AWhen the shapes overlap
    2. BWhen one shape is larger than the other
    3. CAlways subtract — never add
    4. DWhen a piece is cut out or removed from a larger shape

    Hint Think of real examples: a window in a wall versus two rooms joined into an L-shape.

    Answer: D — When a piece is cut out or removed from a larger shape

    Why: Subtract when a piece is removed from a larger shape (like a window cut from a wall). Add when two shapes are joined together (like an L-shape).

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Identify the shapesAn L-shaped room is made of two joined rectangles: 8 ft × 5 ft and 4 ft × 3 ft.
    2. 2Find each areaFirst rectangle = 8 × 5 = 40 sq ft. Second rectangle = 4 × 3 = 12 sq ft.
    3. 3Combine the areasTotal = 40 × 12 = 480 sq ft.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Look at the last step. When two pieces are joined, do you add or multiply their areas?

    Mistake: Step 3. Both rectangle areas are correct — 8 × 5 = 40 and 4 × 3 = 12 — but the last step multiplies them. Multiplying is how you get the area of one rectangle from its length and width; combining two joined pieces means adding, so the total is 40 + 12 = 52 sq ft.

    Correct work: The two rectangles are joined, so add their areas — do not multiply them. Total = 40 + 12 = 52 sq ft.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Identify the shapesA 16 ft × 9 ft floor has a 4 ft × 5 ft supply closet cut out of one corner. Find the usable floor area.
    2. 2Find the large rectangle areaA = 16 × 9 = 144 sq ft.
    3. 3Find the cutout areaA = 4 × 5 = 20 sq ft.
    4. 4Find the usable areaUsable area = 144 + 20 = 164 sq ft.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. Both areas are found correctly, 144 sq ft and 20 sq ft, but the final step adds them. The closet was cut out of the floor, so its 20 sq ft is not usable space — subtract it: 144 − 20 = 124 sq ft. An answer of 164 sq ft should also look wrong, since usable floor can never exceed the whole 144 sq ft floor.

    Correct work: The closet is cut out, so subtract its area instead of adding it. Usable area = 144 − 20 = 124 sq ft.

    PartShapeDimensionsArea

    Total——

    Why: The two parts use different formulas: the rectangular walls are 10 × 8 = 80 sq ft, and the triangular roof is ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 sq ft. Because the roof sits on top of the walls with no overlap, the total is 80 + 20 = 100 sq ft.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Area Error

    1. 1Identify the shapesRectangle: 16 ft × 10 ft, with a 4 ft × 6 ft piece cut from the corner
    2. 2Find the large rectangle areaA = 16 × 10 = 160 sq ft
    3. 3Find the cutout areaA = 4 × 6 = 24 sq ft
    4. 4Find total areaTotal = 160 + 24 = 184 sq ft

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The problem says a 4 ft × 6 ft piece was cut from the corner, so its 24 sq ft is missing from the figure, not added to it. The total should be 160 − 24 = 136 sq ft, which is smaller than the original rectangle — the 184 sq ft answer is larger, which is a signal the operation was backwards.

    Correct work: Since the piece is cut out, subtract: 160 − 24 = 136 sq ft. When a piece is removed, subtract its area from the larger shape.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A T-shaped room can be decomposed in two different ways. Show both ways and prove they give the same total area. Use a T-shape where the top is 14 ft × 4 ft and the stem is 6 ft × 10 ft.

    Look for: decompose, add, subtract, rectangle, composite, equivalent, same area

    Why: Way 1 splits the T into its two named rectangles: 14 × 4 = 56 plus 6 × 10 = 60, a total of 116 sq ft. Way 2 wraps the T in a 14 ft × 14 ft rectangle worth 196 sq ft and subtracts the two empty 4 ft × 10 ft corners, 196 − 80 = 116 sq ft — the same region is being measured, so the decomposition you choose cannot change the area.
