Unit 1, Lesson 6: Math is Ours

Standard MPP.3

What students are learning Qué aprenden

Key idea: Explain your thinking using clear math words. Listen to how your partner solved the problem and compare it with your way. Use the class agreements to work toward an answer the whole group agrees on.
Idea clave: Se explica el razonamiento con palabras matemáticas claras. Se escucha cómo resolvió el problema el compañero y se compara con la forma propia. Se usan los acuerdos de la clase para llegar a una respuesta en la que todo el grupo esté de acuerdo.

Try at home Para hacer en casa

Choose a small problem to solve together: A tray holds 9 muffins, and a bakery has 5 trays. Say each step out loud: what do we know, what do we want to find, draw a picture, make a plan, solve, and check. Then name one thing you could try if you got stuck.

Elijan un problema pequeño para resolver juntos: una bandeja tiene 9 panecillos y una panadería tiene 5 bandejas. Digan cada paso en voz alta: qué sabemos, qué queremos hallar, dibujar una imagen, hacer un plan, resolver y comprobar. Luego nombren algo que podrían intentar si se atascan.

Talk about it:

What did you do first to make sense of the problem, and what could you try if you got stuck?

¿Qué hiciste primero para entender el problema y qué podrías intentar si te atascas?

How we did it in class today Cómo lo hicimos hoy en clase

  1. First I make sense of it: one rack holds 8 bikes, each bike has 2 wheels, so this rack has 8 × 2 = 16 wheels. The other rack has 6 times as many wheels — that is what I don't know yet.
  2. I choose a representation: 6 equal groups of 16. Then I make a plan: multiply.
  3. 16 × 6 = 96, so the other rack has about 96 wheels. I check my progress: 96 is 6 groups of 16, and 16 × 6 = 96, so my answer matches my plan.

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

At family game night, everyone is stuck on a tricky puzzle. Which way of working best shows the idea that math belongs to all of us?

  1. Let the quickest person take over, since the puzzle belongs to whoever solves it first
  2. Everyone shares ideas, some think quietly on their own for a bit, and everyone treats each other's ideas — and their own — with respect
  3. One person works the puzzle while everyone else just watches
  4. Anyone who gets stuck should stop, because being stuck means puzzles aren't for them

If they get stuck... Si se traban...

Watch out for this: Thinking that getting stuck means you are bad at math. Everyone gets stuck. What matters is having a next move, like drawing it or trying a smaller number.
Atención: Pensar que atascarse significa que eres malo en matemáticas. Todos nos atascamos. Lo importante es tener un siguiente paso, como dibujarlo o probar con un número más pequeño.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
Pregunte: «¿Puedes mostrarme cómo lo hiciste? ¿Nos saltamos algún paso?» Hablar de las matemáticas en voz alta ayuda mucho.
