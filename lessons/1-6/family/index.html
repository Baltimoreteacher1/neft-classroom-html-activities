Unit 1, Lesson 6: Math is OursMPP.3

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

Problem solving is not one lightning bolt — it is a process you can name, practice, and share. And when the process stalls, getting stuck is part of doing math, not a sign you can't.

Key idea: We make sense of problems, look for patterns, choose representations and tools, make a plan, watch our progress, and shift strategies when needed — together and on our own.

Key words

  • make sense of a problem — To figure out what a problem is asking — what you know, and what you don't know yet.
    Español: comprender un problema — Descubrir qué pide un problema: qué sabes y qué no sabes todavía.
  • representation — A way of showing a problem — a drawing, table, equation, or model — that helps you see it.
    Español: representación — Una forma de mostrar un problema — un dibujo, una tabla, una ecuación o un modelo — que te ayuda a verlo.
  • strategy — A plan of attack for a problem — and something you can switch when you get stuck.
    Español: estrategia — Un plan para atacar un problema — y algo que puedes cambiar cuando te atascas.
  • critique — To examine an idea and say what is convincing or not — about the idea, never about the person.
    Español: criticar constructivamente — Examinar una idea y decir qué es convincente o no — sobre la idea, nunca sobre la persona.
  • community agreement — A rule the whole class agrees to so that everyone can learn together.
    Español: acuerdo comunitario — Una norma que toda la clase acepta para que todos puedan aprender juntos.

Example

  1. First I make sense of it: one rack holds 8 bikes, each bike has 2 wheels, so this rack has 8 × 2 = 16 wheels. The other rack has 6 times as many wheels — that is what I don't know yet.
  2. I choose a representation: 6 equal groups of 16. Then I make a plan: multiply.
  3. 16 × 6 = 96, so the other rack has about 96 wheels. I check my progress: 96 is 6 groups of 16, and 16 × 6 = 96, so my answer matches my plan.

Common mistake

Treating 'stuck' as a stop sign instead of a signal — waiting silently instead of trying a strategy: asking a question, drawing the problem, or recalling a similar problem.

How families can help at home

  • Ask your student to teach you the example above in their own words.
  • Have them say why each step works, not just the answer.
  • Use the sentence starters below so they explain their thinking in full sentences.
