Warm-up

In September we decomposed 78.5 as 70 + 8.5 and 80 − 1.5.

Teaching notes

Key idea: We are all doers of math and use math in our daily lives. Our math stories continue to grow and evolve as we grow and advance.

I do:

  1. In September I estimated the Ferris wheel: 20 cars with about 4 riders each is 20 × 4 = 80 riders — 'about 80.'
  2. Now I can go further with this year's math: at 6 turns an hour, that wheel moves about 80 × 6 = 480 riders per hour.
  3. Same wheel, same me — but my toolbox grew. That difference IS my math story.

You do: Look back at the answers you wrote in Lesson 1-1. Mark one answer you would write differently today, and tell a classmate what changed.

Common misconception

Treating your math story as finished — writing the same answers you wrote in Lesson 1-1 instead of looking for what actually changed. The story grows and evolves; a retrospective that copies September has missed the point.

ESOL / SPED supports

  • Pre-teach: math biography, reflect, growth, confidence, community (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

You and your classmates each wrote about your math stories at the beginning of the school year. Everyone has their own math story, and as you encounter new challenges and experiences, your story might change.
