🚀 Launch

Chef Montoya is preparing her famous tomato soup for a school banquet. Her original recipe serves 8 people, but tonight she needs to serve 32 guests. She needs your help to scale the recipe without changing the flavor!

What are equivalent ratios?

Equivalent ratios are ratios that mean the same thing. You make one by multiplying or dividing BOTH numbers by the same amount.

Key idea: Multiply or divide both numbers by the same amount to make an equivalent ratio.

Watch me

  • Chef Montoya's soup serves 8 people, but she needs to serve 32.
  • I find the scale factor: 32 divided by 8 = 4.
  • The recipe uses 3 cups of tomatoes to 2 cups of broth, a 3:2 ratio.
  • I multiply both by 4: 3 x 4 = 12 cups of tomatoes, and 2 x 4 = 8 cups of broth.
  • So 3:2 and 12:8 are equivalent ratios, and the flavor stays the same.

Let's try together

  • A ratio is 2:5. We want to make it bigger using a scale factor of 3.
  • Multiply both by 3: 2 x 3 = 6 and 5 x 3 = 15.
  • So 2:5 and 6:15 are equivalent ratios.

Now it's your turn

  • Next you will scale the tomato soup recipe in the ratio table.
  • Multiply both ingredients by the same scale factor each time.
