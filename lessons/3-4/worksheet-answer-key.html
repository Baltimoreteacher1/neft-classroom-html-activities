Lesson 3.4Determine Equivalent Ratios Using Graphs

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can graph the values from a ratio table as points on the coordinate plane.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    A recipe uses 1 cup of sugar for every 2 cups of flour. Plot the ordered pairs: (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6).

    Hint Each pair is (sugar, flour): the first number tells you how far RIGHT, the second how far UP.

    Answer: (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6)

    Why: Each row of the ratio table becomes one point written as (sugar, flour), so 1 cup of sugar with 2 cups of flour is (1, 2), and doubling and tripling the recipe gives (2, 4) and (3, 6). Every y-value is twice its x-value, which is why the three points climb in a straight line.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A ratio table shows (2, 6), (4, 12), and (6, 18). Which ordered pair comes next if the pattern continues?

    1. A(7, 20)
    2. B(8, 24)
    3. C(8, 22)
    4. D(10, 24)

    Hint Watch each coordinate separately: x goes 2, 4, 6, … and y goes 6, 12, 18, …

    Answer: B — (8, 24)

    Why: The pattern adds 2 to x and 6 to y each time. After (6, 18): x = 6+2 = 8, y = 18+6 = 24. The next point is (8, 24).

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph Ratio Tables is writing the ordered pair in the wrong order — putting the second table value first instead of matching (x, y) to the table's column order. For example, if a recipe table shows 2 cups of sugar for every 4 cups of flour, a student might plot (4, 2) instead of (2, 4), landing the point in a completely different spot on the grid. Before you plot, check: which quantity goes on the x-axis, and does your ordered pair start with that number?

    1. AA curved line
    2. BA straight line through the origin

    Hint Think about what happened when you plotted equivalent-ratio points earlier in this lesson.

    Answer: B — A straight line through the origin

    Why: Equivalent ratios are proportional, so their graph is always a straight line that passes through the origin (0, 0).

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A ratio table shows (2, 8), (3, 12), and (5, 20). What is the y-value when x = 7?

    1. A21
    2. B24
    3. C28
    4. D35

    Hint Compare each pair: 2 → 8, 3 → 12, 5 → 20. What is done to x to get y every time?

    Answer: C — 28

    Why: The ratio is y/x = 8/2 = 4. For every x, y = 4x. When x = 7: y = 4 × 7 = 28.

    Which point would NOT be on the graph of the ratio 1:5?

    1. A(2, 10)
    2. B(3, 10)
    3. C(4, 20)
    4. D(5, 25)

    Hint On the 1:5 graph, every y-value is exactly 5 times its x-value.

    Answer: B — (3, 10)

    Why: For the ratio 1:5, every y-value should be 5 times the x-value. (3, 10): 5 × 3 = 15, not 10. So (3, 10) does not belong.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 1 — Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the ratioA recipe uses 1 cup of sugar for every 2 cups of flour. Plot the point for 3 cups of sugar.
    2. 2Find the flour3 cups of sugar means 3 + 2 = 5 cups of flour.
    3. 3Write the ordered pair(3, 5)

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Look at Step 2. The student added 2 to the sugar instead of multiplying by 2.

    Mistake: Step 2. The mistake is adding instead of multiplying: the student turned 3 cups of sugar into 3 + 2 = 5 cups of flour. A ratio of 1 sugar to 2 flour means flour is always 2 times the sugar, so 3 cups of sugar needs 2 × 3 = 6 cups of flour and the point is (3, 6).

    Correct work: You should MULTIPLY, not add. The rule is 2 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar, so flour = 2 × sugar. For 3 cups of sugar, flour = 2 × 3 = 6, so the ordered pair is (3, 6), not (3, 5).

    Chef Reyes uses 3 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar. Plot the ordered pairs from the ratio table: (1,3), (2,6), (3,9), (4,12).

    Answer: (1, 3), (2, 6), (3, 9), (4, 12)

    Why: The rule is 3 cups of flour per 1 cup of sugar, so each point is (sugar, 3 × sugar): (1, 3), (2, 6), (3, 9), and (4, 12). Because the y-axis on this grid counts by 3s, (1, 3) sits just one grid line up even though its y-value is 3.

    xyOrdered Pair

    6

    Points from a ratio table are plotted at (1, 4), (2, 8), and (3, 12). What is the ratio of y to x for each point?

    2. B2:8

    Answer: D — 4:1

    Why: For each point: 4÷1 = 4, 8÷2 = 4, 12÷3 = 4. The ratio of y to x is always 4:1.

    Answer: D — Student B

    Why: Student A's ratio is 1:2 (y goes up 2 for each 1 in x). Student B's ratio is 1:3 (y goes up 3 for each 1 in x). A higher rate of change means a steeper line, so Student B's is steeper.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    On Level — Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the ratioA recipe uses 4 cups of water for every 1 cup of rice (rule: y = 4x). A student is plotting points for the line.
    2. 2List the points(1, 4), (2, 8), (3, 12)
    3. 3Add another pointThe student also plots (4, 14) and says it belongs on the same line.
    4. 4ConclusionAll four points form a straight line.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. The first three points all follow y = 4x, but the extra point (4, 14) does not: when x = 4 the rule requires y = 4 × 4 = 16. Since (4, 14) breaks the rule, it falls below the line instead of on it, and the correct fourth point is (4, 16).

    Correct work: The point (4, 14) is off the line. Using the rule y = 4x, when x = 4 the y-value must be 4 × 4 = 16, so the correct point is (4, 16). Because (4, 14) does not satisfy y = 4x, it does not land on the line through the other points.

Learning target I can graph the values from a ratio table as points on the coordinate plane.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Two recipes are graphed: Recipe A uses 2 cups of mix per 1 serving, and Recipe B uses 3 cups of mix per 2 servings. Plot both sets of points and determine which recipe uses more mix per serving.

    Answer: A: (1, 2), A: (2, 4), A: (3, 6), B: (2, 3), B: (4, 6), B: (6, 9)

    Why: Recipe A gives the points (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6), which is 2 cups of mix per serving, while Recipe B gives (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), which is 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 cups per serving. At 2 servings A already needs 4 cups while B needs only 3, so A's line is steeper and A uses more mix per serving.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find Jada's Mistake

    1. 1Read the ratio tableCookies: 2, 4, 6 | Cups of milk: 1, 2, 3
    2. 2Write ordered pairs(1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6)
    3. 3Plot the pointsPlotted (1,2), (2,4), (3,6) on the grid
    4. 4ConclusionThe x-axis is cookies and y-axis is cups of milk

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. Jada wrote the milk value first, so her pairs came out as (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6). Her own conclusion says cookies belong on the x-axis, so the cookie value has to come first: the pairs are (2, 1), (4, 2), and (6, 3).

    Correct work: The ordered pairs should be (2, 1), (4, 2), (6, 3) — cookies go on x and milk on y, matching the table's column order.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A student plots the points (1, 3), (2, 6), (3, 9), and (4, 15) from a ratio table. She says they form a straight line because they go up. Is she correct? Explain how to check.

    Sample answer: She is not correct. While the first three points follow the pattern y = 3x (3, 6, 9), the fourth point should be (4, 12), not (4, 15). If you plot all four points, (4, 15) would not land on the line through the others. The ratio 4:15 does not simplify to 1:3 like the others.

    Look for: not correct, 12 not 15, straight line, pattern, 1:3, off the line

    Why: She is not correct. Rising is not enough — every point must give the same value of y ÷ x: 3÷1 = 3, 6÷2 = 3, and 9÷3 = 3, but 15÷4 = 3.75. To check, divide y by x at each point (or look for the same right-and-up step repeating), and (4, 15) sits above where the line would go.
