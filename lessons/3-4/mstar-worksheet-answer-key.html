MSTAR Practice Worksheet

Lesson 3-4: Determine Equivalent Ratios Using Graphs

Grade 6 Mathematics · Standard 6.AT.3a

These are MSTAR-style questions written for this curriculum to rehearse the format. They are not official Maryland assessment items. MSTAR — Maryland's new state test, first given in spring 2027 — uses the same kinds of questions you see here: selected response, select-all, two-part evidence questions, and written responses.

Question 1Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: A

A ratio table pairs 1 hour with 45 miles, 2 hours with 90 miles, and 3 hours with 135 miles. Which ordered pair belongs on the graph of this relationship?

  • A(4, 180)
  • B(4, 45)
  • C(4, 140)
  • D(180, 4)

The relationship is 45 miles per hour, so 4 hours pairs with 4 × 45 = 180 miles, giving the point (4, 180).

  • B: 45 miles is the distance for ONE hour. At 4 hours it is 4 x 45.
  • C: 140 is not a multiple of 45. Four hours at 45 miles per hour gives 4 x 45.
  • D: The coordinates are swapped. Hours is the first coordinate and miles the second.

Part B — correct answer: A

What does it mean that the graph of this relationship passes through the origin (0, 0)?

  • AWhen zero hours have passed, zero miles have been traveled.
  • BThe graph must be a curve.
  • CThe unit rate is zero.
  • DThe relationship only works for whole numbers.

A proportional relationship passes through the origin because zero of one quantity corresponds to zero of the other — no distance is covered in no time.

Question 2Select all that apply — correct: A, B, C, E

Select ALL statements that are true about graphing equivalent ratios:

  • AThe points of a proportional relationship lie on a straight line.
  • BThe line passes through the origin (0, 0).
  • CEach ordered pair comes from a row of the ratio table.
  • DThe points form a curve that bends upward.
  • EThe unit rate can be read as the y-value when x = 1.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — equivalent ratios graph as a straight line, not a curve.
