Unit 6, Lesson 11: Fraction Division Problem Solving6.NOS.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

In a word problem, first decide what the question asks: how many groups fit (total ÷ size of each group) or how much is in one group (total ÷ number of groups). The total amount is the dividend and the other number is the divisor. Write the equation, solve it with a model, common denominators, or by multiplying by the reciprocal, then check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor.

Key idea: In a word problem, decide what the question asks. 'How many 1/6-gallon tests fit in 2/3 gallon?' is 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 4. 'Three people share 1/2 pound — how much each?' is 1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/6 pound. Check by multiplying back: 4 × 1/6 = 2/3.

How we did it in class today

  1. Agent Torres has 2/3 gallon of dust and each test uses 1/6 gallon. I ask: how many tests fit?
  2. The total 2/3 is the dividend. The size of each test 1/6 is the divisor. My equation is 2/3 ÷ 1/6.
  3. In sixths, 2/3 = 4/6, and 4 sixths ÷ 1 sixth = 4. Multiplying by the reciprocal gives the same answer: 2/3 × 6/1 = 12/3 = 4.
  4. So she can run 4 tests. I check by multiplying back: 4 × 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3. It matches the total, so the answer is reasonable.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Fraction Division Problem Solving is reversing which fraction is the dividend and which is the divisor when setting up the equation from a word problem. For example: a pitcher holds 3/4 cup of punch, and each serving is 1/8 cup — how many servings? The correct setup is total ÷ group size = 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 servings. A student who reverses the setup calculates 1/8 ÷ 3/4 = 1/8 × 4/3 = 4/24 = 1/6, an answer smaller than 1 that can't possibly be a serving count. Before you submit, check: does my answer make sense as a NUMBER OF GROUPS? If the setup gives a fraction less than 1 for a 'how many' question, you likely swapped the dividend and divisor.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
