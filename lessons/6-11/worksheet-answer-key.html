Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning

    Sort each word problem: should you DIVIDE or MULTIPLY to solve it?

    Groups: Division Problem Multiplication Problem
    • Division ProblemHow many 1/3-cup scoops fit into 2 cups? (2 ÷ 1/3)
    • Division ProblemA ribbon is 3/4 yard. Each piece is 1/8 yard. How many pieces? (3/4 ÷ 1/8)
    • Multiplication ProblemYou run 1/2 mile, 3 times. How far total? (1/2 × 3)
    • Multiplication ProblemA recipe uses 2/3 cup. You make half a recipe. How much? (2/3 × 1/2)

    Hint Re-read each story: does it ask 'how many fit?' or 'how much is a part of the amount?'

    Why: Division answers "how many equal groups fit inside a total," so filling 2 cups with 1/3-cup scoops (2 ÷ 1/3) and cutting 3/4 yard into 1/8-yard pieces (3/4 ÷ 1/8) are division stories. Multiplication finds a repeat or a part of an amount, so running 1/2 mile three times (1/2 × 3) and making half of a 2/3-cup recipe (2/3 × 1/2) are multiplication stories.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A ribbon is 4/5 of a yard long. Each bow uses 1/10 of a yard. Which equation finds how many bows can be made?

    1. A1/10 ÷ 4/5
    2. B4/5 ÷ 1/10
    3. C4/5 × 1/10
    4. D4/5 + 1/10

    Hint Name the roles: 4/5 yard is ALL the ribbon; 1/10 yard is what one bow uses.

    Answer: B — 4/5 ÷ 1/10

    Why: The total (4/5 yard) is divided into groups of 1/10 yard each. The equation is 4/5 ÷ 1/10 = 4/5 × 10/1 = 40/5 = 8 bows.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Fraction Division Problem Solving is reversing which fraction is the dividend and which is the divisor when setting up the equation from a word problem. For example: a pitcher holds 3/4 cup of punch, and each serving is 1/8 cup — how many servings? The correct setup is total ÷ group size = 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 servings. A student who reverses the setup calculates 1/8 ÷ 3/4 = 1/8 × 4/3 = 4/24 = 1/6, an answer smaller than 1 that can't possibly be a serving count. Before you submit, check: does my answer make sense as a NUMBER OF GROUPS? If the setup gives a fraction less than 1 for a 'how many' question, you likely swapped the dividend and divisor.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1ProblemA detective has 4/5 gallon of cleaning fluid. Each spray bottle holds 1/5 gallon. How many bottles can she fill?
    2. 2Student's equation1/5 ÷ 4/5
    3. 3Solve1/5 × 5/4 = 5/20 = 1/4
    4. 4Answer1/4 of a bottle

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Which value is the total being split up — 4/5 or 1/5?

    Mistake: Step 2. In a division story the total comes first and the size of one group comes second. The 4/5 gallon is the total and 1/5 gallon is one bottle, so the equation is 4/5 ÷ 1/5, not 1/5 ÷ 4/5. Solving gives 4/5 × 5/1 = 20/5 = 4 bottles, which fits, because 4/5 is exactly four times 1/5.

    Correct work: The total amount (4/5 gallon) must be the dividend and each bottle (1/5 gallon) the divisor, so the equation is 4/5 ÷ 1/5, not 1/5 ÷ 4/5. Solve: 4/5 × 5/1 = 20/5 = 4 bottles.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Solve each word problem by writing and solving an equation.

    ProblemEquationSolution
    A board is 7/8 foot long. Each shelf bracket needs 1/4 foot. How many brackets fit?
    A jug holds 5/6 liter. Each cup holds 1/3 liter. How many cups can be filled?
    A path is 3/4 mile long. A runner does laps of 3/8 mile. How many laps fit?

    Why: Every row is total ÷ group size, and Keep-Change-Flip turns each into a multiplication: 7/8 × 4/1 = 28/8 = 3 1/2 brackets, 5/6 × 3/1 = 15/6 = 2 1/2 cups, and 3/4 × 8/3 = 24/12 = 2 laps. A fractional answer like 3 1/2 just means the last piece is only part of a full bracket.

  2. 2Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort: is the equation and solution CORRECT, or is there an error?

    Groups: Correct Equation and Solution Incorrect Equation or Solution
    • Correct Equation and SolutionA tank has 5/6 gallon. Each bucket holds 1/3 gallon. → 5/6 ÷ 1/3 = 2 1/2 buckets
    • Correct Equation and SolutionA pipe is 2/3 foot long. Each piece is 1/6 foot. → 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 4 pieces
    • Correct Equation and SolutionA bag has 3/4 lb. Each scoop is 1/8 lb. → 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6 scoops
    • Incorrect Equation or SolutionA rope is 1/2 yard. Each section is 1/4 yard. → 1/4 ÷ 1/2 = 1/2 section
    • Incorrect Equation or SolutionA jug has 4/5 liter. Each cup is 2/5 liter. → 4/5 × 2/5 = 8/25 cups
    • Incorrect Equation or SolutionA shelf is 7/8 foot. Each book is 1/4 foot. → 7/8 ÷ 1/4 = 7/32 books

    Why: Three cards are set up as total ÷ group size and solved correctly: 5/6 ÷ 1/3 = 2 1/2, 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 4, and 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6. The other three break down: 1/4 ÷ 1/2 reverses the total and the piece size, 4/5 × 2/5 multiplies where the story asks how many cups fit, and 7/8 ÷ 1/4 = 7/32 comes from multiplying by 1/4 instead of flipping it to 4/1.

  3. 3Use the model to solve. Show your work.

    How many 1/8-pound molds can be made from 3/4 pound?

    Answer: 6

    Why: Rewriting 3/4 as 6/8 shows the clay measured in eighth-pound pieces, which is exactly what the bar model lays out. Each mold takes 1/8 pound, so six molds can be made: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6.

    1. AHis math and reasoning are both correct — the answer should be less than 1

    Why: 2/3 ÷ 1/4 = 2/3 × 4/1 = 8/3 = 2 2/3. This IS correct. When you divide by a number less than 1, the quotient is greater than the dividend.

For example: a pitcher holds 3/4 cup of punch, and each serving is 1/8 cup — how many servings? The correct setup is total ÷ group size = 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 servings. A student who reverses the setup calculates 1/8 ÷ 3/4 = 1/8 × 4/3 = 4/24 = 1/6, an answer smaller than 1 that can't possibly be a serving count.     Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1ProblemA chemist has 3/4 liter of solution. Each test tube holds 1/8 liter. How many test tubes can be filled?
    2. 2Student's equation3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 1/8
    3. 3Solve3/4 × 1/8 = 3/32
    4. 4Answer3/32 of a test tube

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The setup 3/4 ÷ 1/8 was right, but the divisor was never flipped. Keep-Change-Flip means multiplying by the reciprocal of 1/8, which is 8/1: 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 test tubes. Checking confirms it, since 6 × 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.

    Correct work: The student set up the division correctly but forgot to flip the divisor. Keep, Change, Flip means multiply by the reciprocal of 1/8, which is 8/1: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 test tubes. Check: 6 × 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Solve each multi-step problem. Show the equation and verify with multiplication.

    ProblemEquationSolutionCheck (quotient × divisor = dividend)
    A chef has 2 1/2 cups of flour. Each batch of cookies needs 1/4 cup. How many batches?
    A detective has 1 1/3 gallons of solution. Each test uses 2/3 gallon. How many tests?
    A board is 3 3/4 feet long. Each piece must be 5/8 foot. How many pieces?

    Why: Each mixed number becomes an improper fraction before Keep-Change-Flip: 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 5/2 × 4/1 = 10 batches, 1 1/3 ÷ 2/3 = 4/3 × 3/2 = 2 tests, and 3 3/4 ÷ 5/8 = 15/4 × 8/5 = 6 pieces. The Check column proves each one, because multiplying the quotient back by the divisor returns the original total.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Problem-Solving Error

    1. 1ProblemA tank has 3/4 gallon of water. Each bucket holds 1/6 gallon. How many buckets can be filled?
    2. 2Student's equation1/6 ÷ 3/4
    3. 3Solve1/6 × 4/3 = 4/18 = 2/9
    4. 4Answer2/9 buckets

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The dividend and divisor are swapped. The 3/4 gallon is the total being split up and 1/6 gallon is one bucket, so the equation must be 3/4 ÷ 1/6 = 3/4 × 6/1 = 18/4 = 4 1/2 buckets. An answer of 2/9 of a bucket is a warning sign, since a 1/6-gallon bucket clearly fits inside 3/4 gallon more than once.

    Correct work: The equation should be 3/4 ÷ 1/6 (total ÷ group size). Then 3/4 × 6/1 = 18/4 = 4 1/2 buckets.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    Write your own fraction division word problem where the answer is 8. Then show the equation, solve it step by step, and explain how you know your answer is reasonable.

    Look for: divide, = 8, reasonable, word problem, equation, reciprocal

    Why: The safest way to build this problem is backwards: choose a group size, multiply it by 8, and let that product be the total in your story. For example, 8 groups of 1/2 make 4, so "4 cups of trail mix packed into 1/2-cup bags" gives 4 ÷ 1/2 = 4 × 2/1 = 8. The answer is reasonable because 8 groups of the size you chose rebuild the original total exactly.
