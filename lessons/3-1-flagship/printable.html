📚 Vocabulary

WordWhat it meansExample
Ratio
Razón		A way to compare two amounts, like 3 to 2.
Una manera de comparar dos cantidades, como 3 a 2.		3 cats to 5 dogs — It compares two quantities.
Comparison
Comparación		Looking at two or more amounts to see how they are related.
Mirar dos o más cantidades para ver cómo se relacionan.
Part-to-part
Parte a parte		A ratio comparing one part of a group to another part.
Una razón que compara una parte de un grupo con otra parte.		2 red marbles to 3 blue marbles — It compares one part to another part.
Part-to-whole
Parte a todo		A ratio comparing one part to the whole group.
Una razón que compara una parte con todo el grupo.		2 red marbles to 5 total marbles — It compares a part to the whole group.
Colon notation
Notación con dos puntos		Writing a ratio with two dots between the numbers, like 3:2.
Escribir una razón con dos puntos entre los números, como 3:2.

🚀 Launch

Welcome to Chef Academy! Head Chef Reyes is teaching new students how to read recipes like a pro. Her signature cookie recipe calls for 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar. Before the students can start baking, they need to understand how these ingredient amounts compare to each other.

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two amounts. It tells how much of one thing there is for another thing.

Key idea: A ratio compares two quantities, and the order you write them in matters.

Watch me

  • Chef Reyes uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar.
  • I am comparing two amounts: flour and sugar.
  • I write flour to sugar, in that order, as 3 to 2.
  • In colon notation I write it as 3:2.
  • So the ratio of flour to sugar is 3:2.

Let's try together

  • A bowl has 4 apples and 1 orange. What two amounts are we comparing?
  • We write apples to oranges in order: 4 to 1.
  • In colon notation that is 4:1.

Now it's your turn

  • Next you will sort the chef's statements into ratios and not-ratios.
  • Remember: a real ratio compares two amounts.
