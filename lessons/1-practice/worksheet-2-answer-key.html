Learning target I can divide with fractions and mixed numbers and give the quotient as a mixed number in simplest form, say how many whole groups fit and what the leftover means, divide multi-digit whole numbers, and divide decimals in money problems.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Use the model to solve. Show your work.

    How many 1/6-size pieces fit into 3 wholes?

    Answer: 18

    Why: The bar shows whole 1, whole 2 and whole 3 each cut into 6 sixths, so 6 + 6 + 6 = 18 pieces of size 1/6. The steps land on the same answer: put a 1 under the 3 and use Keep, Change, Flip, 3/1 × 6/1 = 18.

  2. 2Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Solve each decimal division problem.

    Division ProblemQuotient
    9.6 ÷ 0.8
    15.6 ÷ 1.3
    24.5 ÷ 3.5
    5.76 ÷ 0.24
    11.34 ÷ 0.9

    Why: Once the divisor is shifted to a whole number, each row is ordinary division: 96 ÷ 8 = 12, 156 ÷ 13 = 12, 245 ÷ 35 = 7, and 576 ÷ 24 = 24 (that row needs ×100 because 0.24 has two decimal places). The last row becomes 113.4 ÷ 9 = 12.6, since only the divisor has to end up whole.

Part 2Practice the skillshow every step
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 756 ÷ 9?

    1. A83
    2. B84
    3. C86
    4. D94

    Answer: B — 84

    Why: 9 × 84 = 756. Running the cycle: 75 ÷ 9 → 8 with 3 left, then 36 ÷ 9 → 4 with 0 left, so the quotient is 84 and the remainder is 0.

  2. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 8.4 ÷ 0.7?

    1. A0.12
    2. B1.2
    3. C12
    4. D120

    Answer: C — 12

    Why: 8.4 ÷ 0.7 = 84 ÷ 7 = 12.

  3. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 1/2 ÷ 1/4?

    1. A1/8
    2. B1/2
    3. C2
    4. D4

    Answer: C — 2

    Why: 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4/1 = 4/2 = 2. Two 1/4-size pieces fit into 1/2.

Part 3Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 6Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the first step in evaluating 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4?

    1. ARewrite 2 1/2 as the improper fraction 5/2
    2. BFlip 2 1/2 to 1/2 2
    3. CDivide 2 by 1/4 and ignore the half
    4. DAdd 2 + 1/2 + 1/4

    Answer: A — Rewrite 2 1/2 as the improper fraction 5/2

    Why: A mixed number cannot be flipped or multiplied as it stands, so rewrite it first: 2 1/2 = 5/2. Then Keep, Change, Flip gives 5/2 × 4/1 = 10.

  2. 7Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Divide with a mixed number: 1 1/2 ÷ 1/4.

    1. A6
    2. B3/8
    3. C12
    4. D3/4

    Answer: A — 6

    Why: Rewrite 1 1/2 as 3/2. Use the reciprocal of 1/4, which is 4/1: 3/2 × 4/1 = 12/2 = 6.

  3. 8Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Divide with a mixed number: 3 1/2 ÷ 3/4.

    1. A4 2/3
    2. B2 5/8
    3. C3 1/2
    4. D1 5/8

    Answer: A — 4 2/3

    Why: Rewrite 3 1/2 as 7/2. Use the reciprocal of 3/4, which is 4/3: 7/2 × 4/3 = 28/6 = 4 2/3.

  4. 9Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A recipe needs 1 1/2 cups of flour per batch. How many full batches can be made from 6 cups of flour?

    1. A4 batches
    2. B9 batches
    3. C4/9 of a batch
    4. D1 1/2 batches

    Answer: A — 4 batches

    Why: This is 6 ÷ 1 1/2. Rewrite 1 1/2 as 3/2, then Keep, Change, Flip: 6/1 × 2/3 = 12/3 = 4 batches.

  5. 10Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A board is 6 3/4 feet long. It is cut into pieces that are each 3/4 foot long, and then EVERY one of those pieces is cut in half again. How many final pieces are there?

    1. A9 pieces
    2. B18 pieces
    3. C4 1/2 pieces
    4. D6 pieces

    Answer: B — 18 pieces

    Why: First: 6 3/4 ÷ 3/4 = 27/4 × 4/3 = 108/12 = 9 pieces. Then every one of those 9 is cut in half: 9 × 2 = 18 final pieces.

  6. 11Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A rope is 4 feet long. How many 1/2-foot pieces can be cut from it?

    1. A1/2 piece
    2. B2 pieces
    3. C4 pieces
    4. D8 pieces

    Answer: D — 8 pieces

    Why: Write 4 ÷ 1/2. Put a 1 under the 4, then Keep, Change, Flip: 4/1 × 2/1 = 8 pieces.

Part 4Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 12Answer in complete sentences.

    A gas station sells fuel at $3.45 per gallon. A customer pays $27.60 total. How many gallons did the customer buy? Set up the decimal division, show how to convert it to whole-number division, and solve.

    Look for: divide, decimal, quotient, multiply by 100, 8, whole number, equivalent

    Why: Total cost divided by price per gallon gives the number of gallons, so the setup is 27.60 ÷ 3.45. The divisor has two decimal places, so multiplying both numbers by 100 gives 2,760 ÷ 345 = 8 gallons, and 8 × $3.45 = $27.60 confirms it.
