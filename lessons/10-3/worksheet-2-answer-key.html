Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each move: legal or against the rules?

    Groups: Legal move Against the rules
    • Legal moveMove the top disc of any stack
    • Legal movePlace a small disc on a bigger disc
    • Legal moveMove exactly one disc at a time
    • Against the rulesMove two discs at once
    • Against the rulesPlace a big disc on a smaller disc
    • Against the rulesPull a disc out from the middle of a stack

    Hint Only the TOP disc of a stack can move, and only one at a time.

    Why: The three legal moves are the whole rule set: take only a top disc, move one at a time, and rest smaller on bigger. The illegal moves each break exactly one rule — two at once, big on small, or grabbing from mid-stack. Every 2ⁿ − 1 solution is built from nothing but the legal three.

  2. 2Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Use the doubling pattern to complete the table of fewest moves.

    DiscsPattern: double the previous, add 1Fewest moves
    3
    4
    6

    Hint Each new disc means: solve the smaller tower, move the big disc, solve the smaller tower again.

    Why: The recursion is the reason for the rule: to move n discs you solve n − 1 discs (7 moves), carry the biggest disc (1 move), and solve n − 1 again (7 moves) — so 4 discs need 15, 5 need 31, and 6 need 2 × 31 + 1 = 63. The pattern 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63 is one less than the doubling numbers 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Will the fewest number of steps for a Tower of Hanoi puzzle always be an ODD number?

    1. AYes — doubling any whole number gives an even number, and adding 1 makes it odd
    2. BNo — every other tower size gives an even number of steps
    3. CYes — but only because 7 happens to be odd
    4. DNo — the steps alternate between odd and even

    Hint Look at the whole list: 1, 3, 7, 15, 31.

    Answer: A — Yes — doubling any whole number gives an even number, and adding 1 makes it odd

    Why: Each answer comes from doubling the one before (always even) and adding 1 (always odd): 1, 3, 7, 15, 31 are all odd, and the rule guarantees the next one will be too.

    Watch for: Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have?

    1. A2
    2. B3
    3. C6
    4. D12

    Answer: C — 6

    Why: A regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry — one through each pair of opposite vertices and each pair of opposite sides.

    Watch for: Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    You solved the small towers by hand: 1 disc takes 1 step, 2 discs take 3 steps, 3 discs take 7 steps. Now someone hands you a five-disc tower and asks for the fewest steps — but you do not have time to play it out. The table shows: 1 disc = 1 step, 2 discs = 3 steps, 3 discs = 7 steps. What is the pattern rule from one row to the next?

    1. AAdd 2 to the previous number of steps
    2. BMultiply the previous number of steps by 3
    3. CDouble the previous number of steps and add 1
    4. DAdd the disc number to the previous steps

    Answer: C — Double the previous number of steps and add 1

    Why: 1 × 2 + 1 = 3 and 3 × 2 + 1 = 7, so the rule is double and add 1. Adding 2 matches 1→3 but fails on 3→5≠7, multiplying by 3 matches 1→3 but fails on 3→9≠7, and adding the disc number matches 1+2=3 but fails on 3+3=6≠7.

    Watch for: Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    You solved the small towers by hand: 1 disc takes 1 step, 2 discs take 3 steps, 3 discs take 7 steps. Now someone hands you a five-disc tower and asks for the fewest steps — but you do not have time to play it out. Using the rule "double the steps and add 1," how many steps does a 4-disc tower take, starting from the 3-disc value of 7 steps?

    1. A8
    2. B14
    3. C15
    4. D21

    Answer: C — 15

    Why: 7 × 2 + 1 = 15. 8 comes from only adding 1 without doubling, 14 comes from only doubling without adding 1, and 21 comes from tripling instead of doubling.

    Watch for: Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    You solved the small towers by hand: 1 disc takes 1 step, 2 discs take 3 steps, 3 discs take 7 steps. Now someone hands you a five-disc tower and asks for the fewest steps — but you do not have time to play it out. Continuing the pattern from 15 steps for a 4-disc tower, how many steps does a 5-disc tower take?

    1. A16
    2. B30
    3. C31
    4. D45

    Answer: C — 31

    Why: 15 × 2 + 1 = 31. 16 comes from only adding 1, 30 comes from only doubling, and 45 comes from tripling 15 instead of doubling and adding 1.

    Watch for: Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.
