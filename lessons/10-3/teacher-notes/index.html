Unit 10, Lesson 3: Math is PlayfulMPP.7

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can use patterns and relationships to make sense of the Tower of Hanoi puzzle and think logically about its solutions.

Language: I can describe a puzzle strategy using the words pattern, rule, and predict.

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below before the lesson.

Warm-up

Discs: 1, 2, 3 — Steps: 1, 3, 7. What is happening from each row to the next?

Teaching notes

Key idea: Algorithmic Thinking & Recursive Patterns. 1. Deconstruct complex multi-step problems into smaller, repeatable sub-tasks. 2. Trace patterns from simple cases to formulate general mathematical rules. 3. Verify algorithmic rules across edge cases and increasing problem sizes

I do:

  1. Before moving anything, I describe what the solved puzzle will look like: the discs in the same order — smallest on top to largest on bottom — but on a different rod.
  2. Then I play the small cases: a one-disc tower takes 1 move, and a two-disc tower takes 3 moves.
  3. A three-disc tower takes at least 7 moves. Small cases are how mathematicians play.

You do: Use the pattern rule to predict how many steps a six-disc tower needs. Then tell a partner: will the number of steps ALWAYS be odd? Why or why not?

Common misconception

Doubling the previous number of steps but forgetting to add 1 — answering 14 instead of 15 for four discs, or 30 instead of 31 for five.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: puzzle, pattern, pattern rule, predict, organize (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Which statement best shows what this lesson means by 'math is playful'?

  • We play with math by imagining possible solutions and looking for patterns that help us think logically about puzzles ✓ correct
  • Math is playful only when there are no rules to follow
  • Puzzles like the Tower of Hanoi can only be solved by trial and error
  • Playing with math means guessing until something works

The summary names both moves: imagining different ways to visualize the problem, and looking for patterns or relationships that help us think logically.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

You solved the small towers by hand: 1 disc takes 1 step, 2 discs take 3 steps, 3 discs take 7 steps. Now someone hands you a five-disc tower and asks for the fewest steps — but you do not have time to play it out.
