Understand Rates and Unit Rates

Core Foundation · Step-by-Step Scaffolds

Name: Date: Period: Learning Goal:
  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A booth charges $4.50 for 9 games. What is the unit rate?

    1. A$0.50 per game
    2. B$0.45 per game
    3. C$2.00 per game
    4. D$4.50 per game

    💡 Solution Rationale: $4.50 ÷ 9 = $0.50 per game.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Rates and Unit Rates is comparing total costs instead of unit rates: a student sees Pack X at $4.00 for 10 tokens and Pack Y at $6.00 for 16 tokens and picks Pack X just because $4.00 is the smaller number, without dividing to find the cost per token ($0.40 for Pack X vs. $0.375 for Pack Y — Pack Y is actually the better buy). A second common mistake is dividing backwards — finding tokens ÷ cost instead of cost ÷ tokens — which turns 'dollars per token' into 'tokens per dollar' and gives an answer that looks reasonable but answers the wrong question. Always divide the total cost by the number of items, and check that your unit rate answers 'the cost for ONE item,' not the reverse.

  6. 6 ERROR ANALYSIS

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the problem:A booth charges $6.00 for 4 games. Find the unit rate (cost per game).
    2. 2Set up the division:$6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50, but the student writes $1.05 per game.
    3. 3State the unit rate:The student says each game costs $1.05.

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: $6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per game. The student divided correctly but wrote the digits in the wrong place value ($1.05 instead of $1.50). The cost per game is $1.50.. Misconception: The division itself was fine, but the digits landed in the wrong place values: $6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50, while $1.05 reads as one dollar and five cents. An estimate catches it quickly, since 4 games at $1.05 would total only $4.20, not $6.00.

      Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

      Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: To find cost per ticket, divide $8 ÷ 12 = $0.67 per ticket (rounded). The student divided tickets by cost instead of cost by tickets.. Misconception: The two quantities were divided in the wrong order. "Dollars per ticket" puts dollars on top, so the correct division is $8 ÷ 12 ≈ $0.67 per ticket. The student's 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 answers a different question, and $1.50 per ticket would mean 12 tickets cost $18 instead of $8.
    2. 2 OPEN RESPONSE

      An arcade sells tokens in three packs: 25 tokens for $5, 60 tokens for $10.80, and 100 tokens for $19. A student says the 100-token pack is always the best deal because you get the most tokens. Is the student correct? Find all three unit rates and explain.

      💡 Exemplar Response: Key Terms to Look For: unit rate, $0.20, $0.18, $0.19, 60 tokens, divide, not correct Explanation: The student is not correct. Dividing each price by its tokens gives $5 ÷ 25 = $0.20 per token, $10.80 ÷ 60 = $0.18 per token, and $19 ÷ 100 = $0.19 per token. The 60-token pack has the lowest unit rate, so it is the best deal — getting the most tokens is not the same as paying the least for each one.
