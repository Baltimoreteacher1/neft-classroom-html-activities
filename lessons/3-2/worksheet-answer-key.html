Lesson 3.2Understand Rates and Unit Rates

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can describe a rate using its two different units and use a model to find a travel time, and I can find a unit rate to compare prices and decide the better buy.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Find the unit rate for each arcade deal.

    DealTotal CostNumber of GamesUnit Rate
    Deal A$6.0012 games$0.50 per game
    Deal B$7.5010 games
    Deal C$4.808 games

    Hint Look at Deal A's finished row: $6.00 for 12 games became $0.50 per game. Your rows work the same way.

    Why: A unit rate tells the cost of one game, so you divide the total cost by the number of games — the same move that turned Deal A's $6.00 for 12 games into $0.50 per game. Deal B is $7.50 ÷ 10 = $0.75 per game, and Deal C is $4.80 ÷ 8 = $0.60 per game.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A booth charges $4.50 for 9 games. What is the unit rate?

    1. A$0.45 per game
    2. B$0.50 per game
    3. C$2.00 per game
    4. D$4.50 per game

    Hint You know the total cost ($4.50) and the total games (9). A unit rate tells the cost of just ONE game.

    Answer: B — $0.50 per game

    Why: $4.50 ÷ 9 = $0.50 per game.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Rates and Unit Rates is comparing total costs instead of unit rates: a student sees Pack X at $4.00 for 10 tokens and Pack Y at $6.00 for 16 tokens and picks Pack X just because $4.00 is the smaller number, without dividing to find the cost per token ($0.40 for Pack X vs. $0.375 for Pack Y — Pack Y is actually the better buy). A second common mistake is dividing backwards — finding tokens ÷ cost instead of cost ÷ tokens — which turns 'dollars per token' into 'tokens per dollar' and gives an answer that looks reasonable but answers the wrong question. Always divide the total cost by the number of items, and check that your unit rate answers 'the cost for ONE item,' not the reverse.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the problemA booth charges $6.00 for 4 games. Find the unit rate (cost per game).
    2. 2Set up the division$6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50, but the student writes $1.05 per game.
    3. 3State the unit rateThe student says each game costs $1.05.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Check the place value: is the answer about one dollar and a half, or one dollar and five cents?

    Mistake: Step 2. The division itself was fine, but the digits landed in the wrong place values: $6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50, while $1.05 reads as one dollar and five cents. An estimate catches it quickly, since 4 games at $1.05 would total only $4.20, not $6.00.

    Correct work: $6.00 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per game. The student divided correctly but wrote the digits in the wrong place value ($1.05 instead of $1.50). The cost per game is $1.50.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the problemPack X is 10 tokens for $4.00. Pack Y is 16 tokens for $6.00. Which is the better buy (lower cost per token)?
    2. 2Compare the dealsPack X costs $4.00 and Pack Y costs $6.00. The student says Pack X is the better buy because $4.00 is the lower total price.
    3. 3State the answerThe student chooses Pack X.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. Comparing the two total prices is not a fair comparison, because Pack X holds 10 tokens and Pack Y holds 16. Dividing gives the cost per token: $4.00 ÷ 10 = $0.40 and $6.00 ÷ 16 = $0.375. Pack Y costs less for each token, so Pack Y is the better buy even though its total price is higher.

    Correct work: Compare unit rates, not total prices. Pack X: $4.00 ÷ 10 = $0.40 per token. Pack Y: $6.00 ÷ 16 = $0.375 per token. Pack Y has the lower cost per token, so Pack Y is the better buy.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Two water fountains fill bottles at different rates. Fountain A fills 3 bottles in 5 minutes. Fountain B fills 7 bottles in 10 minutes. Compare their rates.

    FountainBottlesMinutesBottles per Minute
    Fountain A35
    Fountain B710
    Faster?

    Why: "Bottles per minute" means bottles divided by minutes, so Fountain A is 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6 bottles per minute and Fountain B is 7 ÷ 10 = 0.7 bottles per minute. Since 0.7 > 0.6, Fountain B is faster — comparing only the bottle counts or only the minutes would not settle it.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Rate Error

    1. 1Find the unit rate: 12 tickets for $812 tickets for $8
    2. 2Set up the division12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
    3. 3Write the unit rateThe unit rate is $1.50 per ticket

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The two quantities were divided in the wrong order. "Dollars per ticket" puts dollars on top, so the correct division is $8 ÷ 12 ≈ $0.67 per ticket. The student's 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 answers a different question, and $1.50 per ticket would mean 12 tickets cost $18 instead of $8.

    Correct work: To find cost per ticket, divide $8 ÷ 12 = $0.67 per ticket (rounded). The student divided tickets by cost instead of cost by tickets.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    An arcade sells tokens in three packs: 25 tokens for $5, 60 tokens for $10.80, and 100 tokens for $19. A student says the 100-token pack is always the best deal because you get the most tokens. Is the student correct? Find all three unit rates and explain.

    Look for: unit rate, $0.20, $0.18, $0.19, 60 tokens, divide, not correct

    Why: The student is not correct. Dividing each price by its tokens gives $5 ÷ 25 = $0.20 per token, $10.80 ÷ 60 = $0.18 per token, and $19 ÷ 100 = $0.19 per token. The 60-token pack has the lowest unit rate, so it is the best deal — getting the most tokens is not the same as paying the least for each one.
