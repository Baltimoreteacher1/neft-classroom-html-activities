THE BIG HOOK · WHY THIS MATTERS

You're comparing ticket prices at different game booths in the arcade. Booth A charges $3 for 5 games, and Booth B charges $5 for 8 games. Which booth gives you a better deal? To find out, you need to figure out the cost per game — that's the unit rate!

Content: I can describe a rate using its two different units and use a model to find a travel time, and I can find a unit rate to compare prices and decide the better buy.

Language: I can explain a rate like "150 miles per hour" using distance and time units, and explain my choice using the words rate, unit rate, per, and better buy.

🚀 Launch

You're comparing ticket prices at different game booths in the arcade. Booth A charges $3 for 5 games, and Booth B charges $5 for 8 games. Which booth gives you a better deal? To find out, you need to figure out the cost per game — that's the unit rate!

What is a unit rate, and how does it help me find the better buy?

A rate compares two amounts with different units. A unit rate tells you the amount for just 1 of something, like the cost for 1 game.

Key idea: Rates & Unit Rates. Formula: Unit Rate = (Total Quantity) ÷ (Number of Units). 1. Rate: A ratio comparing two quantities with different units (e.g. $12 for 3 lbs). 2. Unit Rate: A rate simplified to a denominator of 1 unit ($4 per 1 lb). 3. Divide numerator by denominator to compute the per-one amount

Watch me

GIVEN DATA / MODEL
  • Booth A charges $3 for 5 games. I want the cost for just 1 game.
  • I divide the cost by the games: $3 ÷ 5 = $0.60.
  • So Booth A costs $0.60 per game. That is the unit rate.
  • Because this unit rate is money for one item, it has its own name: it is the unit price.
  • Now I can compare this booth's unit price fairly with any other booth's.
