Teaching notes

Key idea: When we do math, we make sense of problems, develop a solution plan, check our progress, and try other strategies when we come to dead ends.

I do:

  1. A bowl is full of peas — far too many to count one at a time. About how many peas are in the bowl?
  2. I do not count every pea — I look for a group I CAN count, like one small cluster of about 10.
  3. Then I ask how many of those clusters would cover the whole bowl. I count about 12.
  4. 12 clusters of about 10 is 12 × 10 = 120, so my estimate is about 120 peas — a reasoned answer, close enough to be useful.

You do: Three students walked different distances. Deon walked a shorter distance than Miguel, and Evelyn walked a longer distance than Miguel. Before computing anything, say what you KNOW and what you DON'T know — then pick possible distances for all three that fit the relationships.

Common misconception

Expecting a fraction of a number to be bigger than the number — 5/6 × 540 must be LESS than 540 because 5/6 is less than 1; only a fraction greater than 1, like 6/5, makes the product bigger.

ESOL / SPED supports

  • Pre-teach: quantity, relationship, strategy, reasonable, persevere (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.
