🚀 Launch

Doing math is exploring: you make sense of a problem, plan a strategy, check your progress, and try new paths when you hit a dead end. Today's problems — walking distances and skyscraper heights — reward thinkers, not just calculators.

Make sense, plan, check

The book opens with two warm-ups: about how many peas are in the picture, and decompose 105.76 five different ways. Both are about thinking before computing.

Key idea: When we do math, we make sense of problems, develop a solution plan, check our progress, and try other strategies when we come to dead ends.

Watch me estimate the peas

  • I do not count every pea — I look for a group I CAN count, like one small cluster of about 10.
  • Then I ask how many of those clusters would cover the whole picture.
  • If about 12 clusters fit, my estimate is about 120 peas — a reasoned answer, close enough to be useful.

Let's decompose 105.76 five ways

  • 105.76 = 100 + 5.76.
  • 105.76 = 105 + 0.76.
  • 105.76 = 110 − 4.24.
  • 105.76 = 52.88 × 2, and 105.76 = 211.52 ÷ 2.
  • Same number, five different breaks. Find one more of your own.

Now you try

  • Three students walked different distances. Deon walked a shorter distance than Miguel, and Evelyn walked a longer distance than Miguel.
  • Before computing anything, say what you KNOW and what you DON'T know — then pick possible distances for all three that fit the relationships.
