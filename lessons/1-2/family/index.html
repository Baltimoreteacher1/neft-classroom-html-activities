What students are learning

The book opens with two warm-ups: about how many peas are in the picture, and decompose 105.76 five different ways. Both are about thinking before computing.

Key idea: Math Practices: Sense Making & Strategy Planning. 1. Identify known quantities and the unknown question. 2. Choose a strategy (draw a model, write an equation, or build a table). 3. Evaluate progress and pivot to an alternate approach if needed

Key words

  • quantity — An amount in a problem — a number with a meaning attached, like 540 meters.
    Español: cantidad — Una cantidad en un problema: un número con un significado, como 540 metros.
  • relationship — How two quantities compare or connect to each other.
    Español: relación — Cómo se comparan o se conectan dos cantidades entre sí.
  • strategy — A plan for how to solve a problem before you start computing.
    Español: estrategia — Un plan para resolver un problema antes de empezar a calcular.
  • reasonable — A solution that makes sense in the context of the problem.
    Español: razonable — Una solución que tiene sentido en el contexto del problema.
  • persevere — To keep working on a problem, checking your progress and adjusting your plan when you get stuck.
    Español: perseverar — Seguir trabajando en un problema, revisando tu progreso y ajustando tu plan cuando te atascas.

Example

  1. A bowl is full of peas — far too many to count one at a time. About how many peas are in the bowl?
  2. I do not count every pea — I look for a group I CAN count, like one small cluster of about 10.
  3. Then I ask how many of those clusters would cover the whole bowl. I count about 12.
  4. 12 clusters of about 10 is 12 × 10 = 120, so my estimate is about 120 peas — a reasoned answer, close enough to be useful.

Common mistake

Expecting a fraction of a number to be bigger than the number — 5/6 × 540 must be LESS than 540 because 5/6 is less than 1; only a fraction greater than 1, like 6/5, makes the product bigger.

How families can help at home

  • Ask your student to teach you the example above in their own words.
  • Have them say why each step works, not just the answer.
  • Use the sentence starters below so they explain their thinking in full sentences.
