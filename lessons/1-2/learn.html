Why we're learning this

Doing math is exploring: you make sense of a problem, plan a strategy, check your progress, and try new paths when you hit a dead end. Today's problems — walking distances and skyscraper heights — reward thinkers, not just calculators.

👁️Picture itSee what the math looks like before you read about it

These are the words you will use today.

  • relationshipHow two quantities compare or connect to each other.
  • strategyA plan for how to solve a problem before you start computing.
  • reasonableA solution that makes sense in the context of the problem.
  • persevereTo keep working on a problem, checking your progress and adjusting your plan when you get stuck.

👀Watch me solve itI do — read one step at a time

Read one step, then press the button for the next one. Tap any blue word to see what it means.

  1. I do not count every pea — I look for a group I CAN count, like one small cluster of about 10.
  2. Then I ask how many of those clusters would cover the whole picture.
  3. If about 12 clusters fit, my estimate is about 120 peas — a reasoned answer, close enough to be useful.

⚠️ Watch out — mistakes students make here

  • 54 meters is only 1/10 of 540 — you still need 9 of those tenths.
  • 4,860 meters multiplies 540 by 9 but never divides by 10.
  • 594 meters ADDS one tenth (540 + 54) instead of taking 9 tenths of 540.

Check your work against this list before you say you are done.

Solve: The Rio de Janeiro building will be 9/10 as tall as One World Trade Center (540 meters). How tall will it be?

My answer

Stuck? Open one hint at a time.

Hint 1

First find one tenth of 540.

Hint 2

540 ÷ 10 = 54.

Hint 3

Multiply that tenth by 9.

Check my answer
✅ Answer: 486 meters
9/10 × 540: one tenth of 540 is 54, and 9 tenths is 9 × 54 = 486 meters.
