Unit 7, Lesson 7: Represent Polygons on the Coordinate Plane

What students are learning

You can draw a shape on a graph by plotting its corner points and connecting them in order. The corner numbers also tell you how long each side is. For example, from (-2, 3) to (4, 3) is 6 units. Add all the sides to get the perimeter.

  • vertex — A corner point of a polygon, named by an ordered pair on the coordinate plane.
  • polygon — A closed figure made of line segments; on the plane you draw it by plotting the vertices and connecting them in order.
  • perimeter — The total distance around a polygon — the sum of all its side lengths.
  • distance between vertices — When two vertices share an x- or y-coordinate, the distance between them is the absolute difference of the other coordinates.
  • scale — What one grid unit stands for in the real situation.
  • Side length — The distance between two vertices that are joined by a side.

  • vértice — Un punto de esquina de un polígono, nombrado por un par ordenado en el plano de coordenadas.
  • polígono — Una figura cerrada hecha de segmentos; en el plano se dibuja marcando los vértices y conectándolos en orden.
  • perímetro — La distancia total alrededor de un polígono: la suma de todos sus lados.

  • escala — Lo que representa una unidad de la cuadrícula en la situación real.
  • Longitud del lado — La distancia entre dos vértices unidos por un lado.

  • محیط (perimeter) — مجموع فاصلهٔ دور یک چندضلعی؛ یعنی جمع طول همهٔ ضلع‌های آن.

  • مقیاس (scale) — آنچه یک واحد شبکه در وضعیت واقعی نشان می‌دهد.
  • طول ضلع (Side length) — فاصلهٔ بین دو رأسی که با یک ضلع به هم وصل شده‌اند.

How we did it in class today

  1. (-4.5, 3) and (5, 3) share y = 3, so the side between them is horizontal.
  2. Its length is the distance between the x-coordinates: |5 − (−4.5)| = 9.5 units.
  3. (5, 3) and (5, -3.75) share x = 5, so that side is vertical: |3 − (−3.75)| = 6.75 units.

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

A rectangle has vertices (-3, 2), (4, 2), (4, -3), (-3, -3). What is its perimeter?

  1. 5 units
  2. 7 units
  3. 24 square units
  4. 24 units
Check the answer

The answer is: 24 units

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
