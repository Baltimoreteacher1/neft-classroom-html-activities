Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can draw polygons from their vertex coordinates on the coordinate plane, and use coordinates to find side lengths and solve perimeter and area problems.

Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Vertices (-4.5, 3) and (5, 3) share y = 3. What is the distance between them?

    1. A0.5 units
    2. B1.5 units
    3. C8 units
    4. D9.5 units

    Hint Which coordinate is the same? Which differs?

    Answer: D — 9.5 units

    Why: They differ only in x: |5 − (−4.5)| = 5 + 4.5 = 9.5 units.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Vertices (5, 3) and (5, -3.75) share x = 5. What is the distance between them?

    1. A0.75 units
    2. B5 units
    3. C6.75 units
    4. D8.75 units

    Hint Which coordinate differs?

    Answer: C — 6.75 units

    Why: They differ only in y: |3 − (−3.75)| = 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is the perimeter of the rectangle with side lengths 9.5 and 6.75 units?

    1. A16.25 units
    2. B26 units
    3. C32.5 units
    4. D64.125 units

    Hint A rectangle has four sides.

    Answer: C — 32.5 units

    Why: Perimeter = 2(9.5) + 2(6.75) = 19 + 13.5 = 32.5 units.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

  4. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Why are two of the terms multiplied by 2 in the perimeter calculation?

    1. ABecause perimeter always doubles the area
    2. BBecause there are two coordinates in each ordered pair
    3. CIt is a rounding rule
    4. DA rectangle's opposite sides are equal, so each length appears on two sides

    Hint Walk the rectangle: how many 9.5 sides?

    Answer: D — A rectangle's opposite sides are equal, so each length appears on two sides

    Why: The two 9.5 sides and the two 6.75 sides each come in pairs — that is what the 2 counts.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

  5. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    To draw a polygon from a list of vertices, you:

    1. APlot only the first vertex
    2. BConnect the points in any random order
    3. CAdd all the coordinates together
    4. DPlot each ordered pair, then connect them in order with segments

    Hint What did the deck say to do first?

    Answer: D — Plot each ordered pair, then connect them in order with segments

    Why: Graph the points on the plane and connect them in order — the closed figure that appears is the polygon.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Answer in complete sentences.

    A square has vertices (1, 2), (6, 2), (6, -3), (1, -3). Find one side length and the perimeter.

    Sentence starter The vertices ___ and ___ share ___ .
    So one side is ___ , and the perimeter is ___ .

    Sample answer: From (1, 2) to (6, 2): |6 − 1| = 5 units. A square has four equal sides, so the perimeter is 4 × 5 = 20 units.

Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can draw polygons from their vertex coordinates on the coordinate plane, and use coordinates to find side lengths and solve perimeter and area problems.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Plot the vertices of rectangle PQRS, then use the coordinates to find its perimeter.

    Answer: P(1, 2), Q(6, 2), R(6, 5), S(1, 5)

    Why: P and Q share y = 2, so PQ = 6 − 1 = 5 units; Q and R share x = 6, so QR = 5 − 2 = 3 units. The rectangle is 5 by 3, giving perimeter 2 × (5 + 3) = 16 units and area 5 × 3 = 15 square units — all read straight off the coordinates.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Maria is choosing between two store spaces on a grid where each unit is 1 foot and each grid line is 5 feet apart. Space A is a rectangle 25 ft by 40 ft; Space B is 30 ft by 30 ft. Which has more floor area?

    1. ASpace A — 1,000 sq ft vs 900 sq ft
    2. BSpace B — 900 sq ft vs 850 sq ft
    3. CThey are equal
    4. DSpace B — because a square always beats a rectangle

    Answer: A — Space A — 1,000 sq ft vs 900 sq ft

    Why: A: 25 × 40 = 1,000 sq ft. B: 30 × 30 = 900 sq ft. Space A has 100 more square feet.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A square has vertices (a, b) and (a, b + s) on one side. Which operation gives the side length for ANY values?

    1. AAdd all four coordinates
    2. BSubtract the y-coordinates and take the absolute value
    3. CMultiply a × b
    4. DAverage the coordinates

    Answer: B — Subtract the y-coordinates and take the absolute value

    Why: Same x means the side is vertical, and |(b + s) − b| = |s| — subtraction with absolute value works for every case.

    Watch for: Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Answer in complete sentences.

    The park design used width from coordinates, then length from the area, then the scale to place the missing vertices. Design your own version: pick an area and a scale, mark two vertices, and show where the other two go.

    Sample answer: My park has area 60,000 sq ft with each unit = 100 ft. I mark vertices at (1, 5) and (3, 5) — 2 units apart, so width 200 ft. Length = 60,000 ÷ 200 = 300 ft = 3 units, so the other vertices go at (1, 2) and (3, 2), 3 units below.

  2. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Check the deck's perimeter of 32.5 units a DIFFERENT way than adding the four sides in order.

    Sample answer: Instead of walking the sides, I can use the formula P = 2(l + w): 2(9.5 + 6.75) = 2(16.25) = 32.5 units. Both methods must agree, which is what makes it a check.
