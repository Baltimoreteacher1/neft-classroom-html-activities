Warm-up

The shape is a rectangle, so the opposite sides match: two sides of 9.5 and two of 6.75.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Polygons on the Coordinate Plane. 1. Plot all given vertices and connect them in order to form the polygon. 2. Find side lengths by counting grid units or using coordinate distance formulas. 3. Perimeter = Sum of all side lengths; Area = Use standard polygon formulas

I do:

  1. (-4.5, 3) and (5, 3) share y = 3, so the side between them is horizontal.
  2. Its length is the distance between the x-coordinates: |5 − (−4.5)| = 9.5 units.
  3. (5, 3) and (5, -3.75) share x = 5, so that side is vertical: |3 − (−3.75)| = 6.75 units.

You do: A square has vertices (1, 2), (6, 2), (6, -3), (1, -3). Find one side length from the coordinates, then the perimeter. Say which pair of vertices you used and why their shared coordinate mattered.

Common misconception

Subtracting coordinates without the absolute value — from y = 3 down to y = −3.75 the distance is 3 + 3.75 = 6.75 units, not 3.75 − 3 = 0.75. Distances that cross zero are the whole reason the absolute value is there.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: vertex, polygon, perimeter, distance between vertices, scale (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

A rectangle has vertices (-1, 4), (5, 4), (5, 1), (-1, 1). What is its perimeter?

  • 18 units ✓ correct
  • 9 units
  • 6 units
  • 18 square units

Width |5 − (−1)| = 6, height |4 − 1| = 3. Perimeter = 2(6) + 2(3) = 18 units.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension
