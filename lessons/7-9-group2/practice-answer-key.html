Where we left off

Our goal: I can reflect points across the x-axis and y-axis on the coordinate plane, explain why the method works, and use it on a problem I have not seen before.

The big idea: Reflect over the x-axis: the y-coordinate changes sign. Reflect over the y-axis: the x-coordinate changes sign — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. I will reflect (3, 2) over the y-axis.
  2. The y-axis is the vertical line, so the point flips left or right. That changes the x-coordinate.
  3. The x changes sign: 3 becomes -3. The y stays the same: 2 stays 2.
  4. So the reflection of (3, 2) over the y-axis is (-3, 2).

  1. 5

    Think it throughWhere is the ship after reflecting (3, 2) over the y-axis?

    1. A(2, 3)
    2. B(−3, 2)
    3. C(3, −2)
    4. D(−3, −2)

    Why is that the answer?

    The y-axis is a vertical mirror, so only the x-coordinate flips sign: (3, 2) → (−3, 2).

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhere is the ship after reflecting (3, 2) over the x-axis?

    1. A(2, 3)
    2. B(3, −2)
    3. C(−3, 2)
    4. D(−3, −2)

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    The x-axis is a horizontal mirror, so only the y-coordinate flips sign: (3, 2) → (3, −2).

  3. 7

    Think it throughReflecting over the y-axis changes which coordinate?

    1. AOnly the y-coordinate
    2. BOnly the x-coordinate
    3. CBoth coordinates
    4. DNeither coordinate

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    The y-axis is a vertical line. Reflecting across it moves a point left-to-right. So the horizontal position, x, flips. The height, y, stays the same.

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • Reflecting across an axis keeps that coordinate and flips the ___ one. → Reflect Points Across Axes

  • The vertical number line on the coordinate plane is the ___. → y-axis

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Reflect Points Across Axes is flipping the sign of the wrong coordinate — changing the x-coordinate when reflecting over the x-axis, instead of the y-coordinate (or vice versa for the y-axis). For example, to reflect (3, -5) over the x-axis, a student might flip the x-coordinate and write (-3, -5), but reflecting over the x-axis only changes the sign of the y-coordinate: (3, -5) becomes (3, 5). Before you submit, ask: 'Did I flip the sign of the coordinate that matches the axis I'm reflecting over — y for the x-axis, x for the y-axis?'

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    From Lesson 7.8Explain your thinking — A point has a negative x-coordinate and a negative y-coordinate. Which quadrant is it in?

    1. AQuadrant III
    2. BQuadrant I
    3. CQuadrant II
    4. DQuadrant IV

    How do you know?

    Quadrant III is where both x and y are negative. This means the point is left of the origin and below the origin.

    3. CThe library (−3, 4)
    4. DThe hospital (−4, −1)

    How do you know?

    Quadrant III is where BOTH coordinates are negative — the lower-left region. The hospital at (−4, −1) is the only one.
