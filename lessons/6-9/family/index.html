Unit 6, Lesson 9: Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions

6.NOS.1

What students are learning

Key idea: Dividing a whole number by a fraction asks how many of those pieces fit. 4 ÷ 1/2 means: how many half-pound bags can 4 pounds fill? Each pound fills 2 bags, so 4 × 2 = 8 bags. Check: 8 halves make 4.
Idea clave: Dividir un número entero entre una fracción pregunta cuántas de esas piezas caben. 4 ÷ 1/2 significa: ¿cuántas bolsas de media libra se llenan con 4 libras? Cada libra llena 2 bolsas, así que 4 × 2 = 8 bolsas. Comprueba: 8 medios forman 4.

Dividing a whole number by a fraction asks how many of those pieces fit. 4 ÷ 1/2 means: how many half-pound bags can 4 pounds fill? Each pound fills 2 bags, so 4 × 2 = 8 bags. Check: 8 halves make 4.

  • Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions — Find how many fractional groups fit in a whole number by multiplying by the reciprocal.
  • Whole Number — A counting number with no fraction or decimal, like 0, 1, 2, 3.
  • Fraction — A number that shows part of a whole, like 3/4.
  • Reciprocal — A fraction turned upside down.
  • Keep, Change, Flip — A shortcut for dividing fractions: keep the first number, change ÷ to ×, flip the second. It works because dividing by a fraction gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal.
  • Quotient — The answer when you divide.

How we did it in class today

  1. Agent Park has 4 pounds of powder and each bag holds 1/2 pound. I want 4 ÷ 1/2.
  2. Each pound fills 2 half-pound bags, so 4 pounds fill 4 × 2 = 8 bags.
  3. That is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal of 1/2: 4 × 2/1 = 8. Dividing by 1/2 doubles the count, because every whole holds 2 halves.
  4. Check by multiplying: 8 bags × 1/2 pound = 4 pounds. So 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8. He can fill 8 bags.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
