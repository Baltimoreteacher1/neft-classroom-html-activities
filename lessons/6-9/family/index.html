What students are learning

Dividing a whole number by a fraction asks how many of those pieces fit, or how much is in one whole. 4 ÷ 1/2 counts halves: every whole holds 2 halves, so the answer is 4 × 2 = 8. That is why the shortcut called Keep, Change, Flip works — dividing by a fraction gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal. Check by multiplying: 8 × 1/2 = 4.

Key idea: Dividing a whole number by a fraction asks how many of those pieces fit. 4 ÷ 1/2 means: how many half-pound bags can 4 pounds fill? Each pound fills 2 bags, so 4 × 2 = 8 bags. Check: 8 halves make 4.
Idea clave: Dividir un número entero entre una fracción pregunta cuántas de esas piezas caben. 4 ÷ 1/2 significa: ¿cuántas bolsas de media libra se llenan con 4 libras? Cada libra llena 2 bolsas, así que 4 × 2 = 8 bolsas. Comprueba: 8 medios forman 4.

Example

  1. Agent Park has 4 pounds of powder and each bag holds 1/2 pound. I want 4 ÷ 1/2.
  2. Each pound fills 2 half-pound bags, so 4 pounds fill 4 × 2 = 8 bags.
  3. That is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal of 1/2: 4 × 2/1 = 8. Dividing by 1/2 doubles the count, because every whole holds 2 halves.
  4. Check by multiplying: 8 bags × 1/2 pound = 4 pounds. So 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8. He can fill 8 bags.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?
