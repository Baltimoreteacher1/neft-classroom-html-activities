Lesson 6.9Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can divide a whole number by a fraction by multiplying by the reciprocal.

Start here pageNow you try — model solution

  1. Change ÷ to ×, then flip 1/4 to 4/1, which is 4.
  2. Multiply: 3 × 4 = 12. So 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12.
Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Mark and label each value on the number line.

    How many jumps of 1/4 does it take to get from 0 to 2?

    Hint Each jump is exactly 1/4, and your goal is to land right on 2.

    Why: Dividing 2 by 1/4 asks how many 1/4-size jumps fill 2 wholes. Each whole holds 4 fourths, so 2 wholes hold 2 × 4 = 8 of them, and the eighth jump lands exactly on 2. That is why 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8, an answer larger than the 2 you started with.

Part 2Practice the skillshow every step
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 6 ÷ 1/3?

    1. A2
    2. B3
    3. C6/3
    4. D18

    Hint 6 ÷ 1/3 asks how many 1/3-size pieces fit into 6 wholes. Predict: bigger or smaller than 6?

    Answer: D — 18

    Why: 6 ÷ 1/3 = 6 × 3/1 = 18. There are 18 thirds in 6 wholes.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem4 ÷ 1/5
    2. 2Keep, Change, FlipKept the 4 and changed ÷ to ×, but forgot to flip: 4 × 1/5
    3. 3Multiply4 × 1/5 = 4/5

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint After 'Change,' you still have to 'Flip' the second fraction.

    Mistake: Step 2. The Keep, Change, Flip step changed the ÷ to × but never flipped the divisor, so it multiplied by 1/5 instead of by 5/1. The reciprocal of 1/5 is 5, so 4 ÷ 1/5 = 4 × 5 = 20. That size makes sense too: 4 wholes contain twenty 1/5-size pieces, so the answer has to be bigger than 4, not the 4/5 that was written.

    Correct work: Flip the divisor 1/5 to its reciprocal 5/1 before multiplying: 4 × 5/1 = 20. The mistake was multiplying by 1/5 instead of flipping it to 5. (The answer should be larger than 4, not smaller.)

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Complete the table: Whole Number ÷ Fraction. Use Keep, Change, Flip.

    ExpressionRewrite as MultiplicationQuotient
    5 ÷ 1/4
    8 ÷ 1/2
    3 ÷ 1/6
    7 ÷ 1/3

    Why: Each row keeps the whole number, changes ÷ to ×, and flips the unit fraction, so 5 ÷ 1/4 becomes 5 × 4/1 = 20 and 3 ÷ 1/6 becomes 3 × 6/1 = 18. The pattern the table reveals is that dividing by 1/n is the same as multiplying by n, because every whole contains n of those pieces.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each division expression to its quotient.

    1. A4 ÷ 1/3
    2. B7 ÷ 1/2
    3. C5 ÷ 1/6
    4. D2 ÷ 1/8
    5. E9 ÷ 1/4
    6. F6 ÷ 1/5
    • A12
    • B14
    • C30
    • D16
    • E36
    • F30

    Why: Dividing by a unit fraction multiplies by its denominator, since each whole holds that many pieces: 4 ÷ 1/3 = 4 × 3 = 12 and 9 ÷ 1/4 = 9 × 4 = 36. Two different expressions can share a quotient — 5 ÷ 1/6 and 6 ÷ 1/5 both equal 30 — so compute every card instead of matching by size alone.

  3. 3Use the model to solve. Show your work.

    How many groups of 2/3 fit into 3?

    Answer: 4 1/2

    Why: Three wholes cut into thirds give 9 one-third pieces, and each group needs 2 of them. Pairing the 9 thirds makes 4 full groups with 1 third left over, and that leftover third is half of a 2/3 group. So 3 ÷ 2/3 = 4 1/2.

Part 2Practice the skillshow every step
  1. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    What is 6 ÷ 2/3?

    1. A2/18
    2. B4
    3. C9
    4. D12

    Answer: C — 9

    Why: 6 ÷ 2/3 = 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9. Keep 6, change ÷ to ×, flip 2/3 to 3/2.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem8 ÷ 3/4
    2. 2Keep, Change, FlipFlipped the wrong number — wrote 1/8 × 3/4 instead of 8 × 4/3
    3. 3Multiply1/8 × 3/4 = 3/32

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. Only the divisor gets flipped. The Keep, Change, Flip step turned the dividend 8 into 1/8 and left 3/4 alone, which is backwards; the correct rewrite is 8 × 4/3 = 32/3 = 10 2/3. Since 3/4 is less than 1, more than 8 of those pieces fit into 8 wholes, so a tiny answer like 3/32 cannot be right.

    Correct work: Keep the dividend 8 the same and flip only the divisor: 8 × 4/3 = 32/3 = 10 2/3. The mistake was flipping the whole number 8 into 1/8 instead of flipping the divisor 3/4 into 4/3.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Compare: what happens when you divide the same whole number by different fractions?

    ExpressionQuotientObservation
    12 ÷ 1/2
    12 ÷ 1/3
    12 ÷ 1/4
    12 ÷ 2/3
    12 ÷ 3/4

    Why: The dividend stays 12 while the divisor changes, and smaller pieces mean more of them fit: 12 ÷ 1/2 = 24, 12 ÷ 1/3 = 36, and 12 ÷ 1/4 = 48. Larger divisors reverse the trend — 12 ÷ 2/3 = 18 and 12 ÷ 3/4 = 16 — because each piece takes up more of a whole.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Division Error

    1. 1Problem6 ÷ 2/3
    2. 2Keep, Change, Flip6 × 2/3
    3. 3Multiply12/3
    4. 4Simplify4

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The Keep, Change, Flip step kept the divisor as 2/3 instead of flipping it to its reciprocal 3/2. The correct work is 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9. Nine also makes sense as a count: each whole holds one and a half 2/3-size pieces, so 6 wholes hold 9 of them — more than the 6 you started with, not less.

    Correct work: 6 × 3/2 (flip the divisor 2/3 to get 3/2, not keep it as 2/3). Then 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A pizza shop has 8 whole pizzas. Each serving is 2/3 of a pizza. How many servings can they make? Write and solve an equation, then explain why the answer is greater than 8.

    Look for: 12, 8 × 3/2, greater, less than 1, servings, reciprocal

    Why: The question asks how many 2/3-pizza servings fit in 8 pizzas, so the equation is 8 ÷ 2/3. Keep, change, flip gives 8 × 3/2 = 24/2 = 12 servings. The count beats 8 because each serving is smaller than a whole pizza, so every pizza makes more than one serving.
