Unit 6, Lesson 9: Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions6.NOS.1

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can explain what dividing a whole number by a fraction means — how many pieces fit, or how much is in one whole — find the quotient with a model or by multiplying by the reciprocal, and check it by multiplying.

Language: I can explain why multiplying by the reciprocal works, using the words fraction, reciprocal, quotient, and Keep, Change, Flip.

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below before the lesson.

Warm-up

Let's try 3 ÷ 1/4 together. How many 1/4s fit in 3? Each whole holds 4 fourths.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions. Formula: a ÷ (b/c) = a × (c/b). 1. Name the question: 4 ÷ 1/2 asks how many 1/2s fit in 4. 2. Count with a model: each whole holds 2 halves, so 4 wholes hold 4 × 2 = 8 halves. 3. Shortcut: multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor, 6 ÷ 3/4 = 6 × 4/3 = 8, then check by multiplying: 8 × 3/4 = 6

I do:

  1. Agent Park has 4 pounds of powder and each bag holds 1/2 pound. I want 4 ÷ 1/2.
  2. Each pound fills 2 half-pound bags, so 4 pounds fill 4 × 2 = 8 bags.
  3. That is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal of 1/2: 4 × 2/1 = 8. Dividing by 1/2 doubles the count, because every whole holds 2 halves.
  4. Check by multiplying: 8 bags × 1/2 pound = 4 pounds. So 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8. He can fill 8 bags.

You do: Now use the number line to show how many 1/4-jumps fit into 3 — a new problem, not the 4 ÷ 1/2 we just watched. Then try one that asks how much is in ONE whole: 6 cups of rice fill 3/4 of a pot. 6 ÷ 3/4 = 6 × 4/3 = 8 cups fill the whole pot, and 3/4 × 8 = 6 checks it.

Common misconception

A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions, Whole Number, Fraction, Reciprocal, Keep, Change, Flip, Quotient (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

What is 9 ÷ 1/3?

  • 1/27
  • 3
  • 9/3
  • 27 ✓ correct

9 ÷ 1/3 = 9 × 3/1 = 27. There are 27 thirds in 9 wholes. Check by multiplying: 27 × 1/3 = 9.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

A detective has 10 feet of rope to section off a crime scene. Each section needs to be 1/4 of a foot long for detailed evidence markers. How many markers can the detective set up?
