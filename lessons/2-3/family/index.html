Unit 2, Lesson 3: Describe the Data Using the Median6.DS.4

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

Mean, median, and mode are three ways to find the center, or "typical" value, of a data set. The mean is the average, the median is the middle, and the mode is the most common value.

Key idea: Measures of Center: Median. Formula: Median = Middle value in an ordered set. 1. Order all data values from least to greatest. 2. Odd count: The median is the exact center number. 3. Even count: The median is the mean of the two middle numbers

Key words

  • Describe the Data Using the Median — Using the middle value of the ordered data to say what is typical for the whole set.
    Español: Describir los datos con la mediana — Usar el valor del medio de los datos ordenados para decir qué es típico en todo el conjunto.
  • Median — The middle number when you put them in order.
    Español: Mediana — El número del medio cuando los pones en orden.
  • Mode — The number that shows up most often.
    Español: Moda — El número que aparece más veces.
  • Outlier — A number that is much bigger or smaller than the rest.
    Español: Valor atípico — Un número mucho mayor o menor que los demás.
  • Data distribution — How the data looks: where it sits and how spread out it is.
    Español: Distribución de datos — Cómo se ven los datos: dónde están y qué tan separados están.
  • Variability — How spread out the numbers are.
    Español: Variabilidad — Qué tan separados están los números.

Example

  1. My data set: 2, 4, 4, 6, 9 — five values, already in order. Ordering first is what makes a middle exist at all.
  2. With 5 values, the middle is the 3rd one. Counting in from both ends: 2 and 9 pair off, 4 and 6 pair off, and 4 is left alone.
  3. So the median is 4. Half the values sit at or below it and half at or above it.
  4. Now an even set: 2, 4, 6, 9. There is no single middle, so I take the mean of the two middle values: (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5. The median is 5, even though 5 is not in the data.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Mean, Median, and Mode is finding the median without ordering the data first — students pick the middle number straight from the list as given (for 9, 4, 7, 2, 6 they say the median is 7, the middle position in that original order) instead of sorting least to greatest (2, 4, 6, 7, 9) and finding the true middle value, 6. Before finalizing a median, always reorder the data smallest to largest, then count to the middle position — and if there is an even number of values, average the two middle numbers.
