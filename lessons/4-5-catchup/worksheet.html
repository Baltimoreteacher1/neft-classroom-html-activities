  1. 1

    (Lesson 4.4) What is 20% of 150?

    1. A30
    2. B20
    3. C35
    4. D75

    20% = 0.20. Multiply: 0.20 × 150 = 30.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Use Percent to Solve Problems is combining the discount and tax into one single percent change instead of applying them one after another. For a $40 item with a 25% discount and 8% sales tax, a student might compute 40 × (1 − 0.25 + 0.08) = 40 × 0.83 = $33.20, treating the tax as if it applies to the original price. But tax is charged on the discounted price, not the original price: first take 25% off $40 to get $40 × 0.75 = $30, then add 8% tax on that $30: $30 × 1.08 = $32.40. The correct final price is $32.40, not $33.20 — always compute the discount first, then apply tax to the new lower price.

    1. A42
    2. B50
    3. C34

  1. 1

    (Lesson 4.4) Find the Percent Error

    1. 1Find 30% of 9030% of 90
    2. 2Convert percent to decimal30% = 3.0
    3. 3Multiply3.0 × 90 = 270
    4. 4Final answer30% of 90 = 270

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 30% = 0.30 (divide by 100, not by 10). 0.30 × 90 = 27. The correct answer is 27. The conversion step is where it goes wrong: the student moved the decimal only one place and wrote 30% = 3.0. A percent is divided by 100, which moves the decimal two places, so 30% = 0.30 and 0.30 × 90 = 27.

  2. 2

    (Lesson 4.5) Find the Discount Error

    1. 1A prize costs 160 tickets at 25% off. Find the sale price.160 tickets, 25% off
    2. 2Find 25% of 16025 × 160 = 4,000
    3. 3Subtract discount160 − 4,000 = −3,840
    4. 4Final answerSale price is −3,840 tickets

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 25% = 0.25 (not 25). 0.25 × 160 = 40. Sale price: 160 − 40 = 120 tickets. The "Find 25% of 160" step multiplies by 25 instead of by 0.25, which is 100 times too big. Rewrite the percent as a decimal first: 25% = 0.25, so the discount is 0.25 × 160 = 40 tickets and the sale price is 160 − 40 = 120 tickets. A negative sale price is a signal that the percent was never converted.
