Unit 6, Lesson 10: Divide Mixed Numbers6.NOS.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A mixed number, like 3 1/2, has a whole part and a fraction part. Before you can divide, you change each mixed number into an improper fraction (top heavier than bottom). Then ask how many of the divisor fit — with common denominators or by multiplying by the reciprocal — and check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor.

Key idea: To divide with mixed numbers, first rewrite them as fractions. 3 1/2 becomes 7/2. Then 7/2 ÷ 1/4 asks how many quarters fit in 3 1/2. There are 14, because 14 quarters make 3 1/2.

Example

  1. Agent Chen has a 3 1/2-mile route cut into 1/4-mile segments. I want 3 1/2 ÷ 1/4.
  2. First I convert 3 1/2. Multiply the whole by the bottom: 3 × 2 = 6. Add the top: 6 + 1 = 7. So 3 1/2 = 7/2.
  3. Now I ask how many 1/4s fit in 7/2. In fourths, 7/2 = 14/4, and 14 fourths ÷ 1 fourth = 14 — the common-denominator method.
  4. The reciprocal shortcut gives the same answer: 7/2 × 4/1 = 28/2 = 14.
  5. Check by multiplying: 14 × 1/4 = 14/4 = 3 1/2. So 3 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 14 segments.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Divide Mixed Numbers is dividing the whole-number parts and fraction parts separately instead of converting first — for example, treating 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/2 as (4 ÷ 1) + (1/2 ÷ 1/2) = 5. Always convert each mixed number to one improper fraction first, then divide using the reciprocal shortcut: 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/2 = 9/2 ÷ 3/2 = 9/2 × 2/3 = 3.
