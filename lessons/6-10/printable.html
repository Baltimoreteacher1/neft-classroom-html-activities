🚀 Launch

Agent Chen is mapping a suspect's route. The total distance is 3 1/2 miles, and each segment between landmarks is 1/4 mile. She needs to know how many segments are on the route to place surveillance cameras at every landmark.

How do I divide with mixed numbers?

A mixed number, like 3 1/2, has a whole part and a fraction part. Before you can divide, you change each mixed number into an improper fraction (top heavier than bottom). Then ask how many of the divisor fit — with common denominators or by multiplying by the reciprocal — and check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor.

Key idea: Dividing Mixed Numbers. 1. Convert each mixed number to an improper fraction: 3 1/2 = 7/2. 2. Name the question and divide: 7/2 ÷ 1/4 asks how many 1/4s fit in 7/2. With common denominators, 7/2 = 14/4, and 14 fourths ÷ 1 fourth = 14, or multiply by the reciprocal: 7/2 × 4/1 = 28/2 = 14. 3. Simplify, then check by multiplying: 14 × 1/4 = 14/4 = 3 1/2

Watch me

  • Agent Chen has a 3 1/2-mile route cut into 1/4-mile segments. I want 3 1/2 ÷ 1/4.
  • First I convert 3 1/2. Multiply the whole by the bottom: 3 × 2 = 6. Add the top: 6 + 1 = 7. So 3 1/2 = 7/2.
  • Now I ask how many 1/4s fit in 7/2. In fourths, 7/2 = 14/4, and 14 fourths ÷ 1 fourth = 14 — the common-denominator method.
  • The reciprocal shortcut gives the same answer: 7/2 × 4/1 = 28/2 = 14.
  • Check by multiplying: 14 × 1/4 = 14/4 = 3 1/2. So 3 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 14 segments.

Let's try together

  • Let's try 2 1/2 ÷ 1/2. First convert 2 1/2: 2 × 2 = 4, plus 1 = 5, so 2 1/2 = 5/2.
  • How many halves fit in 5/2? Five — the halves are already counted: 5/2 ÷ 1/2 = 5. The reciprocal shortcut agrees: 5/2 × 2/1 = 10/2 = 5.
  • Check by multiplying: 5 × 1/2 = 5/2 = 2 1/2. So 2 1/2 ÷ 1/2 = 5.

Now it's your turn

  • Now fill in the table: convert each mixed number to an improper fraction, divide by multiplying by the reciprocal, then check each quotient by multiplying it by the divisor.
