Unit 2, Lesson 10: Choose Appropriate Measures

Standard 6.DS.6d

What students are learning Qué aprenden

Key idea: If the data has no outliers (numbers far from the rest), use the mean and the mean absolute deviation (MAD) to describe it. If the data has outliers or leans to one side, use the median and the interquartile range (IQR), because an outlier pulls the mean away from most of the numbers. To compare two data sets, look at both the typical value and the spread.
Idea clave: Si los datos no tienen valores atípicos (números muy lejos del resto), usa la media y la desviación media absoluta para describirlos. Si los datos tienen valores atípicos o están cargados hacia un lado, usa la mediana y el rango intercuartílico, porque un valor atípico jala la media lejos de la mayoría de los números. Para comparar dos conjuntos de datos, mira tanto el valor típico como la dispersión.

How we did it in class today Cómo lo hicimos hoy en clase

  1. My data, the top scorer's points: 22, 24, 20, 25, 23, 21, 58. I notice 58 is much bigger than the rest, so it is an outlier.
  2. Mean: 22 + 24 + 20 + 25 + 23 + 21 + 58 = 193, then 193 ÷ 7 ≈ 27.6.
  3. Median: in order they are 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 58. The middle (4th) value is 23.
  4. The mean (27.6) is higher than 6 of the 7 games because 58 pulled it up. So the median (23) better shows a typical game.

If they get stuck... Si se traban...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Appropriate Measures is defaulting to the mean just because "it uses all the data," without first checking whether an outlier or skewed shape is present. For example, given a player's points per game 14, 16, 15, 17, 16, 58, a student calculates the mean (136 ÷ 6 ≈ 22.7) and reports that as the typical game — but 58 is far from the cluster of 14–17, so it pulls the mean above 5 of the 6 games. The median (16) actually represents a typical game here. Before choosing a measure, always scan the data for an outlier or a long tail first: only use the mean once you've confirmed the data has neither.
