Lesson 2.10Choose Appropriate Measures

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can choose the best measure of center for a data set based on its shape.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each data description with the best measure of center.

    1. ATest scores: 88, 90, 85, 92, 87 (no outliers)
    2. BSalaries: $30K, $32K, $35K, $31K, $250K
    3. CRace times: 12.1s, 12.3s, 12.0s, 12.2s, 12.1s
    4. DGoals scored: 1, 2, 1, 0, 2, 1, 15
    • AMean
    • BMedian
    • CMean
    • DMedian

    Hint Scan each data list for a value that's wildly different from its neighbors — like one huge salary or one giant goal count.

    Why: The shape of the data decides the measure. Symmetric data with no outliers is summarized well by the mean, data with an outlier or a long tail is summarized better by the median, and data made of categories leaves only the mode.

  2. 2Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each feature by which measure of center it supports.

    Groups: Use Mean Use Median
    • Use MeanNo outliers in the data
    • Use MeanData is symmetric
    • Use MeanAll values are close together
    • Use MedianData has one or more outliers
    • Use MedianData is skewed to one side
    • Use MedianOne extreme value pulls the average

    Hint The mean is sensitive to extreme values — outliers pull it up or down.

    Why: The mean uses every value, so a single extreme number drags it toward itself, while the median is just the middle of the ordered list and barely moves. That is why "no outliers," "symmetric," and "all values close together" point to the mean, and "has outliers," "skewed to one side," and "one extreme value pulls the average" point to the median.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A gymnast's scores are: 8.5, 8.8, 8.7, 8.6, 8.9. There are no outliers. Which measure best represents a typical score?

    1. AMedian
    2. BNeither — the data is too small
    3. CBoth are equally bad
    4. DMean

    Hint Look at the scores: 8.5 up to 8.9 — all five are bunched tightly together with no unusual value.

    Answer: D — Mean

    Why: The data is symmetric with no outliers, so the mean (8.7) best represents the typical score.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Appropriate Measures is defaulting to the mean just because "it uses all the data," without first checking whether an outlier or skewed shape is present. For example, given a player's points per game 14, 16, 15, 17, 16, 58, a student calculates the mean (136 ÷ 6 ≈ 22.7) and reports that as the typical game — but 58 is far from the cluster of 14–17, so it pulls the mean above 5 of the 6 games. The median (16) actually represents a typical game here. Before choosing a measure, always scan the data for an outlier or a long tail first: only use the mean once you've confirmed the data has neither.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake — Forgetting to Order the Data

    1. 1DataA swimmer's lap counts: 9, 12, 8, 11, 10 (no outliers).
    2. 2Find the medianThe student keeps the numbers as written (9, 12, 8, 11, 10) and picks the middle one: median = 8.
    3. 3Choose a measureThe student reports the median (8) as the typical lap count.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint The median is the MIDDLE value only after the numbers are sorted from least to greatest.

    Mistake: Step 2. The median is the middle value only after the data is sorted. Ordering 9, 12, 8, 11, 10 gives 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, so the median is 10 — the 8 that was reported is actually the smallest value in the set. With no outliers here, the mean is also 10, so either measure describes a typical lap count.

    Correct work: You must put the data in order before finding the median. In order: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. The middle value is 10, so the median is 10 (not 8). Since there are no outliers, the mean (10) and median (10) agree, and either is a fine measure of a typical lap.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Mistake — Choosing the Mean Despite an Outlier

    1. 1DataA receiver's catches per game: 12, 14, 13, 15, 11, 60 (the 60 was a record-setting playoff game).
    2. 2Find the meanSum = 125, Count = 6, Mean = 125 ÷ 6 ≈ 20.8.
    3. 3Find the medianOrdered: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 60 → Median = (13 + 14) ÷ 2 = 13.5.
    4. 4Choose a measureThe student reports the mean (≈ 20.8) as the typical number of catches because it uses every game.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. Using every value is exactly the problem here: the 60-catch playoff game pulls the mean up to about 20.8, which is higher than five of the six games. The median 13.5 sits inside the cluster of 11 to 15 and is not moved by that extreme value, so it is the better report of a typical game.

    Correct work: The student should report the median (13.5), not the mean. The 60-catch game is an outlier that pulls the mean up to about 20.8 — higher than 5 of the 6 games. The median (13.5) is resistant to that outlier and better represents a typical game.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Reasoning Error

    1. 1DataPlayer minutes per game: 32, 34, 30, 35, 33, 5
    2. 2Find meanSum = 169, Count = 6, Mean = 28.2
    3. 3Find medianOrdered: 5, 30, 32, 33, 34, 35 → Median = (32+33) ÷ 2 = 32.5
    4. 4ConclusionThe mean (28.2) is the best measure because it uses all the data.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The value 5 is an outlier from an injury game, and it drags the mean down to 28.2 — lower than every healthy game in the set. The median 32.5 sits among the 30-to-35 minute games, so it describes typical minutes far better. Using all the data is not an advantage when one value is that extreme.

    Correct work: The median (32.5) is the better measure. The value 5 is an outlier (the player was injured that game) that pulls the mean down to 28.2, which is lower than any of the player's healthy games. The median (32.5) better represents typical minutes played.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A local newspaper reports that the average home price in a neighborhood is $450,000. The actual prices are: $180K, $200K, $190K, $210K, $195K, $1,725K. Is the reporter's claim misleading? Calculate the mean and median and explain which better represents a 'typical' home price.

    Look for: mean, median, outlier, misleading, pulls, higher, typical, skewed

    Why: The six prices add to $2,700K, so the mean is 2,700 ÷ 6 = $450K, matching the reporter — but the median is the average of the middle two prices, $195K and $200K, which is $197.5K. Five of the six homes cost between $180K and $210K, so the $1,725K home is an outlier pulling the mean far above what most homes cost. The claim is misleading, and the median better represents a typical price.
