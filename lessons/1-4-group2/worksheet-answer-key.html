  1. Give a reason with numbers for the jumbo boxes. (800 cubic inches is more than 720 cubic inches.)
  2. Give two reasons with words for the jumbo boxes. (Less packaging, and two boxes are easier to store than three.)
  3. Give a reason for the 3 large boxes. (The cereal stays fresher, because each box is open for less time.)
  4. Why does more volume not always mean more cereal? (Volume measures the space inside the box. A box may not be full of cereal.)
  1. 1Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Ana claims, “A longer box always holds more.” Box A is 12 in long, 2 in wide, and 5 in tall. Box B is 6 in long, 5 in wide, and 5 in tall. Is Ana right?

    1. 1Find the volume of Box A.120 cubic inches
    2. 2Find the volume of Box B.150 cubic inches
    3. 3Box A is longer. Does it hold more?No. 120 < 150.
    Final answer:No. Box A is longer but holds 120 cubic inches. Box B holds 150 cubic inches. This counterexample shows the claim is false.
  2. 2Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Ben claims, “If you double the height of a box, the volume doubles.” Test it with a box that is 3 in by 4 in by 5 in.

    1. 1Find the volume of the box.60 cubic inches
    2. 2Double the height to 10 in. Find the new volume.120 cubic inches
    3. 3Is 120 double 60?Yes. 60 × 2 = 120.
    4. 4One example worked. Why does that not prove “always”?One example only shows one case. One counterexample can break a claim, but one example cannot prove it for every box.
    Final answer:For this box, the volume doubles from 60 to 120 cubic inches. That supports Ben's claim, but one example does not prove it.
  3. 3Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Box C is 2 in by 3 in by 7 in. Box D is 3 in by 3 in by 5 in. How many more cubic inches does Box D hold?

    Final answer:3 cubic inches

    Why: Find the volume of Box C: 2 × 3 × 7 = 42 Find the volume of Box D: 3 × 3 × 5 = 45 Subtract the volumes: 45 − 42 = 3

  4. 4Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Pack A has 3 boxes, each 10 in by 2 in by 9 in. Pack B has 2 boxes, each 9 in by 4 in by 8 in. How many more cubic inches does Pack B hold?

    Final answer:36 cubic inches

    Why: Find the total for Pack A: 10 × 2 × 9 = 180; 180 × 3 = 540 Find the total for Pack B: 9 × 4 × 8 = 288; 288 × 2 = 576 Subtract the totals: 576 − 540 = 36

  5. 5Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Mia claims, “A bigger bottom always means more volume.” Box E: 6 in by 6 in bottom, 3 in tall. Box F: 4 in by 5 in bottom, 10 in tall. How many more cubic inches does Box F hold?

    Final answer:92 cubic inches

    Why: Find the volume of Box E: 6 × 6 = 36; 36 × 3 = 108 Find the volume of Box F: 4 × 5 = 20; 20 × 10 = 200 Subtract the volumes: 200 − 108 = 92

  6. 6Complete each step, then write the final answer.

    Zoe claims, “Two boxes always hold more than one box.” One big box is 10 in by 6 in by 10 in. Two small boxes are each 5 in by 4 in by 10 in. How many more cubic inches does the big box hold?

    Final answer:200 cubic inches

    Why: Find the volume of the big box: 10 × 6 × 10 = 600 Find the total for two small boxes: 5 × 4 × 10 = 200; 200 × 2 = 400 Subtract the totals: 600 − 400 = 200
