📚 Vocabulary

WordWhat it meansExample
Statistical Questions and Data
Preguntas estadísticas y datos		A statistical question expects varied answers that can be collected as data.
Una pregunta estadística espera respuestas variadas que se pueden recopilar como datos.		“How tall is each student in my class?” — Different students give different answers, so the data varies.
Statistical Question
Pregunta estadística		A question where the answers will be different for different people.
Una pregunta cuyas respuestas serán distintas para distintas personas.		How tall are the students in my class? — The answers vary, so data must be collected.
Non-statistical Question
Pregunta no estadística		A question that has just one fixed answer that is the same no matter who you ask, so there is no data to collect.
Una pregunta que tiene una sola respuesta fija, igual sin importar a quién le preguntes, por lo que no hay datos que recolectar.		How many days are in a week? — The answer is always 7 — it never varies, so there is no data to collect.
Data
Datos		Facts and numbers you collect, like answers to a survey.
Datos y números que recoges, como respuestas a una encuesta.		The 20 quiz scores collected from the class — Facts gathered from a group, ready to describe.
Variability
Variabilidad		How spread out the numbers are.
Qué tan separados están los números.		Scores running from 68 to 94 — The values differ, which is what variability measures.
Survey
Encuesta		Collecting facts by asking people questions.
Recoger datos haciendo preguntas a las personas.		Asking every student how many minutes they read last night — Facts collected by asking a group the same question.
Data distribution
Distribución de datos		How the data looks: where it sits and how spread out it is.
Cómo se ven los datos: dónde están y qué tan separados están.		“Most scores cluster near 85, with one down at 68” — It says where the data sits and how it spreads.

🚀 Launch

You've just been hired as the data analyst for a youth basketball league. The head coach hands you a list of questions the staff wants to investigate about their players. Some questions will give you interesting data with variety, while others have just one fixed answer. Your job: figure out which questions are worth investigating with data.

What makes a question a statistical question?

A statistical question is one you answer by collecting data, and the answers are different for different people or cases. A non-statistical question has just one fixed answer.

Key idea: Statistical Questions & Variability. 1. Statistical Question: Expects answers that vary across people, trials, or items. 2. Non-Statistical Question: Has one single fixed answer. 3. Data Collection: Gather varied data points to create a distribution

Watch me

  • Question 1: "How many points did our top player score last night?" There is one player and one game, so there is one fixed answer, like 18 points.
  • One answer, no variety, so this is NOT a statistical question.
  • Question 2: "How many points does each player score per game?" The answers vary: 8, 12, 12, 15, 18, 20, 22.
  • Because the answers are different for different players, I must collect data. This IS a statistical question.

Let's try together

  • New question: "How many pets does each student in our class have?" Will every student give the same answer, or different answers?
  • Some students have 0 pets and others have 2 or 3, so the answers vary. Is this statistical or not statistical?
  • Now a different question: "How many days are in one week?" Does this have one fixed answer or many?

Now it's your turn

  • Next, you will sort questions into Statistical or Not Statistical, just like I did.
  • For each one, ask yourself: will the answers be different, or always the same?
