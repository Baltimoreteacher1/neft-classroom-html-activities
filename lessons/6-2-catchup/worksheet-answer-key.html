    (Lesson 6.1) What does 3 ÷ 1/4 mean?

    1. AHow many 1/4-size pieces fit into 3
    2. B3 groups of 1/4
    3. C3 minus 1/4
    4. D1/4 of 3

    3 ÷ 1/4 asks: how many 1/4-size pieces fit into 3? The answer is 12, because each whole has 4 fourths, and 3 × 4 = 12.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

    (Lesson 6.1) A shelf is 5 feet long. Books are each 1/4 foot wide. How many books fit on the shelf? Which expression represents this?

    1. A5 ÷ 1/4 = 20 books
    2. B5 × 1/4 = 1 1/4 books
    3. C1/4 ÷ 5 = 1/20 book
    4. D5 + 1/4 = 5 1/4 books

    You are finding how many 1/4-foot pieces fit into 5 feet, which is 5 ÷ 1/4 = 5 × 4 = 20 books.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

  2. 2

    (Lesson 6.1) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1ProblemHow many 1/5-cup scoops fill a 6-cup container? Find 6 ÷ 1/5.
    2. 2Student writesI will flip the first number instead of the divisor: 6 ÷ 1/5 = 1/6 × 5 = 5/6 scoop.
    3. 3Student's answer5/6 of a scoop

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the DIVISOR (1/5), not the dividend. The reciprocal of 1/5 is 5, so 6 ÷ 1/5 = 6 × 5 = 30 scoops. In the "Student writes" step the reciprocal was taken of the dividend 6 instead of the divisor 1/5. Only the divisor gets flipped: the reciprocal of 1/5 is 5, so 6 ÷ 1/5 = 6 × 5 = 30 scoops. That matches the story too — each cup holds 5 one-fifth scoops, and there are 6 cups.

    1. A4 scoops
    2. B3 scoops
    3. C6 scoops
    4. D2 scoops

    2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/3 × 6/1 = 12/3 = 4 scoops.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?

  4. 4

    (Lesson 6.2) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem2/3 ÷ 1/2
    2. 2Change ÷ to × (but forgot to flip)2/3 × 1/2
    3. 3Multiply2/6
    4. 4Simplify1/3

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student changed ÷ to × but forgot to flip the divisor. Keep 2/3, change ÷ to ×, and flip the divisor 1/2 to its reciprocal 2/1: 2/3 × 2/1 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. The correct quotient is 4/3 (1 1/3), not 1/3. The step that changed ÷ to × left the divisor as 1/2 instead of flipping it, so the problem turned into multiplication of the original fractions. Keep, Change, Flip turns the divisor into 2/1, giving 2/3 × 2/1 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. That size makes sense, because 1/2 is smaller than 2/3, so more than one copy fits.

  6. 6

    (Lesson 6.9) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Problem8 ÷ 3/4
    2. 2Keep, Change, FlipFlipped the wrong number — wrote 1/8 × 3/4 instead of 8 × 4/3
    3. 3Multiply1/8 × 3/4 = 3/32

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: Keep the dividend 8 the same and flip only the divisor: 8 × 4/3 = 32/3 = 10 2/3. The mistake was flipping the whole number 8 into 1/8 instead of flipping the divisor 3/4 into 4/3. Only the divisor gets flipped. The Keep, Change, Flip step turned the dividend 8 into 1/8 and left 3/4 alone, which is backwards; the correct rewrite is 8 × 4/3 = 32/3 = 10 2/3. Since 3/4 is less than 1, more than 8 of those pieces fit into 8 wholes, so a tiny answer like 3/32 cannot be right.

    (Lesson 6.1) Find the Interpretation Error

    1. 1ProblemInterpret 4 ÷ 1/5
    2. 2Student says4 ÷ 1/5 means 1/5 of 4
    3. 3Student's answer4/5

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 4 ÷ 1/5 means 'how many 1/5-size pieces fit into 4,' not '1/5 of 4.' The correct answer is 4 × 5 = 20. The "Student says" step rewrites division as multiplication: "1/5 of 4" means 1/5 × 4, which is a different question. 4 ÷ 1/5 asks how many 1/5-size pieces fit inside 4, and each whole holds 5 of them, so the quotient is 4 × 5 = 20. The answer 4/5 is smaller than the dividend, which cannot happen when you divide by a number less than 1.

  2. 2

    (Lesson 6.2) Find the Division Error

    1. 1Problem2/5 ÷ 3/4
    2. 2Keep, Change, Flip2/5 × 3/4
    3. 3Multiply6/20
    4. 4Simplify3/10

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: The student forgot to flip. 2/5 × 4/3 (flip the divisor 3/4 to get 4/3). Then 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15. The Keep, Change, Flip step swapped the operation but left the divisor as 3/4, so no fraction was actually flipped. The reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3, so the problem is 2/5 × 4/3 = 8/15, not 3/10.

    (Lesson 6.9) Find the Division Error

    1. 1Problem6 ÷ 2/3
    2. 2Keep, Change, Flip6 × 2/3
    3. 3Multiply12/3
    4. 4Simplify4

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: 6 × 3/2 (flip the divisor 2/3 to get 3/2, not keep it as 2/3). Then 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9. The Keep, Change, Flip step kept the divisor as 2/3 instead of flipping it to its reciprocal 3/2. The correct work is 6 × 3/2 = 18/2 = 9. Nine also makes sense as a count: each whole holds one and a half 2/3-size pieces, so 6 wholes hold 9 of them — more than the 6 you started with, not less.
