  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    An arcade prize costs 240 tickets. If you have a coupon for 15% off, how many tickets do you save?

    1. A15 tickets
    2. B24 tickets
    3. C36 tickets
    4. D204 tickets

    💡 Solution Rationale: 15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36 tickets saved.

  2. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A student scored 72 out of 90 on a test. What percent did they score?

    1. A72%
    2. B75%
    3. C80%
    4. D90%

    💡 Solution Rationale: 72/90 simplifies to 8/10, and 10 × 10 = 100, so 8 × 10 = 80. That makes 72/90 = 80/100 = 80%.

  3. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    The claw machine prize is 80 tickets and today it is 25% off. How many tickets do you save?

    1. A16
    2. B20
    3. C25
    4. D55

    💡 Solution Rationale: 25% = 0.25. Multiply: 0.25 × 80 = 20 tickets saved.

SECTION 1 REAL-WORLD CONTEXTS & PROBLEM SOLVING [Applications]
  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    At West Middle School, 45% of the 360 sixth-grade students participate in after-school sports. How many sixth-grade students participate in sports?

    1. A144 students
    2. B160 students
    3. C162 students
    4. D180 students

    💡 Solution Rationale: 45% of 360 = 0.45 × 360 = 162 students.

SECTION 2 MATHEMATICAL WRITING & ERROR ANALYSIS [Reasoning & Critique]
  1. 2 OPEN RESPONSE

    A class has 32 students. 25% are in the math club, 50% are in the reading club, and 12.5% are in both. How many students are in each group? Can the total of all three groups be more than 32? Explain why or why not.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Key Terms to Look For: 8, 16, 4, overlap, can, counted twice, both Explanation: Taking each percent of the same 32 students gives 25% = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 in math club, 50% = 32 ÷ 2 = 16 in reading club, and 12.5% = 32 ÷ 8 = 4 in both. Adding 8 + 16 + 4 = 28 is misleading, because the 4 students in both are already inside the 8 and the 16 — counting them a third time overlaps, and the number of different students in at least one club is 8 + 16 − 4 = 20.
