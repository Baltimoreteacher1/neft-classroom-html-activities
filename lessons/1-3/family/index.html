Unit 1, Lesson 3: Math is In My World5.NBT.B.7

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

The book opens with a warm-up — about how many coins are in the picture? — then rides an aerial tramway that must move 4,000 passengers.

Key idea: When we do math, we visualize and represent problems, use tools to show relationships among quantities, and make strategic decisions about which tool fits the problem.

Key words

  • representation — A picture, diagram, table, or equation that shows the mathematics in a situation.
    Español: representación — Un dibujo, diagrama, tabla o ecuación que muestra las matemáticas de una situación.
  • tape diagram — A rectangle cut into equal parts that helps you visualize how a total breaks apart.
    Español: diagrama de cintas — Un rectángulo dividido en partes iguales que te ayuda a visualizar cómo se separa un total.
  • tool — Anything you choose to help you see relationships and solve a problem — a table, a diagram, a graph.
    Español: herramienta — Cualquier cosa que eliges para ver relaciones y resolver un problema: una tabla, un diagrama, una gráfica.
  • ordered pair — Two numbers that go together, like a time and a number of trips, that you can plot as a point.
    Español: par ordenado — Dos números que van juntos, como un tiempo y un número de viajes, que puedes graficar como un punto.
  • round trip — A complete journey from the start, to the destination, and back to the start.
    Español: viaje de ida y vuelta — Un recorrido completo desde el inicio hasta el destino y de regreso al inicio.

Example

  1. I do not count every coin — I find a small stack I CAN count, about 10 coins.
  2. Then I estimate how many stacks like that are in the picture.
  3. If about 15 stacks fit, my estimate is about 150 coins — reasoned, not random.

Common mistake

Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

How families can help at home

  • Ask your student to teach you the example above in their own words.
  • Have them say why each step works, not just the answer.
  • Use the sentence starters below so they explain their thinking in full sentences.
