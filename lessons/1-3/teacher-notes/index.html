Unit 1, Lesson 3: Math is In My World5.NBT.B.7

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can represent a real-world situation with a tape diagram or table and use decimal operations to solve it.

Language: I can describe my tool using the words tape diagram, table, and this represents ___ .

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below before the lesson.

Warm-up

One tram ride takes 12.5 minutes, so 10 rides take 12.5 × 10 = 125 minutes and 50 rides take 12.5 × 50 = 625 minutes.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Math Practices: Visual Models & Tools. 1. Select the mathematical tool (tape diagram, number line, grid) that best represents the quantities. 2. Label all parts and units clearly. 3. Connect visual relationships directly to numerical expressions

I do:

  1. A pile of coins is too big to count one at a time. About how many coins are in the pile?
  2. I do not count every coin — I find a small stack I CAN count, about 10 coins.
  3. Then I estimate how many stacks like that are in the pile. I count about 15.
  4. 15 stacks of about 10 is 15 × 10 = 150, so my estimate is about 150 coins — reasoned, not random.

You do: One tram car makes its 5-mile round trip in 35 minutes. Build the table — 35 minutes for 1 trip, 175 for 5, 350 for 10 — and use it to find how many round trips fit in 10 hours.

Common misconception

Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: representation, tape diagram, tool, ordered pair, round trip (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

It takes 35 minutes for one tram car to make the 5-mile round trip, from valley to mountain top and back down — including time for passengers to disembark and embark at each station. You want to know how many round trips a single tram car completes in 10 hours.
