    Sort each expression by whether it equals 125.

    Groups: Equals 125 Does NOT equal 125
    • Equals 12512.5 × 10
    • Equals 1251.25 × 100
    • Equals 12562.5 × 2
    • Does NOT equal 12512.5 × 100
    • Does NOT equal 1251.25 × 10
    • Does NOT equal 12512.5 + 10

    Hint Multiplying by 10 moves the decimal point one place; by 100, two places.

    Why: 12.5 × 10 = 125, 1.25 × 100 = 125, and 62.5 × 2 = 125. The others miss: 12.5 × 100 = 1,250 (decimal moved too far), 1.25 × 10 = 12.5 (not far enough), and 12.5 + 10 = 22.5 (adding is not scaling).

  2. 2Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Each tram ride takes 12.5 minutes. Complete the table of total minutes.

    RidesExpressionTotal minutes
    10
    20
    50

    Hint Use the first row to build the others: 20 rides is double 10 rides.

    Why: 10 × 12.5 = 125 minutes. Doubling the rides doubles the time: 20 rides take 250 minutes. And 50 rides is five times 10 rides, so 5 × 125 = 625 minutes — the same 625 the lesson converts to about 10.4 hours.

You want to know how many round trips a single tram car completes in 10 hours. Using the table (35→1, 175→5, 350→10, 525→15, 700→20 minutes→trips), why does the answer end up being 17 round trips, not 18?

    1. A18 trips is closer to 600 minutes, so it is the better estimate
    2. B17 trips take 595 minutes, which fits within 600, but an 18th trip would need 630 minutes, past the limit
    3. CA round trip can be counted as complete even if it is only partly finished
    4. DThe table only lists rows up to 20, so 17 is the safe middle choice

    Answer: B — 17 trips take 595 minutes, which fits within 600, but an 18th trip would need 630 minutes, past the limit

    Why: 17 × 35 = 595 minutes, still inside the 600-minute limit, while 18 × 35 = 630 minutes goes past it — a round trip only counts once it is fully complete.

    1. ARounding down happens to work here, but only the table confirms 17 × 35 stays under 600 while 18 × 35 does not
    2. BRounding down never works for time problems
    3. C17.14 should round up to 18 because .14 rounds upward
    4. DThe table and division always disagree with each other

    Answer: A — Rounding down happens to work here, but only the table confirms 17 × 35 stays under 600 while 18 × 35 does not

    Why: The .14 left over represents an unfinished trip, not extra completed trips — checking the table's actual minute totals is what proves 17 is right, rather than trusting the rounded quotient alone.
