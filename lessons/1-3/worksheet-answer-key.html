  1. The tram needs 50 rides to move 4,000 passengers, and 625 minutes is 625 ÷ 60 hours — about 10.42 hours.
  2. A tape diagram of 4,000 cut into equal rides shows WHY we divide, and the table shows how minutes grow with rides.
Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each expression by whether it equals 125.

    Groups: Equals 125 Does NOT equal 125
    • Equals 12512.5 × 10
    • Equals 1251.25 × 100
    • Equals 12562.5 × 2
    • Does NOT equal 12512.5 × 100
    • Does NOT equal 1251.25 × 10
    • Does NOT equal 12512.5 + 10

    Hint Multiplying by 10 moves the decimal point one place; by 100, two places.

    Why: 12.5 × 10 = 125, 1.25 × 100 = 125, and 62.5 × 2 = 125. The others miss: 12.5 × 100 = 1,250 (decimal moved too far), 1.25 × 10 = 12.5 (not far enough), and 12.5 + 10 = 22.5 (adding is not scaling).

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    One tram ride takes 12.5 minutes. How many minutes do 10 rides take?

    1. A1.25 minutes
    2. B22.5 minutes
    3. C125 minutes
    4. D1,250 minutes

    Hint You need 10 equal groups of 12.5.

    Answer: C — 125 minutes

    Why: 12.5 × 10 = 125. Multiplying by 10 shifts every digit one place to the left.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

    About how many coins are in the picture in your book? Explain how you estimated WITHOUT counting every coin.

    Sentence starter First I counted ___ .
    Then I estimated about ___ because ___ .

    Sample answer: I counted one small stack of about 10 coins, then estimated that about 15 stacks like it fill the picture, so about 150 coins. An estimate is a reasoned answer that is close enough to be useful.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Each tram ride takes 12.5 minutes. Complete the table of total minutes.

    RidesExpressionTotal minutes
    10
    20
    50

    Why: 10 × 12.5 = 125 minutes. Doubling the rides doubles the time: 20 rides take 250 minutes. And 50 rides is five times 10 rides, so 5 × 125 = 625 minutes — the same 625 the lesson converts to about 10.4 hours.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The 50 rides take 625 minutes. About how many HOURS is that?

    1. AAbout 6.25 hours
    2. BAbout 10.42 hours
    3. CAbout 12.5 hours
    4. DAbout 62.5 hours

    Answer: B — About 10.42 hours

    Why: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42, so it would take about 10.42 hours to transport 4,000 passengers.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    How does the answer change for 2,000 passengers? Each ride carries 80 passengers and takes 12.5 minutes. How many MINUTES are needed?

    1. A31.25 minutes
    2. B300 minutes
    3. C312.5 minutes
    4. D3,125 minutes

    Answer: C — 312.5 minutes

    Why: 2,000 ÷ 80 = 25 rides, and 25 × 12.5 = 312.5 minutes — half the passengers, half the 625 minutes.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    One round trip takes 35 minutes. Using the table (35 → 1, 350 → 10, 525 → 15, 700 → 20), how many round trips does a tram car complete in 10 hours?

    1. A15 round trips
    2. B17 round trips
    3. C18 round trips
    4. D20 round trips

    Answer: B — 17 round trips

    Why: 10 hours is 600 minutes. 17 trips take 17 × 35 = 595 minutes, and an 18th trip would need 630 — past the 600. So the tram completes 17 round trips.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

  4. 5Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The round trip is 5 miles. How far does a tram car travel in 5 round trips?

    1. A1 mile
    2. B10 miles
    3. C25 miles
    4. D50 miles

    Answer: C — 25 miles

    Why: 5 round trips × 5 miles per trip = 25 miles.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Answer in complete sentences.

    The two tram problems both use the 35-minute or 12.5-minute ride times. How are the two problems the SAME, and how are they DIFFERENT?

    Sample answer: They are the same because both relate time to a number of tram trips and both can use a table of values. They are different because the first starts from passengers and asks for total time, while the second starts from a total time (10 hours) and asks how many round trips fit inside it.

  2. 7Answer in complete sentences.

    How does the answer change if the tram must transport 6,000 passengers? (Each ride carries 80 passengers and takes 12.5 minutes.) Show your steps.

    Sample answer: 6,000 ÷ 80 = 75 rides. Then 75 × 12.5 = 937.5 minutes. Dividing by 60, that is 937.5 ÷ 60 = 15.625, so about 15.63 hours — half again as long as the 10.42 hours for 4,000 passengers, which makes sense because 6,000 is one and a half times 4,000.

Lesson 1.3Math is In My World

Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can represent a real-world situation with a tape diagram or table and use decimal operations to solve it.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each tram question to the operation that answers it.

    1. AMinutes for 40 rides (12.5 min each)
    2. BRides needed for 400 passengers (80 per ride)
    3. CHours in 625 minutes
    4. DPassengers on 30 full rides
    • A40 × 12.5
    • B400 ÷ 80
    • C625 ÷ 60
    • D30 × 80

    Why: Choosing the operation is the modeling step: totals built from equal groups multiply (40 × 12.5 minutes; 30 × 80 passengers), while totals split into equal groups divide (400 ÷ 80 rides; 625 ÷ 60 to convert minutes to hours).

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Apply — What Time Is It? The new year starts at midnight on January 1st. What time will it be 2,022 minutes after midnight?

    1. A8:22 p.m. on January 1
    2. B9:42 a.m. on January 2
    3. C9:42 a.m. on January 1
    4. D9:42 p.m. on January 2

    Answer: B — 9:42 a.m. on January 2

    Why: 2,022 ÷ 60 = 33 remainder 42, so 2,022 minutes is 33 hours 42 minutes. That is 24 hours (one full day) plus 9 hours 42 minutes — 9:42 a.m. on January 2.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    How many rides would the tram need for 10,000 passengers, at 80 passengers per ride?

    1. A12.5 rides
    2. B125 rides
    3. C250 rides
    4. D1,250 rides

    Answer: B — 125 rides

    Why: 10,000 ÷ 80 = 125 rides. Those rides would take 125 × 12.5 = 1,562.5 minutes.

    Watch for: Converting minutes to hours by moving the decimal point — 625 minutes is NOT 6.25 hours. An hour is 60 minutes, so divide by 60: 625 ÷ 60 ≈ 10.42 hours.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Answer in complete sentences.

    The lesson says we make strategic decisions about which tool to use. For the 10-hour round-trip question, would you choose a table of values, a coordinate plane, or an equation? Defend your choice.

    Sample answer: I would choose the table of values, because I can extend the rows by 35 minutes at a time until I pass 600 minutes, and I can see that 600 lands between 525 and 700. A coordinate plane shows the same ordered pairs visually, and an equation like 35 × t ≤ 600 is fastest — any tool works if it shows the relationship between time and round trips, so the strategic part is picking the one I can read most clearly.

  2. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Math is in YOUR world. Describe a real situation from your life that works like the tram problem — a total split into equal groups, or a time built from equal chunks — and show the math.

    Sample answer: My bus ride to school takes 22.5 minutes each way, so one round trip is 45 minutes. In a 5-day school week I spend 45 × 5 = 225 minutes on the bus — that is 225 ÷ 60 = 3.75 hours, almost four hours a week, which I never realized until I did the math.
