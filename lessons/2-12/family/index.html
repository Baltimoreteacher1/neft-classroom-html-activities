Unit 2, Lesson 12: Multiply Decimals

Standard 6.NOS.3

What students are learning Qué aprenden

Key idea: To multiply decimals, first multiply as if there were no decimal points. Then count how many digits come after the decimal point in both numbers together. Put the decimal point in the answer so it has that same number of digits after the point.
Idea clave: Para multiplicar decimales, primero multiplica como si no hubiera puntos decimales. Luego cuenta cuántos dígitos hay después del punto decimal en los dos números juntos. Pon el punto decimal en la respuesta para que tenga esa misma cantidad de dígitos después del punto.

How we did it in class today Cómo lo hicimos hoy en clase

  1. I want 4.5 × 12.60, the cost of 4.5 meters at $12.60 each.
  2. First I multiply without points, like whole numbers: 45 × 1260 = 56,700.
  3. Now I count decimal places: 4.5 has 1, and 12.60 has 2, so 1 + 2 = 3 decimal places.
  4. I put the point 3 places from the right in 56,700: that gives 56.700, or $56.70.
  5. I check with an estimate: 5 × $13 = $65, and $56.70 is close, so my answer is reasonable.

If they get stuck... Si se traban...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Multiply Decimals is counting decimal places from only one factor instead of adding the decimal places from BOTH factors. For example, in 0.4 × 0.06, a student multiplies 4 × 6 = 24, then places the decimal point using just one factor's place count (1 place), writing 0.24 instead of the correct answer: 0.4 has 1 decimal place and 0.06 has 2 decimal places, for a total of 3, so 24 becomes 0.024. Before you submit, count the decimal places in EACH factor separately and add them together — that total is how many decimal places belong in your final answer.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
Pregunte: «¿Puedes mostrarme cómo lo hiciste? ¿Nos saltamos algún paso?» Hablar de las matemáticas en voz alta ayuda mucho.
