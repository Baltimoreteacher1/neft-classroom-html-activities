Where we left off

Our goal: With my small group, I can place rational numbers, including fractions and decimals, on a number line — one step at a time, with support.

The big idea: To plot a rational number, find the two whole numbers it falls between, then split the space into equal parts.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. I will plot -2.5 on the number line.
  2. First, the whole numbers it falls between: -2.5 is between -2 and -3.
  3. The .5 means halfway, so I split the space between -2 and -3 in half.
  4. I place my dot exactly halfway between -2 and -3. That is -2.5.

    Think it throughWhere does −1.25 fall on the number line?

    1. ABetween −1 and −2
    2. BBetween −1 and 0
    3. CBetween 1 and 2
    4. DExactly at −1

    Why is that the answer?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    −1.25 is 1.25 units below zero — past −1, but not as far down as −2.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhich day had the water level closest to the painted mark?

    1. AWednesday at −0.5 ft
    2. BMonday at +0.75 ft
    3. CTuesday at −1.25 ft
    4. DThursday at +1.5 ft

    How do you know?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Compare absolute values: |−0.5| = 0.5 is the smallest distance from 0, so Wednesday was closest to the mark.

  3. 7

    Think it throughWhy are rational numbers more useful here than integers alone?

    1. AThey let the scientist record part-of-a-foot changes like 0.75 and −1.25
    2. BThey are easier to add
    3. CIntegers cannot be negative
    4. DThey make the lake bigger

    Explain your thinking.

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Integers would force every reading to a whole foot. Rational numbers — fractions and decimals — capture the in-between values, so the readings are far more precise.

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • A rational number always has one exact spot on the ___ ___. → Rational Numbers on the Number Line
  • A number you can write as a fraction of two integers, with a bottom number that is not zero, is a ___. → Rational number
  • A number that shows part of a whole, like 3/5, is a ___. → Fraction
  • A number that uses a point to show parts of a whole is a ___. → Decimal

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Rational Numbers on the Number Line is treating the numerator and denominator of a negative fraction as two separate whole numbers instead of one value between two integers — for example, plotting -3/4 by going to -3 and then counting 4 more units to the left, landing (incorrectly) at -7, instead of recognizing that -3/4 = -0.75, which sits between 0 and -1. Before you submit an answer, find the two whole numbers your value falls between, then split that space into equal parts.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    From Lesson 7.1Quick check — you've got this: Which situation is best represented by the integer -15?

    1. AA diver 15 meters below the surface of the water
    2. BA hill 15 meters above sea level
    3. CA deposit of $15 into a bank account
    4. DA football play with a 15-yard gain

    A negative integer represents a quantity below the chosen 0. With the water surface as 0, a diver 15 meters below it is -15.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 7.1What does 0 represent in this football situation?

    1. AA play in which nobody touched the ball
    2. BThe line of scrimmage, the spot where the series started
    3. CThe end zone at the edge of the field
    4. DThe number of yards still needed for a first down

    Every play is measured from the spot the team started, so that spot is 0. Gains are positive integers above it and losses are negative integers below it.
