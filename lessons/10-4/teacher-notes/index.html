Unit 10, Lesson 4: Math is IngenuityMPP.4

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can use ratios and models to explain how the Penny Farthing and gear-driven bicycles were ingenious solutions to a real problem.

Language: I can explain a gear ratio using the words rotation, teeth, and 'for each ___, the wheel turns ___.'

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below before the lesson.

Warm-up

The smallest rear gear has 12 teeth, and the pedal gear still turns 48 teeth per rotation.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Gear Ratios, Circumference & Mechanical Advantage. Formula: Distance = Rotations × Circumference | Gear Ratio = Teeth_Driver / Teeth_Driven. 1. Circumference is the distance traveled in one full wheel rotation. 2. Gear ratios determine speed and rotational force (torque). 3. Apply proportional reasoning to calculate travel distance and mechanical advantage

I do:

  1. A later bicycle design uses a chain: the pedal gear has 48 teeth, and the largest rear gear has 32 teeth.
  2. For each rotation of the 48-tooth pedal gear, the wheel gear also turns 48 teeth.
  3. The 32-tooth gear needs to turn 32 teeth to make one full rotation, so I find the equivalent ratio: 48 ÷ 32 = 1.5.
  4. The rear wheel makes 1.5 rotations for each pedal rotation — this gear makes it easier to pedal at slower speeds or go uphill.

You do: Explain to a partner why the Penny Farthing's front wheel was so large. Then say which rear gear — 32 teeth or 12 teeth — you would choose for a steep hill, and why.

Common misconception

Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: ingenuity, rotation, gear, ratio, equivalent ratios (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.
