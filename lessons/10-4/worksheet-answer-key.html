  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    On the Penny Farthing, what is the ratio of pedal rotations to front-wheel rotations?

    1. A1 to 1
    2. B1 to 2
    3. C2 to 1
    4. D1 to 4

    💡 Solution Rationale: The pedals are attached directly to the front wheel, so each pedal rotation rotates the wheel one time — a 1 to 1 ratio.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  2. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    How far does the Penny Farthing travel with each pedal rotation?

    1. AThe length of the perimeter of the front wheel
    2. BThe height of the rider
    3. CThe length of the pedal
    4. DIt does not move a fixed distance

    💡 Solution Rationale: One wheel rotation rolls the bike forward exactly the perimeter of the wheel, and one pedal rotation makes one wheel rotation.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  3. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Why was the front wheel of the Penny Farthing so large?

    1. ASo the bicycle travels a greater distance with one pedal rotation
    2. BSo the bicycle is easier to stop
    3. CSo the rider sits closer to the ground
    4. DSo the rear wheel spins faster

    💡 Solution Rationale: A bigger wheel has a longer perimeter, so each pedal rotation carries the rider farther.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  4. 4 OPEN RESPONSE

    How does the size of the front wheel affect the distance the bicycle travels?

    💬 Sentence Starter: A bigger wheel travels ___ with each pedal rotation because ___ .<br>A smaller wheel would travel ___ .
    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: The bigger the front wheel, the longer its perimeter, so one pedal rotation carries the bicycle a greater distance. A smaller front wheel would travel a shorter distance with each pedal rotation.

  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    The pedal gear turns 48 teeth per rotation. The largest rear gear needs 32 teeth for one full rotation. How many rotations does the rear wheel make for each pedal rotation?

    1. A1.5 rotations
    2. B16 rotations
    3. C0.5 rotations
    4. D2 rotations

    💡 Solution Rationale: 48 ÷ 32 = 1.5, so the rear wheel makes 1.5 rotations for each pedal rotation.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  2. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    The smallest rear gear has 12 teeth. How many rotations will the rear wheel make for each rotation of the 48-tooth pedal gear?

    1. A4 rotations
    2. B36 rotations
    3. C3 rotations
    4. D0.25 rotations

    💡 Solution Rationale: 48 ÷ 12 = 4, so the rear wheel makes 4 rotations for each pedal rotation.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  3. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    You are about to ride up a steep hill. Which rear gear should the chain be on, and why?

    1. AThe 32-tooth gear, because 1.5 rotations per pedal makes pedaling easier at slow speeds
    2. BThe 12-tooth gear, because 4 rotations per pedal is always better
    3. CThe 32-tooth gear, because it makes the bike go faster
    4. DIt does not matter which gear you use on a hill

    💡 Solution Rationale: The larger gear turns the wheel fewer times per pedal rotation, which makes it easier to pedal at slower speeds or go uphill.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  4. 4 OPEN RESPONSE

    Why do you think the larger gear makes it easier to pedal at slower speeds?

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: With the larger gear the rear wheel only makes 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation instead of 4, so each pedal push moves the bike a shorter distance. Moving a shorter distance per push takes less force, which is easier when going slowly or uphill.
  5. 5 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    With the chain on the 12-tooth gear, the rider makes 10 pedal rotations. How many rotations does the rear wheel make?

    1. A40 rotations
    2. B14 rotations
    3. C22 rotations
    4. D120 rotations

    💡 Solution Rationale: The rear wheel makes 4 rotations per pedal rotation, so 10 pedal rotations give 10 × 4 = 40 wheel rotations.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  6. 6 OPEN RESPONSE

    When the chain is on the largest gear the rear wheel makes 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation; on the smallest gear it makes 4. What do you notice about the size of the wheel gears and the distance the bike travels?

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: The smaller the rear gear, the more rotations the wheel makes per pedal rotation, so the bike travels farther with each pedal push. The larger gear gives fewer rotations and a shorter distance, but easier pedaling.

  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    The rider makes 3 full pedal rotations with the chain on the 32-tooth gear. How many rotations does the rear wheel make?

    1. A4.5 rotations
    2. B3 rotations
    3. C96 rotations
    4. D6 rotations

    💡 Solution Rationale: Three pedal rotations move 3 × 48 = 144 teeth of chain, and 144 ÷ 32 = 4.5 rear-wheel rotations.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  2. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A designer adds a middle gear so the rear wheel makes exactly 2 rotations per rotation of the 48-tooth pedal gear. How many teeth must the new gear have?

    1. A24 teeth
    2. B22 teeth
    3. C96 teeth
    4. D16 teeth

    💡 Solution Rationale: The gear must use 48 ÷ 2 = 24 teeth per rotation, so a 24-tooth gear gives exactly 2 rotations.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Thinking a rear gear with MORE teeth makes the wheel spin more — it is the opposite: the 32-tooth gear gives only 1.5 rotations per pedal rotation, while the 12-tooth gear gives 4.

  3. 3 OPEN RESPONSE

    How would the distance traveled differ with a smaller front wheel on the Penny Farthing? Use the 1 to 1 pedal ratio in your answer.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: The pedal-to-wheel ratio stays 1 to 1, so each pedal rotation still gives exactly one wheel rotation. But a smaller wheel has a shorter perimeter, so each rotation rolls the bike a shorter distance — the rider would have to pedal many more times to go the same distance.
  4. 4 OPEN RESPONSE

    How did math help the bicycle's designers come up with ingenious solutions — first with the Penny Farthing, then with chains and gears?

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: The Penny Farthing used the math of perimeter: a bigger wheel means more distance per pedal rotation. Gears improved on it with ratios: by choosing 32-tooth or 12-tooth gears against a 48-tooth pedal gear, riders can pick 1.5 or 4 wheel rotations per pedal rotation, trading distance for easier pedaling. Math let designers improve and enhance the solution instead of just making wheels bigger.
