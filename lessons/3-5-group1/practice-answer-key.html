Where we left off

Our goal: With my small group, I can compare ratios by finding unit rates or using equivalent ratios — one step at a time, with support.

The big idea: Compare ratios fairly by making one part equal or by finding the unit rate.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. Chef Reyes uses 3 tbsp cocoa for 5 oz milk (3:5). Chef Tran uses 4 tbsp for 7 oz milk (4:7).
  2. I cannot compare yet because the milk amounts are different.
  3. I scale both to 35 oz of milk. For Reyes: 5 x 7 = 35, so 3 x 7 = 21 tbsp, giving 21:35.
  4. For Tran: 7 x 5 = 35, so 4 x 5 = 20 tbsp, giving 20:35.
  5. Now the milk is equal, so I compare cocoa: 21 > 20, so Chef Reyes's cocoa is more chocolatey.

    2. B(0, 1)
    3. C(1, 1)
    4. D(1, 0)

    Zero of one quantity pairs with zero of the other, so the line goes through the origin.

Words and reasoning

Word bank · Banco de palabras

Compare Ratios (Comparar razones)Unit rate (Tasa unitaria)Equivalent ratio (Razón equivalente)Compare (Comparar)Simplify (Simplificar)Common denominator (Denominador común)

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • Make the ___ match, or find each ___ ___, before deciding which ratio is greater. → Compare Ratios
  • A rate that tells the amount for exactly one unit, like 5 miles per 1 hour, is a ___. → Unit rate
  • Two ratios that make the same comparison are ___ ___. → Equivalent ratio
  • To decide which ratio is greater or if they are equal is to ___ them. → Compare

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Compare Ratios is thinking the ratio with the bigger raw number automatically wins — like assuming Chef Tran's 8 tablespoons of cocoa must be more chocolatey than Chef Reyes's 6 tablespoons, without checking that the milk amounts (14 oz vs. 10 oz) are different. Never compare the raw numbers alone — always scale both ratios to the same amount or find each unit rate first.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 10

    Say moreYou scaled both recipes to 35 oz of milk: Chef Reyes became 21:35 and Chef Tran became 20:35. How does scaling to a common denominator let you compare the recipes fairly?

    After scaling to 35 oz, Chef Reyes has ___ tbsp and Chef Tran has ___ tbsp.

    When both ratios share 35 oz of milk, you only have to compare the cocoa amounts.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowQuick check — you've got this: A bakery sells 4 muffins for $10 and another bakery sells 6 muffins for $14. Which bakery has the lower price per muffin?

    1. AThe second bakery
    2. BThe first bakery
    3. CSame price
    4. DCannot determine

    Explain your choice.

    I know it is ___ because ___ .

    First bakery: $10÷4 = $2.50 per muffin. Second bakery: $14÷6 ≈ $2.33 per muffin. The second bakery has the lower price.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 3.4Quick check — you've got this: A ratio table shows cups of rice to cups of water: (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6). If you plot these points, the line passes through which point?

    1. A(5, 10)
    2. B(4, 6)
    3. C(5, 8)
    4. D(6, 10)

    The ratio is 1:2, so for 5 cups of rice you need 5 × 2 = 10 cups of water. The point (5, 10) continues the pattern.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 3.4Which point would NOT be on this line?

    1. A(4, 8)
    2. B(10, 20)
    3. C(6, 12)
    4. D(5, 12)

    Every point must follow 2 oz per taco. (4,8), (10,20) and (6,12) all do. (5,12) would need 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 oz per taco — a different rate.
