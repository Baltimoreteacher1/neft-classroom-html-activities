Where we left off

Our goal: With my small group, I can compare and order integers using a number line — one step at a time, with support.

The big idea: The number farther to the left is always less; the number farther to the right is always greater.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. I will compare -7 and -5. They are both negative, so I look at the number line.
  2. -7 is 7 steps left of zero. -5 is only 5 steps left of zero.
  3. -7 is farther to the left, so -7 is the smaller number.
  4. I write -7 < -5. The open side of the symbol points to the bigger number, -5.

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake on this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Compare and Order Integers is thinking that a negative number with a bigger digit is the bigger value — for example, believing -15 is greater than -8 because 15 is greater than 8. It's the opposite for negatives: the number farther to the left on the number line (farther from zero) is always the LESSER value, so -15 < -8. Before you submit, check your comparison against the number line, not just the digits.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.
