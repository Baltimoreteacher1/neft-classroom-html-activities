How it worksWorked examplefinding the mean of a data set

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

Five afternoons of dog walking earned 14, 21, 17, 19, and 24 dollars. Find the mean.

  1. Add every value. The five amounts give a total of 95 dollars.
  2. Count the values. There are five of them.
  3. Share the total evenly: divide it by the count of values.
  4. Check: the mean should land between the least and the greatest value.

Answer: The mean is 19 dollars — what each afternoon would earn if the money were shared evenly.

Watch outA common mistake

The most common mistake with the mean is adding correctly and then dividing by the wrong number — or not dividing at all. Students report the total (for 8, 10, 6, 12 they say the mean is 36) or they divide by a count that does not match the data, such as dividing a 4-value total by 5, or skipping a zero when counting the values. Before you finish, do two things: count the values in the data set out loud, and check that your mean lands between the smallest and largest value. A mean of 36 for data that never goes above 12 is impossible.
