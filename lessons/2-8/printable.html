📚 Vocabulary

WordWhat it meansExample
Mean
Media		The average of a data set. Add all the values, then divide by how many values there are.
El promedio de un conjunto de datos. Suma todos los valores y divide entre cuántos valores hay.		(8 + 6 + 10) ÷ 3 = 8 — Add the values, then divide by how many there are.
Average
Promedio		Another word for the mean — the amount each value would be if the total were shared equally.
Otra palabra para la media: la cantidad que tendría cada valor si el total se repartiera en partes iguales.		11 from 8, 12, 10, 14 — Total 44 shared equally across 4 values.
Measure of center
Medida de centro		A single number used to describe the middle, or typical value, of a whole data set.
Un solo número que se usa para describir el centro, o valor típico, de todo un conjunto de datos.		A median of 83.5 — One number standing for the typical value.
Balance point
Punto de equilibrio		The value where the total distance of the data above it equals the total distance below it. The mean is the balance point.
El valor donde la distancia total de los datos por encima es igual a la distancia total por debajo. La media es el punto de equilibrio.		The mean of 10 in 7, 10, 13 — Distances above and below it cancel out.
Data set
Conjunto de datos		The whole collection of values that were gathered or measured.
Toda la colección de valores que se recopilaron o midieron.		All 20 history scores together — The whole collection that was gathered.

🚀 Launch

Every week, Miguel walks dogs after school to earn money, and he keeps his earnings in a notebook. Across 4 days he earned $60 in all — but not the same amount each day. Later this lesson you'll help Sofia, who tracks her points every basketball game and wants to finish the season averaging 15 points per game.

What is the mean?

The mean, or average, is a measure of center that describes a whole data set with one number. It is what every value would be if the total were leveled out and shared equally.

Key idea: Mean = total of all the values ÷ how many values there are.

Watch me

  • Miguel's 4 days of earnings are shown with counters. When I combine all of the counters, there are 60 of them, which means Miguel earned $60 in all.
  • Now I redistribute the 60 counters equally among the 4 days. Each of the 4 groups ends up with 15 counters.
  • The arithmetic does exactly the same thing: I find the total, 60, and divide it by the number of days, 4. So 60 ÷ 4 = 15.
  • We can use $15 to describe Miguel's typical daily earnings. That leveled value is the mean.

Let's try together

  • New data set: a dog walker earns $9, $11, $16, and $12 over 4 days. First, what is the total?
  • Next, how many values are in the data set? That number is what we divide by.
  • Last, divide the total by 4. Is your answer bigger than the smallest day and smaller than the biggest day? It should be.

Now it's your turn

  • Next, you will sort Sofia's game scores against her target mean of 15 to see the mean as a balance point.
  • Then you will use that target mean to figure out what she must score in her last game.
