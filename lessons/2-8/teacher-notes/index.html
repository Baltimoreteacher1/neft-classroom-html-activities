Unit 2, Lesson 8: Describe Data Using the Mean6.DS.4

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can determine the mean of a data set, use a target mean to find a missing value, and explain the mean as the fair share and the balance point of the data.

Language: I can explain my reasoning using the words mean, average, measure of center, balance point, and data set.

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below with ESOL students.

Warm-up

New data set: a dog walker earns $9, $11, $16, and $12 over 4 days. First, what is the total?

Teaching notes

Key idea: Mean = total of all the values ÷ how many values there are.

I do:

  1. Miguel's 4 days of earnings are shown with counters. When I combine all of the counters, there are 60 of them, which means Miguel earned $60 in all.
  2. Now I redistribute the 60 counters equally among the 4 days. Each of the 4 groups ends up with 15 counters.
  3. The arithmetic does exactly the same thing: I find the total, 60, and divide it by the number of days, 4. So 60 ÷ 4 = 15.
  4. We can use $15 to describe Miguel's typical daily earnings. That leveled value is the mean.

You do: Next, you will sort Sofia's game scores against her target mean of 15 to see the mean as a balance point. Then you will use that target mean to figure out what she must score in her last game.

Common misconception

The most common mistake with the mean is adding correctly and then dividing by the wrong number — or not dividing at all. Students report the total (for 8, 10, 6, 12 they say the mean is 36) or they divide by a count that does not match the data, such as dividing a 4-value total by 5, or skipping a zero when counting the values. Before you finish, do two things: count the values in the data set out loud, and check that your mean lands between the smallest and largest value. A mean of 36 for data that never goes above 12 is impossible.

ESOL / SPED supports

  • Pre-teach: Mean, Average, Measure of center, Balance point, Data set (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Data set: 8, 12, 10, 14. What is the mean?

  • 11 ✓ correct
  • 44
  • 12
  • 10

Add the values: 8 + 12 + 10 + 14 = 44. There are 4 values, so the mean is 44 ÷ 4 = 11.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.

Extension

Sofia tracks the number of points she scores in each basketball game. In games 1 through 9 she scored 12, 20, 16, 11, 17, 14, 15, 10, and 18 points. She wants to end the season with a mean of 15 points per game across all 10 games. Compared with that target of 15, her nine games came in 3 below, 5 above, 1 above, 4 below, 2 above, 1 below, even, 5 below, and 3 above.
