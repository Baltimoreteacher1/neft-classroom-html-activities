Warm-up

The Green-Team recycled 216 pounds of material; 8.6% was metal.

Teaching notes

Key idea: Estimating Percents of Numbers. 1. Use benchmark percents: 10% (move decimal left 1), 1% (move left 2), 50% (half), 25% (quarter). 2. Combine benchmarks (e.g. 15% = 10% + 5%; 75% = 50% + 25%). 3. Round numbers to compatible values for quick mental estimates

I do:

  1. The whole is 6 GB — that is 100%.
  2. I mark the bottom line in benchmark jumps of 25%: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%.
  3. Equivalent ratios give the top line: 25% of 6 GB is 1.5 GB, so the top reads 0, 1.5, 3, 4.5, 6.
  4. 4 GB used lands between 4.5 (75%) and 3 (50%), close to 4.5 — so about 70% of the storage is used.

You do: Estimate 24% of 81 using a benchmark. Say which benchmark you chose and why. Then check: is your estimate a little high or a little low?

Common misconception

Keeping the same whole for every question — when the problem says 'of the used storage,' the whole changes from 6 GB to 4 GB, and estimates anchored to the old whole are wrong before any arithmetic happens.

Language & learning supports

  • Pre-teach: estimate, benchmark percent, compatible numbers, double number line, the whole (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Estimate 19% of 412 using benchmarks or compatible numbers.

  • About 80 ✓ correct
  • About 8
  • About 200
  • About 40

19% is about 20%, and 412 is about 400. 20% of 400 is 0.2 × 400 = 80.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.
