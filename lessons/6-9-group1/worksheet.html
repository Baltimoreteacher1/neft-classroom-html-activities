WordWhat it meansExample
Whole NumberSpanish: Número entero A counting number with no fraction or decimal, like 0, 1, 2, 3. 4 is a whole number; as a fraction it is 4/1
FractionSpanish: Fracción A number that shows part of a whole, like 3/4. 1/2 means 1 out of 2 equal parts — like cutting a sandwich in half and taking one piece
ReciprocalSpanish: Recíproco A fraction turned upside down. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2. Flip the fraction to divide: 6 ÷ 2/3 = 6 × 3/2
Keep, Change, FlipSpanish: Mantener, cambiar, invertir A way to divide fractions: keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second. 6 ÷ 1/3 → Keep 6, Change ÷ to ×, Flip 1/3 to 3/1 → 6 × 3 = 18
QuotientSpanish: Cociente The answer when you divide. In 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8, the quotient is 8

How it worksWorked example

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

Agent Park has 4 pounds of powder and each bag holds 1/2 pound. I want 4 ÷ 1/2.

  1. Keep the 4. Change ÷ to ×. Flip 1/2 to make 2/1, which is just 2.
  2. Now I multiply: 4 × 2 = 8.
  3. So 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8. He can fill 8 bags. That makes sense: each pound fills 2 bags, and 4 pounds × 2 = 8.

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake in Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions is doing 'Keep, Change' but skipping the 'Flip' — students multiply by the divisor as it is instead of by its reciprocal. For example, for 6 ÷ 3/4, a student might write 6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 and stop there, but the divisor 3/4 must be flipped to 4/3 first: 6 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8. Before you submit, check: did I flip the second fraction (the divisor) upside down before multiplying?
