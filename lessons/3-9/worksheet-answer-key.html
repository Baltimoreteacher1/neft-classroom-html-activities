  2. So 2:5 and 6:15 are equivalent ratios.
Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each pair of ratios: are they equivalent or not equivalent?

    Groups: Equivalent Ratios NOT Equivalent
    • Equivalent Ratios2:4 and 3:6
    • Equivalent Ratios5:10 and 1:2
    • NOT Equivalent3:7 and 6:12
    • NOT Equivalent4:9 and 8:16

    Hint Two ratios are equivalent if one is the other with BOTH parts scaled by the same multiplier.

    Why: 2:4 and 3:6 both simplify to 1:2, and 5:10 also simplifies to 1:2, so those two pairs are equivalent. In 3:7 and 6:12 the first part doubled but the second went from 7 to 12, which is not doubling, so they are not equivalent — and 6:12 simplifies to 1:2 while 3:7 cannot be reduced at all. The same happens in 4:9 and 8:16: 4 doubled to 8, but 9 doubled would be 18, not 16.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which ratio is equivalent to 2:5?

    1. A2:10
    2. B3:6
    3. C4:10
    4. D5:2

    Hint Equivalent ratios come from multiplying BOTH parts of 2:5 by the same number.

    Answer: C — 4:10

    Why: Multiply both parts by 2: 2×2 = 4 and 5×2 = 10, so 4:10 is equivalent to 2:5.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Equivalent Ratios is ADDING the scale factor to one part instead of MULTIPLYING both parts by it. For example, scaling 2:5 by a factor of 4 should give 8:20 (2×4 and 5×4) — not 6:9 (2+4 and 5+4). Adding changes the taste of the recipe (and the value of the ratio); only multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same amount keeps it equivalent.

    Spot the Common Mistake — Lemonade Ratio

    1. 1Set up the ratio2 cups lemon : 5 cups water = 6 cups lemon : ? cups water
    2. 2Find the multiplier6 ÷ 2 = 3
    3. 3Apply to the water5 + 3 = 8
    4. 4Answer6 cups lemon : 8 cups water

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Look at Step 3. The lemon was multiplied by 3, so what should you do to the water?

    Mistake: Step 3. The "Apply to the water" step adds the scale factor instead of multiplying by it. The lemon went from 2 to 6, which is × 3, so the water must also be × 3: 5 × 3 = 15. The equivalent ratio is 6 cups lemon : 15 cups water, and you can check it because 6:15 and 2:5 both simplify to 2:5.

    Correct work: 5 × 3 = 15 (multiply both parts by the scale factor, do not add it), so the answer is 6 cups lemon : 15 cups water.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake — Simplifying a Ratio

    1. 1Set up the ratioA student wants to write 12:18 in simplest form.
    2. 2Find the common factorBoth 12 and 18 can be divided by 6.
    3. 3Divide each part12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 3 = 6, so they wrote 2:6.
    4. 4Answer12:18 simplifies to 2:6

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. The "Divide each part" step uses two different divisors: 12 ÷ 6 but 18 ÷ 3. Simplifying only keeps a ratio equivalent when both parts are divided by the same number, so 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3 give 2:3. The student's 2:6 simplifies to 1:3, which is not the same comparison as 12:18.

    Correct work: Divide BOTH parts by the same number: 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3, so 12:18 simplifies to 2:3.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find Marcus's Mistake

    1. 1Set up the ratio3 cups : 5 cups = ? : 20 cups
    2. 2Find the multiplier20 ÷ 5 = 4
    3. 3Apply to both parts3 + 4 = 7
    4. 4Answer7 cups : 20 cups

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. In the "Apply to both parts" step Marcus adds the multiplier instead of multiplying by it. Since 5 became 20, the scale factor is × 4, so the first part must be 3 × 4 = 12, giving 12 cups : 20 cups. His 7:20 does not simplify to 3:5, which confirms the ratios are not equivalent.

    Correct work: 3 × 4 = 12 (multiply, don't add)

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A store sells 3 pens for $2. Another store sells 9 pens for $6. Are these equivalent ratios? If so, prove it using at least two different methods (simplifying, scaling, or cross-multiplying).

    Sample answer: Yes, they are equivalent. Method 1 (simplify): 9:6 ÷ 3 = 3:2, which matches 3:2. Method 2 (cross-multiply): 3 × 6 = 18 and 2 × 9 = 18. Since both cross products are equal, the ratios are equivalent.

    Look for: equivalent, simplify, 3:2, cross-multiply, 18, equal

    Why: The ratios are equivalent. Scaling shows it: multiplying both parts of 3:2 by 3 gives 9:6. Simplifying shows it as well, since 9:6 reduces to 3:2, and cross-multiplying gives 3 × 6 = 18 and 2 × 9 = 18 — the same product.
