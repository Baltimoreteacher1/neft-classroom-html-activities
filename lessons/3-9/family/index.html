What students are learning

Equivalent ratios are ratios that mean the same thing. You make one by multiplying or dividing BOTH numbers by the same amount.

Key idea: Equivalent ratios are equal ratios, like 1:2 and 3:6. To find a missing number, find what one number was multiplied or divided by, then do the same thing to the other number.

Example

  1. Chef Montoya's soup serves 8 people, but she needs to serve 32.
  2. I find the scale factor: 32 divided by 8 = 4.
  3. The recipe uses 3 cups of tomatoes to 2 cups of broth, a 3:2 ratio.
  4. I multiply both by 4: 3 x 4 = 12 cups of tomatoes, and 2 x 4 = 8 cups of broth.
  5. So 3:2 and 12:8 are equivalent ratios, and the flavor stays the same.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Equivalent Ratios is ADDING the scale factor to one part instead of MULTIPLYING both parts by it. For example, scaling 2:5 by a factor of 4 should give 8:20 (2×4 and 5×4) — not 6:9 (2+4 and 5+4). Adding changes the taste of the recipe (and the value of the ratio); only multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same amount keeps it equivalent.
