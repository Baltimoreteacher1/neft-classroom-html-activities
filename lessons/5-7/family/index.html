What students are learning

Surface area is the total area of all the faces of a solid. A rectangular prism has 6 faces; a triangular prism has 5 faces (2 triangles + 3 rectangles). Add every face to get the total.

Key idea: Surface area is the total area of every face on the outside of a solid: a box has 6 rectangles, and a triangular prism has 2 triangle ends and 3 rectangle sides. Add the areas of all the faces. A box 3 in by 2 in by 1 in has faces of 6, 6, 3, 3, 2, and 2 square inches, so its surface area is 22 square inches.

How we did it in class today

  1. My triangular prism has triangle bases (base 6 in., height 4 in.), is 10 in. long, and the two slant sides are 5 in. each.
  2. Each triangle base is ½ × 6 × 4 = 12, and there are 2 of them: 2 × 12 = 24.
  3. Now the 3 rectangles: 6 × 10 = 60, 5 × 10 = 50, and 5 × 10 = 50, which add to 160.
  4. I add the bases and the rectangles: 24 + 160 = 184.
  5. So the surface area is 184 in². I counted 5 faces because it is a triangular prism.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Surface Area of Prisms is confusing surface area with volume — multiplying l × w × h (which finds the space INSIDE the prism, in cubic units) instead of adding the areas of all the faces (SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh, in square units). A second common slip is forgetting to double one face pair, like writing 2(l×w) + 2(l×h) + (w×h) instead of + 2(w×h) — always count all 6 faces (or all 5 for a triangular prism) before you submit.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
