🚀 Launch

You're planning a concert at the music studio. Tickets cost $12 each, and there's a flat $50 venue fee no matter how many people attend. If t represents the number of tickets sold, how would you write an expression for the total revenue?

How do we turn words into an expression?

An algebraic expression uses numbers, a variable (a letter for an unknown number), and operations. We read a word phrase and write it with math symbols.

Key idea: Words like "each" or "per" mean multiply, and "plus" or "more than" mean add.

Watch me

  • Tickets cost $12 each, plus a flat $50 venue fee. Let t be the number of tickets.
  • "$12 each ticket" means 12 times t, which I write as 12t.
  • The flat fee $50 is added once, so I add 50.
  • I put them together: total revenue = 12t + 50.

Let's try together

  • Let's write a simpler one: "$5 for each drink, d drinks."
  • What does "$5 for each drink" tell us to do? Yes, multiply 5 by d.
  • So the expression is 5d.

Now it's your turn

  • Sort each word phrase into the matching expression in the activity.
  • Remember: "more than" adds and "product" multiplies.
