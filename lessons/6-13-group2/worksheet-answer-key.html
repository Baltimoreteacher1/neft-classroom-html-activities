Lesson 6.136.13 Small Group · Group 2

Group 2 · Answer KeyChallenge practice

Learning target I can write a number as a product of its prime factors using a factor tree, explain what each part stands for, and build one for a situation I have not seen before.

Start here pageNow you try — model solution

  1. Is 3 prime? Yes, so we keep it. Is 4 prime? No — what is 4 split apart? (4 = 2 × 2.)
  2. So put the prime factors together: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3. Every factor is prime, so we are done.
  3. Now prove it: say why that move had to work at all — not just that it did.
  4. Sentence frame — convince a skeptic: "My method works because ___ . It would stop working if ___ ."

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.
    Factor Tree for 90

    The first lock panel shows 90. What is the prime factorization of 90?

    1. A2 × 3 × 3 × 5
    2. B2 × 45
    3. C3 × 30
    4. D9 × 10

    Answer: A — 2 × 3 × 3 × 5

    Why: 90 = 2 × 45 = 2 × 9 × 5 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5. Every factor is prime, so the lock accepts 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Prime Factorization is stopping the factor tree too soon — leaving a composite number like 4, 6, 8, or 9 in the final answer instead of continuing to split it. For example, writing 20 = 4 × 5 as the prime factorization (4 is composite) instead of finishing with 20 = 2 × 2 × 5. Before you submit, check every number in your final answer: if any factor can still be broken into smaller factors, the tree isn't finished yet.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 2 Extension — Fix the Broken Factor Tree

    1. 1Find prime factorization of 96Start: 96
    2. 2First split96 = 8 × 12
    3. 3Break down 88 = 2 × 2 × 2
    4. 4Break down 1212 = 2 × 6
    5. 5Final answer96 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 6

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The 12 branch stopped while 6 was still composite: 6 = 2 × 3, so 12 = 2 × 2 × 3, not 2 × 6. Finishing that branch gives 96 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁵ × 3.

    Correct work: 12 = 2 × 6, but 6 is not prime. 6 = 2 × 3, so 12 = 2 × 2 × 3. The complete prime factorization is 96 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁵ × 3.
