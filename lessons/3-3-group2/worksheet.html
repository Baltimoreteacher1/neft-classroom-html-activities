WordWhat it meansExample
Ratio TablesSpanish: Tablas de razones Tables that organize pairs of equivalent ratios. 2:3, 4:6, and 6:9 belong in the same ratio table.
Ratio tableSpanish: Tabla de razones A table of equal ratios that shows a pattern. 5, 10, 15 / 2, 4, 6
Equivalent ratioSpanish: Razón equivalente Two ratios that mean the same thing, like 1:2 and 2:4. 2:3 = 4:6 = 6:9
Scale factorSpanish: Factor de escala The number you multiply both parts of a ratio by to get an equal ratio. 2:3 × 2 → 4:6 (scale factor is 2)
PatternSpanish: Patrón A rule that numbers follow again and again. Each row adds 2 cups juice and 3 cups water
Additive patternSpanish: Patrón aditivo Building new rows of a ratio table by adding each column's own constant amount, so the two columns grow together. 2:3 → 4:6 → 6:9 (add 2 and 3 each time)

How it worksWorked examplefilling in a ratio table

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A fruit bowl uses 5 apples for every 2 oranges. Fill the table for 15 apples.

  1. Write the base ratio. 5 apples : 2 oranges
  2. Find the factor. 5 × ? = 15, so the factor is 3
  3. Multiply BOTH rows by the same factor. 5 × 3 = 15 2 × 3 = 6
  4. Never ADD to make equivalent ratios — adding changes the comparison.

Answer: 15 apples go with 6 oranges.

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake in Ratio Tables is scaling only ONE part of the ratio, or adding the scale factor to a part instead of multiplying by it — for example, turning 2:3 into 6:3 (multiplying only the first number by 3 and forgetting the second) or into 4:5 (adding the scale factor 2 to the second number instead of multiplying, since 3+2=5 but 3×2=6). Before you submit, multiply BOTH numbers by the same scale factor and check that your new ratio simplifies back to the original.
