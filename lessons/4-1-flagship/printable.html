🚀 Launch

You're comparing ticket prices at different game booths in the arcade. Booth A charges $3 for 5 games, and Booth B charges $5 for 8 games. Which booth gives you a better deal? To find out, you need to figure out the cost per game — that's the unit rate!

What is a unit rate, and how does it help me find the better buy?

A rate compares two amounts with different units. A unit rate tells you the amount for just 1 of something, like the cost for 1 game.

Key idea: To find a unit rate, divide the total cost by the number of items. The lower cost per item is the better buy.

Watch me

  • Booth A charges $3 for 5 games. I want the cost for just 1 game.
  • I divide the cost by the games: $3 ÷ 5 = $0.60.
  • So Booth A costs $0.60 per game. That is the unit rate.
  • Now I can compare this booth fairly with any other booth.

Let's try together

  • A booth charges $4 for 8 games. What do we divide?
  • We divide cost by games: $4 ÷ 8 = ?
  • What is the cost for just 1 game? ($0.50 per game.)

Now it's your turn

  • In the table, find the cost per game for each booth by dividing.
  • Then decide which booth has the lowest unit rate — that is the better buy.
