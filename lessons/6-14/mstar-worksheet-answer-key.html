Part A — Skills Check

One point each.

Question 1Selected response — correct: A

Expand 6(n + 3) using the distributive property.

  • A6n + 18
  • B6n + 3
  • C6n + 9
  • Dn + 18

6(n + 3) = 6 × n + 6 × 3 = 6n + 18.

Question 2Selected response — correct: B

Expand 4(5 − 2) using the distributive property.

  • A9
  • B12
  • C20 − 2
  • D22

4(5 − 2) = 4 × 5 − 4 × 2 = 20 − 8 = 12.

Question 3Selected response — correct: A

Expand 3(x + 4) using the distributive property.

  • A3x + 12
  • B3x + 4
  • C7x
  • Dx + 12

3(x + 4) = 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12.

Question 4Selected response — correct: D

When using the distributive property with a(b + c), you must multiply 'a' by:

  • AOnly b
  • BOnly c
  • Cb + c first, then multiply
  • DBoth b and c

The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac. You multiply the outside factor by EACH term inside the parentheses.

Question 5Selected response — correct: A

Light Cue 1: Expand 3(x + 4) for the stage-light controller.

  • A3x + 12
  • B3x + 4
  • C3x + 7
  • Dx + 12

Distribute the 3 to each term: 3 × x + 3 × 4 = 3x + 12.

Question 6Selected response — correct: B

Light Cue 2: Expand 5(2 + n) to set two spotlight banks.

  • A7 + 5n
  • B10 + 5n
  • C10 + n
  • D10 + 5

Distribute the 5 to each term: 5 × 2 + 5 × n = 10 + 5n.

Part B — Test-Format Questions

Scoring notes and distractor rationales below each item.

Question 7Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: B

Which expression is equivalent to 6(2x + 5)?

  • A2x + 30
  • B12x + 30
  • C12x + 5
  • D12x + 11

The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the outside factor by each term inside: 6 · 2x = 12x and 6 · 5 = 30, so the expression is 12x + 30.

  • A: The 6 reached the 5 but not the 2x. Distribute it to both terms inside.
  • C: The 6 reached 2x but not the 5. Distribute it to BOTH terms inside.
  • D: The 6 and the 5 were added rather than multiplied. Six times five is 30.

Part B — correct answer: B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

  • AMultiply the outside factor by the second term only and copy the first term: 2x + 30.
  • BMultiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses: 6 · 2x = 12x and 6 · 5 = 30, giving 12x + 30.
  • CMultiply 6 by 2x, then add 6 and 5 instead of multiplying them: 12x + 11.
  • DMultiply the outside factor by the first term only and copy the second term: 12x + 5.

Expanding requires the outside factor to reach every term inside the parentheses. Skipping a term or adding instead of multiplying produces an expression that is not equivalent.

Question 8Written response · 2 points

Type II Reasoning: Pulling Out a Smaller Factor

Elena was asked to factor 18m + 24 completely. She wrote 18m + 24 = 2(9m + 12) and said she was finished because 2 divides both terms.

Explain Elena's misconception and give the completely factored expression.

Model answer: Elena pulled out a common factor instead of the greatest common factor. Her expression is equivalent to the original, but 9m and 12 still share the factor 3. The GCF of 18 and 24 is 6, so 18m + 24 = 6(3m + 4). Checking by expanding: 6 · 3m = 18m and 6 · 4 = 24.

2 pointsStudent explains that 2 is a common factor but not the greatest common factor of 18 and 24, so the expression is not completely factored, and gives 6(3m + 4).
1 pointStudent gives 6(3m + 4) but does not explain that Elena used a common factor instead of the greatest common factor.
0 pointsStudent agrees that 2(9m + 12) is completely factored or gives an unrelated response.

Part C — Show Your Thinking

Model answer and scoring guidance.

Question 9Written response · 2 points

Use the distributive property to explain why 6 × 98 can be calculated as 6 × 100 − 6 × 2 = 588. Then create your own example of using the distributive property to make multiplication easier.

Model answer: Because 98 = 100 − 2, you can rewrite 6 × 98 as 6(100 − 2) and distribute: 6 × 100 − 6 × 2 = 600 − 12 = 588. The distributive property is what makes that rewrite legal, and it turns one hard multiplication into two easy ones — the same trick works for problems like 7 × 99 or 4 × 52.

Award 2 points for a complete explanation with correct mathematics, 1 point for a correct answer with thin reasoning, 0 for an unrelated response.
