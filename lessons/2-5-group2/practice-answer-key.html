Describe Data by Range and Interquartile Range

Where we left off

Our goal: I can find the range and the interquartile range of a data set and use them to describe how spread out the data is, explain how I can tell, and judge a case I have not seen before.

The big idea: Range = greatest − least, and it covers every value. Interquartile range = Q3 − Q1, and it covers only the middle half, so one faraway value cannot stretch it — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me measure the spread of the test scores

  1. Here are 20 history test scores in order: 68, 70, 73, 74, 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 86, 88, 88, 90, 90, 93, 94. Ordering them first is what makes every step after this possible.
  2. Range: I subtract the least value from the greatest. 94 − 68 = 26. The scores span 26 points from end to end.
  3. Now the quartiles. The median splits the 20 scores into two halves of 10. The median is halfway between the 10th and 11th values: (83 + 84) ÷ 2 = 83.5.
  4. Q1 is the median of the lower 10 values: halfway between 75 and 76, so Q1 = 75.5. Q3 is the median of the upper 10: halfway between 88 and 88, so Q3 = 88.
  5. Interquartile range: IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 88 − 75.5 = 12.5. The middle half of the students' scores differed by only 12.5 points, even though the full range was 26.

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • Subtract the least value from the ___ value to find the range. → Range
  • Numbers that split ordered data into four equal parts are ___. → Quartile

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake with Range and Interquartile Range is adding the two quartiles instead of subtracting them — writing IQR = Q3 + Q1. With Q1 = 42 and Q3 = 58 that gives 100, which is larger than the data set's entire range of 20. Every measure of variation is a DIFFERENCE, and the IQR can never exceed the range, because the middle half is part of the whole span. If your IQR comes out bigger than your range, you added when you should have subtracted.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 10

    Say moreWhy does the range change a lot when one unusual value is added, but the IQR barely moves?

    The range is built from the two extreme values; the IQR is built from the quartiles, which sit inside the data.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — A box plot has Q1 = 20 and Q3 = 36, with a least value of 10 and a greatest value of 50. What is the IQR, and what does it tell you?

    1. AIQR = 16; the middle half of the data spans 16 units
    2. BIQR = 28; 28 is halfway between the quartiles
    3. CIQR = 40; the data spans 40 units from end to end
    4. DIQR = 56; the quartiles add to 56

    Explain your choice.

    IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 36 − 20 = 16. Because it runs from Q1 to Q3, it describes only the middle half of the values — the width of the box.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 2.4Explain your thinking — A box plot has Q1 = 20 and Q3 = 36. What is the IQR and what does it represent?

    1. AIQR = 16; it is the spread of the middle 50% of the data
    2. BIQR = 28; it is the median between Q1 and Q3
    3. CIQR = 36; it is the value of Q3
    4. DIQR = 56; it is the total of Q1 and Q3

    How do you know?

    IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 36 − 20 = 16. The IQR tells you the range of the middle 50% of the data.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 2.4Is the fan right that Team B is better because of the 80-point game?

    1. AYes — 80 is the highest score in the data
    2. BNo — Team A's maximum is higher
    3. CYes — the maximum always decides
    4. DNo — one high game is a single extreme value, not typical performance

    How do you know?

    The 80 is the tip of a whisker — one game. Team B does look more consistent, but for a better reason: its middle 50% (55–65) is both higher and tighter than Team A's (52–68).
