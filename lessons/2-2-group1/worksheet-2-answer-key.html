SECTION 1 CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING & VISUAL MODELS [Visual Modeling]
  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A histogram bar for the interval 20–29 reaches a height of 7. What does the 7 tell you?

    1. AThe largest value is 7
    2. BThere are 7 intervals
    3. CThe interval is 7 wide
    4. D7 data values fall between 20 and 29

    💡 Solution Rationale: A bar's height is the frequency — here, 7 values land in the 20–29 interval.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Display Data: Histograms is counting a boundary value in two intervals at once — for example, putting the value 10 in both the '0–9' bin and the '10–19' bin. Each data value belongs to exactly ONE interval. Before you submit, add up all your frequencies and check that the total matches the number of data values you started with.

  2. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A histogram of goals-per-game shows: 0–1: 9 players, 2–3: 6, 4–5: 3, 6–7: 1. What shape is this distribution?

    1. APerfectly symmetric
    2. BSkewed right (most players low, tail toward high values)
    3. CFlat / uniform (all bars equal)
    4. DSkewed left (most players high, tail toward low values)

    💡 Solution Rationale: Find the tallest bars first: they are at the low end, 0–1. The bars shrink as goals increase. That means the tail stretches to the right. A tail pointing right means the distribution is skewed right.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Display Data: Histograms is counting a boundary value in two intervals at once — for example, putting the value 10 in both the '0–9' bin and the '10–19' bin. SECTION 3 MATHEMATICAL WRITING & ERROR ANALYSIS [Reasoning & Critique]
  1. 5 ERROR ANALYSIS

    Spot the Counting Mistake

    1. 1Data (points per game):3, 7, 11, 13, 16, 22
    2. 2Intervals:0–9, 10–19, 20–29
    3. 3Frequency count:0–9: 2, 10–19: 2, 20–29: 1
    4. 4Conclusion:So the histogram bars have heights 2, 2, and 1.

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 3. Correct Work: The student missed a value in the 10–19 interval. The values 11, 13, AND 16 all fall in 10–19, so its frequency is 3, not 2. The correct frequency counts are 0–9: 2 (the 3 and 7), 10–19: 3 (the 11, 13, and 16), and 20–29: 1 (the 22), which totals 6 — the same as the 6 data values.. Misconception: There are 6 data values but the student's frequencies add to only 5, which is the tip-off. The interval 10–19 holds 11, 13, and 16, so its frequency is 3, making the correct bar heights 2, 3, and 1.
  2. 6 ERROR ANALYSIS

    Spot the Interval Mistake

    1. 1Data (assists per game):1, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18
    2. 2Intervals the student chose:0–5, 6–10, 12–18
    3. 3Frequency count:0–5: 2, 6–10: 2, 12–18: 3
    4. 4Conclusion:The student drew three bars and said the histogram was complete.

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 2. Correct Work: The intervals are not equal in width and they leave a gap. 0–5 and 6–10 each cover 6 values, but 12–18 covers 7 values and the value 11 has no bin at all. Histogram intervals must be equal-width and cover every value with no gaps. Using equal intervals of 5 (0–4, 5–9, 10–14, 15–19) gives frequencies 0–4: 2 (the 1 and 4), 5–9: 2 (the 6 and 9), 10–14: 1 (the 12), and 15–19: 2 (the 15 and 18), totaling 7.. Misconception: The problem starts at the interval-choosing step: 0–5 and 6–10 are 6 wide, 12–18 is 7 wide, and the value 11 has nowhere to go. Equal-width bins of 5 fix both problems — 0–4: 2, 5–9: 2, 10–14: 1, 15–19: 2, totaling all 7 assist values.
