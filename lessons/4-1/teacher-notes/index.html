Unit 4, Lesson 1: Understand Percent6.AT.4

Everything you need to teach this tomorrow. ~45 min.

Objectives

Content: I can explain a percent as a rate per 100, model it on a decimal grid or tape diagram, and interpret percents greater than 100%.

Language: I can explain what a percent means using the words per hundred, part, whole, and equivalent ratio.

Before class

  • Print / open: guided notes, homework, slides.
  • Have the family page link ready for absent students.
  • Preview the key vocabulary below with ESOL students.

Warm-up

Shanice's gray kitten weighs 4 ounces. The orange kitten weighs 1.5 times as much. What percent of the gray kitten's weight is the orange kitten's weight?

Teaching notes

Key idea: A percent is a ratio comparing a number to 100. The whole is always 100%, so a percent above 100 means MORE than one whole.

I do:

  1. Students voted on a mascot, and 4 out of every 10 voters chose the tiger. I want to know how many that is out of 100.
  2. "4 out of every 10" is a ratio, so I write an equivalent ratio with 100 as the second term. Multiplying both terms by 10 gives 40 out of 100.
  3. On a decimal grid of 100 squares, I shade 40 squares — one square for each percent.
  4. 40 out of 100 is written 40%. So 40% of the students voted for the tiger.

You do: In the same vote, 23 out of every 100 voters chose the eagle. Write that comparison as a percent, and say how you would shade it on a decimal grid. Now a player scores twice the weekly goal. Is that percent more or less than 100%? Explain how you know, using the words whole and per hundred.

Common misconception

A common mistake with percent is scaling only one term of the ratio: reading 7 out of 10 as 7%. A percent compares to 100, so BOTH terms must be multiplied by the same factor — 10 × 10 = 100, so 7 × 10 = 70, giving 70%. Reading it as 7% describes seats that are nearly empty when they are nearly full. A second mistake is treating a percent as an amount: 150% of a 200-point goal is 1.5 × 200 = 300 points, not 150 points.

ESOL / SPED supports

  • Pre-teach: Understand Percent, Percent, Per hundred, Decimal grid, Part-to-whole, Greater than 100% (Spanish + visuals are in the lesson vocabulary).
  • Offer the sentence frames: “I know ___ because ___.” “First, I ___. Then, I ___.” “The answer is ___, so ___.”
  • Chunk the I-do steps; let students restate each step to a partner before moving on.
  • Provide the worked example as a reference while they practice.

Exit ticket

Four out of every ten voters chose the tiger. What percent chose the tiger, and how do you know?

  • 40%, because 4 out of 10 equals 40 out of 100 ✓ correct
  • 4%, because 4 voters chose the tiger
  • 10%, because the ratio is out of 10
  • 14%, because 4 + 10 = 14

Percent means per hundred. Multiplying both terms of 4 : 10 by 10 gives the equivalent ratio 40 : 100, which is 40%.

Reteach

Re-run the I-do example with smaller numbers and have students narrate each step using the key idea.
