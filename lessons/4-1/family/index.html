A percent tells how many out of 100. If 4 out of every 10 people like soup, that is the same as 40 out of 100, or 40%. 100% means the whole thing, and more than 100% means more than the whole.

  • Understand Percent — A percent is a ratio that compares a number to 100, so every percent answers the question “how many out of 100?”
  • Percent — A ratio that compares a number to 100. The word means “per hundred,” and the symbol is %.
  • Per hundred — The meaning hidden inside the word percent: for every group of 100. It is why every percent can be written as a fraction over 100.
  • Decimal grid — A 10-by-10 grid of 100 squares. Shading squares shows a percent directly, one square per percent.
  • Part-to-whole — A comparison of one part against the whole amount. In a percent, the whole is always 100%.
  • Greater than 100% — More than one whole. A percent above 100 means the amount is larger than the thing it is being compared to.
  • Equivalent ratio — A ratio with the same value, made by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number.
  • Tape diagram — A bar split into equal parts to show a percent.

How we did it in class today

  1. Students voted on a mascot, and 4 out of every 10 voters chose the tiger. I want to know how many that is out of 100.
  2. "4 out of every 10" is a ratio, so I write an equivalent ratio with 100 as the second term. Multiplying both terms by 10 gives 40 out of 100.
  3. On a decimal grid of 100 squares, I shade 40 squares — one square for each percent.
  4. 40 out of 100 is written 40%. So 40% of the students voted for the tiger.

Try this together

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

Seven out of every ten socks in the laundry basket are white. What percent of the socks are white, and how do you know?

  1. 7%, because 7 socks are white
  2. 70%, because 7 out of 10 equals 70 out of 100
  3. 10%, because the ratio is out of 10
  4. 17%, because 7 + 10 = 17
Check the answer

The answer is: 70%, because 7 out of 10 equals 70 out of 100

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake with percent is scaling only one term of the ratio: reading 7 out of 10 as 7%. A percent compares to 100, so BOTH terms must be multiplied by the same factor — 10 × 10 = 100, so 7 × 10 = 70, giving 70%. Reading it as 7% describes seats that are nearly empty when they are nearly full. A second mistake is treating a percent as an amount: 150% of a 200-point goal is 1.5 × 200 = 300 points, not 150 points.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
