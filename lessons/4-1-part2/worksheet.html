WordWhat it meansExample
PercentSpanish: Porcentaje A ratio that compares a number to 100. The word means “per hundred,” and the symbol is %. 100% = one whole
Per hundredSpanish: Por cada cien The meaning hidden inside the word percent: for every group of 100. It is why every percent can be written as a fraction over 100. 23% = 23/100
Decimal gridSpanish: Cuadrícula decimal A 10-by-10 grid of 100 squares. Shading squares shows a percent directly, one square per percent. 40 shaded squares out of 100 = 40%
Part-to-wholeSpanish: Parte a todo A comparison of one part against the whole amount. In a percent, the whole is always 100%. The whole = 100%; the part = the percent
Greater than 100%Spanish: Mayor que 100 % More than one whole. A percent above 100 means the amount is larger than the thing it is being compared to. 150% = one and a half wholes
Equivalent ratioSpanish: Razón equivalente A ratio with the same value, made by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number. 2 : 5 → 40 : 100

How it worksWorked examplewriting 'times as much' as a percent

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A puppy weighs 2.5 times as much as a kitten. What percent of the kitten's weight is the puppy?

  1. The kitten's weight is the whole, so it stands for one hundred percent.
  2. Break 2.5 into pieces: 2 wholes plus one half of a whole.
  3. Each whole is one hundred percent; the half is fifty percent.
  4. Add the pieces: two hundred percent plus fifty percent.

Answer: The puppy weighs 250% of the kitten's weight.

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake with percent is scaling only one term of the ratio: reading 7 out of 10 as 7%. A percent compares to 100, so BOTH terms must be multiplied by the same factor — 10 × 10 = 100, so 7 × 10 = 70, giving 70%. Reading it as 7% describes seats that are nearly empty when they are nearly full. A second mistake is treating a percent as an amount: 150% of a 200-point goal is 1.5 × 200 = 300 points, not 150 points.
