Start hereWords for this lesson

Read each word before you begin. Say it out loud.

WordWhat it meansExample
argumentSpanish: argumento A chain of reasons — equations, drawings, or words — used to defend your thinking. “Two jumbo boxes hold more, because 400 × 2 = 800 > 720.”
conjectureSpanish: conjetura A mathematical statement you believe is true but have not yet proven. “A bigger box ALWAYS holds more cereal” — believed, not yet proven.
counterexampleSpanish: contraejemplo A single example that shows a statement is not always true. One jumbo box sold half full of air breaks “bigger always holds more”.
volumeSpanish: volumen The amount of space a solid figure takes up, measured in cubic units. 8 in × 3 in × 10 in = 240 cubic inches of cereal space.
cubic unitSpanish: unidad cúbica A cube with edges 1 unit long — the unit used to measure volume. One cube, 1 unit on every edge — the block volume is counted in.

How it worksWorked example

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

Suppose each large box is 8 inches long, 3 inches wide, and 10 inches tall. Its volume is 8 × 3 × 10 = 240 cubic inches, so three large boxes hold 240 × 3 = 720 cubic inches.

  1. Suppose each jumbo box is 10 inches long, 4 inches wide, and 10 inches tall. Its volume is 10 × 4 × 10 = 400 cubic inches, so two jumbo boxes hold 400 × 2 = 800 cubic inches.
  2. I recommend buying two jumbo boxes because 800 cubic inches is greater than 720 cubic inches — the total volume is greater, so there is more cereal for the same price.

Now you trySame steps, your turn

The steps are the same as the worked example. The numbers are yours. Do the work.

Watch outA common mistake

Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.
