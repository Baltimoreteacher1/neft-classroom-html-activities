Unit 1, Lesson 4: Math is Explaining and Sharing

Standard 5.MD.C.5

What students are learning Qué aprenden

Key idea: Make a clear claim and back it up with math. Use the right units and words, like cubic units for volume. Then test your idea with more examples, and look for one example that would show it is not always true.
Idea clave: Hay que hacer una afirmación clara y respaldarla con cálculos. Se usan las unidades y palabras correctas, como unidades cúbicas para el volumen. Luego se pone a prueba la idea con más ejemplos y se busca un ejemplo que muestre que no siempre es verdad.

Try at home Para hacer en casa

Find two small boxes at home, such as a tissue box and a snack box. Measure each one in inches with a ruler or a paper strip: length, width, and height. Multiply to find each volume in cubic inches, then write one sentence saying which holds more and why.

Busca dos cajas pequeñas en casa, como una caja de pañuelos y una caja de meriendas. Mide cada una en pulgadas con una regla o una tira de papel: largo, ancho y alto. Multiplica para hallar cada volumen en pulgadas cúbicas y escribe una oración que diga cuál cabe más y por qué.

Talk about it:

Which box holds more, and what numbers prove it?

¿Cuál caja tiene más capacidad y qué números lo demuestran?

Words to know Palabras clave

Una afirmación con razones que otros pueden revisar.

A cube with sides 1 unit long. It measures volume.
Un cubo de 1 unidad por lado. Sirve para medir volumen.

How we did it in class today Cómo lo hicimos hoy en clase

  1. Suppose each large box is 8 inches long, 3 inches wide, and 10 inches tall. Its volume is 8 × 3 × 10 = 240 cubic inches, so three large boxes hold 240 × 3 = 720 cubic inches.
  2. Suppose each jumbo box is 10 inches long, 4 inches wide, and 10 inches tall. Its volume is 10 × 4 × 10 = 400 cubic inches, so two jumbo boxes hold 400 × 2 = 800 cubic inches.
  3. I recommend buying two jumbo boxes because 800 cubic inches is greater than 720 cubic inches — the total volume is greater, so there is more cereal for the same price.

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

Two brothers disagree about which of two phone plans is the better deal. Which way of settling it best fits the idea that math is explaining and sharing?

  1. Each brother keeps his work hidden so the other one can't borrow his idea
  2. Each brother explains his reasoning, listens to the other, decides if the other's argument is convincing, and double-checks the math
  3. Only the brother who wrote an equation gets to count his reasons
  4. Whoever finishes first wins, and checking the math again would just waste time

If they get stuck... Si se traban...

Watch out for this: Thinking the taller box always holds more. Volume depends on length, width, AND height, and the answer is in cubic units.
Atención: Pensar que la caja más alta siempre contiene más. El volumen depende del largo, el ancho Y el alto, y la respuesta es en unidades cúbicas.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
Pregunte: «¿Puedes mostrarme cómo lo hiciste? ¿Nos saltamos algún paso?» Hablar de las matemáticas en voz alta ayuda mucho.
