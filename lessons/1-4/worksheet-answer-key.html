Lesson 1.4Math is Explaining and Sharing

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can construct an argument using equations, drawings, or words, and use volume to defend a recommendation.

Start here pageNow you try — model solution

  1. The book offers two word-based reasons: there is less packaging with two boxes, and two boxes are easier to store than three boxes.
  2. Now listen to the other side. One student recommends the three large boxes because the cereal will stay fresher — each box is open for less time — and because greater volume does not always mean more cereal inside.
  3. How convincing is each argument? That judgment is math too.
Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    An egg muffin is about 60 calories. A plate holds 4 egg muffins. About how many calories are on the plate?

    1. AAbout 15
    2. BAbout 64
    3. CAbout 240
    4. DAbout 600

    Hint How many muffins, and how many calories in each one?

    Answer: C — About 240

    Why: 4 muffins at about 60 calories each is 60 × 4 = 240 calories.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A large cereal box is 8 in long, 3 in wide, and 10 in tall. What is its volume?

    1. A21 cubic inches
    2. B80 cubic inches
    3. C240 cubic inches
    4. D240 square inches

    Hint Volume of a box is length × width × height.

    Answer: C — 240 cubic inches

    Why: Volume of a rectangular prism is length × width × height: 8 × 3 × 10 = 240 cubic inches.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Each jumbo box holds 400 cubic inches. How much do TWO jumbo boxes hold?

    1. A200 cubic inches
    2. B402 cubic inches
    3. C800 cubic inches
    4. D4,000 cubic inches

    Hint Two equal boxes — what operation combines them?

    Answer: C — 800 cubic inches

    Why: Two boxes of 400 cubic inches each is 400 × 2 = 800 cubic inches.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  4. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which unit should the garden group use when reporting how much soil fills the beds?

    1. AFeet
    2. BCubic feet
    3. CSquare feet
    4. DDegrees

    Hint Soil fills SPACE, not a line or a flat surface.

    Answer: B — Cubic feet

    Why: Volume is measured in cubic units, so soil that fills a space is measured in cubic feet.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Yuzuki can buy three large boxes or two jumbo boxes for the same price. Give ONE reason in words (no equations) for either choice.

    Sentence starter I recommend ___ because ___ .
    A reason that is not about volume is ___ .

    Sample answer: I recommend two jumbo boxes because there is less packaging, and two boxes are easier to store than three.

  2. 6Answer in complete sentences.

    Why does it help to explain your thinking to others instead of just giving the answer?

    Sentence starter Explaining helps because ___ .
    When someone hears my reasons, they can ___ .

    Sample answer: When I explain my thinking, classmates can decide whether my argument is convincing, catch mistakes I missed, and connect my ideas to their own.

Version B · Answer KeyCore practice

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which statement is a CONJECTURE?

    1. A“8 × 3 × 10 = 240.”
    2. B“I think the two jumbo boxes hold more cereal than the three large boxes.”
    3. C“Volume is measured in cubic units.”
    4. D“Yuzuki bought cereal.”

    Answer: B — “I think the two jumbo boxes hold more cereal than the three large boxes.”

    Why: A conjecture is a mathematical claim you believe but have not yet proven — the jumbo-box claim needs an argument to back it up.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Three large boxes hold 240 cubic inches each. Two jumbo boxes hold 400 cubic inches each. Which is the correct volume comparison?

    1. A240 cubic inches vs. 400 cubic inches — the jumbo option holds more
    2. B480 cubic inches vs. 800 cubic inches — the jumbo option holds more
    3. C720 cubic inches vs. 800 cubic inches — the jumbo option holds more
    4. D720 cubic inches vs. 800 cubic inches — the large option holds more

    Answer: C — 720 cubic inches vs. 800 cubic inches — the jumbo option holds more

    Why: 240 × 3 = 720 and 400 × 2 = 800; since 800 > 720, the two jumbo boxes hold more in total.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    One garden bed is 4 ft long, 3 ft wide, and 2 ft deep. A second bed is 6 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 2 ft deep. How much soil fills BOTH beds?

    1. A19 cubic feet
    2. B24 cubic feet
    3. C48 cubic feet
    4. D96 cubic feet

    Answer: C — 48 cubic feet

    Why: First bed: 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubic feet. Second bed: 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 cubic feet. Together: 24 + 24 = 48 cubic feet.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The group found the volume by decomposing the garden into prisms and adding. Why would they compute it a SECOND, different way?

    1. ABecause the first way is always wrong
    2. BTo get a bigger number for the soil order
    3. CIf both ways give the same answer, the solution is accurate
    4. DBecause volume changes each time you measure it

    Answer: C — If both ways give the same answer, the solution is accurate

    Why: The book models exactly this: two different decompositions both gave 48 cubic feet, so the solutions match and the answer is accurate.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    A student recommends the three large boxes because the cereal stays fresher — each box is open for less time. Is this argument convincing? Explain.

    Sample answer: It is somewhat convincing because it uses a real reason that matters to Yuzuki, not just volume. But it would be stronger with evidence — like how long a box stays fresh once opened. An argument can be convincing without equations if the reasoning is clear.

  2. 6Answer in complete sentences.

    Use precise vocabulary to explain how you would find the soil needed for a bed 5 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 2 ft deep. Include the answer with its unit.

    Sample answer: I would find the volume of the rectangular prism by multiplying length × width × height: 5 × 2 × 2 = 20. Volume is measured in cubic units, so the bed needs 20 cubic feet of soil.

Lesson 1.4Math is Explaining and Sharing

Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can construct an argument using equations, drawings, or words, and use volume to defend a recommendation.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each prism to a DIFFERENT prism with the SAME volume.

    1. A8 × 3 × 10
    2. B4 × 3 × 2
    3. C5 × 8 × 10
    4. D6 × 2 × 4
    • A6 × 4 × 10
    • B6 × 2 × 2
    • C20 × 10 × 2
    • D8 × 3 × 2

    Why: 8 × 3 × 10 and 6 × 4 × 10 both equal 240; 4 × 3 × 2 and 6 × 2 × 2 both equal 24; 5 × 8 × 10 and 20 × 10 × 2 both equal 400; 6 × 2 × 4 and 8 × 3 × 2 both equal 48. Two boxes with different shapes can hold the same amount — a taller box does not always mean more cereal, which is the counterexample that tests the claim in this lesson.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A student claims: “Greater box volume ALWAYS means more cereal inside.” Which is the best counterexample?

    1. AA large box that costs more than a jumbo box
    2. BA jumbo box with more volume that is sold half-filled with air
    3. CTwo jumbo boxes that are easier to store than three large boxes
    4. DA box of a different cereal brand

    Answer: B — A jumbo box with more volume that is sold half-filled with air

    Why: A counterexample must break the claim itself: a bigger box holding less cereal (more air) shows greater volume does not guarantee more cereal.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The garden needs 48 cubic feet of soil. One decomposition used two prisms of 24 cubic feet each. Which of these is ANOTHER decomposition of the same garden volume?

    1. AA 3 × 3 × 3 prism and a 3 × 3 × 2 prism
    2. BA 4 × 4 × 2 prism and a 4 × 2 × 2 prism
    3. CA 4 × 4 × 2 prism and a 4 × 4 × 2 prism
    4. DA 6 × 4 × 2 prism and a 2 × 2 × 2 prism

    Answer: B — A 4 × 4 × 2 prism and a 4 × 2 × 2 prism

    Why: 4 × 4 × 2 = 32 and 4 × 2 × 2 = 16, and 32 + 16 = 48 cubic feet — the same total by a different split.

    Watch for: Reporting volume without cubic units — saying the garden needs '48 feet' of soil, which is a length, or '48 square feet', which is an area.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Answer in complete sentences.

    Another garden bed design is also being considered. What information would you need to compare the two designs, and how would you argue for the one you recommend?

    Sample answer: I would need the dimensions of each design so I could compute both volumes in cubic feet. Then I would build my argument with equations — the volumes — and with words, like which design fits the space better or is easier to reach. I would state my recommendation and defend it with both kinds of evidence.

  2. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Write TWO arguments for the same recommendation about Yuzuki's cereal — one using equations, one using only words. Then say which you think would convince a classmate more, and why.

    Sample answer: Equation argument: 400 × 2 = 800 cubic inches and 240 × 3 = 720 cubic inches, and 800 > 720, so the two jumbo boxes hold more. Word argument: two boxes make less packaging waste and are easier to store. I think the equation argument convinces more people because the numbers can be checked, but the word argument matters to anyone with a small pantry.
