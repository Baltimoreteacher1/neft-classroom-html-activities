How it worksWorked exampledividing a decimal by a whole number

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A ribbon 82.2 meters long is cut into 4 equal pieces. How long is each piece?

  1. Write 82.2 inside the bracket with 4 outside. The divisor is already a whole number.
  2. Put the quotient's decimal point straight above the point in 82.2, then start dividing.
  3. Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — one place-value column at a time.
  4. An amount is still left over, so annex a zero to 82.2 and keep dividing.

Answer: Each piece is 20.55 meters long.

Now you trySame steps, your turn

The steps are the same as the worked example. The numbers are yours. Do the work.

A load weighing 57.6 kilograms is split evenly into 3 boxes. How much does each box hold?

  1. Write inside the bracket and outside. Is the divisor whole?
  2. Place the quotient's decimal point directly above the point in the dividend.
  3. Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down until no digits are left.
  4. Each box holds kilograms. Check by multiplying your quotient.
Answer:kilograms in each box

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake in Multiply Decimals is counting decimal places from only one factor instead of adding the decimal places from BOTH factors. For example, in 0.4 × 0.06, a student multiplies 4 × 6 = 24, then places the decimal point using just one factor's place count (1 place), writing 0.24 instead of the correct answer: 0.4 has 1 decimal place and 0.06 has 2 decimal places, for a total of 3, so 24 becomes 0.024. Before you submit, count the decimal places in EACH factor separately and add them together — that total is how many decimal places belong in your final answer.
