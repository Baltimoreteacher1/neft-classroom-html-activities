Where we left off

Our goal: I can simplify algebraic expressions by combining like terms, explain why the method works, and use it on a problem I have not seen before.

The big idea: Only combine terms that have the same variable part; numbers without a variable combine with each other — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. The rooms need microphones and stands: (4 + 2 + 6) microphones and (3 + 5 + 1) stands.
  2. Microphones are like items, so I add: 4 + 2 + 6 = 12 microphones.
  3. Stands are like items, so I add: 3 + 5 + 1 = 9 stands.
  4. In algebra this is the same as 4m + 2m + 6m + 3s + 5s + 1s = 12m + 9s.

    Warm restartWhich shows 6n + 18 factored using its GREATEST common factor?

    1. A6(n + 3)
    2. B3(2n + 6)
    3. C2(3n + 9)
    4. D6(n + 18)

    How do you know?

    Check each option first. 6(n + 3), 3(2n + 6) and 2(3n + 9) all equal 6n + 18. So all three are correct factorings. But the question asks for the GREATEST common factor, and the GCF of 6 and 18 is 6.

Answer, then say how you know — the reason is the part that counts.

  1. 5

    Think it throughSimplify (8m + 5) + (3m + 7) + (4m + 2).

    1. A15m + 14
    2. B15m + 15
    3. C29m
    4. D12m + 17

    Why is that the answer?

    Add the m-terms: 8m + 3m + 4m = 15m. Add the constants: 5 + 7 + 2 = 14. 15m and 14 are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhat does the 15m tell the festival?

    1. A15 cables are needed for every microphone
    2. BThere are 15 microphones
    3. C15 stages need cables
    4. DThe cables cost $15

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    15 is the coefficient of m, so for each microphone the festival needs 15 cables across all three stages.

  3. 7

    Think it throughIf each stage has 4 microphones (m = 4), how many cables in total?

    1. A74
    2. B60
    3. C64
    4. D29

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    15(4) + 14 = 60 + 14 = 74 cables.

    Back to the modelWhat rule determines whether two terms are 'like terms'? Why can't you combine unlike terms?

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake on this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Simplify Algebraic Expressions is sweeping a constant into the variable terms — for example, simplifying 6x + 4 + 2x as 12x by adding all three numbers (6 + 4 + 2 = 12) instead of recognizing that 4 has no variable and cannot combine with 6x and 2x. The correct simplified expression is 8x + 4 (combine 6x + 2x = 8x, and let the 4 stay on its own). Before you submit, check that every term you combined has the exact same variable part.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 10

    Say moreTo simplify 6x + 4 + 2x + 7, which are the like terms, and how do you combine them?

    Combine the x-terms by adding coefficients; combine the constants separately.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — Simplify 6x + 3 + 2x + 5.

    1. A8x + 8
    2. B8x² + 8
    3. C62x + 35
    4. D16x

    Explain your choice.

    6x + 2x = 8x and 3 + 5 = 8, so the simplified expression is 8x + 8.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 6.14Explain your thinking — Which expression is equivalent to 7(x + 3)?

    1. A7x + 21
    2. B7x + 3
    3. Cx + 21
    4. D7x + 10

    How do you know?

    7(x + 3) = 7 × x + 7 × 3 = 7x + 21. The 7 must multiply BOTH terms — multiplying only the x gives 7x + 3, multiplying only the 3 gives x + 21, and adding 7 + 3 instead of multiplying gives 7x + 10.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 6.14A student wrote 8(12 + 9) = 8 × 12 + 9 = 105. What went wrong?

    1. AThey forgot to multiply the 9 by 8 as well
    2. BThey added instead of multiplying
    3. CThey used the wrong prices
    4. DThey distributed too many times

    How do you know?

    Distributing means the 8 multiplies BOTH terms: 8 × 12 AND 8 × 9. Skipping the second one loses $63.
