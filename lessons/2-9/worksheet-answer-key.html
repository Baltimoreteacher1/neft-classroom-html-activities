Lesson 2.9Describe Data by Mean Absolute Deviation

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can find the mean absolute deviation (MAD) to describe how spread out data is.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Mark and label each value on the number line.

    The mean is 10. Place each data value on the number line, then find how far each is from the mean: Data: 7, 10, 13.

    Hint 7 is 3 units below the mean. 10 is exactly at the mean. 13 is 3 units above the mean.

    Answer: 3 away at 7; 0 away at 10; 3 away at 13

    Why: With the mean at 10, the distance of each value is what you count on the line: 7 sits 3 ticks below, 10 sits right on the mean for a distance of 0, and 13 sits 3 ticks above. Distance never carries a sign, so the absolute deviations are 3, 0, and 3, and their average is (3 + 0 + 3) ÷ 3 = 2.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    The mean of a data set is 15. One value is 11. What is the absolute deviation of that value from the mean?

    1. A4
    2. B−4
    3. C11
    4. D26

    Hint Absolute deviation asks: how FAR is the value 11 from the mean of 15? A distance is always a positive number.

    Answer: A — 4

    Why: Deviation = 11 − 15 = −4. Absolute deviation = |−4| = 4.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Mean Absolute Deviation is adding the signed deviations (value − mean) instead of their absolute values, so the positives and negatives cancel out to 0. For 6, 8, 10, 12 (mean = 9), the deviations are −3, −1, 1, 3 — they sum to 0, but that is NOT the MAD. Before averaging, take the absolute value of every deviation so each becomes a positive distance: |−3| = 3, |−1| = 1, |1| = 1, |3| = 3. Sum = 8, so MAD = 8 ÷ 4 = 2. A MAD of exactly 0 is a warning sign — it only happens if every value in the data set equals the mean.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A data set has absolute deviations of 3, 1, 5, 2, 4. What is the MAD?

    1. A1
    2. B3
    3. C5
    4. D15

    Hint You're given the five absolute deviations already — no subtracting needed. MAD is their average.

    Answer: B — 3

    Why: Sum of absolute deviations = 3 + 1 + 5 + 2 + 4 = 15. MAD = 15 ÷ 5 = 3.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Mean Absolute Deviation is adding the signed deviations (value − mean) instead of their absolute values, so the positives and negatives cancel out to 0. For 6, 8, 10, 12 (mean = 9), the deviations are −3, −1, 1, 3 — they sum to 0, but that is NOT the MAD. A MAD of exactly 0 is a warning sign — it only happens if every value in the data set equals the mean.

  3. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    1. A6
    2. B−6
    3. C26

    Hint Same skill as before: absolute deviation is the distance between the value 26 and the mean 20.

    Answer: A — 6

    Team A has MAD = 2.1 points. Team B has MAD = 6.8 points. Which team is more consistent?

    1. ATeam B — higher MAD means better performance
    2. BBoth are equally consistent
    3. CCannot tell from MAD alone
    4. DTeam A — lower MAD means scores are closer to the mean

    Hint Consistent means scores stay close to the team's average game after game. MAD measures exactly that closeness.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Data setA player's points: 6, 10, 8. Mean = (6 + 10 + 8) ÷ 3 = 8.
    2. 2Find each deviation (value − mean)6 − 8 = −2, 10 − 8 = 2, 8 − 8 = 0
    3. 3Find the MADMAD = (−2 + 2 + 0) ÷ 3 = 0 ÷ 3 = 0

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint The deviations −2 and 2 cancel to 0 — that's the warning sign. MAD is never 0 unless every value equals the mean.

    Mistake: Step 3. The deviations −2, 2, and 0 were found correctly, but the "Find the MAD" step adds them with their signs, so the −2 and the +2 cancel and force a total of 0. The A in MAD means absolute, so take each distance first: 2, 2, and 0. MAD = (2 + 2 + 0) ÷ 3 = 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.33 points. A MAD of 0 would mean every score equals 8, which is not true here.

    Correct work: Take the ABSOLUTE value of each deviation before adding, so positives and negatives can't cancel: |−2| = 2, |2| = 2, |0| = 0. MAD = (2 + 2 + 0) ÷ 3 = 4 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.33 points.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Data setA swimmer's lap times: 2, 5, 8, 9. Mean = (2 + 5 + 8 + 9) ÷ 4 = 24 ÷ 4 = 6.
    2. 2Find each absolute deviation|2 − 6| = 4, |5 − 6| = 1, |8 − 6| = 2, |9 − 6| = 3
    3. 3Find the MADSum of absolute deviations = 4 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 10. MAD = 10 ÷ 3 ≈ 3.33

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. The absolute deviations 4, 1, 2, and 3 are all correct, and they total 10. The mistake is in the "Find the MAD" step, which divides by 3 instead of by the 4 values in the data set. MAD = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5, so the swimmer's times sit an average of 2.5 away from the mean of 6.

    Correct work: Divide by the NUMBER OF VALUES, not one less. There are 4 values, so MAD = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. The student divided by 3 instead of 4.

Lesson 2.9Describe Data by Mean Absolute Deviation

Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can find the mean absolute deviation (MAD) to describe how spread out data is.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort each scenario by whether low or high variability is desirable.

    Groups: Want LOW MAD (consistent) Want HIGH MAD (lots of spread)
    • Want LOW MAD (consistent)A factory making bolts that must be exactly 5 cm long
    • Want LOW MAD (consistent)A bus schedule where arrival times should be reliable
    • Want HIGH MAD (lots of spread)A game show where contestants try to score as many different point values as possible

    Why: Bolts that must be exactly 5 cm and buses that should arrive on schedule both depend on results staying close to the target, so a low MAD is what you want — a large one would mean unusable bolts or unreliable buses. The game show is the opposite: its whole point is variety in the point values, so a high MAD is desirable there.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the MAD Error

    1. 1Data set5, 10, 15, 20, 25
    2. 2Find meanSum = 75, Count = 5, Mean = 15
    3. 3Find deviations−10, −5, 0, 5, 10
    4. 4Find MADMAD = (−10 + −5 + 0 + 5 + 10) ÷ 5 = 0

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 4. The mean of 15 and the deviations −10, −5, 0, 5, 10 are all correct. The "Find MAD" step is where it breaks: adding signed deviations always gives 0, because the values below the mean exactly balance the ones above. Use absolute values instead — 10, 5, 0, 5, 10 — which sum to 30, so MAD = 30 ÷ 5 = 6.

    Correct work: You must use absolute values of the deviations: |−10| = 10, |−5| = 5, |0| = 0, |5| = 5, |10| = 10. MAD = (10 + 5 + 0 + 5 + 10) ÷ 5 = 30 ÷ 5 = 6.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    Two basketball teams both average 75 points per game. Team A's last 5 scores: 73, 76, 74, 77, 75. Team B's last 5 scores: 60, 90, 65, 85, 75. Calculate the MAD for each team and explain which team a coach would prefer if they need predictable scoring.

    Look for: MAD, consistent, spread, lower, higher, predictable, absolute deviation

    Why: Both teams average 75, so the mean cannot tell them apart — the MAD can. Team A's distances from 75 are 2, 1, 1, 2, 0, which total 6, so its MAD is 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2. Team B's distances are 15, 15, 10, 10, 0, which total 50, so its MAD is 50 ÷ 5 = 10. A coach who needs predictable scoring would choose Team A, because a MAD of 1.2 means its scores stay within about a point or two of 75 every game.
