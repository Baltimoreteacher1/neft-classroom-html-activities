Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Point A is at (-3, 5). It is reflected over the x-axis to A', then A' is reflected over the y-axis to A''. What are the coordinates of A''?

    1. A(-3, -5)
    2. B(-3, 5)
    3. C(3, -5)
    4. D(3, 5)

    Hint Two flips happen in a row — do them one at a time, not both at once.

    Answer: C — (3, -5)

    Why: Over x-axis: (-3, 5) → (-3, -5). Then over y-axis: (-3, -5) → (3, -5). Both coordinates changed sign.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Reflect Points Across Axes is flipping the sign of the wrong coordinate — changing the x-coordinate when reflecting over the x-axis, instead of the y-coordinate (or vice versa for the y-axis). For example, to reflect (3, -5) over the x-axis, a student might flip the x-coordinate and write (-3, -5), but reflecting over the x-axis only changes the sign of the y-coordinate: (3, -5) becomes (3, 5). Before you submit, ask: 'Did I flip the sign of the coordinate that matches the axis I'm reflecting over — y for the x-axis, x for the y-axis?'

    1. A(-7, 2)
    2. B(-7, -2)
    3. C(2, 7)
    4. D(7, -2)

    Hint Folding along an axis makes that axis the mirror line, and the point lands the same distance away on the other side.

    Answer: D — (7, -2)

    Why: Folding along (reflecting across) the x-axis flips the sign of the y-coordinate only. (7, 2) becomes (7, -2).

    Vega reflects a palm-tree marker at (8, -6) across the x-axis. Which coordinate changes its sign?

    1. AThe x-coordinate
    2. BThe y-coordinate
    3. CBoth coordinates
    4. DNeither coordinate

    Hint A mirror line only changes the direction you travel to cross it — everything parallel to that line stays put.

    Answer: B — The y-coordinate

    Why: Across the x-axis the rule is (x, y) -> (x, -y), so only the y-coordinate flips sign. (8, -6) becomes (8, 6).

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 2 Extension — Vega's Fold Mix-Up

    1. 1ProblemReflect the marker (6, 4) across the x-axis.
    2. 2Step 1The x-axis is the fold line, so flip the sign of the x-coordinate.
    3. 3Step 2Change 6 to -6 and keep y as 4.
    4. 4Answer(-6, 4)

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint The axis you fold over is the one that stays put. Folding over the x-axis means the x-value should NOT change.

    Mistake: Step 2. Step 1 says to flip the x-coordinate for an x-axis fold, but the axis you fold over is the one whose coordinate stays put. The rule is (x, y) → (x, -y), so the marker (6, 4) mirrors to (6, -4), landing straight below the original.

    Correct work: Reflecting across the x-axis follows the rule (x, y) -> (x, -y): keep the x-coordinate and flip the y-coordinate. Vega flipped the x instead of the y. The correct mirror image of (6, 4) is (6, -4).

  2. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find the Reflection Error

    1. 1ProblemReflect (-3, 5) over the y-axis
    2. 2Student thinkingThe y-axis is vertical, so I change the y-coordinate
    3. 3Student answer(-3, -5)

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint The y-AXIS is the vertical line. Points flip ACROSS it, meaning they move left/right. Which coordinate controls left/right?

    Mistake: Step 2. The student's thinking step says the vertical y-axis changes the y-coordinate, but the axis name tells you which coordinate stays the same. Over the y-axis the rule is (x, y) → (-x, y), so (-3, 5) reflects to (3, 5), not (-3, -5).

    Correct work: Reflecting over the y-axis changes the x-coordinate, not the y-coordinate. The correct reflection of (-3, 5) over the y-axis is (3, 5). The student confused which coordinate changes for each axis.
