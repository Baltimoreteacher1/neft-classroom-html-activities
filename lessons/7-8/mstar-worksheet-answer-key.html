Lesson 7-8: Ordered Pairs in All Four Quadrants

Grade 6 Mathematics · Standard 6.NOS.7

These are MSTAR-style questions written for this curriculum to rehearse the format. They are not official Maryland assessment items. MSTAR — Maryland's new state test, first given in spring 2027 — uses the same kinds of questions you see here: selected response, select-all, two-part evidence questions, and written responses.

Part A — Skills Check

One point each.

Question 1Selected response — correct: C

In which quadrant is the point (-4, 5)?

  • AQuadrant I
  • BQuadrant III
  • CQuadrant II
  • DQuadrant IV

The x-coordinate is negative (left) and the y-coordinate is positive (up). Left and up is Quadrant II.

Question 2Selected response — correct: C

A point has a positive x-coordinate and a negative y-coordinate. Which quadrant is it in?

  • AQuadrant I
  • BQuadrant II
  • CQuadrant IV
  • DQuadrant III

Positive x (right) and negative y (down) = Quadrant IV (bottom-right).

Question 3Selected response — correct: C

Which point is located on the x-axis?

  • A(-4, -4)
  • B(0, 4)
  • C(4, 0)
  • D(4, 4)

A point is on the x-axis when its y-coordinate is 0. (4, 0) is on the x-axis.

Question 4Selected response — correct: D

Point A is at (-5, 3). If you move 8 units to the right, what are the new coordinates?

  • A(-13, 3)
  • B(-5, 11)
  • C(-5, -5)
  • D(3, 3)

Moving right means adding to the x-coordinate: -5 + 8 = 3. The y stays the same. New point: (3, 3).

Question 5Selected response — correct: D

In which quadrant is the point (-3, 5)?

  • AQuadrant I
  • BQuadrant III
  • CQuadrant IV
  • DQuadrant II

Negative x with positive y places the point in Quadrant II.

Question 6Selected response — correct: D

What are the coordinates of a point 4 units right and 2 units down from the origin?

  • A(-4, 2)
  • B(-2, 4)
  • C(2, -4)
  • D(4, -2)

Right is +x and down is -y, so the point is (4, -2).

Part B — Test-Format Questions

Scoring notes and distractor rationales below each item.

Question 7Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: A

In which quadrant does the point (−4, 3) lie?

  • AQuadrant II
  • BQuadrant I
  • CQuadrant III
  • DQuadrant IV

A negative x with a positive y is the upper-left region, which is Quadrant II.

  • B: Quadrant I holds two positive coordinates. Here x is negative.
  • C: Quadrant III holds two negative coordinates. Here y is positive.
  • D: Quadrant IV has x positive and y negative. This point is the other way round.

Part B — correct answer: C

What sign pattern identifies Quadrant IV?

  • A(−, −)
  • B(−, +)
  • C(+, −)
  • D(+, +)

Quadrant IV is the lower-right region: x is positive (right of the y-axis) and y is negative (below the x-axis).

Question 8Select all that apply — correct: A, B, C, E

Select ALL statements that are true about the four quadrants:

  • AQuadrant I contains points with both coordinates positive.
  • BQuadrant III contains points with both coordinates negative.
  • CA point on an axis belongs to no quadrant.
  • DThe quadrants are numbered clockwise starting from the upper right.
  • EQuadrant II contains points of the form (−, +).

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — the quadrants are numbered COUNTERCLOCKWISE from the upper right: I, II, III, IV.

Part C — Show Your Thinking

Model answer and scoring guidance.

Question 9Written response · 2 points

A point starts in Quadrant I at (4, 6). Describe a move that puts it in Quadrant III. What must happen to both coordinates? Can you give the exact new coordinates after your move?

Model answer: Quadrant III is the bottom-left region, so both coordinates have to end up negative. Starting at (4, 6), you need to move more than 4 units left to push x below 0 and more than 6 units down to push y below 0. Moving 6 left and 8 down, for example, lands on (-2, -2), which is Quadrant III because x and y are both negative.

Award 2 points for a complete explanation with correct mathematics, 1 point for a correct answer with thin reasoning, 0 for an unrelated response.
