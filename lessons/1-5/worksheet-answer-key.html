Lesson 1.5Math is Finding Patterns

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can find patterns and pattern rules, use them to make generalizations, and check my solutions with a table of values.

Start here pageNow you try — model solution

  1. Add 25 dimes: 25 × $0.10 = $2.50, bringing the total to $52.50.
  2. Add 15 nickels: 15 × $0.05 = $0.75, and 25 pennies: 25 × $0.01 = $0.25.
  3. $50.00 + $2.50 + $0.75 + $0.25 = $53.50. It works — and 25 pennies is a multiple of 5, just as the pattern predicted. Now use the patterns to find a different combination.

    Name a pattern you noticed in the coin values, and how it could help you count money faster.

    Sentence starter I noticed that ___ .
    This helps because instead of counting one at a time, I can ___ .

    Sample answer: Every 100 coins is worth 100 times one coin: 100 pennies are $1.00, 100 nickels are $5.00, 100 dimes are $10, 100 quarters are $25. So I can count coins in groups of 100 instead of one at a time.

Part 1Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Player A had 18 points before making 10 two-pointers in a row. How many points does Player A have after the streak?

    1. A20
    2. B28
    3. C38
    4. D48

    Answer: C — 38

    Why: Ten two-pointers add 10 × 2 = 20 points, and 18 + 20 = 38 points.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Akela's jar totals $53.50. Why can the number of pennies only be a multiple of 5?

    1. ABecause the total's cents end in 0, and only pennies make amounts that aren't multiples of 5 cents
    2. BBecause pennies always come in rolls of 5
    3. CBecause there are exactly 5 kinds of coins in the jar
    4. DBecause $53.50 is divisible by 5

    Answer: A — Because the total's cents end in 0, and only pennies make amounts that aren't multiples of 5 cents

    Why: Nickels, dimes, and quarters are each worth a multiple of 5 cents. For the total to land on a 0, the pennies must also add up to a multiple of 5 cents — so their count is a multiple of 5.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  3. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which combination of coins totals exactly $53.50?

    1. A100 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies
    2. B200 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies
    3. C200 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 20 pennies
    4. D200 quarters, 30 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies

    Answer: B — 200 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies

    Why: $50.00 + $2.50 + $0.75 + $0.25 = $53.50 exactly.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  4. 4Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    How does a table of values check that Player A really scored 38 points?

    1. AIt rounds the answer to the nearest ten
    2. BIt replaces the pattern rule so you don't need one
    3. CIt proves the answer without doing any calculations
    4. DIt lists the score after every shot, so you can see the pattern land exactly on 38

    Answer: D — It lists the score after every shot, so you can see the pattern land exactly on 38

    Why: The book's table lists each step — 20, 22, 24, … — and verifies that the calculations were correct: Player A scored 38.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    Use the multiple-of-5 pattern to find a DIFFERENT combination of coins that totals $53.50. Show that it works.

    Sample answer: I'll use 210 quarters: 210 × $0.25 = $52.50. Then 5 dimes: $0.50. Then 9 nickels: $0.45. Then 5 pennies: $0.05. Total: $52.50 + $0.50 + $0.45 + $0.05 = $53.50, and 5 pennies is a multiple of 5.

  2. 6Answer in complete sentences.

    After the streaks, Player A has 38 and Player B has 36. Player B started 12 points behind but nearly caught up. What about the two pattern rules explains that?

    Sample answer: Player B's rule adds 3 while Player A's adds 2, so every shot the gap shrinks by 1. The streak was 10 shots, so the 12-point gap shrank by 10, from 12 down to 2.

Lesson 1.5Math is Finding Patterns

Challenge · Answer KeyExtension

Learning target I can find patterns and pattern rules, use them to make generalizations, and check my solutions with a table of values.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each input-output table to its pattern rule.

    1. AIn: 1, 2, 3 → Out: 5, 10, 15
    2. BIn: 1, 2, 3 → Out: 3, 5, 7
    3. CIn: 2, 4, 6 → Out: 1, 2, 3
    4. DIn: 1, 2, 3 → Out: 2.5, 5, 7.5
    • AMultiply by 5
    • BDouble, then add 1
    • CDivide by 2
    • DMultiply by 2.5

    Why: A rule must hold for every row: 5, 10, 15 is ×5; 3, 5, 7 is 2n + 1 (the pair 2 → 5 rules out 'add 2'); 1, 2, 3 from 2, 4, 6 is ÷2; and 2.5, 5, 7.5 is ×2.5 — the same kind of decimal rate as the whale's 2.5 cm per day. Checking every pair is how a generalization earns trust.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    If both players kept their streaks going, after how many MORE shots would Player B tie Player A?

    1. A1 more shot
    2. BThey can never tie
    3. C2 more shots
    4. D10 more shots

    Answer: C — 2 more shots

    Why: The gap is 38 − 36 = 2 and shrinks by 1 each shot (3 − 2 = 1). After 2 more shots: Player A has 38 + 4 = 42 and Player B has 36 + 6 = 42.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A sei whale measures 4.5 meters at birth and grows about 2.5 centimeters each day. About how many days until it reaches 15 meters?

    1. AAbout 4.2 days
    2. BAbout 42 days
    3. CAbout 420 days
    4. DAbout 600 days

    Answer: C — About 420 days

    Why: It must grow 15 − 4.5 = 10.5 meters = 1,050 centimeters. At 2.5 cm per day: 1,050 ÷ 2.5 = 420 days.

    Watch for: Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 4Answer in complete sentences.

    Write a generalization about ANY player who makes n shots in a row worth p points each, starting from s points. Then test it on Player A.

    Sample answer: Final score = s + n × p. For Player A: 18 + 10 × 2 = 38, which matches the table of values, so the generalization holds.

  2. 5Answer in complete sentences.

    The lesson says we 'evaluate the reasonableness of solutions as we work and make adjustments.' Describe a moment solving the coin-jar problem where you would stop and adjust.

    Sample answer: If my running total passed $53.50 while I still had coins to place, I would stop — the solution is no longer reasonable — and trade some quarters for dimes or nickels to come back down. Checking as I go beats discovering the overshoot at the end.
