    Each quarter has a value of $0.25. Following the pattern, what is the value of 100 quarters?

    1. A$2.50
    2. B$25.00
    3. C$100.25
    4. D$250.00

    💡 Solution Rationale: 100 × $0.25 = $25.00 — the same pattern as 100 pennies making $1.00 and 100 nickels making $5.00.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  3. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Player A scores a two-pointer on every shot. What is Player A's pattern rule?

    1. AAdd 2
    2. BMultiply by 2
    3. CAdd 3
    4. DAdd 18

    💡 Solution Rationale: Each number in Player A's points is 2 greater than the number before it, so the rule is to add 2.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  4. 4 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Player B had 6 points, then made 10 three-pointers in a row. How many points does Player B have now?

    1. A9
    2. B16
    3. C30
    4. D36

    💡 Solution Rationale: Ten three-pointers add 10 × 3 = 30 points, and 6 + 30 = 36 points.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

SECTION 2 REAL-WORLD CONTEXTS & PROBLEM SOLVING [Applications]
  1. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Player A had 18 points before making 10 two-pointers in a row. How many points does Player A have after the streak?

    1. A20
    2. B28
    3. C38
    4. D48

    💡 Solution Rationale: Ten two-pointers add 10 × 2 = 20 points, and 18 + 20 = 38 points.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  2. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Akela's jar totals $53.50. Why can the number of pennies only be a multiple of 5?

    1. ABecause the total's cents end in 0, and only pennies make amounts that aren't multiples of 5 cents
    2. BBecause pennies always come in rolls of 5
    3. CBecause there are exactly 5 kinds of coins in the jar
    4. DBecause $53.50 is divisible by 5

    💡 Solution Rationale: Nickels, dimes, and quarters are each worth a multiple of 5 cents. For the total to land on a 0, the pennies must also add up to a multiple of 5 cents — so their count is a multiple of 5.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  3. 4 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    Which combination of coins totals exactly $53.50?

    1. A100 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies
    2. B200 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies
    3. C200 quarters, 25 dimes, 15 nickels, 20 pennies
    4. D200 quarters, 30 dimes, 15 nickels, 25 pennies

    💡 Solution Rationale: $50.00 + $2.50 + $0.75 + $0.25 = $53.50 exactly.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  4. 5 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    How does a table of values check that Player A really scored 38 points?

    1. AIt rounds the answer to the nearest ten
    2. BIt replaces the pattern rule so you don't need one
    3. CIt proves the answer without doing any calculations
    4. DIt lists the score after every shot, so you can see the pattern land exactly on 38

    💡 Solution Rationale: The book's table lists each step — 20, 22, 24, … — and verifies that the calculations were correct: Player A scored 38.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

SECTION 3 MATHEMATICAL WRITING & ERROR ANALYSIS [Reasoning & Critique]
  1. 6 OPEN RESPONSE

    Use the multiple-of-5 pattern to find a DIFFERENT combination of coins that totals $53.50. Show that it works.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: I'll use 210 quarters: 210 × $0.25 = $52.50. Then 5 dimes: $0.50. Then 9 nickels: $0.45. Then 5 pennies: $0.05. Total: $52.50 + $0.50 + $0.45 + $0.05 = $53.50, and 5 pennies is a multiple of 5.
  2. 7 OPEN RESPONSE

    After the streaks, Player A has 38 and Player B has 36. Player B started 12 points behind but nearly caught up. What about the two pattern rules explains that?

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: Player B's rule adds 3 while Player A's adds 2, so every shot the gap shrinks by 1. The streak was 10 shots, so the 12-point gap shrank by 10, from 12 down to 2.

SECTION 2 REAL-WORLD CONTEXTS & PROBLEM SOLVING [Applications]
  1. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    If both players kept their streaks going, after how many MORE shots would Player B tie Player A?

    1. A1 more shot
    2. BThey can never tie
    3. C2 more shots
    4. D10 more shots

    💡 Solution Rationale: The gap is 38 − 36 = 2 and shrinks by 1 each shot (3 − 2 = 1). After 2 more shots: Player A has 38 + 4 = 42 and Player B has 36 + 6 = 42.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

  2. 3 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A sei whale measures 4.5 meters at birth and grows about 2.5 centimeters each day. About how many days until it reaches 15 meters?

    1. AAbout 4.2 days
    2. BAbout 42 days
    3. CAbout 420 days
    4. DAbout 600 days

    💡 Solution Rationale: It must grow 15 − 4.5 = 10.5 meters = 1,050 centimeters. At 2.5 cm per day: 1,050 ÷ 2.5 = 420 days.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): Reporting only the points from the streak (20 or 30) and forgetting to add the points each player had before the streak began.

SECTION 3 MATHEMATICAL WRITING & ERROR ANALYSIS [Reasoning & Critique]
  1. 4 OPEN RESPONSE

    Write a generalization about ANY player who makes n shots in a row worth p points each, starting from s points. Then test it on Player A.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: Final score = s + n × p. For Player A: 18 + 10 × 2 = 38, which matches the table of values, so the generalization holds.
  2. 5 OPEN RESPONSE

    The lesson says we 'evaluate the reasonableness of solutions as we work and make adjustments.' Describe a moment solving the coin-jar problem where you would stop and adjust.

    💡 Exemplar Response: Sample Answer: If my running total passed $53.50 while I still had coins to place, I would stop — the solution is no longer reasonable — and trade some quarters for dimes or nickels to come back down. Checking as I go beats discovering the overshoot at the end.
