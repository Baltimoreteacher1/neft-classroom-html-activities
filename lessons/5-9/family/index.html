Unit 5, Lesson 9: Area of Regular Polygons6.GR.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A regular polygon is a closed shape with straight sides where every side is the same length and every angle is the same size. We can decompose it (break it apart) into equal triangles from the center, then add up their areas.

Key idea: Area of Regular Polygons. Formula: Area = (Number of Sides) × (Area of One Triangle). 1. Decompose the regular polygon from center into n congruent triangles. 2. Find the area of one triangle using A = ½ × side × apothem (height). 3. Multiply by the total number of sides

Example

  1. My hexagon has 6 equal sides. Each side is 6 feet, and each center triangle has a height of 5.2 feet.
  2. First I find one triangle: A = ½ × base × height = ½ × 6 × 5.2.
  3. I multiply: 6 × 5.2 = 31.2, then ½ × 31.2 = 15.6 square feet.
  4. A hexagon has 6 sides, so it has 6 equal triangles.
  5. I multiply: 6 × 15.6 = 93.6. So the total area is 93.6 square feet.

Common mistake

A common mistake in Area of Regular Polygons is stopping after finding the area of ONE triangle and reporting that as the total area — or adding the number of triangles instead of multiplying. Remember: total area = (one triangle's area) × (number of sides), not (one triangle's area) + (number of sides).
