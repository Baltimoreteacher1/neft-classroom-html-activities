Unit 5, Lesson 9: Area of Regular Polygons

What students are learning

Key idea: A regular polygon has equal sides. Split it from the middle into matching triangles. Find the area of one triangle, then multiply by how many triangles there are. A hexagon made of 6 triangles of 12 square cm each has area 6 × 12 = 72 square cm.

How we did it in class today

  1. My hexagon has 6 equal sides. Each side is 6 feet, and each center triangle has a height of 5.2 feet. Use the rounded height to estimate the area.
  2. First I find one triangle: A = ½ × base × height = ½ × 6 × 5.2.
  3. I multiply: 6 × 5.2 = 31.2, then ½ × 31.2 = 15.6 square feet.
  4. A hexagon has 6 sides, so it has 6 equal triangles.
  5. I multiply: 6 × 15.6 = 93.6. So the total area is 93.6 square feet.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Area of Regular Polygons is stopping after finding the area of ONE triangle and reporting that as the total area — or adding the number of triangles instead of multiplying. Remember: total area = (one triangle's area) × (number of sides), not (one triangle's area) + (number of sides).

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
