Start hereWords for this lesson

Read each word before you begin. Say it out loud.

WordWhat it meansExample
HistogramSpanish: Histograma A bar graph that groups data into equal ranges. The bars touch. Bars side by side: 0-9 pts (3 players), 10-19 pts (8 players), 20-29 pts (4 players)
FrequencySpanish: Frecuencia How many times a value shows up. Bar height shows the count
IntervalSpanish: Intervalo A range of numbers used to group data. 0-9, 10-19, 20-29 are intervals of width 10 — each covers 10 values
DistributionSpanish: Distribución How the data is spread out. Most values near the peak
VariabilitySpanish: Variabilidad How spread out the numbers are. Data in just 2 intervals = low variability. Data across 6 intervals = high variability
Frequency tableSpanish: Tabla de frecuencias A table that lists each interval and how many values fall in it. 10–19 . . . 3 means three values are between 10 and 19.

How it worksWorked examplereading the shape of a histogram

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A histogram has a bar of height 2 over 60-69, height 4 over 70-79, and height 2 over 80-89.

  1. Sketch the bars at heights 2, 4, and 2 over the three intervals, touching with no gaps.
  2. Find the peak: the tallest bar covers the interval 70-79.
  3. Compare sides: the bar left of the peak and the bar right of it are the same height.
  4. Matching sides mean the data is nearly symmetric around the peak.

Answer: The histogram peaks over 70-79 and is symmetric, so a typical value falls in 70-79.

Watch outA common mistake

A common mistake in Display Data: Histograms is counting a boundary value in two intervals at once — for example, putting the value 10 in both the '0–9' bin and the '10–19' bin. Each data value belongs to exactly ONE interval. Before you submit, add up all your frequencies and check that the total matches the number of data values you started with.
