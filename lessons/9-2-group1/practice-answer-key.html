Where we left off

Our goal: With my small group, I can use a table and a graph to determine and analyze the relationship between two variable quantities — one step at a time, with support.

The big idea: Each row of the table becomes one ordered pair — (independent variable, dependent variable) — plotted as one point on the graph.

Model to copy — Watch me build the ticket graph

  1. Each ticket costs $45, so the total cost is 45 times the number of tickets: 1 ticket is $45, 2 tickets are $90, 3 tickets are $135.
  2. The number of tickets is the independent variable, so it goes first in each ordered pair: (1, 45), (2, 90), (3, 135).
  3. I label one axis 'Number of tickets' and the other 'Total cost ($)', then plot each pair as a point.
  4. The points rise to the right: as the number of tickets increases, the total cost increases.

    Let's graph the Sandia Peak tram together: The tram travels 1,200 feet every minute. Which quantity is independent — time or distance? Time is independent, so our pairs look like (minutes, feet): (1, 1200), (2, 2400), (5, 6000). The ascent takes 15 minutes, so the last point is (15, 18000) — because 15 times 1,200 is 18,000 feet. Each point means: after THIS many minutes, the tram has traveled THIS many feet.

  1. 5

    Think it throughOn Nakai's graph, which variable is plotted on the independent axis (x-axis)?

    1. Ayears
    2. Binterest earned
    3. Cprincipal amount
    4. Daccount balance

    Why is that the answer?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Years is the independent variable because interest earned changes as a result of how many years have passed, not the other way around. Interest earned is dependent; the principal ($750) is a fixed starting value, not a changing quantity being graphed; account balance combines principal and interest, which isn't what this graph plots.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhy does the graph rise by the same amount every year instead of curving upward more steeply over time?

    1. ANakai earns simple interest — the same $26.25 each year, calculated only on the original $750 principal
    2. BNakai earns compound interest, which grows faster each year
    3. CThe interest rate increases by 3.5% every year
    4. DNakai deposits an extra $26.25 into the account every year

    How do you know?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal each year, so the account gains a constant $26.25 annually, producing a straight (linear) rise. Compound interest would make the graph curve upward faster, a rising rate would change the yearly amount, and extra deposits would not be interest at all.

  3. 7

    Think it throughA point on the graph is located at (4, x). Based on the interest rate, what value of x makes this point correct?

    1. A$105
    2. B$78.75
    3. C$855
    4. D$131.25

    Explain your thinking.

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    After 4 years Nakai has earned 4 × $26.25 = $105 in interest. $78.75 is only 3 years' worth of interest, $855 confuses interest earned with the total account balance (principal plus interest), and $131.25 is 5 years' worth.

  4. 8

    Back to the modelFootball tickets cost $45 each: 1 ticket is $45, 2 tickets are $90, 3 tickets are $135. When you write the ordered pair for 3 tickets, which number goes first — 3 or 135 — and why?

    The ordered pair is (___, ___) because ___ comes first.

    The number of tickets is the independent variable, and the independent variable always comes first in an ordered pair, so the point is (3, 135), not (135, 3).

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Reversing the coordinates of a point — writing (cost, tickets) instead of (tickets, cost). The independent variable always comes first in the ordered pair.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    A point (tickets, cost) matches the relationship only if cost equals 45 times the number of tickets.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowQuick check — you've got this: On the football ticket graph, what does the point (3, 135) represent?

    1. A3 tickets cost a total of $135
    2. B135 tickets cost a total of $3
    3. C3 tickets cost $135 each
    4. DThe game lasts 135 minutes

    Explain your choice.

    I know it is ___ because ___ .

    The independent variable comes first in an ordered pair. Here that is the number of tickets, so 3 is the tickets. Then find the cost: 3 × $45 = $135. The point pairs 3 tickets with a total cost of $135.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 9.1Quick check — you've got this: A dog walker charges by the hour. Which sentence is correct?

    1. AThe total charge depends on the number of hours, so hours is the independent variable.
    2. BThe number of hours depends on the total charge, so the charge is the independent variable.
    3. CBoth quantities are independent variables.
    4. DNeither quantity depends on the other.

    The walker chooses how many hours to work; the money that results responds to that choice. Hours is subject to choice, so hours is independent and the total charge is dependent.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 9.1The shop makes 20 pizzas in a week. Which of these does NOT depend on the number of pizzas sold?

    1. Athe total cost of cheese
    2. Bthe total cost of sauce
    3. Cthe number of employees
    4. Dthe total spent on cheese and sauce combined

    Staff count (6 employees) is fixed and stays the same regardless of pizza volume. Cheese cost, sauce cost, and their combined total all scale up as more pizzas are made, so those three DO depend on the number of pizzas.
