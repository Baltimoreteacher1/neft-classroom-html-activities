    1. 1ProblemFind the LCM of 6 and 9.
    2. 2Multiples of 66, 12, 18, 24
    3. 3Multiples of 99, 18, 27
    4. 4Choose the answerMaya saw that 9 is bigger than 6, so she wrote LCM = 9.

The LCM has to be in both lists, and the first number in both is 18, so the LCM of 6 and 9 is 18.

    (Lesson 6.13) Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Find the prime factorization of 45, using exponentsStart: 45
    2. 2First split45 = 5 × 9
    3. 3Break down 99 = 3 × 3, so 45 = 5 × 3 × 3
    4. 4Write with exponents45 = 3² × 5²

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: 45 = 5 × 3 × 3 has one 5 and two 3's — not two 5's. The exponent on a prime factor shows how many times that factor appears, so the correct form is 45 = 3² × 5 (the 5 has no exponent shown because it appears only once). An exponent records how many times a prime appears, and the last step gave the 5 an exponent of 2 even though 45 = 3 × 3 × 5 contains only one 5. Two 3s and one 5 means the correct form is 45 = 3² × 5.

    (Lesson 6.7) Find the error

    1. 1ProblemFind the LCM of 6 and 8.
    2. 2Step 1Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48
    3. 3Step 2Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48
    4. 4Step 3Both lists contain 48, so the LCM is 48.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: Step 3 picks a common multiple, but not the LEAST one. Both lists also contain 24, and 24 comes first — so the LCM is 24. (48 is the product 6 × 8, which is always A common multiple, just rarely the smallest.) Two numbers have infinitely many common multiples. The word LEAST is doing real work in the name, and scanning the lists from the start rather than the end is what finds it.

Correct work: LCM = 24 (the least common multiple is the SMALLEST shared multiple, not just any common multiple) Jaylen listed the common multiples 24 and 48 correctly, but in the final step he chose 48 instead of the smaller one. LEAST common multiple means the smallest shared multiple, so the answer is 24.

    (Lesson 6.13) Find Marcus's Mistake

    1. 1Find prime factorization of 48Start: 48
    2. 2First split48 = 6 × 8
    3. 3Break down 66 = 2 × 3
    4. 4Break down 88 = 2 × 4
    5. 5Final answer48 = 2 × 3 × 2 × 4

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: 8 = 2 × 4, but 4 is not prime. 4 = 2 × 2, so 8 = 2 × 2 × 2. The correct prime factorization is 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁴ × 3. Marcus stopped the 8 branch while a composite factor was still in it: 4 is not prime because 4 = 2 × 2, so 8 = 2 × 2 × 2, not 2 × 4. Finishing that branch gives 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁴ × 3.
