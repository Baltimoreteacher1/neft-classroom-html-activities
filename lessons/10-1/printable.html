🚀 Launch

Some people think math is just a school subject with little relevance outside the classroom. But math is everywhere — chefs use it all day in restaurant kitchens, gardeners use it in planter beds, and it is hiding in jobs and hobbies all around you.

Math is an integral part of nearly every profession

Chefs supervise a team of cooks preparing everything from appetizers to desserts. Watch how much of that job is quietly mathematical.

Key idea: People use math every day in their professions and their hobbies — and it is a good tool for solving problems around your house, too.

Watch me find the math in a chef's day

  • When chefs plan menus, they determine the number of items on the menu and calculate the price point for each item.
  • They decide on the portion size of each item and project how much food is needed for each day.
  • They calculate how much of each ingredient to order — that is counting, measuring, and multiplying, all before a single dish is cooked.

Let's find the math in a garden together

  • A planter that is 4 feet long and 2 feet wide has an area of 4 × 2 = 8 square feet — that tells us how many plants fit.
  • If the soil needs to be 1 foot deep, the volume is 4 × 2 × 1 = 8 cubic feet — that tells us how much soil to buy.
  • Gardeners also measure seed depth and the distance between seeds. What other garden decisions need math?

Now you try

  • Choose another person who works in a restaurant — a server, a dishwasher, a manager — and describe the math they would use.
  • Then name one hobby you have and one place math hides inside it.
