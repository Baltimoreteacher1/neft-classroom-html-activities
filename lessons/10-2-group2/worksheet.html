WordWhat it meansExample
symmetricSpanish: simétrico Having two halves that match — one side is a mirror image of the other. A butterfly: the left wing is the mirror of the right.
mirror imageSpanish: imagen reflejada A copy of a figure flipped across a line, so the two halves face each other. Fold the layer down the middle: each leaf lands on its partner.
bilateral symmetrySpanish: simetría bilateral Reflective symmetry in plants and animals — the two sides of the body mirror each other. All 17,000+ butterfly species have it — two sides, one mirror line.
line of symmetrySpanish: eje de simetría The line you can draw through a figure so each side is a reflection of the other. The line straight down the butterfly's body.
balanceSpanish: equilibrio An even, steady arrangement — neither side feels heavier than the other. Two matching halves — neither side feels heavier than the other.

How it worksWorked example

These numbers are not on your problems. The steps are. Follow them with your own numbers.

A shape has corners at (−3, 0), (3, 0), (2, 4), and (−1, 4). Mia says the y-axis is a line of symmetry. Is she right?

  1. Test the two bottom corners: (−3, 0) and (3, 0)
  2. Find the mirror point of (2, 4): (2, 4) → (−2, 4)
  3. Look for that point on the shape: (−2, 4) is not a corner
  4. No. The corner (2, 4) has no mirror corner at (−2, 4), so the y-axis is not a line of symmetry.

Watch outA common mistake

Thinking a figure lacks bilateral symmetry just because its line of symmetry is not vertical — the mirror line can run in any direction, and the test is whether the two sides match across it.
