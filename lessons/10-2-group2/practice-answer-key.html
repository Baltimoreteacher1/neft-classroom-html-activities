Where we left off

Our goal: I can use bilateral symmetry to describe structure in nature and in human creations, explain how I can tell, and judge a case I have not seen before.

The big idea: Bilateral symmetry — two sides that are mirror images — appears throughout nature, and it shapes human creations from art to music to architecture — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me find the symmetry in a butterfly

  1. I look at the two sides of the butterfly, and they look like mirror images — the butterfly is symmetric.
  2. All butterflies have reflective symmetry. In plants and animals, this is called bilateral symmetry.
  3. To show it, I draw a line down the middle of the butterfly's body: everything on the left is reflected on the right.

    Warm restartA rectangular garden is 8 ft by 5 ft. What is its area?

    1. A40 square feet
    2. B26 square feet
    3. C13 square feet
    4. D40 cubic feet

    How do you know?

    Area multiplies the two sides: 8 × 5 = 40 square feet. 26 is the distance around it — that is perimeter.

    Warm restartA box measures 3 by 4 by 2 units. What is its volume?

    1. A24 cubic units
    2. B9 cubic units
    3. C12 cubic units
    4. D24 square units

    How do you know?

    Volume multiplies all three edge lengths: 3 × 4 × 2 = 24 cubic units. Adding them gives 9.

  4. 4

    Warm restartA recipe makes 4 servings using 2 cups of rice. How much rice is in one serving?

    1. A1/2 cup
    2. B2 cups
    3. C8 cups
    4. D4 cups

    How do you know?

    Split the 2 cups evenly among 4 servings: 2 ÷ 4 = 1/2 cup per serving.

Answer, then say how you know — the reason is the part that counts.

  1. 5

    Think it throughA butterfly's two wings look like mirror images of each other across a line down the center of its body. What is this type of symmetry called?

    1. ARotational symmetry
    2. BBilateral symmetry
    3. CRadial symmetry
    4. DPoint symmetry

    Why is that the answer?

    Bilateral symmetry means one line divides a shape into two mirror-image halves, like the butterfly's wings. Rotational and radial symmetry involve a shape repeating around a center point, and point symmetry involves matching after a 180° turn — neither describes a single mirror line.

  2. 6

    Think it throughTo test whether a flower has bilateral symmetry, what should you do first?

    1. ACount the number of petals
    2. BMeasure the flower's height
    3. CDraw a line through the center and compare the two sides
    4. DCompare the flower to a different type of flower

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    Bilateral symmetry is tested by drawing a line through the center and checking whether the two sides are mirror images. Counting petals or measuring height doesn't test whether the halves match, and comparing to a different flower doesn't test this flower's own symmetry.

  3. 7

    Think it throughYour friend says only one of two flowers has bilateral symmetry. You test both by drawing a line through each flower's center and find both sides match on both flowers. What is most likely true?

    1. AThe friend is right — only one flower truly has symmetry
    2. BBoth flowers have bilateral symmetry, but the second flower's line of symmetry likely runs in a direction the friend didn't try
    3. CSymmetry cannot be tested in flowers
    4. DThe two flowers must be identical in shape to both be symmetric

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    Since the test showed both flowers have a line where the sides mirror each other, both are bilaterally symmetric — the friend likely only checked one direction on the second flower and missed its line of symmetry. Flowers don't need to look alike to each have their own symmetry.

  4. 8

    Back to the modelThe butterfly's two wings are mirror images across a line down the middle of its body. Why does drawing that line matter for proving the butterfly is symmetric?

    A figure is symmetric only if there is a specific line where one side is a mirror image of the other.

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • Having two halves that match as mirror images is being ___. → symmetric

  • Reflective symmetry in plants and animals is called ___ ___. → bilateral symmetry
  • The line you draw so each side reflects the other is the ___ ___ ___. → line of symmetry

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Thinking a figure lacks bilateral symmetry just because its line of symmetry is not vertical — the mirror line can run in any direction, and the test is whether the two sides match across it.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    The test is whether some line divides the figure into two mirror-image halves; the letter R fails that test, but a ladybug's left and right sides match.

  1. 12

    Show what you knowExplain your thinking — Which statement best shows what this lesson means by 'math is beauty'?

    1. AMath creates beautiful designs like bilateral symmetry, found in nature and used in human art, music, and architecture
    2. BOnly things made by mathematicians can be beautiful
    3. CButterflies are the only living things with symmetry
    4. DBeauty and math are opposites — one is feeling and one is numbers

    Explain your choice.

    The summary makes the claim in two parts. Bilateral symmetry appears naturally in many plants and animals. It also influences human creations. You can see it in art, in music, and in architecture.

  2. 13

    From Lesson 10.1Explain your thinking — Which statement best shows what this lesson means by 'math is everywhere'?

    1. APeople use math every day in their professions and hobbies, and it solves problems around the house too
    2. BMath only appears in jobs that involve money
    3. CMath is a school subject with little relevance to life outside the classroom
    4. DOnly trained professionals like chefs are able to use math

    How do you know?

    The summary makes the claim in three parts: professions use math, hobbies use math, and it is a good tool for problems around your house.

  3. 14

    From Lesson 10.1Leaving the water running while brushing wastes an average of 4 gallons compared to turning the water on only to rinse. If one person brushes twice a day for a year (365 days), how many gallons would they save by turning the water off?

    1. A730 gallons
    2. B1,460 gallons
    3. C2,920 gallons
    4. D5,840 gallons

    How do you know?

    4 gallons × 2 brushings per day × 365 days = 2,920 gallons. 1,460 comes from forgetting to count both daily brushings, 730 comes from using too small a savings amount per brushing, and 5,840 comes from doubling the whole product instead of just the daily brushings.
