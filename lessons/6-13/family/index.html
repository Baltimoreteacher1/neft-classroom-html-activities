What students are learning

Prime factorization means writing a number as a product of prime numbers only. A prime number can be divided only by 1 and itself, like 2, 3, 5, and 7.

Key idea: A prime number can only be divided evenly by 1 and itself, like 2, 3, 5, and 7. Prime factorization means breaking a number into primes that multiply to make it. Keep splitting until every number is prime: 40 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 5.

A prime number can only be divided evenly by 1 and itself, like 2, 3, 5, and 7. Prime factorization means breaking a number into primes that multiply to make it. Keep splitting until every number is prime: 40 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 5.

  • Prime Factorization — Writing a whole number as a product of only prime numbers.
  • Prime number — A number bigger than 1 that you can only divide by 1 and itself.
  • Composite number — A number bigger than 1 that you can divide by more than just 1 and itself.
  • Factor — A factor of a whole number divides it evenly, with no remainder. For example, 3 is a factor of 12 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
  • Factor tree — A picture that splits a number into its prime numbers, step by step.
  • Exponent — A small number that tells how many times to multiply a number by itself.

How we did it in class today

  1. I want the prime factorization of 60. I start by splitting it: 60 = 6 × 10.
  2. 6 is not prime, so I break it: 6 = 2 × 3. Both 2 and 3 are prime, so I stop those branches.
  3. 10 is not prime, so I break it: 10 = 2 × 5. Both 2 and 5 are prime, so I stop.
  4. Now every factor is prime: 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
  5. Using exponents, I write it as 2² × 3 × 5. I check: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 60. Correct.

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Prime Factorization is stopping the factor tree too soon — leaving a composite number like 4, 6, 8, or 9 in the final answer instead of continuing to split it. For example, writing 20 = 4 × 5 as the prime factorization (4 is composite) instead of finishing with 20 = 2 × 2 × 5. Before you submit, check every number in your final answer: if any factor can still be broken into smaller factors, the tree isn't finished yet.

What to ask: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
