Lesson 6-13: Prime Factorization

Grade 6 Mathematics · Standard 6.NOS.4

These are MSTAR-style questions written for this curriculum to rehearse the format. They are not official Maryland assessment items. MSTAR — Maryland's new state test, first given in spring 2027 — uses the same kinds of questions you see here: selected response, select-all, two-part evidence questions, and written responses.

Part A — Skills Check

One point each.

Question 1Selected response — correct: C

Which of the following is a prime number?

  • A9
  • B15
  • C17
  • D21

17 has exactly two factors: 1 and 17. 15 = 3 × 5, 21 = 3 × 7, and 9 = 3 × 3, so they are all composite.

Question 2Selected response — correct: A

What is the prime factorization of 30?

  • A2 × 3 × 5
  • B2 × 15
  • C3 × 10
  • D5 × 6

30 = 2 × 15 = 2 × 3 × 5. All three factors (2, 3, 5) are prime, so 2 × 3 × 5 is the prime factorization.

Question 3Selected response — correct: A

What is the prime factorization of 18?

  • A2 × 3 × 3
  • B2 × 9
  • C3 × 6
  • D6 × 3

18 = 2 × 9 = 2 × 3 × 3. Both 2 and 3 are prime, so 2 × 3 × 3 is the prime factorization.

Question 4Selected response — correct: B

Which of these numbers is composite?

  • A23
  • B27
  • C29
  • D31

27 = 3 × 9 = 3 × 3 × 3, so it has more than two factors. 23, 29, and 31 are all prime.

Question 5Selected response — correct: D

Two students found different factor trees for 60. Student A started with 2 × 30. Student B started with 6 × 10. Which statement is true?

  • AOnly Student A gets the correct prime factorization
  • BOnly Student B gets the correct prime factorization
  • CThey will get different prime factorizations
  • DBoth get the same prime factorization: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic says every composite number has exactly one prime factorization. No matter how you start the factor tree, you always end with 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.

Question 6Selected response — correct: A

The first lock panel shows 90. What is the prime factorization of 90?

  • A2 × 3 × 3 × 5
  • B2 × 45
  • C3 × 30
  • D9 × 10

90 = 2 × 45 = 2 × 9 × 5 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5. Every factor is prime, so the lock accepts 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

Part B — Test-Format Questions

Scoring notes and distractor rationales below each item.

Question 7Two-part question

Part A — correct answer: A

What is the prime factorization of 72?

  • A2³ × 3²
  • B2² × 3³
  • C2 × 3 × 12
  • D8 × 9

A factor tree for 72 ends in the primes 2, 2, 2, 3, and 3. Written with exponents in ascending order, that is 2³ × 3², and 8 × 9 = 72 confirms the value.

  • B: The exponents are swapped: four times twenty-seven is 108. Seventy-two holds three 2s and two 3s.
  • C: 12 is not prime. Keep factoring it into 2 times 2 times 3.
  • D: Both factors are right but neither is prime. Break 8 into three 2s and 9 into two 3s.

Part B — correct answer: B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

  • AThe tree gives two 2s and three 3s, so the prime factorization is 2² × 3³.
  • BEvery branch of the factor tree for 72 ends in a prime: 2, 2, 2, 3, 3. Grouping the repeated primes with exponents gives 2³ × 3², and 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72.
  • C72 splits into 2 × 3 × 12, and the branches can stop there.
  • D72 splits into 8 × 9, and those are the factors to report.

A prime factorization must have every branch ending in a prime number. The number 12 is composite, and 8 and 9 are both composite, so those splits are unfinished. Counting the primes gives three 2s and two 3s.

Question 8Select all that apply — correct: A, B, E

Select ALL statements that are true about the number 45.

  • A45 is a composite number.
  • BThe prime factorization of 45 is 3² × 5.
  • CThe prime factorization of 45 is 5 × 9.
  • D45 is a prime number.
  • EThe only prime factors of 45 are 3 and 5.
  • F1 is a prime factor of 45.

45 has the factors 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, and 45, so it is composite, not prime. Its factor tree ends in the primes 3, 3, and 5, giving 3² × 5 = 9 × 5 = 45. The expression 5 × 9 is not a prime factorization because 9 is composite, and 1 is never a prime factor because a prime has exactly two factors.

Part C — Show Your Thinking

Model answer and scoring guidance.

Question 9Written response · 2 points

Choose any two-digit composite number. Show TWO different factor trees that both lead to the same prime factorization. Explain why every composite number has only one prime factorization.

Model answer: A strong answer picks one two-digit composite number and finishes two trees that begin with different first splits — for 24, starting with 2 × 12 and starting with 4 × 6 both end at 2 × 2 × 2 × 3. The lists always match because splitting only pulls composite factors apart into primes that were already inside the number, so the set of primes cannot change.

Award 2 points for a complete explanation with correct mathematics, 1 point for a correct answer with thin reasoning, 0 for an unrelated response.
