Unit 5, Lesson 2: Determine the Area of Triangles6.GR.1

A quick guide for families and for any student who missed class.

What students are learning

A triangle has three sides. The base is one side, and the height is the straight-up (perpendicular) distance from the base to the top corner.

Key idea: Area of Triangles. Formula: Area = ½ × Base × Height (A = ½ b · h). 1. Identify the base and the perpendicular height to that base. 2. A triangle is exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height. 3. Multiply base by height, then divide by 2

Key words

  • Area of a Triangle — The space inside a triangle. A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and height, so A = ½ × b × h.
    Español: Área de un triángulo — El espacio dentro de un triángulo. Un triángulo es la mitad de un rectángulo con la misma base y altura, por eso A = ½ × b × h.
  • Base — Any side of a triangle can be the base. Once you pick a base, the height must be measured perpendicular (at a 90° angle) to it.
    Español: Base — Cualquier lado de un triángulo puede ser la base. Una vez que eliges la base, la altura debe medirse perpendicular (en un ángulo de 90°) a ella.
  • Height — The straight-up distance from the base to the top corner.
    Español: Altura — La distancia recta desde la base hasta la esquina de arriba.
  • Area — How much space is inside a flat shape.
    Español: Área — Cuánto espacio hay dentro de una figura plana.
  • Perpendicular — Two lines that meet to make a square corner (90 degrees).
    Español: Perpendicular — Dos líneas que se unen formando una esquina recta (90 grados).
  • Composite figure — A shape made by putting two or more simple shapes together.
    Español: Figura compuesta — Una figura formada al juntar dos o más figuras simples.
  • Formula — A math rule written with symbols.
    Español: Fórmula — Una regla matemática escrita con símbolos.
  • Parallel — Two lines or sides that stay the same distance apart and never meet.
    Español: Paralelas — Dos líneas o lados que se mantienen a la misma distancia y nunca se cruzan.

Example

  1. My triangular garden has a base of 12 feet and a height of 8 feet.
  2. I write the formula: A = ½ × base × height.
  3. I put in the numbers: A = ½ × 12 × 8.
  4. I multiply base × height first: 12 × 8 = 96.
  5. I take half: ½ × 96 = 48. So the area is 48 square feet.

Practice at home

Match each shape to its area. Watch the rule: triangles use A = ½ × base × height, but parallelograms and rectangles use A = base × height (do NOT take half).

  1. Triangle b=8, h=6
  2. Triangle b=10, h=8
  3. Triangle b=12, h=5

Common mistake

A common mistake in Area of Triangles is forgetting the ½ — students multiply base × height like a rectangle and stop there, or they use the triangle's slanted side instead of the perpendicular height that's already given. Always check: did I take half, and did I use the straight-up height, not the slanted side?
