  1. 1 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    What is the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm?

    1. A30 sq cm
    2. B60 sq cm
    3. C16 sq cm
    4. D30 cm

    💡 Solution Rationale: A = ½ × b × h = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 square centimeters.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Area of Triangles is forgetting the ½ — students multiply base × height like a rectangle and stop there, or they use the triangle's slanted side instead of the perpendicular height that's already given. Always check: did I take half, and did I use the straight-up height, not the slanted side?

  2. 2 MULTIPLE CHOICE

    A triangle has an area of 24 sq ft and a base of 8 ft. What is the height?

    1. A6 ft
    2. B3 ft
    3. C16 ft
    4. D12 ft

    💡 Solution Rationale: A = ½ × b × h → 24 = ½ × 8 × h → 24 = 4h → h = 6 ft.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Area of Triangles is forgetting the ½ — students multiply base × height like a rectangle and stop there, or they use the triangle's slanted side instead of the perpendicular height that's already given. Always check: did I take half, and did I use the straight-up height, not the slanted side?

    Why do we divide by 2 when finding the area of a triangle?

    1. AA triangle is exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height
    2. BTriangles have 2 equal sides
    3. CThe base is always twice the height
    4. DWe always divide area formulas by 2

    💡 Solution Rationale: A triangle with base b and height h fits inside a rectangle with the same base and height. The triangle covers exactly half the rectangle, so A = ½ × b × h.

    ⚠️ Watch for (Misconception): A common mistake in Area of Triangles is forgetting the ½ — students multiply base × height like a rectangle and stop there, or they use the triangle's slanted side instead of the perpendicular height that's already given. Always check: did I take half, and did I use the straight-up height, not the slanted side?

    4. D42 ft

    💡 Solution Rationale: A = ½ × 14 × 6 = ½ × 84 = 42 sq ft.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Identify base and height:base = 9 ft, height = 4 ft
    2. 2Write the formula:A = ½ × base × height
    3. 3Multiply base × height:9 × 4 = 36
    4. 4Final answer:A = 36 sq ft

    Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

    Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 4. Correct Work: The student multiplied 9 × 4 = 36, but that is the area of the whole rectangle around the triangle. A triangle is only half of that rectangle, so the answer must still be cut in half: ½ × 36 = 18 sq ft.. Misconception: Multiplying 9 × 4 = 36 is a good first move, but the answer stops there. 36 sq ft is the area of the rectangle that surrounds the triangle, and a triangle covers only half of that rectangle. Taking the ½ once gives A = ½ × 36 = 18 sq ft.

      Spot the Common Mistake

      1. 1Identify base and height:base = 12 ft, height = 7 ft
      2. 2Write the formula:A = ½ × base × height
      3. 3Substitute values:A = ½ × 12 × 7
      4. 4Calculate:A = (½ × 12) × (½ × 7) = 6 × 3.5 = 21 sq ft

Multiply base × height first, then take half just once: 12 × 7 = 84, then ½ × 84 = 42 sq ft.. Misconception: There is only ONE ½ in A = ½ × base × height, but the calculation halved the base and the height separately. Multiply first, then halve a single time: 12 × 7 = 84, and ½ × 84 = 42 sq ft. Halving twice cuts the area to a quarter of the rectangle, which is why 21 came out as exactly half of the true answer.

      Which step contains the error? Explain the mathematical misconception and write the correct calculation below.

      Key Analysis: Mistake is in Step 4. Correct Work: 14 × 9 = 126, then ½ × 126 = 63 sq m. The student added 14 + 9 = 23 instead of multiplying 14 × 9 = 126.. Misconception: The setup A = ½ × 14 × 9 is written correctly, but the calculation added 14 + 9 = 23 instead of multiplying. Base and height must be multiplied: 14 × 9 = 126, and then ½ × 126 = 63 sq m.
    2. 2 OPEN RESPONSE

      A rectangle is 12 ft by 8 ft. A diagonal line cuts it into two triangles. What is the area of each triangle? Explain how the triangle area formula relates to the rectangle area formula.

      💡 Exemplar Response: Key Terms to Look For: rectangle, triangle, half, diagonal, base, height, formula Explanation: The rectangle's area is 12 × 8 = 96 sq ft, and one diagonal cuts it into two identical triangles, so each triangle is 96 ÷ 2 = 48 sq ft. The formula says the same thing: each triangle keeps the rectangle's base of 12 and height of 8, and A = ½ × 12 × 8 = 48 sq ft. The ½ in the triangle formula IS that diagonal cut.
