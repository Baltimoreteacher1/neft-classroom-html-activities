🚀 Launch

Your architecture firm is designing a park with triangular garden beds. The client wants to know exactly how much soil to order, which means calculating the area of each triangular section. The first garden has a base of 12 feet and a height of 8 feet.

How do we find the area of a triangle?

A triangle has three sides. The base is one side, and the height is the straight-up (perpendicular) distance from the base to the top corner.

Key idea: Area of Triangles. Formula: Area = ½ × Base × Height (A = ½ b · h). 1. Identify the base and the perpendicular height to that base. 2. A triangle is exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height. 3. Multiply base by height, then divide by 2

Watch me

  • My triangular garden has a base of 12 feet and a height of 8 feet.
  • I write the formula: A = ½ × base × height.
  • I put in the numbers: A = ½ × 12 × 8.
  • I multiply base × height first: 12 × 8 = 96.
  • I take half: ½ × 96 = 48. So the area is 48 square feet.

Let's try together

  • Now a smaller triangle: base = 10 cm, height = 6 cm.
  • First, what is 10 × 6? Yes, 60.
  • Now take half: what is ½ × 60? Yes, 30.
  • So the area is 30 square centimeters.

Now it's your turn

  • Next you will plot the triangle on a grid and see how it fits inside a rectangle.
  • Remember: multiply base × height, then take half.

Write the formulaA = ½ × base × height
