🚀 Launch

The basketball league wants to compare scoring across teams. Here are the points scored by each player on the Wildcats in their last game: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 18, 22, 24, 28. The league needs a quick visual that shows the spread, the middle 50%, and any high or low scorers. Time to build a box plot!

What is a box plot?

A box plot is a simple picture that shows how a set of data is spread out, using just five key numbers: the lowest value, the first quartile (Q1), the median (middle), the third quartile (Q3), and the highest value.

Key idea: The box always holds the middle 50% of the data, and the line inside the box is the median.

Watch me build one

  • My data, in order: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 18, 22, 24, 28. There are 11 numbers.
  • The middle number is the 6th one, 14, so the median = 14.
  • The lower half is 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. Its middle is 8, so Q1 = 8.
  • The upper half is 15, 18, 22, 24, 28. Its middle is 22, so Q3 = 22.
  • The lowest is 4 and the highest is 28. I draw a box from 8 to 22, a line at 14, and whiskers out to 4 and 28.

Let's try one together

  • New, smaller data set: 2, 5, 5, 8, 11. First, what is the median (the middle value)?
  • The lower half is 2, 5 and the upper half is 8, 11. What is Q1? What is Q3?
  • What are the lowest and highest values? Now picture the box stretching from Q1 to Q3 with the median line inside.

Now it's your turn

  • On the next screens you'll find the five-number summary for the Wildcats' scores yourself: the minimum, Q1, the median, Q3, and the maximum.
  • Then you'll place each value on the number line to build the box plot — just like I did.
