Learning target I can graph the solutions of an inequality on a number line.

  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    Which inequality represents: 'You must be at least 48 inches tall to ride'?

    1. Ah > 48
    2. Bh ≥ 48
    3. Ch < 48
    4. Dh ≤ 48

    Answer: B — h ≥ 48

    Why: 'At least' means greater than or equal to, so h ≥ 48.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph Inequalities is treating ≥ and ≤ the same as > and < and drawing the wrong circle. For example, students often graph x ≥ 6 with an OPEN circle (as if it were x > 6), which wrongly excludes 6 even though 6 itself makes x ≥ 6 true. A second common mistake is choosing the right circle but shading the wrong direction — for x < 9 students sometimes shade right instead of left. Always check the symbol twice: once for the circle (does it have an 'or equal to' line?) and once for the shading direction (does the symbol point toward larger or smaller numbers?).

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Detective Okafor is filing clues. Sort each inequality by the circle its graph uses.

    Groups: Open circle (boundary NOT included) Closed circle (boundary IS included)
    • Open circle (boundary NOT included)c > 20
    • Open circle (boundary NOT included)t < 11
    • Closed circle (boundary IS included)w ≥ 14
    • Closed circle (boundary IS included)m ≤ 2

    Why: c > 20 and t < 11 use strict symbols, so 20 and 11 are not solutions and both get open circles. w ≥ 14 and m ≤ 2 include their boundaries — 14 ≥ 14 and 2 ≤ 2 are both true — so both get closed circles. The direction of the shading does not affect this sort at all.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each clue inequality to the correct graph in the case file.

    1. An > 13
    2. Bn ≤ 16
    3. Cn ≥ 20
    4. Dn < 17
    • AOpen circle at 13, shaded right
    • BClosed circle at 16, shaded left
    • CClosed circle at 20, shaded right
    • DOpen circle at 17, shaded left

    Why: Read the circle from the symbol and the shading from the direction it points: n > 13 → open circle at 13, shaded right; n ≤ 16 → closed circle at 16, shaded left; n ≥ 20 → closed circle at 20, shaded right; n < 17 → open circle at 17, shaded left. Checking the boundary number first eliminates half the cards fast.

    A detective determines that fewer than 12 people were at the scene. The inequality p < 12 represents the number of people p. She graphs this with an open circle at 12 and shading to the left. How would the graph change if 12 or fewer people were at the scene?

    1. AThe shading would flip to the right
    2. BNothing would change
    3. CThe circle would be closed (filled in) at 12

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Level 2 Extension — Crack the Graphing Mistake

    1. 1Clue inequalityk > 19 (the loot weighed more than 19 pounds)
    2. 2Circle typeClosed circle at 19
    3. 3Shade directionShade right
    4. 4Final graphClosed circle at 19, shaded right

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 2. The loot weighed MORE than 19 pounds, so 19 itself is ruled out and k > 19 needs an open circle — but the circle type step filled it in. Shading right is correct, so the finished graph is an open circle at 19 shaded right.

    Correct work: k > 19 uses the strict symbol >, so 19 is NOT a solution. The circle must be OPEN, not closed. The shading right is correct. Final graph: open circle at 19, shaded right.
