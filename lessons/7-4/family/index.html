How we did it in class today

  1. I will compare -7 and -5. They are both negative, so I look at the number line.
  2. -7 is 7 steps left of zero. -5 is only 5 steps left of zero.
  3. -7 is farther to the left, so -7 is the smaller number.
  4. I write -7 < -5. The open side of the symbol points to the bigger number, -5.

  1. 9, 4, 0, -1, -6
  2. -1, -6, 0, 4, 9
  3. -6, -1, 0, 4, 9

Check the answer

The answer is: -6, -1, 0, 4, 9

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake in Compare and Order Integers is thinking that a negative number with a bigger digit is the bigger value — for example, believing -15 is greater than -8 because 15 is greater than 8. It's the opposite for negatives: the number farther to the left on the number line (farther from zero) is always the LESSER value, so -15 < -8. Before you submit, check your comparison against the number line, not just the digits.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
