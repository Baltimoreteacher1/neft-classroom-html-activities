    Find the unit rate for each option to compare them.

    OptionRatioUnit Rate
    Juice A6 oz for 2 servings3 oz per serving
    Juice B10 oz for 4 servings
    Juice C9 oz for 3 servings

    Hint Row 1 shows the move: 6 oz for 2 servings became 3 oz per serving by dividing.

    Why: A unit rate tells you how many ounces are in exactly 1 serving, so divide ounces by servings. Juice B is 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 oz per serving and Juice C is 9 ÷ 3 = 3 oz per serving. Written per serving, Juice A and Juice C pour the same amount and Juice B pours the least.

    Chef A uses 2 cups of cheese for every 8 crackers. Chef B uses 3 cups of cheese for every 9 crackers. Who uses more cheese per cracker?

    1. AChef A
    2. BChef B
    3. CThey use the same amount
    4. DCannot determine

    Hint You can't compare 2 cups and 3 cups directly — the chefs use different numbers of crackers.

    Answer: B — Chef B

    Why: Chef A: 2÷8 = 0.25 cups per cracker. Chef B: 3÷9 ≈ 0.33 cups per cracker. Chef B uses more cheese per cracker.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Compare Ratios is thinking the ratio with the bigger raw number automatically wins — like assuming Chef Tran's 8 tablespoons of cocoa must be more chocolatey than Chef Reyes's 6 tablespoons, without checking that the milk amounts (14 oz vs. 10 oz) are different. Never compare the raw numbers alone — always scale both ratios to the same amount or find each unit rate first.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the problemChef Priya makes hot chocolate with 2 tbsp cocoa for 4 oz milk. Chef Owen makes hot chocolate with 3 tbsp cocoa for 8 oz milk. Whose hot chocolate is more chocolatey?
    2. 2Compare the cocoaChef Owen has 3 tbsp cocoa. Chef Priya has 2 tbsp cocoa.
    3. 3Draw a conclusionSince 3 is greater than 2, Chef Owen's hot chocolate must be more chocolatey.
    4. 4ConclusionChef Owen's hot chocolate is more chocolatey.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Look at Step 3. The chef compared only the cocoa amounts (2 and 3) without checking whether the milk amounts were equal.

    Mistake: Step 3. The conclusion compared 3 tbsp against 2 tbsp even though the milk amounts differ (8 oz vs. 4 oz), and cocoa amounts only tell you something when the milk matches. The unit rates settle it: Chef Priya has 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5 tbsp of cocoa per ounce and Chef Owen has 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375, so Chef Priya's hot chocolate is the more chocolatey one.

    Correct work: You cannot compare 2 tbsp and 3 tbsp directly because the milk amounts are different (4 oz vs. 8 oz). Find the unit rate instead: Chef Priya = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5 tbsp per oz; Chef Owen = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 tbsp per oz. Since 0.5 > 0.375, Chef Priya's hot chocolate is actually more chocolatey — the opposite of what comparing raw cocoa amounts suggested.

  2. 2Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Two frosting recipes both mix drops of food coloring to make green frosting for a cake. Recipe A: 3 drops blue to 5 drops yellow. Recipe B: 4 drops blue to 7 drops yellow. Scale both to a common amount of yellow to compare which recipe is more blue.

    RecipeBlue DropsYellow Drops
    Recipe A35
    Recipe B47
    Recipe A (scaled to 35)35
    Recipe B (scaled to 35)35

    Why: Scaling both recipes to the same amount of yellow makes the blue drops comparable. Recipe A's 5 yellow needs × 7 to reach 35, so its blue becomes 3 × 7 = 21; Recipe B's 7 yellow needs × 5, so its blue becomes 4 × 5 = 20. With 35 yellow drops in each, Recipe A has 21 blue to Recipe B's 20, so Recipe A is the bluer green.

  3. 3Mark and label each value on the number line.

    Place each unit rate on the number line to compare: A = $0.60/oz, B = $0.75/oz, C = $0.50/oz

    Answer: A at 0.6; B at 0.75; C at 0.5

    Why: Writing all three prices as dollars per ounce puts them on the same scale, so they can be ordered on one number line. C ($0.50) sits at 5 tenths, A ($0.60) at 6 tenths, and B ($0.75) three quarters of the way from 0 to 1. Seeing them in that order shows C costs the least per ounce and B costs the most.

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1Read the problemCompare Chef Reyes's dressing (4 tbsp oil : 6 tbsp vinegar) to Chef Tran's dressing (5 tbsp oil : 8 tbsp vinegar). Which dressing is oilier?
    2. 2Find a common denominatorLCD of 6 and 8 is 24.
    3. 3Scale each ratioChef Reyes: 6 × 4 = 24, so oil = 4 × 4 = 16, giving 16:24. Chef Tran: to reach 24 he also multiplies by 4: oil = 5 × 4 = 20, giving 20:24.
    4. 4ConclusionSince 20 > 16, Chef Tran's dressing is oilier.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. Each ratio needs its own multiplier to reach 24. Chef Reyes's vinegar 6 × 4 = 24 is right, but Chef Tran's vinegar is 8, and 8 × 3 = 24, so both parts of his ratio scale by 3: 5 × 3 = 15, giving 15:24 instead of 20:24. Compared at equal vinegar, 16 tbsp of oil beats 15, so Chef Reyes's dressing is the oilier one.

    Correct work: Each ratio needs its own multiplier to reach 24 — Chef Reyes and Chef Tran do not use the same multiplier. Chef Tran's vinegar is 8, and 8 × 3 = 24 (not 8 × 4 = 32), so multiply BOTH parts of his ratio by 3, not 4: oil = 5 × 3 = 15, giving 15:24. Now compare with equal vinegar (24): Chef Reyes has 16 tbsp oil and Chef Tran has 15 tbsp oil. Since 16 > 15, Chef Reyes's dressing is actually oilier.

  1. 1Use the model to solve. Show your work.

    Chef A: 2:3 → with 15 tbsp vinegar, oil = 10 tbsp. Chef B: 3:5 → with 15 tbsp vinegar, oil = 9 tbsp. Chef A uses more oil.

    Why: Fixing the vinegar at 15 tbsp for both chefs makes the oil amounts comparable. Chef A's 2:3 scales by 5 because 3 × 5 = 15, so the oil is 2 × 5 = 10 tbsp; Chef B's 3:5 scales by 3 because 5 × 3 = 15, so the oil is 3 × 3 = 9 tbsp. Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Find Kai's Mistake

    1. 1Read the problemCompare 3:5 and 4:7. Which ratio is greater?
    2. 2Find common denominatorLCD of 5 and 7 is 35
    3. 3Scale the ratios3:5 = 21:35 (3 × 7 = 21). For 4:7, Kai multiplies by 7 again: 4 × 7 = 28, giving 28:35.
    4. 4ConclusionSince 28 > 21, the ratio 4:7 is greater.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. Kai reused the multiplier 7 that belonged to the first ratio instead of finding the one 4:7 actually needs. To reach 35ths you multiply by 35 ÷ 7 = 5, so 4:7 = 20:35, not 28:35. Comparing 21:35 with 20:35 shows 3:5 is the greater ratio — the opposite of the conclusion.

    Correct work: To scale 4:7 to 35ths, multiply by 35 ÷ 7 = 5, not by 7 again: 4 × 5 = 20, so 4:7 = 20:35 (not 28:35). Now compare correctly: 21:35 vs. 20:35. Since 21 > 20, the ratio 3:5 is actually greater, not 4:7.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    Store A sells 5 notebooks for $12. Store B sells 8 notebooks for $20. Without calculating, a student says Store B must be cheaper because you get more notebooks. Is the student's reasoning valid? Find the unit rates to prove your answer.

    Sample answer: The student's reasoning is not valid — getting more items does not mean a lower price per item. Store A: $12 ÷ 5 = $2.40 per notebook. Store B: $20 ÷ 8 = $2.50 per notebook. Store A is actually cheaper per notebook, even though Store B offers more notebooks per purchase.

    Look for: $2.40, $2.50, Store A, cheaper, unit rate, not valid

    Why: Getting more items does not make a deal cheaper; only the price per item does. Store A works out to $12 ÷ 5 = $2.40 per notebook and Store B to $20 ÷ 8 = $2.50 per notebook, so the reasoning is not valid.
