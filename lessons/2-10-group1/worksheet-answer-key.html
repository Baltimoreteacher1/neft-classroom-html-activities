  1. 1

    A gymnast's scores are: 8.5, 8.8, 8.7, 8.6, 8.9. There are no outliers. Which measure best represents a typical score?

    1. AMean
    2. BMedian
    3. CNeither — the data is too small
    4. DBoth are equally bad

    The data is symmetric with no outliers, so the mean (8.7) best represents the typical score.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Appropriate Measures is defaulting to the mean just because "it uses all the data," without first checking whether an outlier or skewed shape is present. For example, given a player's points per game 14, 16, 15, 17, 16, 58, a student calculates the mean (136 ÷ 6 ≈ 22.7) and reports that as the typical game — but 58 is far from the cluster of 14–17, so it pulls the mean above 5 of the 6 games. The median (16) actually represents a typical game here. Before choosing a measure, always scan the data for an outlier or a long tail first: only use the mean once you've confirmed the data has neither.

    2. BMean — it uses all the data
    3. CMode — it shows the most common time
    4. DNeither measure works

    The 12:00 is an outlier that pulls the mean up. The median (7:15) better represents the runner's typical time.

    The mean (13.8) is higher than 5 of the 6 values because the outlier 50 pulls it up. The median (7) better represents a typical value.

  4. 4

    Spot the Mistake — Forgetting to Order the Data

    1. 1DataA swimmer's lap counts: 9, 12, 8, 11, 10 (no outliers).
    2. 2Find the medianThe student keeps the numbers as written (9, 12, 8, 11, 10) and picks the middle one: median = 8.
    3. 3Choose a measureThe student reports the median (8) as the typical lap count.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: You must put the data in order before finding the median. In order: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. The middle value is 10, so the median is 10 (not 8). Since there are no outliers, the mean (10) and median (10) agree, and either is a fine measure of a typical lap. The median is the middle value only after the data is sorted. Ordering 9, 12, 8, 11, 10 gives 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, so the median is 10 — the 8 that was reported is actually the smallest value in the set. With no outliers here, the mean is also 10, so either measure describes a typical lap count.

  6. 6

    Spot the Mistake — Choosing the Mean Despite an Outlier

    1. 1DataA receiver's catches per game: 12, 14, 13, 15, 11, 60 (the 60 was a record-setting playoff game).
    2. 2Find the meanSum = 125, Count = 6, Mean = 125 ÷ 6 ≈ 20.8.
    3. 3Find the medianOrdered: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 60 → Median = (13 + 14) ÷ 2 = 13.5.
    4. 4Choose a measureThe student reports the mean (≈ 20.8) as the typical number of catches because it uses every game.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: The student should report the median (13.5), not the mean. The 60-catch game is an outlier that pulls the mean up to about 20.8 — higher than 5 of the 6 games. The median (13.5) is resistant to that outlier and better represents a typical game. Using every value is exactly the problem here: the 60-catch playoff game pulls the mean up to about 20.8, which is higher than five of the six games. The median 13.5 sits inside the cluster of 11 to 15 and is not moved by that extreme value, so it is the better report of a typical game.

    A coach wants to know how SPREAD OUT the team's free-throw makes are: 6, 7, 6, 8, 18. Which measure of spread is most affected by the lone 18?

    1. ARange, because it is the highest minus the lowest value

    3. 3Find medianOrdered: 2, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 → Median = (20 + 21) ÷ 2 = 20.5
    4. 4Analyst's choiceThe analyst reports the mean (17.8) as the player's typical scoring because it uses all the data.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

It pulls the mean down to 17.8, which is lower than 5 of the 6 games, so it does not represent a typical performance. The median (20.5) is resistant to the outlier and better describes a typical game. The 2-point game was cut short by an ejection, so it is an outlier, and it drags the mean down to about 17.8 — lower than five of the six games. The median 20.5 sits among the 19-to-23 point games, so it describes the player's typical scoring much better.

The median 32.5 sits among the 30-to-35 minute games, so it describes typical minutes far better. Using all the data is not an advantage when one value is that extreme.
