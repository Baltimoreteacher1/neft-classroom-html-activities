  2. 2

    Warm restartA bead pattern goes A, B, C, A, B, C, A, B, C. What is the pattern unit?

    1. AA
    2. BA, B, C, A, B, C
    3. CA, B, C
    4. DA, A, B, B, C, C

    The pattern unit is the shortest chunk that repeats — here, A, B, C.

  3. 3

    Warm restartA bead pattern repeats the unit A, B, C. What is the 10th bead?

    1. AA
    2. BB
    3. CC
    4. DThere is no 10th bead

    Three beads repeat, and 10 = 3 × 3 + 1. After 9 beads the unit restarts, so the 10th is A.

  4. 4

    Warm restartWhat makes a repeating design predictable?

    1. AUsing many different colors
    2. BMaking every piece a different size
    3. CA pattern unit that repeats in the same order
    4. DLeaving gaps between the pieces

    Once you know the pattern unit and its order, you can predict any position in the design.

Answer, then say how you know — the reason is the part that counts.

  1. 5

    Think it throughIn September you estimated the Ferris wheel as 20 cars × 4 riders = about 80. Which of these uses a tool you did NOT have in September to extend that same estimate?

    1. AMultiplying the 80 riders per turn by 6 turns per hour to find a rate of 480 riders per hour
    2. BAdding the 20 cars and 4 riders together to get 24
    3. CGuessing a completely different, unrelated number
    4. DDeciding not to estimate the Ferris wheel at all

    Why is that the answer?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    80 × 6 = 480 uses a rate, a tool this year's math added — the September estimate of 20 × 4 could not do this. Adding 20 + 4 or guessing does not use any new math tool at all.

  2. 6

    Think it throughWhich statement best distinguishes a real comparison of math stories with a classmate from just repeating what you already believe?

    1. AWriting 'we both got better at math' and stopping there
    2. BNaming something the classmate actually said, plus one genuine similarity AND one genuine difference between the two stories
    3. CCopying your own September answer word for word
    4. DSkipping the classmate entirely and writing only about yourself

    How do you know?

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    The check asks for something the classmate actually said. It also asks you to name a genuine similarity. And a genuine difference. “We both got better” shows none of that, so it does not prove a real conversation happened.

  3. 7

    Think it throughA student writes, 'My classmate's story changed the same way mine did — we both feel more confident now.' What is missing from this comparison?

    1. AThe classmate's full name and grade
    2. BA genuine DIFFERENCE between the two stories, not just a shared similarity
    3. CThe exact date Lesson 1-1 was taught
    4. DA drawing of the Ferris wheel

    Explain your thinking.

    I chose ___ because ___ .

    A complete comparison names two things. It names a similarity. It also names a difference. This answer gives only a similarity, so it does not meet the lesson's check.

Explain the thinking

  1. 8

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: Treating your math story as finished — writing the same answers you wrote in Lesson 1-1 instead of looking for what actually changed. The story grows and evolves; a retrospective that copies September has missed the point.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

    Show what you know(Catch-up check, from Lesson 10.5) Which statement best matches this lesson's summary of 'Math is Boundless'?

    Explain your choice.

    The summary names all three elements — repetition, patterns, and rhythm — as the ways math creates beautiful, endless designs.

  2. 11

    From 10.1–10.3 Catch-Up(Catch-up check, from Lesson 10.2) Which statement best shows what this lesson means by 'math is beauty'?

    1. AMath creates beautiful designs like bilateral symmetry, found in nature and used in human art, music, and architecture
    2. BOnly things made by mathematicians can be beautiful
    3. CButterflies are the only living things with symmetry
    4. DBeauty and math are opposites — one is feeling and one is numbers

    The summary makes the claim in two parts. Bilateral symmetry appears naturally in many plants and animals. It also influences human creations. You can see it in art, in music, and in architecture.

  3. 12

    From 10.1–10.3 Catch-UpContinuing the pattern from 15 steps for a 4-disc tower, how many steps does a 5-disc tower take?

    1. A16
    2. B30
    3. C31
    4. D45

    15 × 2 + 1 = 31. 16 comes from only adding 1, 30 comes from only doubling, and 45 comes from tripling 15 instead of doubling and adding 1.
