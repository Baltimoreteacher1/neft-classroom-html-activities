    (Lesson 3.1) A recipe uses 4 cups of milk and 1 cup of cream. What is the ratio of milk to cream?

    1. A4:1
    2. B1:4
    3. C4:5
    4. D5:1

    A ratio compares quantities in the order given. Milk to cream = 4 to 1, or 4:1.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Ratio Tables is scaling only ONE part of the ratio, or adding the scale factor to a part instead of multiplying by it — for example, turning 2:3 into 6:3 (multiplying only the first number by 3 and forgetting the second) or into 4:5 (adding the scale factor 2 to the second number instead of multiplying, since 3+2=5 but 3×2=6). Before you submit, multiply BOTH numbers by the same scale factor and check that your new ratio simplifies back to the original.

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 3. Correct work: The total is 12, not 8. The correct ratio is 4:12. Aliya counted the total correctly: 6 + 4 + 2 = 12 pieces. But the ratio she wrote uses 8, which is not the total — it is only the almonds and raisins together. Comparing the 4 raisins to the whole 12 pieces gives 4:12.

  2. 2

    (Lesson 3.2) Find the Rate Error

    1. 1Find the unit rate: 12 tickets for $812 tickets for $8
    2. 2Set up the division12 ÷ 8 = 1.5
    3. 3Write the unit rateThe unit rate is $1.50 per ticket

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 2. Correct work: To find cost per ticket, divide $8 ÷ 12 = $0.67 per ticket (rounded). The student divided tickets by cost instead of cost by tickets. The two quantities were divided in the wrong order. "Dollars per ticket" puts dollars on top, so the correct division is $8 ÷ 12 ≈ $0.67 per ticket. The student's 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 answers a different question, and $1.50 per ticket would mean 12 tickets cost $18 instead of $8.

    3. 3Scale the oats5 × 3 = 15 cups of oats
    4. 4Scale the honey2 + 3 = 5 cups of honey

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: 2 × 3 = 6 cups of honey (multiply, don't add the scale factor) Devon multiplied the oats by the scale factor (5 × 3 = 15) but switched to addition for the honey (2 + 3 = 5). Both quantities have to use the same operation and the same scale factor, so the honey is 2 × 3 = 6 cups.
