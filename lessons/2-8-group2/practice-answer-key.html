Where we left off

Our goal: I can determine the mean of a data set, use a target mean to find a missing value, and explain the mean as the fair share and the balance point of the data, explain why the method works, and use it on a problem I have not seen before.

The big idea: Mean = total of all the values ÷ how many values there are — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. Miguel's 4 days of earnings are shown with counters. When I combine all of the counters, there are 60 of them, which means Miguel earned $60 in all.
  2. Now I redistribute the 60 counters equally among the 4 days. Each of the 4 groups ends up with 15 counters.
  3. The arithmetic does exactly the same thing: I find the total, 60, and divide it by the number of days, 4. So 60 ÷ 4 = 15.
  4. We can use $15 to describe Miguel's typical daily earnings. That leveled value is the mean.

Start here

You did these with your group. Now do them on your own — the model above is yours to copy.

    Try it together — then prove it: New data set: a dog walker earns $9, $11, $16, and $12 over 4 days. First, what is the total? Next, how many values are in the data set? That number is what we divide by. Last, divide the total by 4. Is your answer bigger than the smallest day and smaller than the biggest day? It should be. Now prove it: say why that move had to work at all — not just that it did.

Answer, then say how you know — the reason is the part that counts.

  1. 2

    Think it throughHow many points has Sofia scored in games 1 through 9?

    1. A133 points
    2. B150 points
    3. C135 points
    4. D15 points

    Why is that the answer?

    12 + 20 + 16 + 11 + 17 + 14 + 15 + 10 + 18 = 133 points.

  2. 3

    Think it throughTo finish with a mean of 15 points across 10 games, how many points does Sofia need in all?

    1. A150 points
    2. B133 points
    3. C15 points
    4. D25 points

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    If every game hit the target, the total would be 15 points per game for 10 games: 15 × 10 = 150 points.

  3. 4

    Think it throughHer nine games total 13 points below the target mean and 11 points above it. What must game 10 be for the data to balance at 15?

    1. A17 points — 2 above the target mean
    2. B13 points — 2 below the target mean
    3. C15 points — exactly the target mean
    4. D24 points — enough to double the points above

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    The mean is the balance point, so the total below must equal the total above. Below leads by 13 − 11 = 2, so game 10 must be 2 points above 15, which is 17. That matches 150 − 133 = 17.

  4. 5

    Back to the modelFour games are below 15 and four games are above 15. Does that already mean the data balances at 15? What else would you have to measure?

    Look for: distance, how far, 15, above, below, balance point, total.

Words and reasoning

Word bank · Banco de palabras

Mean (Media)Average (Promedio)Measure of center (Medida de centro)Balance point (Punto de equilibrio)Data set (Conjunto de datos)

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • The value found by adding all the data and dividing by how many values there are is the ___. → Mean
  • A common everyday word that means the same thing as the mean is the ___. → Average
  • A single number that describes the typical value of a whole data set is a ___. → Measure of center
  • The value where the total amount above equals the total amount below is the ___. → Balance point

Explain the thinking

  1. 6

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake on this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: The most common mistake with the mean is adding correctly and then dividing by the wrong number — or not dividing at all. Students report the total (for 8, 10, 6, 12 they say the mean is 36) or they divide by a count that does not match the data, such as dividing a 4-value total by 5, or skipping a zero when counting the values. Before you finish, do two things: count the values in the data set out loud, and check that your mean lands between the smallest and largest value. A mean of 36 for data that never goes above 12 is impossible.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 7

    Say moreWe piled all 60 counters together and then redistributed them equally into 4 bins. How is that the same as arithmetic?

    Combining the counters is adding; redistributing them equally is dividing.

  3. 8

    Write your ownWrite your own problem about this situation, then solve it and show the answer. The situation: Every week, Miguel walks dogs after school to earn some money. He keeps track of his earnings in his notebook. Over 4 days he earned $60 in all. How can Miguel describe his earnings on a typical day?

    Answers vary. Look for a problem that actually requires this lesson's move, plus a worked solution that matches the problem as the student wrote it.
