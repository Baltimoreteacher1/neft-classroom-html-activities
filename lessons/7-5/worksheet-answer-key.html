Lesson 7.5Represent Rational Numbers on the Coordinate Plane

Version A · Answer KeySupported practice

Learning target I can plot and identify points on the coordinate plane using ordered pairs.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A point is at (6, 2). What does the 6 tell you?

    1. AMove 6 units to the right
    2. BMove 6 units up
    3. CMove 6 units to the left
    4. DThe point is in Quadrant 6

    Hint In (6, 2), the 6 is the FIRST number. First and second positions have different jobs.

    Answer: A — Move 6 units to the right

    Why: The first number (x-coordinate) tells you horizontal movement. 6 means go right 6 units from the origin.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Graph on the Coordinate Plane is swapping the order of the coordinates — moving up/down first instead of right/left first. For example, when plotting (5, 2), students often move up 5 and right 2, landing on the wrong point (2, 5) instead of (5, 2). Remember: the x-coordinate (first number) always tells you how far to move right or left along the x-axis; the y-coordinate (second number) tells you how far up or down along the y-axis.

  2. 2Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Put these steps in order for plotting the point (4, 5).

    Groups:
    • Start at the origin (0, 0)
    • Move 4 units to the right along the x-axis
    • Move 5 units up from that spot
    • Place a dot at the point (4, 5)

    Hint Every plotting routine begins at the same home spot before any moving happens.

    Why: Plotting always begins at the origin (0, 0), because every ordered pair counts its moves from there. The first number, 4, is the x-coordinate, so the move right along the x-axis comes next; the second number, 5, is the y-coordinate, so the move up follows; then you mark the dot at (4, 5).

  3. 3Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each ordered pair to its description.

    1. A(0, 0)
    2. B(4, 2)
    3. C(1, 6)
    4. D(5, 5)
    5. E(3, 0)
    6. F(0, 4)
    • AOrigin
    • BRight 4, Up 2
    • CRight 1, Up 6
    • DRight 5, Up 5
    • EOn the x-axis
    • FOn the y-axis

    Hint Each pair is (x, y): first number = right, second = up. A 0 means you don't move in that direction.

    Why: Every ordered pair is (x, y): the first number is the move right and the second is the move up. So (4, 2) is right 4, up 2; (1, 6) is right 1, up 6; and (5, 5) is right 5, up 5. A 0 means no movement in that direction — (3, 0) never leaves the x-axis, (0, 4) never leaves the y-axis, and (0, 0) is the origin where the two axes meet.

    Hint Re-read the moves: 3 units right, then 7 units up. Which move comes first in an ordered pair?

    Answer: B — (3, 7)

    Why: The first coordinate is horizontal (right 3), the second is vertical (up 7). So (3, 7).

Part 3Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 6Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1CluePlot the point (5, 2).
    2. 2StartStart at the origin (0, 0). Good.
    3. 3Move upMove 5 units UP along the y-axis.
    4. 4Move rightMove 2 units to the right. Place the dot at (2, 5).

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Hint Which direction does the first number in (x, y) tell you to move — right or up?

    Mistake: Step 3. The first number of an ordered pair is always the x-coordinate, which is a horizontal move. Here the 5 should send you 5 units RIGHT, and then the 2 sends you 2 units UP. Moving up first swaps the coordinates and lands on (2, 5), a completely different point from (5, 2).

    Correct work: The student moved UP first. The FIRST number (5) is the x-coordinate, so move 5 units RIGHT; then the SECOND number (2) is the y-coordinate, so move 2 units UP. The point is (5, 2), not (2, 5).

Version B · Answer KeyCore practice

Learning target I can plot and identify points on the coordinate plane using ordered pairs.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Plot these points to reveal a hidden shape: (1, 1), (1, 5), (6, 5), (6, 1)

    Answer: A, B, C, D

    Why: Each point starts at the origin, moves right the x amount, then up the y amount: A(1, 1), B(1, 5), C(6, 5), D(6, 1). A and B share x = 1, so B sits straight above A, and B and C share y = 5, so they line up across. Connected in order, the four points form a rectangle 5 units wide and 4 units tall.

  2. 2Write the letter of the matching item on each line.

    Match each point to its quadrant or axis.

    1. A(3, 2)
    2. B(-4, 5)
    3. C(-2, -6)
    4. D(7, -1)
    5. E(0, 3)
    6. F(5, 0)
    • AQuadrant I
    • BQuadrant II
    • CQuadrant III
    • DQuadrant IV
    • Ey-axis
    • Fx-axis

    Why: The signs of the two coordinates place each point. (3, 2) is (+, +) in Quadrant I; (-4, 5) is (-, +) in Quadrant II; (-2, -6) is (-, -) in Quadrant III; and (7, -1) is (+, -) in Quadrant IV. A coordinate of 0 puts the point on an axis instead of inside a quadrant, so (0, 3) is on the y-axis and (5, 0) is on the x-axis.

  3. 3Complete the table. Show how you found each value.

    Complete the table. Write the ordered pair for each description.

    DescriptionOrdered Pair
    Right 7, Up 2
    Right 0, Up 5
    Right 4, Up 0
    Right 3, Up 3

    Why: 'Right' gives the x-coordinate and 'Up' gives the y-coordinate, in that order: Right 7, Up 2 is (7, 2); Right 0, Up 5 is (0, 5); Right 4, Up 0 is (4, 0); and Right 3, Up 3 is (3, 3). A move of 0 still gets written into the pair — that 0 is what shows the point is sitting on an axis.

  4. 4Write the name of the correct group on each line.

    Sort these ordered pairs by their x-coordinate from smallest to largest.

    Groups:
    • (5, 3)
    • (1, 7)
    • (3, 2)
    • (0, 4)
    • (7, 1)

    Why: Only the first number in each pair matters here. The x-values are 5, 1, 3, 0, and 7, and putting those in order from smallest to largest gives (0, 4), (1, 7), (3, 2), (5, 3), (7, 1). Notice (0, 4) comes first even though its y-value of 4 is fairly large — the second number plays no part in this sort.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 5Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Spot the Common Mistake

    1. 1CluePlot the point (4, 0).
    2. 2Move rightMove 4 units to the right along the x-axis. Good.
    3. 3Move upMove 4 units up, because the pair has a 4 in it.
    4. 4Plot the pointPlace the dot at (4, 4).

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. The up move comes from the SECOND number, and the second number in (4, 0) is 0. Reusing the 4 pushes the point off the axis to (4, 4). With a y-coordinate of 0 there is no upward move at all, so the point stays right on the x-axis at (4, 0).

    Correct work: The y-coordinate is 0, not 4. After moving 4 right, the up move is 0 units, so the point stays on the x-axis at (4, 0). The student reused the x-value instead of reading the second number (0).

Learning target I can plot and identify points on the coordinate plane using ordered pairs.

Part 1Understand the ideamodels and meaning
  1. 1Plot and label each point on the grid.

    Plot the points (2, 3), (2, 7), (6, 7), (6, 3). Connect them. Name the shape and find its perimeter using the coordinates.

    Answer: A, B, C, D

    Why: The points A(2, 3), B(2, 7), C(6, 7), D(6, 3) form a square, because A and B share x = 2 while B and C share y = 7. Side AB measures 7 − 3 = 4 units and side BC measures 6 − 2 = 4 units, so all four sides are 4 units and the perimeter is 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16 units.

Part 2Explain and correctreasoning
  1. 2Find the mistake. Explain it, then show the correct work.

    Miscounted Grid Lines

    1. 1Ordered pair(7, 3)
    2. 2Move along the x-axisStart at the origin (0, 0) and count 7 units to the right.
    3. 3Move along the y-axisCount up from the x-axis: 1, 2, 3, 4 — stop on 4.
    4. 4Plot the pointPlace the dot at (7, 4).

    Which step has the mistake? Explain what went wrong, then show the correct work.

    Mistake: Step 3. Counting up has to start with the x-axis as 0, not 1. The first line above the x-axis is 1, the second is 2, and the third is 3, so 3 units up lands on y = 3. Treating the axis itself as unit 1 pushes every count one gridline too high, which is how (7, 3) turned into (7, 4).

    Correct work: The x-axis itself is 0, not 1. Counting up correctly: 1, 2, 3 — the third count lands on y = 3, so the point is (7, 3), not (7, 4). The student started counting one gridline too high by treating the x-axis as unit 1 instead of unit 0.

  2. 3Answer in complete sentences.

    A student says that (3, 5) and (5, 3) are the same point because they use the same numbers. Is the student correct? Explain why or why not, and describe where each point is located on the coordinate plane.

    Look for: order, x-coordinate, y-coordinate, horizontal, vertical, different, right, up

    Why: The student is not correct. In (3, 5) the 3 is the x-coordinate, so you move right 3 and up 5, landing high and toward the left. In (5, 3) the 5 is the x-coordinate, so you move right 5 and up 3, landing lower and farther right. Same digits, different jobs — the order inside an ordered pair decides which direction each number controls.
