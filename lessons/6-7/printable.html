Find Factors and Multiples
Hallar factores y múltiplos		Listing what divides a number evenly (its factors) and what it divides into evenly (its multiples), then finding what two numbers share.
Listar lo que divide un número exactamente (sus factores) y lo que el número forma al contar de esa cantidad (sus múltiplos), y luego hallar lo que dos números comparten.		Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — Each divides 12 exactly.
Factor
Factor		A whole number that divides another number evenly, with no remainder.
Un número entero que divide a otro exactamente, sin residuo.		4 dividing 12 exactly — It divides with nothing left over.
Multiple
Múltiplo		The result of multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, and so on — where you land when you skip-count by it.
El resultado de multiplicar un número por 1, 2, 3, y así sucesivamente: donde caes al contar de esa cantidad.		24 as a multiple of 8 — 8 × 3 reaches it.
Greatest common factor (GCF)
Máximo común divisor (MCD)		The largest number that is a factor of two or more numbers. It answers “how many equal groups can I make?”
El número más grande que es factor de dos o más números. Responde “¿cuántos grupos iguales puedo formar?”		6 for 12 and 18 — The largest they share.
Least common multiple (LCM)
Mínimo común múltiplo (mcm)		The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. It answers “when will these line up again?”
El número más pequeño que es múltiplo de dos o más números. Responde “¿cuándo volverán a coincidir?”		12 for 4 and 6 — The first shared multiple.
Factor pair
Par de factores		Two factors that multiply to make a number. In a word problem, one is the number of groups and the other is the size of each group.
Dos factores que se multiplican para formar un número. En un problema, uno es la cantidad de grupos y el otro el tamaño de cada grupo.		4 and 9 for 36 — Their product is 36.
Prime factorization
Descomposición en factores primos		Writing a number as a product of prime numbers, usually with a factor tree. Multiplying the primes two numbers share also gives the GCF.
Escribir un número como producto de números primos, normalmente con un árbol de factores. Multiplicar los primos que dos números comparten también da el MCD.		30 = 2 × 3 × 5 — Every factor is prime.
Common factor
Factor común		A factor that two numbers both have.
Un factor que dos números tienen en común.		4, for 16 and 24 — 4 divides both numbers evenly.
Greatest common factor
Máximo común divisor		The largest factor two numbers share.
El mayor factor que dos números tienen en común.		8, for 16 and 24 — 8 divides both, and no larger number does.
Least common multiple
Mínimo común múltiplo		The smallest multiple two numbers share.
El menor múltiplo que dos números tienen en común.		18, for 6 and 9 — It is the smallest number in both lists of multiples.

🚀 Launch

The station quartermaster has two jobs today. First, split the pencil and notebook stock into identical combo packs with nothing left over — that is a factor question. Second, work out when two supply runs that repeat on different cycles will land in the same week — that is a multiple question. Same lesson, opposite tools.

Factors split things up; multiples count forward

A factor divides a number evenly, so factors answer questions about splitting a stock into equal groups. A multiple is where you land when you skip-count, so multiples answer questions about when repeating events line up.

Key idea: Greatest Common Factor (GCF) & Least Common Multiple (LCM). 1. GCF: Largest factor shared by two numbers — used to simplify fractions or divide into equal groups. 2. LCM: Smallest shared multiple — used to find common denominators. 3. Use prime factor trees or list method to find common factors/multiples

Watch me build the combo packs

  • The store has 48 pencils and 36 notebooks. Every combo pack must hold the same number of each, with nothing left over — so the number of packs has to be a factor of BOTH quantities.
  • Factors of 48: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48. Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. Listing them in order is what makes the comparison easy.
  • The numbers in both lists are the common factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.
  • The greatest of those is 12, so the GCF of 48 and 36 is 12. The manager can make 12 combo packs — each holding 48 ÷ 12 = 4 pencils and 36 ÷ 12 = 3 notebooks.

Let's do one together — the same GCF, using prime factors

  • A factor tree breaks each number into primes. 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3, and 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.
  • Now look at what the two share: two 2s and one 3.
  • Multiply the shared primes: 2 × 2 × 3 = 12.
  • Same answer as the lists, 12 — which is the check. For big numbers, the factor tree is usually the faster road.

Your turn — now a multiples question

  • Ayasha has painting class every 8 weeks and pottery class every 6 weeks. Both are this week. When will both fall in the same week again?
  • List multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32 … and multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24 …
  • Find the smallest number in both lists — that is the LCM.
  • Say what the LCM means for Ayasha, and explain why 48 is a common multiple but not the answer.
