Read this in your language · Lea esto en su idioma

A factor divides a number evenly: 4 is a factor of 12. A multiple is what you get when you skip-count: 12, 24, 36 are multiples of 12. Use the greatest common factor to split things into equal groups, and the least common multiple to find when two repeating events happen together.

  • Find Factors and Multiples — Listing what divides a number evenly (its factors) and what it divides into evenly (its multiples), then finding what two numbers share.
  • Factor — A whole number that divides another number evenly, with no remainder.
  • Multiple — The result of multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, and so on — where you land when you skip-count by it.
  • Greatest common factor (GCF) — The largest number that is a factor of two or more numbers. It answers “how many equal groups can I make?”
  • Least common multiple (LCM) — The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. It answers “when will these line up again?”
  • Factor pair — Two factors that multiply to make a number. In a word problem, one is the number of groups and the other is the size of each group.
  • Prime factorization — Writing a number as a product of prime numbers, usually with a factor tree. Multiplying the primes two numbers share also gives the GCF.
  • Common factor — A factor that two numbers both have.
  • Greatest common factor — The largest factor two numbers share.
  • Least common multiple — The smallest multiple two numbers share.

How we did it in class today

  1. The store has 48 pencils and 36 notebooks. Every combo pack must hold the same number of each, with nothing left over — so the number of packs has to be a factor of BOTH quantities.
  2. Factors of 48: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48. Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. Listing them in order is what makes the comparison easy.
  3. The numbers in both lists are the common factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.
  4. The greatest of those is 12, so the GCF of 48 and 36 is 12. The manager can make 12 combo packs — each holding 48 ÷ 12 = 4 pencils and 36 ÷ 12 = 3 notebooks.

Try this together

Pick one question to talk about. You don't need to do them all!

At home, the dog gets a bath every 4 weeks and the cat gets its medicine every 6 weeks, and both happen this week. When will they next happen in the same week?

  1. Week 2, the greatest common factor of 4 and 6
  2. Week 12, the least common multiple of 4 and 6
  3. Week 10, because 4 + 6 = 10
  4. Week 24, because 4 × 6 = 24
Check the answer

The answer is: Week 12, the least common multiple of 4 and 6

If they get stuck...

Watch out for this: A common mistake is reaching for the wrong tool: listing FACTORS for a problem about repeating cycles. Ayasha's classes repeat every 6 and 8 weeks, so the weeks they land on are multiples — answering with the GCF of 2 claims a class that meets every 8 weeks happens in week 2. Use the size of the answer as a check: a GCF is never larger than the smaller number, and an LCM is never smaller than the larger one.

Try asking: "Can you show me how you got that? Did we miss a step?"
