Where we left off

Our goal: I can make and read a histogram to display data in intervals, explain why the method works, and use it on a problem I have not seen before.

The big idea: Each bar's height shows how many data values land inside that interval — and you can say why it is true, and where it would stop being true.

Model to copy — Watch me

  1. My data: 3, 7, 12, 15, 18, 24. I will use intervals of 10: 0–9, 10–19, 20–29.
  2. Interval 0–9: the values 3 and 7 fit, so the frequency is 2.
  3. Interval 10–19: the values 12, 15, and 18 fit, so the frequency is 3.
  4. Interval 20–29: only the value 24 fits, so the frequency is 1.
  5. Now I draw three bars with heights 2, 3, and 1, touching each other. The tallest bar (10–19) is where most values are.

  2. 2

    Warm restartWhich of these is a statistical question?

    1. AHow many minutes are in one hour?
    2. BHow many days are in September?
    3. CHow many sides does a hexagon have?
    4. DHow many minutes did each student read last night?

    How do you know?

    Different students read for different amounts of time, so the answers vary. The others each have one fixed answer.

  3. 3

    Warm restart'How tall is our flagpole?' is NOT a statistical question. Why not?

    1. AIt is about height
    2. BIt uses numbers
    3. CIt has only one answer, so there is no variability
    4. DNobody can measure it

    How do you know?

    A statistical question expects answers that vary. One flagpole has one height.

  4. 4

    Warm restartA class collects this data on pets owned: 0, 2, 1, 0, 3. Which statistical question could these answers come from?

    1. AHow many students are in the class?
    2. BWhat is the largest number of pets allowed?
    3. CHow many pets are in the store?
    4. DHow many pets does each student own?

    How do you know?

    The five different answers vary from student to student, which is what a statistical question produces.

  1. 5

    Think it throughWhich interval has the most players?

    1. A6–8 shots
    2. B3–5 shots
    3. C9–11 shots
    4. D0–2 shots

    Why is that the answer?

    The 6–8 interval has 10 players — the tallest bar in the histogram.

  2. 6

    Think it throughHow many players take between 3 and 8 shots per game?

    1. A18
    2. B10
    3. C8
    4. D23

    How do you know? Give a second reason as well.

    8 players in the 3–5 bar plus 10 players in the 6–8 bar = 18 of the 23 players.

  3. 7

    Think it throughDoes the histogram support the coach's claim?

    1. AYes — 18 of 23 players cluster in the middle intervals
    2. BNo — most players take 0–2 shots
    3. CNo — the data is skewed right
    4. DYou cannot tell from a histogram

    Explain your thinking. What would have to change for a different choice to be right?

    The distribution is roughly symmetric with a clear center at 6–8 shots, and most players (18 of 23) sit in the two middle bars. That backs up 'most of my players take a good number of shots'.

  4. 8

    Back to the modelThe tallest bar is the 10–19 interval with 16 players. What does the shape of this histogram tell you about scoring in the league?

    Look for: 10, 19, right, skewed, most, few, high scorers, cluster.

Word bank · Banco de palabras

Use the words

Fill each blank with a word from the bank.

  • A histogram groups numerical data into equal ___ and shows each one's ___. → Display Data with Histograms
  • A bar graph that shows how often data falls into equal intervals is a ___. → Histogram
  • How many data values fall in an interval is the ___. → Frequency
  • An equal-sized range of values used to group data is an ___. → Interval

Explain the thinking

  1. 9

    Find the mistakeAnother student made this exact mistake in this lesson. Explain why it is wrong, then write what they should have done.

    The mistake: A common mistake in Display Data: Histograms is counting a boundary value in two intervals at once — for example, putting the value 10 in both the '0–9' bin and the '10–19' bin. Each data value belongs to exactly ONE interval. Before you submit, add up all your frequencies and check that the total matches the number of data values you started with.

    The full note above is the teacher's — the student sheet shows only the misconception, not the correction. A correct answer names the step that went wrong, states the rule it broke, and shows the fixed work.

    Watch for: a student who repeats the mistake back without naming why it is wrong.

  2. 10

    Say moreAs you build the frequency table with intervals of 10, what does the height of each bar in the histogram represent?

    The height of a histogram bar is the frequency of values in that interval.

  3. 11

    Write your ownWrite your own problem about this situation, then solve it and show the answer. The situation: A basketball league tracked the points-per-game average for all 30 of its players this season. The histogram groups those averages into equal intervals of 10 points.

    Answers vary. Look for a problem that actually requires this lesson's move, plus a worked solution that matches the problem as the student wrote it.

    From Lesson 2.1Explain your thinking — Which question is a statistical question? A) How many students are in the school? B) How many hours does each student in 6th grade exercise per week? C) What time does school start? D) How many days are in a week?

    2. BHow many students are in the school?
    3. CWhat time does school start?
    4. DHow many days are in a week?

    "How many hours does each student exercise per week?" is statistical because different students exercise different amounts — the data has variability.

    Different students have different heights, so that question produces a data set with variability. Each of the others has one fixed answer.
