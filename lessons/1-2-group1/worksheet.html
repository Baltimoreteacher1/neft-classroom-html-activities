    What is the GCF of 8 and 12?

    1. A4
    2. B2
    3. C8
    4. D24

    Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The largest common factor is 4.

    Watch for: A common mistake in Greatest Common Factor is confusing it with the Least Common Multiple: some students list the MULTIPLES of both numbers and pick the smallest shared one (getting an answer bigger than either number, like 72 for 24 and 36) instead of listing FACTORS and picking the largest one they share (12). Remember: the GCF splits numbers INTO equal groups, so it can never be bigger than the smaller of the two numbers.

Sam's two factor lists are correct, and the factors that 12 and 30 share are 1, 2, 3, and 6. The mistake happens at the last step: 3 really is a common factor, but it is not the greatest one. The GCF is the largest number in the shared list, so GCF(12, 30) = 6.

    Level 2 Extension — The Wrong Mission Number

    1. 1The problemSplit 12 oxygen tanks and 18 ration packs into the most equal crew pods with none left over.
    2. 2List factors of 121, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
    3. 3List factors of 181, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

    Which step has the mistake? Explain it and write the correct work.

    The mistake is in Step 4. Correct work: Cadet Rio found the LCM (least common multiple = 36), but making equal groups with none left over needs the GCF (greatest common factor). The common factors of 12 and 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, and the greatest is 6. So Mission Control can fill 6 equal pods, each with 2 oxygen tanks and 3 ration packs. Cadet Rio found 36, which is the least common multiple of 12 and 18, not the greatest common factor. Splitting supplies into equal groups with none left over needs a number that DIVIDES both counts, so it can never be bigger than 12. The common factors are 1, 2, 3, and 6, so Mission Control fills 6 pods with 12 ÷ 6 = 2 tanks and 18 ÷ 6 = 3 packs each.
