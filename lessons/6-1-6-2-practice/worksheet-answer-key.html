    A 3-Foot Dowel Cut Into 1/6-Foot Pieces

    The model shows a 3-foot dowel cut into 1/6-foot pieces. Which expression does the model represent, and how many pieces are there?

    1. A3 × 1/6 = 1/2 of a piece
    2. B3 ÷ 1/6 = 18 pieces
    3. C1/6 ÷ 3 = 1/18 of a piece
    4. D3 ÷ 6 = 1/2 of a piece

    Hint Read the model first: how long is the whole dowel, and how long is each small piece?

    Answer: B — 3 ÷ 1/6 = 18 pieces

    Why: Each of the 3 feet holds six 1/6-foot pieces, so the model shows 18 pieces in all. Written as an expression that is 3 ÷ 1/6. Put a 1 under the 3, then Keep, Change, Flip: 3/1 × 6/1 = 18.

    A 5-Foot Cable Marked Into 1/2-Foot Segments

    The model shows a 5-foot cable marked into 1/2-foot segments. Which expression does the model represent, and how many segments are there?

    1. A5 × 1/2 = 2 1/2 segments
    2. B5 ÷ 1/2 = 10 segments
    3. C1/2 ÷ 5 = 1/10 of a segment
    4. D5 ÷ 2 = 2 1/2 segments

    Hint Read the model first: how long is the whole cable, and how long is each small segment?

    Answer: B — 5 ÷ 1/2 = 10 segments

    Why: Each of the 5 feet holds two 1/2-foot segments, so the model shows 10 segments in all. Written as an expression that is 5 ÷ 1/2. Put a 1 under the 5, then Keep, Change, Flip: 5/1 × 2/1 = 10.

    A 6-Foot Pipe Cut Into 3/4-Foot Sections

    The model shows a 6-foot pipe cut into 3/4-foot sections. Which expression does the model represent, and how many sections are there?

    1. A6 × 3/4 = 4 1/2 sections
    2. B6 ÷ 3/4 = 8 sections
    3. C3/4 ÷ 6 = 1/8 of a section
    4. D6 ÷ 4 = 1 1/2 sections

    Answer: B — 6 ÷ 3/4 = 8 sections

    Why: Using quarters as the shared unit, 6 feet is 24 quarters and each 3/4-foot section is 3 quarters, so there are 24 ÷ 3 = 8 sections. Written as an expression that is 6 ÷ 3/4. Keep, Change, Flip: 6/1 × 4/3 = 24/3 = 8.

  2. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.
    3/4 Yard of Trim Cut Into 1/8-Yard Pieces

    The model shows 3/4 yard of trim cut into 1/8-yard pieces. Which expression does the model represent, and how many pieces are there?

    1. A3/4 × 1/8 = 3/32 of a piece
    2. B3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6 pieces
    3. C1/8 ÷ 3/4 = 1/6 of a piece
    4. D3/4 ÷ 8 = 3/32 of a piece

    Answer: B — 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6 pieces

    Why: 3/4 yard is the same as six 1/8-yard pieces, so the model shows 6 pieces. Written as an expression that is 3/4 ÷ 1/8. Keep, Change, Flip: 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6.

    A community garden group has 5/6 of a bag of fertilizer left over from last season. They plan to split it evenly across 5 raised garden beds so that every bed gets the same amount before spring planting begins. The lead gardener needs to know what fraction of the WHOLE bag goes into each bed.

    1. A5/6 of the bag
    2. B6/5 of the bag
    3. C1/6 of the bag
    4. D5/11 of the bag

    Answer: C — 1/6 of the bag

    Why: This is 5/6 ÷ 5. Put a 1 under the 5, then Keep, Change, Flip: 5/6 × 1/5 = 5/30 = 1/6 of the whole bag per bed.

    A 5/6-Mile Trail Marked Into 1/12-Mile Rest Points

    The model shows a 5/6-mile trail marked into 1/12-mile rest points. Which expression does the model represent, and how many rest points are there?

    1. A5/6 × 1/12 = 5/72 of a point
    2. B5/6 ÷ 1/12 = 10 points
    3. C1/12 ÷ 5/6 = 1/10 of a point
    4. D5/6 ÷ 12 = 5/72 of a point

    Answer: B — 5/6 ÷ 1/12 = 10 points

    Why: 5/6 mile is the same as ten 1/12-mile stretches, so the model shows 10 rest points. Written as an expression that is 5/6 ÷ 1/12. Keep, Change, Flip: 5/6 × 12/1 = 60/6 = 10.

Part 2Apply itreal-world problems
  1. 2Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A robotics team is testing a new track design ahead of a regional competition. Their prototype track measures 5 1/4 feet from start to finish, and the competition rulebook requires the whole track to be built out of straight sections that are each exactly 3/4 foot long, with no leftover length allowed anywhere along the course. Before the team can submit their design for inspection, their captain needs to know exactly how many straight sections make up the full length of the track.

    1. A7 sections
    2. B4 sections
    3. C5 1/4 sections
    4. D3 3/4 sections

    Answer: A — 7 sections

    Why: Rewrite 5 1/4 as 21/4. This is 21/4 ÷ 3/4. Keep, Change, Flip: 21/4 × 4/3 = 84/12 = 7 sections.

  2. 3Circle the letter of the best answer. Show how you know.

    A board is 6 3/4 feet long. It is cut into pieces that are each 3/4 foot long, and then EVERY one of those pieces is cut in half again. How many final pieces are there?

    1. A9 pieces
    2. B18 pieces
    3. C4 1/2 pieces
    4. D6 pieces

    Answer: B — 18 pieces

    Why: First: 6 3/4 ÷ 3/4 = 27/4 × 4/3 = 108/12 = 9 pieces. Then every one of those 9 is cut in half: 9 × 2 = 18 final pieces.
