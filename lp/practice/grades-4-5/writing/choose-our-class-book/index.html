Grades 4–5 · Writing

Choose our next class book

Students write opinion pieces about class reading choices, supporting a claim with reasons, examples and linking words, and answering another view.

Grades 4–5WritingELD-LAArgue20 min

  1. Task 1 of 2 · Levels 3–4 · Argue · 40–100 words

    Your class is choosing the next read-aloud book. Should the class read a graphic novel or a chapter book without pictures? Write your opinion and give two reasons.

    Plan

    Sentence starters

    0 words (goal: 40–100)

    Check yourself
    Sample answers

    Level 2 sample (8 words)

    Graphic novel. Is fun. Picture help. I like.

    • States a choice.
    • Gives a reason in a short phrase (Picture help).
    • Uses simple, everyday words.

    Level 4 sample (61 words)

    In my opinion our class should read a graphic novel. One reason is that the pictures help us understand the story. For example, if you dont know a word, you can look at the picture. Another reason is that more kids will want to listen because graphic novels are exciting. That is why I think we should read a graphic novel.

    • Gives an opinion, two reasons and a closing.
    • Linking words connect ideas: One reason, For example, Another reason, That is why.
    • Small errors (dont, a missing comma after In my opinion).

    Level 6 sample (78 words)

    Our class should read a chapter book for our next read-aloud. First, a chapter book makes us picture the characters and places in our own minds, which builds our imagination. When our teacher read a mystery last spring, everyone imagined the old lighthouse differently, and we loved comparing our ideas. Second, chapter books include more descriptive language, so we learn new vocabulary. Although graphic novels are fun, a chapter book will help us grow as readers and listeners.

    • Clear opinion, two reasons with an example, and a strong closing.
    • Varied sentences, including When, which and Although clauses.
    • Precise words: imagination, descriptive language, vocabulary.
    • Mentions the other side (graphic novels are fun) before restating the opinion.

  2. Task 2 of 2 · Level 5 · Argue · 70–150 words

    Some people think students should choose any book they want for independent reading. Others think teachers should choose the books. What do you think? Write an opinion piece with reasons and examples, respond to the other side, and end with a strong conclusion.

    Plan

    Sentence starters

    0 words (goal: 70–150)

    Check yourself
    Sample answers

    Level 2 sample (15 words)

    Kids pick book. Kids like. I like dog book. I read more. Teacher pick boring.

    • States an opinion.
    • Gives a personal example (dog book).
    • Short sentences; the word boring shows a feeling but not a reason.

    Level 4 sample (72 words)

    I believe students should choose there own books. The first reason is that kids read more when they like the book. For instance, I read three books about soccer in one month because I love soccer. In addition, choosing books helps kids learn what they like. Some people think teachers should choose because they know good books but kids can ask the teacher for ideas. In conclusion students should choose there books.

    • Gives an opinion, two reasons, a response to the other side, and a conclusion.
    • Uses transitions: The first reason, For instance, In addition, In conclusion.
    • Spelling and punctuation errors (there own, missing commas).

    Level 6 sample (114 words)

    I strongly believe students should be allowed to choose their own books for independent reading. The first reason is that choice makes reading more enjoyable, and students who enjoy reading usually read more often. For instance, my cousin used to avoid books until she discovered a series about young inventors; now she reads every night. In addition, choosing books teaches students to know themselves as readers, including which topics and authors they prefer. Some people argue that teachers should pick books because they know which ones are challenging enough. However, teachers can still guide students by suggesting a few options at the right level. In conclusion, when students choose, they become readers for life.

    • Organized with a clear opinion, two developed reasons, another view and a conclusion.
    • Long and short sentences, with clauses using who, because and when.
    • Precise words: independent, enjoyable, challenging, guide.
    • Answers the other side with However and a fair solution.
