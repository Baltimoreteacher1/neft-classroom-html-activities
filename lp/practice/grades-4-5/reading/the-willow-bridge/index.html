How two towns built a bridge

Students read a local history narrative about two towns building a bridge and practice finding facts, sequencing events, explaining causes and identifying the author's purpose.

Grades 4–5ReadingELD-SSNarrate15 min

The Willow Bridge

Long ago, two small towns sat on opposite sides of the Gray River. Willow Bend was on the north side, and Stonefield was on the south side. Families in both towns were farmers, and they often food and tools. But there was one big problem: there was no bridge.

To cross the river, people had to ride a small wooden . The ferry could carry only six people at a time. In the spring, heavy rain made the river fast and dangerous. Sometimes the ferry could not cross for many days, and farmers could not sell their vegetables before they .

In 1898, a teacher named Rosa Delgado had an idea. She asked both towns to work together and build a bridge. At first, many people said it would cost too much. Rosa collected a few coins from every family. She also asked and to give their time instead of money.

Building the bridge took two years. Workers cut logs from the forest and carried stones from the hills. Children brought water and lunch to the workers. In the winter, the work stopped because the ground was frozen. When spring came, the workers started again.

In 1900, the Willow Bridge finally opened. People from both towns walked across it together and held a large on the riverbank. Trade between the towns grew quickly. Today, a new steel bridge stands in the same place, but a small sign still tells Rosa's story.

Word help
traded
gave things to each other in exchange for other things (intercambiaban: se daban cosas a cambio de otras cosas)
ferry
a boat that carries people across a river again and again (transbordador: un barco que lleva personas de un lado del río al otro)
spoiled
went bad and could not be eaten (se echaron a perder: se pusieron malos y no se podían comer)
carpenters
workers who build things out of wood (carpinteros: trabajadores que hacen cosas de madera)
blacksmiths
workers who make tools out of hot metal (herreros: trabajadores que hacen herramientas con metal caliente)
festival
a big celebration for a community (festival: una gran fiesta para una comunidad)
