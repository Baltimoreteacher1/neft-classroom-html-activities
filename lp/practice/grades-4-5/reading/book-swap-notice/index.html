A plan for a book swap

Students read a student council notice that gives directions and argues for a book swap, and practice following steps, finding main ideas and judging evidence.

Grades 4–5ReadingELD-SIArgue15 min

Student Council Notice: Start a Book Swap!

Dear students and families, the Student Council of Lakeview Elementary has a . We want to start a book swap in the front hallway. A book swap is a shelf where you can leave a book you have finished and take a different one home for free.

Why do we need a book swap? Last month, we asked 120 students about reading at home. In our , 84 students said they want more books at home. Many students also said they have books they have already read many times. A swap would let those books find new readers instead of sitting on a shelf.

A book swap is also good for the planet. When we share books, fewer books end up in the trash. Families can save money, too, because new books can be .

Here is how it will work. First, bring a book that is in good , with no pages. Next, give it to a council member at the hallway table on Monday or Wednesday morning. Then, choose one book from the shelf to take home. Remember: one book in, one book out.

Some people worry that the shelf will get messy. To solve this problem, two council members will check and the shelf every Friday. Please vote yes for the book swap at the class meeting on May 3. Together, we can share the joy of reading!

Word help
proposal
a plan or idea that you ask others to agree with (propuesta: un plan o idea que pides a otros que acepten)
survey
a set of questions you ask many people (encuesta: un grupo de preguntas que haces a muchas personas)
expensive
costing a lot of money (caro: que cuesta mucho dinero)
condition
how good or bad something is (estado: qué tan bueno o malo está algo)
torn
ripped or pulled apart (rotas: rasgadas o separadas)
organize
to put things in order (organizar: poner las cosas en orden)
