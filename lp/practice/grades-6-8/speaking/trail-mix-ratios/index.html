Making trail mix for the class

Students read a recipe, explain how to scale it with a ratio, and evaluate a classmate's adding strategy, using ratio language to explain why multiplying keeps the mix the same.

Grades 6–8SpeakingELD-MAExplain15 min

Your recording stays on this device. It is never uploaded.

  1. Task 1 of 3 · Levels 1–2 · Inform

    Look at the recipe card for trail mix. Name the ingredients and say how many cups of each one the recipe uses.

    Words: cups, cereal, dried apples, seeds, servings.

    Think: 15 seconds · Speak: about 30 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 1 sample

    Cereal three. Apple two. Seeds one.

    Discourse: names each ingredient with a number. Sentence: two-word phrases. Word: ingredient names and number words, without the word cups.

    Level 3 sample

    The recipe has 3 cups of cereal, 2 cups dried apple and 1 cup seeds. It make 6 servings.

    Discourse: lists all ingredients and adds the servings. Sentence: correct first sentence with small errors (missing of, make). Word: uses cups and servings.

    Level 5 sample

    The recipe calls for 3 cups of cereal, 2 cups of dried apples and 1 cup of seeds, and the whole batch makes 6 servings.

    Discourse: complete and organized in one statement. Sentence: one long, correct sentence with a series. Word: recipe language like calls for and batch.

    Check yourself

  2. Task 2 of 3 · Levels 3–4 · Explain

    Your class needs 18 servings of trail mix. The recipe makes 6 servings. Explain, step by step, how you would change the recipe.

    Use: divide, multiply, times, each, ratio, so.

    Think: 30 seconds · Speak: about 60 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 2 sample

    18. Three times. Nine cereal.

    Discourse: gives the key number and one result. Sentence: phrases only. Word: three times shows the idea of multiplying.

    Level 4 sample

    We need 18 servings and the recipe make 6, so 18 divide by 6 is 3. Then I multiply everything by 3. So 9 cups cereal, 6 cups apples and 3 cups seeds.

    Discourse: clear steps in order with so and then. Sentence: mostly complete, with small errors (make, divide by). Word: uses multiply and divide.

    Level 6 sample

    First, I would figure out how many batches we need. Eighteen servings divided by 6 servings per batch is 3, so we need to triple the recipe. Then I would multiply each ingredient by 3: 9 cups of cereal, 6 cups of dried apples and 3 cups of seeds. That keeps the ratio of 3 to 2 to 1 the same, so the mix will taste the same.

    Discourse: steps in order and a reason the method works. Sentence: uses first, then and so, with would for a plan. Word: precise math words like per batch, triple and ratio.

    Check yourself

  3. Task 3 of 3 · Level 5 · Explain

    Dani made the trail mix a different way. She added 2 more cups of each ingredient, so she used 5 cups of cereal, 4 cups of dried apples and 3 cups of seeds. Will her trail mix taste the same as the original? Will it make 18 servings? Explain your reasoning using ratios.

    Dani's mix will / will not ___ because ___. The original ratio is ___, but ___. To fix it, ___.

    Think: 45 seconds · Speak: about 90 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 2 sample

    No. Not same. More seeds.

    Discourse: an answer and one detail. Sentence: short phrases. Word: everyday words not same and more.

    Level 4 sample

    Dani's mix will not taste the same. The original is 3 to 2 to 1, but Dani's is 5 to 4 to 3, so it has too many seeds. Also it is only 12 cups, so it is not enough for 18 servings.

    Discourse: answers both questions with evidence. Sentence: uses but, so and also; correct but repeats one pattern. Word: uses ratio form 3 to 2 to 1, but does not explain why adding changes it.

    Level 6 sample

    Dani's mix will not taste the same, and it will not be enough. Adding the same amount to each ingredient changes the ratio. In the original, cereal is half of the mix, 3 out of 6 cups, but in Dani's mix it is only 5 out of 12, which is less than half, and the seeds jump from one-sixth to one-fourth of the mix. Also, the recipe makes 1 cup per serving, so her 12 cups would only make 12 servings. To keep the flavor, she has to multiply each amount by the same number, not add.

    Discourse: a two-part claim, detailed evidence, and a general rule as a fix. Sentence: uses which, but and so to link reasoning steps. Word: precise math language like out of, one-sixth, per serving and multiply by the same number.

    Check yourself
