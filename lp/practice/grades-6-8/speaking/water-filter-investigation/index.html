Testing two water filters

Students name lab materials, compare two water filter designs and explain why one works better, then evaluate a plan to use a filter for drinking water, building cause-and-effect and claim-evidence language.

Grades 6–8SpeakingELD-SCExplain15 min

Your recording stays on this device. It is never uploaded.

  1. Task 1 of 3 · Levels 1–2 · Inform

    Look at the materials on the lab table. Name the things you see.

    Words: beaker, muddy water, rocks, sand, cotton, timer.

    Think: 10 seconds · Speak: about 30 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 1 sample

    Beaker. Water. Rocks.

    Discourse: a list of single words. Sentence: no sentences yet. Word: names lab objects, including the science word beaker.

    Level 3 sample

    I see a beaker with dirty water. There is rocks and sand. Also a timer.

    Discourse: names most of the materials. Sentence: simple sentences, one agreement error (There is rocks) and one fragment. Word: uses dirty instead of muddy.

    Level 5 sample

    On the lab table there is a clear beaker full of muddy water, a cup of small rocks, some sand, a few cotton balls and a timer, so it looks like we are going to test ways to clean the water.

    Discourse: lists the materials and predicts the purpose of the lab. Sentence: one long, well-built sentence with a series and so. Word: precise words like muddy, clear and test.

    Check yourself

  2. Task 2 of 3 · Levels 3–4 · Explain

    The class built two filters. Filter A has only rocks. Filter B has a layer of rocks, then a layer of sand, then cotton. The water that came out of Filter B was much clearer. Compare the two filters and explain why Filter B worked better.

    Use: layer, particles, gaps, trap, clearer, while, because.

    Think: 25 seconds · Speak: about 60 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 2 sample

    Filter B clean. Sand. Rocks big holes.

    Discourse: names the better filter and two key ideas. Sentence: phrases without verbs. Word: holes stands in for gaps.

    Level 4 sample

    Filter A only have rocks, and Filter B has rocks, sand and cotton. Filter B worked better because the sand is small and it catch the little dirt. The rocks have big spaces so the dirt go through.

    Discourse: compares, then explains the cause. Sentence: uses because and so, with verb errors (have, catch, go). Word: everyday words like little dirt and big spaces for particles and gaps.

    Level 6 sample

    Both filters started with a layer of rocks, but Filter B also had sand and cotton underneath. Filter B produced clearer water because each layer traps smaller particles than the one above it. The gaps between the rocks are large, so fine mud slips right through Filter A, while the tiny spaces in the sand and the cotton fibers catch the particles the rocks miss.

    Discourse: a clear comparison followed by a step-by-step cause. Sentence: uses but, because, so and while to show contrast and cause. Word: precise science words like particles, layer, fibers and produced.

    Check yourself

  3. Task 3 of 3 · Level 5 · Argue

    Filter B made clearer water, but it took 4 minutes. Filter A took only 1 minute. A team wants to use Filter B to clean stream water for drinking on a camping trip. Is that a good plan? Use evidence from the investigation to support your answer.

    I think the plan is ___ because ___. The evidence shows ___. However, ___.

    Think: 45 seconds · Speak: about 90 seconds

    Hear sample answers

    Level 2 sample

    Filter B. Clean. But slow.

    Discourse: a choice and one trade-off. Sentence: short phrases joined by but. Word: everyday words clean and slow.

    Level 4 sample

    I think Filter B is good because the water is more clear than Filter A. It is slow, but clean water is more important than fast water. But maybe they need to boil it too.

    Discourse: a position, evidence, a trade-off and a new idea at the end. Sentence: uses because and but, with one error (more clear). Word: uses important and boil, but the boiling idea is not explained.

    Level 6 sample

    Filter B is a good first step, but it is not enough by itself. Our results show it removed far more mud than Filter A, so it is worth waiting the extra 3 minutes. However, clear water is not always safe water. A sand-and-cotton filter can trap dirt, but it cannot remove germs that are too small to see. So the team should use Filter B to clean the water and then boil it before drinking.

    Discourse: a qualified claim, evidence from the test, a limit of the evidence and a recommendation. Sentence: uses so, however and but to build a careful argument. Word: precise words like results, removed, worth and germs.

    Check yourself
