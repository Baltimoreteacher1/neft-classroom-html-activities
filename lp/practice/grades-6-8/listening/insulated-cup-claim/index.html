Which cup keeps heat in?

Students listen to two classmates debate whether their heat investigation supports a claim and answer questions about data, fair tests and evaluating evidence, focusing on argument language such as claim, evidence, however and if.

Grades 6–8ListeningELD-SCArgue16 min

Is Our Claim Strong?

A science teacher talks with two students about the results of a heat investigation.

This browser cannot read aloud. Read the words below, or ask a teacher to read them.

Show the words

Mr. Osei: Yesterday, your group tested which cup keeps hot water warm the longest. You poured water at 80 degrees Celsius into 3 cups. After 20 minutes, you measured the temperature again. Rafael, what is your group's claim?

Rafael: Our claim is that the foam cup is the best insulator. The water in the foam cup dropped to 68 degrees. The paper cup dropped to 55 degrees, and the metal cup dropped to 49 degrees. The foam cup lost the least heat, so it is the best.

Lina: I agree that the foam cup kept the water warmest. However, I am not sure our test was fair. The foam cup had a plastic lid, but the other 2 cups did not have lids.

Rafael: Why does that matter? The lid is just a small piece of plastic.

Lina: A lid stops warm air from escaping at the top. So we cannot tell if the foam or the lid kept the heat in. We changed 2 variables at the same time.

Mr. Osei: That is a strong piece of reasoning, Lina. In a fair test, you change only 1 variable and keep everything else the same. What else could make your evidence stronger?

Lina: We only did each test 1 time. If we repeat the test 3 times and get similar results, we can trust them more.

Rafael: Okay, I see the problem. Next time, we will test all 3 cups with no lids, and we will repeat it 3 times. If the foam cup still loses the least heat, our claim will be much stronger.

Mr. Osei: Excellent. Remember, a claim is only as strong as the evidence and reasoning behind it.
