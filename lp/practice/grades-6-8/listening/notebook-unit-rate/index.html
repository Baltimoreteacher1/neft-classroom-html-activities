Finding the better deal

Students listen to a class use unit rates to choose between two notebook packs and answer questions about calculations, reasoning and a final argument, focusing on comparison language and explaining a mathematical choice.

Grades 6–8ListeningELD-MAExplain15 min

Comparing Notebook Packs

A math teacher and two students compare prices for a class supply drive.

This browser cannot read aloud. Read the words below, or ask a teacher to read them.

Show the words

Mr. Alvarez: Our class is collecting school supplies for the community center. We have 50 dollars to spend on notebooks. Today, we will use unit rates to decide which pack to buy.

Mr. Alvarez: Here are our 2 choices. Pack A has 4 notebooks for 6 dollars. Pack B has 10 notebooks for 12 dollars and 50 cents.

Hana: At first, I thought Pack A was cheaper because 6 dollars is less than 12 dollars and 50 cents. But that compares the whole packs, not single notebooks.

Mr. Alvarez: Good thinking. So how do we compare them fairly?

Mateo: We find the unit price, which is the cost of 1 notebook. For Pack A, I divide 6 by 4. That equals 1 dollar and 50 cents per notebook. For Pack B, I divide 12 dollars and 50 cents by 10.

Hana: So Pack B has the lower unit price. Each notebook in Pack B costs less.

Mr. Alvarez: Correct. Now think about our budget. With 50 dollars, we can buy exactly 4 packs of Pack B, which gives us 40 notebooks. With Pack A, we could buy 8 packs for 48 dollars.

Hana: However, there is one more thing to think about. The community center said they need small notebooks for young children. Only Pack A has small notebooks.

Mr. Alvarez: That is an important point. The lowest unit price is not always the best choice. We also need to think about what the people we are helping actually need. Tomorrow, we will vote and explain our reasoning.
