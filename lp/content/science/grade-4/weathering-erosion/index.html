Weathering, erosion and cause-and-effect language

Students use measurements of how far water carries sand down a gentle and a steep slope and explain, with cause-and-effect language, how weathering breaks rock and erosion moves it.

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

NGSS
4-ESS2-1: Make observations and/or measurements to provide evidence of the effects of weathering or the rate of erosion by water, ice, wind, or vegetation.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-SC: Language for Science
Key Language Use
Explain

Language demands

Discourse

  • Explain a process in order: what the water, ice, wind or roots did first, what happened to the rock or soil next, and what the result was.
  • Use a measurement from the investigation (centimeters moved, height lost) as evidence for a claim about which condition caused more erosion.
  • Keep two related processes apart in one explanation: weathering breaks rock down in place, while erosion carries the pieces somewhere else.

Sentence

  • Cause-and-effect connectors that link an agent to a result: because, so, as a result, when ... then.
  • Passive voice to put the rock or soil at the center: The rock was cracked by ice. The sand was carried to the bottom.
  • Comparatives with a measurement: The sand on the steep slope moved 7 centimeters farther than the sand on the gentle slope.

Word / phrase

  • Process verbs with exact meanings: crack, split, wear away, loosen, carry, drop (deposit).
  • The pair weathering / erosion, which students often blend; the clue is break versus move.
  • Measurement and time words: centimeters, height, distance, after three pours, faster, slower, rate.

Objectives

Content
Students will use measurements of how far water moves sand on a gentle and a steep slope as evidence of the effect of slope on the rate of erosion.
Language
Students will explain orally and in writing how water causes weathering and erosion using cause-and-effect connectors (because, so, as a result) and one comparative sentence with a measurement.

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • The water ___ the sand. (moves / breaks)
  • This is ___. (weathering / erosion)
  • The sand moved ___ centimeters.
  • Steep slope: ___. Gentle slope: ___. (more / less)

Levels 3–4

  • The sand moved because ___.
  • After ___ pours, the sand moved ___ centimeters, so ___.
  • ___ is weathering, but ___ is erosion because ___.
  • On the steep slope, the sand moved ___ than on the gentle slope.

Levels 5–6

  • Our measurements show that ___, which is evidence that ___.
  • Although water touched both slopes, the steep slope lost more sand because ___.
  • First the water ___; as a result, ___; finally, the sediment ___.
  • If we poured the water faster, I predict ___, because ___.

Activity: Sand Slope Race

15 minutes · small groups

  1. Draw two ramps on the board, one gentle and one steep, with a pile of sand at the top of each. Write these results from a sand-tray test that poured the same cup of water three times on each ramp: steep slope 5, 11 and 18 centimeters after pours 1, 2 and 3; gentle slope 3, 7 and 11 centimeters. (Optional: if a tray, damp sand, books, a ruler and water are on hand, run the test as a demonstration and measure instead.)
  2. Draw a cracked rock and a pile of sand. Ask: 'Which shows breaking? Which shows moving?' Write the anchor on the board: weathering = break; erosion = move.
  3. Groups copy the results into a small table (pour, steep, gentle) and take turns saying how far the front edge of the sand traveled after each pour, in centimeters.
  4. Groups compare their two numbers and complete one L1-2, L3-4 or L5-6 frame aloud with a partner before anyone writes.
  5. Each student writes two sentences: one cause-and-effect sentence (because / so / as a result) and one comparative sentence that includes a measurement.
  6. Close by asking one group to explain how this tray models a real river or a hillside after a storm, and add one new word to the word list on the board.

Problems

  1. The sand pile was 12 centimeters tall before pouring and 7 centimeters tall after. How many centimeters of height did the pile lose?
    Answer
    5 centimeters
  2. On the steep slope the sand moved 18 centimeters in 3 pours. On average, how many centimeters did it move per pour?
    Answer
    6 centimeters per pour
  3. The sand moved 18 centimeters on the steep slope and 11 centimeters on the gentle slope. How much farther did it move on the steep slope?
    Answer
    7 centimeters farther

Five-minute warm-up

Break or Move? Read six short scenes aloud (ice freezes in a crack and splits a boulder; a river carries pebbles downstream; tree roots push a sidewalk apart; wind blows sand across a beach; rain washes soil off a garden; a glacier drags rocks downhill). Students hold up one finger for break (weathering) or two for move (erosion), then one student per scene says: 'It is ___ because the ___ ___ the rock.'
