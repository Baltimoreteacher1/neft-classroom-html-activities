Resources, populations and data-based argument

Students read a four-year data table on an acorn crop and a squirrel population and argue, with claim-evidence-reasoning language, how food availability limits a population.

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

NGSS
MS-LS2-1: Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence for the effects of resource availability on organisms and populations of organisms in an ecosystem.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-SC: Language for Science
Key Language Use
Argue

Language demands

Discourse

  • Build a claim-evidence-reasoning argument in which the evidence is specific numbers from a data table, not a general impression.
  • Account for a time lag: the squirrel count responds to the acorn crop of the previous fall, so the argument must connect the right rows.
  • Acknowledge an alternative explanation (a new predator, a harsh winter) and explain why the pattern across all years still supports the claim.

Sentence

  • Correlation structures that pair two changes: As the acorn crop fell, the squirrel population ___. When food was abundant, ___.
  • Noun groups that package data: a 40 percent decrease in the squirrel population; the largest acorn crop in four years.
  • Hedged claims that match the strength of the evidence: The data suggest ...; This is likely because ...; It is possible that ...

Word / phrase

  • Trend verbs with precise meanings: rose, dropped, peaked, recovered, leveled off.
  • The difference between an organism (one squirrel) and a population (all the squirrels in the forest).
  • Nominalized science terms: availability, competition, limiting factor.

Objectives

Content
Students will analyze a data table of acorn crops and squirrel counts to provide evidence that food availability affects the size of a population in an ecosystem.
Language
Students will argue orally and in writing that food is a limiting factor using a claim, two pieces of numerical evidence introduced with correlation structures (As ___, ___), and hedged reasoning (The data suggest ___).

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • Acorns went ___. Squirrels went ___. (up / down)
  • My claim: Squirrels need ___ to survive.
  • In Year ___, there were ___ squirrels.
  • Less food → ___ squirrels.

Levels 3–4

  • As the acorn crop ___, the squirrel population ___ the next spring.
  • My evidence is that in Year ___, ___, but in Year ___, ___.
  • This happens because acorns are a limiting factor, so when they are scarce, ___.
  • Someone might say ___, but the table shows ___.

Levels 5–6

  • The data suggest that ___; for example, between Year ___ and Year ___, the population showed a ___ percent change.
  • Because the population responds one season after the crop, ___.
  • One might argue that ___; however, the pattern across all four years indicates ___.
  • To make this claim stronger, scientists could also measure ___, because ___.

Activity: Data Detectives: Acorns and Squirrels

18 minutes · partners

  1. Project this table (sample data from an imaginary oak forest): Fall acorn crop in kg per hectare / squirrels counted the next spring. Year 1: 200 / 80. Year 2: 50 / 48. Year 3: 220 / 88. Year 4: 120 / 64.
  2. Partners spend two minutes noticing: draw an up or down arrow beside each number that changed from the year before, then say one sentence using a trend verb (rose, dropped, peaked, recovered).
  3. Partners solve the three problems below, then circle the two rows that give the strongest evidence.
  4. Each partner writes a claim, two pieces of numerical evidence and one sentence of reasoning that uses the term limiting factor. Point students to the frame that matches their band.
  5. Hand each pair a challenge card: 'A hawk family moved into the forest in Year 2. Maybe the hawks caused the drop.' Partners add one sentence that acknowledges this idea and one that uses Year 3 or Year 4 to respond.
  6. Two pairs read their arguments aloud; the class names which hedge word (suggest, likely, possible) each pair used and whether it matched the strength of the evidence.

Problems

  1. The squirrel population went from 80 in Year 1 to 48 in Year 2. What was the percent decrease?
    Answer
    40 percent
  2. The acorn crop went from 200 kg per hectare in Year 1 to 50 kg per hectare in Year 2. What was the percent decrease?
    Answer
    75 percent
  3. How many fewer squirrels were counted in Year 4 than in Year 3?
    Answer
    24 fewer squirrels

Five-minute warm-up

Rise, Drop, Level Off: sketch three quick line graphs on the board (one climbing, one falling, one climbing then flat). Partners match each graph to a trend verb card (rises, drops, levels off), then take turns saying: 'The line ___ between ___ and ___.' Finish by asking two students to invent a living-thing example for one graph, such as frogs in a pond after a dry summer.
