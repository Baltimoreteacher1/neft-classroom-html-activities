Points in all four quadrants

In a barrier game, one partner gives spoken directions to a hidden point in any quadrant and the other plots it, then both describe how reflected points are related.

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

CCSS
6.NS.C.6: Understand a rational number as a point on the number line. Extend number line diagrams and coordinate axes familiar from previous grades to represent points on the line and in the plane with negative number coordinates.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-MA: Language for Mathematics
Key Language Use
Inform

Language demands

Discourse

  • Give step-by-step directions to a point so precisely that a partner who cannot see the grid plots it correctly: start at the origin, move along the x-axis first, then parallel to the y-axis.
  • Describe where a point is using its quadrant, the axes and nearby points, and correct a partner's plot by naming the step that went wrong.
  • Describe how two points that differ only in sign are related: reflections across an axis, the same distance from it on opposite sides.

Sentence

  • Ordered pairs read aloud with x named first: the point negative three, four.
  • Imperatives with direction and distance: Start at the origin. Move 3 units left. Then move 4 units up.
  • Prepositional phrases of position: in Quadrant II, on the x-axis, 2 units to the left of the y-axis, between (−2, 1) and (4, 1).

Word / phrase

  • Axis and axes (irregular plural), origin, quadrant, coordinate, ordered pair; quadrant numbers read as Roman numerals I to IV.
  • Horizontal and vertical linked to left/right and up/down, and to the negative and positive directions.
  • The multiple-meaning word reflect: a mirror image across an axis versus thinking back on your work.

Objectives

Content
Students will locate and plot points with negative coordinates in all four quadrants and recognize that points whose coordinates differ only in sign are reflections across an axis.
Language
Students will give and follow oral directions to a point using sequenced imperatives and positional phrases, naming the quadrant and the axis of any reflection.

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • Start at the origin. Move ___ units left / right.
  • Move ___ units up / down.
  • The point is in Quadrant ___. (Word bank: left, right, up, down, origin, axis)

Levels 3–4

  • The point (___, ___) is in Quadrant ___ because x is ___ and y is ___.
  • Start at the origin, move ___ units ___, then ___ units ___.
  • Point ___ is a reflection of point ___ across the ___-axis.

Levels 5–6

  • Since only the sign of the ___-coordinate changed, the point reflected across the ___-axis and stayed ___ units from it.
  • Your point is off because you used the ___-coordinate for the left-right move; the x-coordinate always tells ___.
  • Both points are ___ units from the y-axis, but they lie in different quadrants because ___.

Activity: Hidden Point Directions

15 minutes · partners

  1. Partners sit with a book or folder standing between them. Each partner draws a coordinate plane from −6 to 6 on both axes (graph paper if available).
  2. Partner A secretly plots a point in Quadrant II, III or IV and gives directions using the frames. Partner B plots it without looking and reads back the ordered pair.
  3. Lower the folder and compare. If the plots differ, Partner A names the step that went wrong (You moved 4 units left, but the x-coordinate is −3). Switch roles; play four rounds.
  4. Round 5: Partner A gives a point, Partner B plots its reflection across the y-axis, then across the x-axis, and describes what changed and what stayed the same.
  5. Partners solve the problems below and share one description of a reflection with the class.

Problems

  1. Plot the point (−3, 4). Which quadrant is it in?
    Answer
    Quadrant II: left of the y-axis and above the x-axis.
  2. Point A is at (−5, 2). Point B is its reflection across the y-axis. What is the x-coordinate of point B?
    Answer
    5, the same distance from the y-axis on the right side
  3. On a town map with the school at the origin, the library is at (−4, 3) and the park is at (6, 3). How many blocks apart are they along the street?
    Answer
    10 blocks

Five-minute warm-up

Play Simon Says with arms as axes: students stand, stretch arms out as the x-axis and point up and down as the y-axis. Call positions (Point to Quadrant III. Point to the negative side of the x-axis. Touch the origin.). Then partners take turns calling two commands each, using left, right, up, down, origin and quadrant.
