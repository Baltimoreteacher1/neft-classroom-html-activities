Unit rates and the better buy

Partners find the price per ounce of two cereal boxes and argue which is the better buy, then use a constant walking speed to predict distance (6.RP.A.3b).

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

CCSS
6.RP.A.2: Understand the concept of a unit rate a/b associated with a ratio a:b with b ≠ 0, and use rate language in the context of a ratio relationship.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-MA: Language for Mathematics
Key Language Use
Argue

Language demands

Discourse

  • State a claim about which option is the better buy and support it with two unit rates that use the same unit (dollars per ounce for both boxes).
  • Explain where a unit rate comes from: which quantity was divided by which, and what the one in the unit rate refers to.
  • Answer a partner's counterclaim (the big box costs more) by separating the total price from the price for one unit.

Sentence

  • Rate phrases with a singular noun after per: dollars per ounce, miles per hour (not per ounces).
  • Division described in words: I divided the price by the number of ounces to find the cost of one ounce.
  • Comparative claims with rates: The 18-ounce box costs less per ounce than the 12-ounce box, so it is the better buy.

Word / phrase

  • Per, each and the slash read aloud: $/oz is said dollars per ounce; mph is said miles per hour.
  • The multiple-meaning word unit: one of something (unit rate), a measurement (ounce, mile) and a chapter of study.
  • Everyday shopping language that carries math meaning: better buy, deal, costs more, costs less, cheaper.

Objectives

Content
Students will find unit rates from ratios, including unit price and constant speed, and use them to compare options and predict values (6.RP.A.2, 6.RP.A.3b).
Language
Students will argue which option is the better buy using per and each, supporting the claim with two unit rates in the same unit.

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • ___ dollars per ounce.
  • The ___ box is the better buy. (big / small)
  • One ounce costs ___. (Word bank: per, each, ounce, costs less)

Levels 3–4

  • I divided ___ by ___ to find the cost of one ___.
  • The ___ box costs $___ per ounce, but the ___ box costs $___ per ounce.
  • The ___ box is the better buy because it costs less for each ___.

Levels 5–6

  • Even though the ___ box has a higher total price, its unit price is lower, because ___.
  • To compare fairly, both rates have to be in ___ per ___, so I ___.
  • At a constant rate of ___ per ___, in ___ we would expect ___, since ___.

Activity: Best Buy Showdown

18 minutes · partners

  1. Write two cereal boxes on the board: Box A is 12 ounces for $3.00; Box B is 18 ounces for $3.96. Ask each pair for a quick gut claim: which is the better buy?
  2. Partner A finds the unit price of Box A and Partner B finds Box B. Each says the division aloud with a frame (I divided ___ by ___ to find the cost of one ___).
  3. Partners write a claim and two pieces of evidence on paper. One partner argues for the other box on purpose; the other answers the counterclaim using unit price, not total price.
  4. Switch contexts: a student walks 6 miles in 2 hours at a constant speed. Partners find the unit rate and use it to predict the distance in 5 hours (6.RP.A.3b).
  5. Two pairs read their claim aloud; the class listens for per plus a singular unit and for both rates being in the same unit.

Problems

  1. Box A holds 12 ounces of cereal and costs $3.00. What is its unit price?
    Answer
    $0.25 per ounce
  2. Box B holds 18 ounces of cereal and costs $3.96. What is its unit price?
    Answer
    $0.22 per ounce
  3. Which box is the better buy? Support your claim with the unit prices.
    Answer
    Box B, the larger box, because it costs less per ounce than Box A.
  4. A student walks 6 miles in 2 hours at a constant speed. At that rate, how far will the student walk in 5 hours?
    Answer
    15 miles

Five-minute warm-up

Write $/oz, mph, $/lb and words/min on the board. Partners take turns reading each one aloud as a full rate (dollars per ounce, miles per hour), then invent one real example for each, such as a car going 30 miles per hour. Finish by asking: in each rate, what does per mean? Collect the answer for each or in one.
