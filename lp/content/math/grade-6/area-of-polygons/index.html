Area by composing and decomposing

Small groups cut and rearrange triangles, parallelograms and trapezoids into rectangles, then argue that their decomposition gives the correct area and critique a classmate's choice of height.

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

CCSS
6.G.A.1: Find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals, and polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles and other shapes; apply these techniques in the context of solving real-world and mathematical problems.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-MA: Language for Mathematics
Key Language Use
Argue

Language demands

Discourse

  • Argue that a rearranged or decomposed figure has the same area as the original, naming which pieces were cut, moved or added.
  • Critique a partner's work by pointing to one specific measurement (the slanted side was used instead of the height) and proposing a fix.
  • Compare two different decompositions of the same figure and explain why both give the same area.

Sentence

  • Procedural claims: I decomposed the trapezoid into a rectangle and two right triangles.
  • Conditional and causal reasoning: If I cut off this triangle and slide it to the other side, the parallelogram becomes a rectangle, so its area is base times height.
  • Polite disagreement: I see why you used 5 meters, but the height has to be perpendicular to the base, so it should be ___.

Word / phrase

  • Base and height as math terms, compared with everyday meanings (a baseball base, how tall a person is); height here must be perpendicular to the base.
  • Compose and decompose (de- 'undo'), parallelogram, trapezoid, right triangle, perpendicular.
  • Square units read aloud: 20 cm² is said twenty square centimeters, not twenty centimeters squared in everyday talk.

Objectives

Content
Students will find the area of right triangles, other triangles, parallelograms, trapezoids and composite polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles and rectangles.
Language
Students will argue that a decomposition gives the correct area using I decomposed ___ into ___ and if/so reasoning, and critique a peer's choice of base and height with polite disagreement frames.

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • I cut the shape into a ___ and a ___.
  • Base: ___. Height: ___. Area: ___ square ___.
  • The height is perpendicular / not perpendicular. (Word bank: base, height, triangle, rectangle, cut, move)

Levels 3–4

  • I decomposed the ___ into ___ and ___, so the area is ___ + ___.
  • The area of the triangle is half of the ___ because ___.
  • I agree / disagree with ___ because the height should be ___.

Levels 5–6

  • Moving this triangle does not change the area, because ___; it only changes ___.
  • I see why you used ___, but ___, so the area would actually be ___.
  • Both decompositions give ___ square units because ___, even though one uses ___ and the other uses ___.

Activity: Cut, Slide, Defend

20 minutes · small groups

  1. Give each group of three paper copies of a parallelogram (base 9 m, height 4 m, slanted side 5 m), a trapezoid and an L-shaped garden, plus scissors.
  2. Each student takes one shape, cuts it into pieces and rearranges or splits it so the area can be found from rectangles and triangles. They write the area with square units.
  3. In turn, each student presents with I decomposed ___ into ___ and if/so reasoning. The other two must each ask one question or raise one disagreement using a critique frame.
  4. Show a sample error: someone found the parallelogram's area as 9 × 5. Groups agree or disagree in writing and fix it.
  5. Groups solve the problems below, then share one case where two members decomposed the same shape differently and still got the same area.

Problems

  1. A right triangle has legs of 8 cm and 5 cm. What is its area?
    Answer
    20 square centimeters
  2. A parallelogram has a base of 9 m, a height of 4 m and a slanted side of 5 m. What is its area?
    Answer
    36 square meters
  3. A trapezoid has parallel sides of 6 ft and 10 ft and a height of 4 ft. Decompose it to find its area.
    Answer
    32 square feet
  4. An L-shaped garden is a 10 m by 6 m rectangle with a 4 m by 3 m corner removed. What is its area?
    Answer
    48 square meters

Five-minute warm-up

Show a picture of a baseball diamond, point to the base of a lamp and ask how to measure the height of the doorway. Partners write each everyday meaning of base and height, then draw a triangle and mark its math base and height. Finish with a turn-and-talk: Can the height of a triangle be outside the triangle? Use I think ___ because ___.
