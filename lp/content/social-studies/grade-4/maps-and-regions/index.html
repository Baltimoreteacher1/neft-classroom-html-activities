Maps, regions and the language of comparison

Students read a practice map, its legend and a data table to compare a coastal region with a mountain region, using comparatives and contrast words to inform.

Make this a game Edit the activity

Standards

C3
D2.Geo.2.3-5 [VERIFY]: Use maps, satellite images, photographs, and other representations to explain relationships between the locations of places and regions and their environmental characteristics.
WIDA ELD Standard
ELD-SS: Language for Social Studies
Key Language Use
Inform

Language demands

Discourse

  • Inform a reader about two regions by organizing facts into categories (location, landforms, climate, how people live) rather than listing details at random.
  • Combine information from several sources at once: the map, the legend and a data table.
  • State both similarities and differences, and support each with a detail read from the map or table.

Sentence

  • Comparatives and superlatives: drier than, higher than, the wettest, the highest elevation.
  • Contrast and similarity structures: Both regions ___. Region A ___, while Region B ___. Region A ___, but Region B ___.
  • Prepositional phrases of location: north of the river, along the coast, between the mountains, near the border.

Word / phrase

  • Cardinal and intermediate directions: north, south, east, west, northeast, southwest.
  • Map-reading terms: legend, symbol, scale, border, elevation.
  • Noun groups that name measurements: average yearly rainfall, height above sea level.

Objectives

Content
Students will use a map, its legend and a data table to explain how two regions are alike and different in location, landforms and climate.
Language
Students will inform a partner and then write about two regions using comparatives (drier than, higher than) and contrast structures (Both ___; ___, while ___).

Sentence frames by proficiency band

Levels 1–2

  • Region A has ___. (mountains / a coast / rivers)
  • Region B is ___. (dry / wet / high / low)
  • Both regions have ___.
  • Region A is ___ of Region B. (north / south / east / west)

Levels 3–4

  • Region A is ___ than Region B.
  • Both regions ___, but Region A ___.
  • The legend shows that ___ means ___.
  • Region B is located ___, so ___.

Levels 5–6

  • While Region A ___, Region B ___, which affects how people ___.
  • According to the map and the table, the greatest difference between the regions is ___.
  • Region A gets ___ centimeters more rain each year than Region B, so ___.
  • Although both regions ___, they differ in ___ because ___.

Activity: Region Detectives

18 minutes · partners

  1. Sketch a simple map of an imaginary country on the board with a compass rose and a legend of three symbols (wavy line = river, triangle = mountain, square = farm). Region A, in the east, is a low coastal plain with farms. Region B, in the west, is dry and mountainous. A river starts in the mountains of Region B and flows east through Region A to the coast. Show this table beside it: Average yearly rainfall: Region A 120 cm, Region B 25 cm. Highest elevation: Region A 50 m, Region B 1,500 m.
  2. Point to three legend symbols and have students tell a partner what each one means: 'This symbol means ___.'
  3. Partners fold a paper into a T-chart labeled Region A and Region B, with rows for location, landforms, climate and how people might live. They fill in each row using words or quick sketches.
  4. Partners solve the two problems below and add the answers to the climate and landforms rows.
  5. Partners circle one similarity (for example, the river flows through both regions, or both are part of the same country) and say it aloud with 'Both regions ___.'
  6. Each student writes three sentences: one similarity, one difference with a comparative and one sentence that uses while or but. Students who have lived in or visited another region or country may add a sentence comparing it to where they live now.

Problems

  1. Region A gets 120 centimeters of rain each year. Region B gets 25 centimeters. How many more centimeters of rain does Region A get?
    Answer
    95 centimeters more
  2. The highest point in Region B is 1500 meters above sea level. The highest point in Region A is 50 meters. How much higher is Region B's highest point?
    Answer
    1450 meters higher

Five-minute warm-up

Where Is It? Post the four cardinal directions on the classroom walls. Name an object (the door, the flag, the sink, the bookshelf) and have students point, then say: 'The ___ is on the ___ side of the room.' Add a challenge round with intermediate directions: 'The pencil sharpener is northeast of my desk.'
