Built in a classroom, for a classroom.

eduwonderlab.com is a complete Grade 6 mathematics learning platform designed, built, and maintained by one public-school teacher — so every student, including multilingual learners and students with IEPs, gets a first-class digital learning experience.

The problem

A Grade 6 math classroom in Baltimore County serves students spanning WIDA language levels 1–6 and a wide range of IEP accommodations. Print curriculum and generic digital tools deliver the same page to every student. Differentiating by hand for every lesson, every day, does not scale — and the students who need the most support get the most generic materials.

The approach

Instead of adapting worksheets one at a time, this platform makes differentiation a property of the system. Every lesson is config-driven and renders with layered supports that follow the individual student:

  • Per-student accommodations that travel. A teacher assigns district-standard IEP and language accommodations (58 verbatim district lines) plus a WIDA level to a student once — the right tools then appear on every lesson that student opens, on any device, with no account required.
  • Language is scaffolded, not simplified. Bilingual vocabulary with visuals, tiered sentence frames and math-talk stems by WIDA band, word banks, home language read-aloud, and bilingual family letters keep grade-level mathematics fully accessible. See how the platform serves multilingual learners.
  • AI as a coach, never an answer key. Socratic hint ladders, a stuck button on every lesson, and misconception-aware feedback scaffold thinking without giving answers away.
  • Work is never lost. A save/resume code system spans devices and days on nearly 2,000 activity pages — no logins, no passwords, minimal data.
  • It meets school reality. Everything runs on shared Chromebooks, exports to Canvas as SCORM packages, works offline where possible, and stays usable on phones.

What makes it different

  • Interactive 3D and AR manipulatives, real math games (math as the mechanic, never a quiz wrapper, never timed pressure), and live class play — engagement without anxiety.
  • A live signal layer: struggle, misconception, and mastery events feed a class radar and weekly family digests, so intervention happens the same day, not after the test.
  • Open-web engineering: static-first pages, progressive enhancement, graceful degradation — if any layer fails, the plain lesson still works.
  • Total build cost: one teacher's time. The stack (Cloudflare Pages, D1, Workers) runs on free tiers.
