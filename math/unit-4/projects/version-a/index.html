Before you start · Vocabulary

Visual Math Notes

Learn these words first. Tap EN / ES help at the top for Spanish.

💲Percent of a Number

A piece of a number, counted out of 100.

Porcentaje de un número: una parte de 100. Multiplica número × (porcentaje ÷ 100).

📈Markup

Money added on top of the cost to make a profit.

Margen / recargo: la cantidad que SUMAS al costo para ganar dinero.

🏷️Discount

Money taken off the price during a sale.

Descuento: la cantidad que RESTAS del precio en una oferta.

🧾Sales Tax

Extra money you pay at checkout, set by the state.

Impuesto de venta: dinero extra que el estado agrega al pagar, como porcentaje.

🔁Conversion Factor

A fraction you multiply by to change one unit to another.

Factor de conversión: una tasa escrita como fracción para cambiar unidades.

⚖️Unit Rate

How much for just one, like miles in 1 hour.

Tasa unitaria: una tasa para UNO — millas por 1 hora o dólares por 1 artículo.

Formula Bank

  • Percent of a number Move the percent to a decimal, then multiply. A percent is just "out of 100." Example: 20% of $50 = 0.20 × 50 = $10
  • Markup (selling price) Add the extra money on top of your cost. Selling price = cost + markup. Example: cost $40, 25% markup = $10, so sell for $50
  • Discount (sale price) Take the savings off the price. Sale price = price − discount. Example: $50, 10% off = $5 off, so sale = $45
  • Sales tax (total) Add the tax the store collects. Total = price + tax. Example: $45, 6% tax = $2.70, so total = $47.70
  • Stacked promo (challenge) Take the discount first, then add tax on the lower price. Example: $50 → 10% off = $45 → 6% tax = $47.70
  • Break-even markup % (challenge) The markup that makes the sale price land exactly on your cost, so you do not lose money. Example: cost $40, sell $50, on sale at 20% off → back to $40

Sentence Frames

"My cost is $___, and a ___% markup gives a selling price of $___."

"A ___% discount saves $___, so the sale price is $___."

"After ___% sales tax the customer pays $___."

"My final price is higher / lower than ___ because ___."

"Mi costo es $___ y un margen de ___% da un precio de venta de $___."

Level 1 tip: a friendly markup is 25%. Markup amount = cost × 0.25.

Need a hint?

Markup amount = cost × (markup ÷ 100). Example: 40.00 × (25 ÷ 100) =10.00. Selling price = $40.00 + $10.00 = $50.00.

💡 Guided Steps & Help

Calculating Percentages, Taxes, and Discounts:

  1. Convert the percentage to a decimal: Divide the percent by 100 (move the decimal point 2 places left, e.g., 6.5% = 0.065).
  2. Calculate tax or tip amount: Multiply the original price by the decimal value.
  3. Find the total cost: Add the tax or tip amount to the original price.
  4. For discounts: Multiply by the discount decimal, then subtract it from the original price.
💡 Example: $20.00 shirt with 8% sales tax. Tax = $20 × 0.08 = $1.60. Total = $21.60.

🏷️ Run a weekend sale (discount)

Level 1 tip: a 10% sale is the easiest. 10% off means you keep 90%. Find 10% by moving the decimal one place left (10% of $50 = $5 off → $45 sale).

Need a hint?

Discount amount = selling price × (discount ÷ 100). Sale price = selling price − discount amount. Example: 50 × 0.20 =10 off → $40 sale price.

Level 2 extension: Stack two deals — take your discount, then take an extra 15% off the new sale price. Is "30% off then 15% off" the same as "45% off"? Show the math and explain why.
