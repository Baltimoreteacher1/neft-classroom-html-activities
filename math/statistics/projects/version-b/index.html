Key Words
Learn these 6 statistics words before you begin
❓
Statistical Question
A question that anticipates different answers (variability) when collecting data.
"What is the daily high temp in Seattle?"
⚖️
Mean (Average)
The balance point: add all values together, then divide by the total count of values.
(70 + 72 + 77) ÷ 3 = 73°F
🎯
Median (Middle)
The middle value in an ordered set. If two middles exist, find their average.
In 65, 72, 80, the median is 72°F
📐
Interquartile Range (IQR)
The distance between the 1st and 3rd quartiles: IQR = Q3 - Q1. Measures typical spread.
Q3 (80°) - Q1 (70°) = 10°F IQR
📊
Box Plot
A display showing the 5-number summary: Min, Q1, Median, Q3, and Max. Highlights quartiles.
Visualizes spread and whiskers.
🏢
Histogram
A bar graph showing data grouped into equal range intervals (like 60-69, 70-79).
Displays frequency of data ranges.

Step 1 — Measures of Center & Spread
Record daily high temperatures and calculate statistics
Your job: Pose a statistical question about daily temperatures. Then, enter daily high temperatures for 5 to 8 days in your chosen city and click Calculate to find the statistical summaries.
What makes a question statistical?

A statistical question expects variability (a range of different answers).
• Statistical: "What was the daily high temperature in Seattle each day last week?" (Expects changes)
• Not Statistical: "What is the freezing point of water?" (Always 32°F; no variability)

LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE — Outlier Analysis

Add an outlier (a temperature much higher or lower than the rest) into one of the Level 2 slots. How does it change the mean versus the median? Which center is more reliable when outliers exist?

Need a calculation guide?

Mean: Add all values, divide by count.
Median: Sort the numbers, find the middle value. If count is even, average the 2 middle numbers.
Range: Maximum value − Minimum value.
IQR: Median of upper half (Q3) − Median of lower half (Q1).

Step 2 — Choose the Display
Identify the best representation & visualize your data
Your job: Compare different data displays. Decide which display best represents the overall spread, quartiles, and IQR of your temperatures, and view your automatically generated box plot.
🎯 Dot Plot
Shows every individual daily temperature. Perfect for small datasets, but does not group data or show quartiles clearly.
🏢 Histogram
Groups values into temperature ranges (like 70–79°F). Shows frequencies, but hides individual values and exact centers.

Region Category Typical IQR Range Climate Characteristics

Desert Regions (e.g. Phoenix, Las Vegas) 15°F – 25°F High variability. Dry air and lack of vegetation allow intense daytime heating and rapid nighttime cooling.
