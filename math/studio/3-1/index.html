UnderstandComprende

Read the idea, follow the worked examples, and connect the picture to the numbers.Lee la idea, sigue los ejemplos resueltos y conecta el dibujo con los números.

What is a ratio?¿Qué es una razón?

A ratio compares two amounts. It tells how much of one thing there is for another thing.Una razón compara dos cantidades. Dice cuánto hay de una cosa por cada cantidad de otra.

Watch me

  1. Chef Reyes uses 3 cups of apple juice for every 2 cups of sparkling water.El chef Reyes usa 3 tazas de jugo de manzana por cada 2 tazas de agua mineral.
  2. I am comparing two amounts: apple juice and sparkling water.Estoy comparando dos cantidades: jugo de manzana y agua mineral.
  3. I write apple juice to sparkling water, in that order, as 3 to 2, or 3:2.Escribo jugo de manzana a agua mineral, en ese orden, como 3 a 2, o 3:2.
  4. I can also draw the ratio. On a tape diagram I give apple juice 3 equal parts and sparkling water 2 equal parts, so one batch is 5 parts in all.También puedo dibujar la razón. En un diagrama de barras le doy al jugo de manzana 3 partes iguales y al agua mineral 2 partes iguales, así que una tanda son 5 partes en total.
  5. Those same 5 parts show a second ratio: apple juice to the whole fruit drink is 3 to 5.Esas mismas 5 partes muestran una segunda razón: jugo de manzana al total de la bebida es 3 a 5.
  6. On a double number line I line the two amounts up: 5 cups of fruit drink pairs with 3 cups of apple juice, and 10 cups of fruit drink pairs with 6 cups of apple juice.En una recta numérica doble alineo las dos cantidades: 5 tazas de bebida se emparejan con 3 tazas de jugo de manzana, y 10 tazas de bebida se emparejan con 6 tazas de jugo de manzana.

Let's try together

  1. A bowl has 4 apples and 1 orange. What two amounts are we comparing?Un tazón tiene 4 manzanas y 1 naranja. ¿Qué dos cantidades estamos comparando?
  2. We write apples to oranges in order: 4 to 1, or 4:1.Escribimos manzanas a naranjas en orden: 4 a 1, o 4:1.
  3. Now draw it: 4 equal parts for apples beside 1 part for oranges, 5 parts of fruit in all.Ahora dibújalo: 4 partes iguales para las manzanas junto a 1 parte para la naranja, 5 partes de fruta en total.
  4. So apples to total fruit is 4 to 5.Entonces manzanas al total de fruta es 4 a 5.

Now it's your turn

  1. Next you will sort the chef's statements into ratios and not-ratios.Luego vas a clasificar las afirmaciones del chef en razones y no razones.
  2. Then you will use a tape diagram and a double number line to scale the fruit-drink recipe.Después vas a usar un diagrama de barras y una recta numérica doble para escalar la receta de la bebida.
  3. Remember: a real ratio compares two amounts, and the order matters.Recuerda: una razón de verdad compara dos cantidades, y el orden importa.

Key ideaIdea clave

Understanding Ratios. Formula: a : b | a to b | a/b.

  1. A ratio compares two quantities; order of quantities matters.
  2. Part-to-Part compares two distinct subgroups (e.g. boys to girls).
  3. Part-to-Whole compares one subgroup to the total (e.g. boys to class)

Comprender las razones. Fórmula: a : b | a a b | a/b. 1. Una razón compara dos cantidades; el orden de las cantidades importa. 2. Parte a parte: compara dos subgrupos distintos (por ejemplo, niños a niñas). 3. Parte al todo: compara un subgrupo con el total (por ejemplo, niños al grupo)

Watch outCuidado

A common mistake in Understand Ratios is writing the ratio in the wrong order: when asked for the ratio of apple juice to sparkling water in a recipe that uses 3 cups of apple juice for every 2 cups of sparkling water, some students write 2:3 (sparkling water to apple juice) because they list the ingredients in whatever order comes to mind rather than the order the question asks for. The two ratios describe different drinks — 3:2 means 3 cups of juice for every 2 cups of water, while 2:3 would mean only 2 cups of juice for every 3 cups of water. Always match the order of your ratio to the order named in the question: "apple juice to sparkling water" means the apple juice number comes first, giving 3:2.

Words to usePalabras para usar
  • Understand RatiosComprender razones

    Use a ratio to compare two quantities by division.Usar una razón para comparar dos cantidades mediante la división.

    3 red tiles to 5 blue tiles can be written 3:5 or 3/5.

  • RatioRazón

    A way to compare two amounts, like 3 to 2.Una manera de comparar dos cantidades, como 3 a 2.

    3 cups flour to 2 cups sugar → 3:2

  • ComparisonComparación

    Looking at two or more amounts to see how they are related.Mirar dos o más cantidades para ver cómo se relacionan.

    5 eggs vs. 3 eggs — which recipe uses more?

  • Part-to-partParte a parte

    A ratio comparing one part of a group to another part.Una razón que compara una parte de un grupo con otra parte.

    3 teal counters to 5 amber counters → 3:5

  • Part-to-wholeParte a todo

    A ratio comparing one part to the whole group.Una razón que compara una parte con todo el grupo.

    3 teal counters out of 8 counters in all → 3:8

  • Colon notationNotación con dos puntos

    Writing a ratio with two dots between the numbers, like 3:2.Escribir una razón con dos puntos entre los números, como 3:2.

    3 to 2 can be written as 3:2

  • Tape diagramDiagrama de cintas

    A bar cut into equal parts that shows how two amounts in a ratio compare, and how they make up the whole.Una barra dividida en partes iguales que muestra cómo se comparan dos cantidades de una razón y cómo forman el total.

    3 equal parts of apple juice beside 2 equal parts of sparkling water — 5 parts of fruit drink in all.

  • Double number lineRecta numérica doble

    Two number lines lined up so matching tick marks show the two quantities in a ratio at the same time.Dos rectas numéricas alineadas para que las marcas correspondientes muestren las dos cantidades de una razón al mismo tiempo.

    Cups of apple juice 0, 3, 6, 12 above cups of fruit drink 0, 5, 10, 20.

Sentence frameMarco de oración

A ratio compares ___ to ___ using words like ___ or symbols like ___.Una razón compara ___ con ___ usando palabras como ___ o símbolos como ___.

Apply: Middle School Fundraiser

Some sixth-grade students will make 50 bags of honey granola to sell at a fundraising event using the recipe shown. They will put 2 cups of granola in each bag and will sell each bag for $5. Choose one question to answer. Question 1: How many cups of rolled oats will the students need to buy? Question 2: One pound of almonds costs $9.99 and has 3 cups of almonds. How much will the students need to budget for almonds?

Quieren ofrecer 3 platos fuertes por cada 2 guarniciones. Si tienen 10 guarniciones, el gerente de la cafetería necesita averiguar cuántos platos fuertes preparar.
