Words to knowPalabras clave

  • Factor

    A whole number that divides another number evenly, with no remainder.Un número entero que divide a otro exactamente, sin residuo.

    Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36

  • MultipleMúltiplo

    The result of multiplying a number by 1, 2, 3, and so on — where you land when you skip-count by it.El resultado de multiplicar un número por 1, 2, 3, y así sucesivamente: donde caes al contar de esa cantidad.

    Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40 …

  • Greatest common factor (GCF)Máximo común divisor (MCD)

    The largest number that is a factor of two or more numbers. It answers “how many equal groups can I make?”El número más grande que es factor de dos o más números. Responde “¿cuántos grupos iguales puedo formar?”

    48 and 36 share 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 → GCF = 12

  • Least common multiple (LCM)Mínimo común múltiplo (mcm)

    The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers. It answers “when will these line up again?”El número más pequeño que es múltiplo de dos o más números. Responde “¿cuándo volverán a coincidir?”

    6, 12, 18, 24 and 8, 16, 24 → LCM = 24

Let's do one together — the same GCF, using prime factors

  1. A factor tree breaks each number into primes. 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3, and 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.Un árbol de factores separa cada número en primos. 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3, y 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.

Key ideaIdea clave

Greatest Common Factor (GCF) & Least Common Multiple (LCM).

  1. GCF: Largest factor shared by two numbers — used to simplify fractions or divide into equal groups.
  2. LCM: Smallest shared multiple — used to find common denominators.
  3. Use prime factor trees or list method to find common factors/multiples

Máximo común divisor y mínimo común múltiplo. 1. Máximo común divisor: el factor más grande que comparten dos números — sirve para simplificar fracciones o repartir en grupos iguales. 2. Mínimo común múltiplo: el múltiplo compartido más pequeño — sirve para hallar denominadores comunes. 3. Usa árboles de factores primos o el método de listas para hallar factores y múltiplos comunes

Watch out for a common mistakeCuidado con un error común

A common mistake is reaching for the wrong tool: listing FACTORS for a problem about repeating cycles. Ayasha's classes repeat every 6 and 8 weeks, so the weeks they land on are multiples — answering with the GCF of 2 claims a class that meets every 8 weeks happens in week 2. Use the size of the answer as a check: a GCF is never larger than the smaller number, and an LCM is never smaller than the larger one.
