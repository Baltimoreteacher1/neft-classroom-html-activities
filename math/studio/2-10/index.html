  • MeanMedia

    The average. Add all the numbers, then divide by how many there are.El promedio. Suma todos los números y divide entre cuántos hay.

    Mean of 10, 20, 30 = (10+20+30) ÷ 3 = 20

  • MedianMediana

    The middle number when you put them in order.El número del medio cuando los pones en orden.

    Data: 5, 8, 12, 15, 20 → median is 12 (the 3rd of 5 values)

  • OutlierValor atípico

    A number that is much bigger or smaller than the rest.Un número mucho mayor o menor que los demás.

    Data: 12, 14, 13, 15, 45 → 45 is far from the cluster, so it is an outlier

  • SkewedSesgado

    When most data sits on one side with a tail on the other.Cuando la mayoría de los datos está de un lado con una cola del otro.

    Scores: 5, 6, 7, 8, 8, 35 → most scores are low, but 35 creates a tail to the right (skewed right)

The mean and the median both describe the center of a data set, but one fits better depending on the shape. When the data has an outlier (a value far from the rest), the median is usually the better choice.La media y la mediana describen el centro de un conjunto de datos, pero una encaja mejor según la forma. Cuando los datos tienen un valor atípico (un valor muy lejano al resto), la mediana suele ser la mejor opción.

Key ideaIdea clave

Choosing Appropriate Measures of Center & Spread.

  1. Symmetric Data (No Outliers): Use Mean and MAD.
  2. Skewed Data (Has Outliers): Use Median and IQR (outliers distort the mean).
  3. Compare distributions using both center (typical value) and spread (variability)

Elegir medidas apropiadas de centro y dispersión. 1. Datos simétricos (sin valores atípicos): usa la media y la desviación media absoluta. 2. Datos sesgados (con valores atípicos): usa la mediana y el rango intercuartílico (los valores atípicos distorsionan la media). 3. Compara distribuciones usando el centro (valor típico) y la dispersión (variabilidad)

Watch out for a common mistakeCuidado con un error común

A common mistake in Appropriate Measures is defaulting to the mean just because "it uses all the data," without first checking whether an outlier or skewed shape is present. For example, given a player's points per game 14, 16, 15, 17, 16, 58, a student calculates the mean (136 ÷ 6 ≈ 22.7) and reports that as the typical game — but 58 is far from the cluster of 14–17, so it pulls the mean above 5 of the 6 games. The median (16) actually represents a typical game here. Before choosing a measure, always scan the data for an outlier or a long tail first: only use the mean once you've confirmed the data has neither.
