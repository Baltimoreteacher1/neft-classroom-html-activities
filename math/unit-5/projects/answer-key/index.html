Version A — Dream Room Designer

Version A · 6.GR.1

Dream Room Designer — Worked Solutions

Step 1  ·  Rectangle Floor Area  ·  6.GR.1

Bedroom Floor Area (Rectangle)

Length (l) = 12 ft Width (w) = 10 ft
Formula: Area = length × width (l × w)
Area = 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft
120 sq ft

These are the Level 1 example numbers suggested in the app (length 12 ft, width 10 ft). Students who measure their own bedroom get a different but similar area. Inputs: f-length, f-width.

Step 2  ·  Triangle Area  ·  6.GR.1

Triangle Reading Nook

Base (b) = 6 ft Height (h) = 4 ft
Formula: Area = ½ × base × height (½ × b × h)
Area = ½ × 6 × 4
= 3 × 4
= 12 sq ft
12 sq ft

A triangle is exactly half a parallelogram. The ½ is critical — a common error is multiplying b × h without dividing by 2. Inputs: t-base, t-height.

Step 3  ·  Composite Total & Cost  ·  6.GR.1

Total Floor Area & Flooring Cost (auto-summed)

Floor = 120 sq ft Nook = 12 sq ft Total = 132 sq ft (auto-summed)
Total auto-sums: floor 120 + nook 12 = 132 sq ft (recalcTotal() adds rectangle + nook only)
Formula: Cost = Total area × Price per sq ft
Carpet ($3.50): 132 × $3.50 = $462.00
Tile ($6.00): 132 × $6.00 = $792.00
Hardwood ($9.00): 132 × $9.00 = $1,188.00
132 sq ft → $462.00 (Carpet)

The flooring dropdown sets the price automatically: Carpet $3.50, Tile $6.00, Hardwood $9.00 per sq ft (updateMaterialPrice()). Students may also type a custom price in range. The total area feeds the cost automatically; when students measure their own room the total and cost differ — that is expected and correct.

Step 4  ·  Partner Comparison  ·  6.GR.1

Compare With a Partner

Students enter a partner's total room area and total flooring cost. The page computes the difference for both and states who has the larger room and who spent more.

Example: my total 132 sq ft vs partner 120 sq ft
Area difference = 132 − 120 = 12 sq ft → "Yours is larger."
Cost (example): my $462.00 vs partner $540.00 → difference $78.00 → "Partner's is more expensive."

Look for a sentence-frame answer: "My room is ___ sq ft and my partner's is ___ sq ft, so mine is larger/smaller by ___ sq ft."

Level 2  ·  Challenge Extensions (open response)

Level 2 Challenge Answers

Double the length: if you double the bedroom length, the floor area doubles too (area is proportional to length: 12 × 10 = 120 → 24 × 10 = 240 sq ft).

Reverse nook: for a nook area of exactly 16 sq ft with height 4 ft → base = (2 × 16) ÷ 4 = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 ft.

$1,000 flooring budget: with the example total 132 sq ft, Carpet costs $462.00, leaving $538.00 of the budget. Hardwood ($1,188.00) is $188.00 over the $1,000 budget. Accept any correct budget math for the student's own numbers.
Sample Expert Response — Design Pitch (4 / Expert) My dream room's main floor is a rectangle 12 ft by 10 ft, giving an area of 120 sq ft (Area = l × w). The triangular reading nook adds 12 sq ft (½ × 6 × 4), so my total composite floor area auto-sums to 132 sq ft. I chose carpet at $3.50 per sq ft, so my flooring costs 132 × $3.50 = $462.00 — well under a $1,000 budget, leaving room for furniture. Hardwood would have cost $1,188.00, which is more than I want to spend for a bedroom this size.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring Note 4 — Expert: Rectangle and triangle areas correct with formulas shown, total correctly summed, flooring cost correct, and the pitch ties all numbers together and justifies the flooring choice.
3 — Proficient: All calculations correct; pitch uses numbers but justification is brief.
2 — Developing: One minor arithmetic error (e.g., forgot the ½ in the triangle, or added the areas incorrectly); pitch attempts to use numbers.
Version B — Room Makeover Budget

Version B · 6.GR.1 & 6.GR.4

Room Makeover Budget — Worked Solutions

Step 1  ·  Rectangle Floor Area  ·  6.GR.1

Room Floor Area (Rectangle)

Length (L) = 15 ft Width (W) = 12 ft
Formula: Area = Length × Width
Area = 15 × 12 = 180 sq ft
180 sq ft

These are the Level 1 example numbers suggested in the app (15 ft × 12 ft). Inputs: floor-length, floor-width. Student-measured rooms give a different but similar area.

Step 2  ·  Wall Surface Coverage  ·  6.GR.4

Wall Paint Coverage & Gallons

Wall Height = 8 ft Windows = 30 sq ft Doors = 20 sq ft
Formula: Gross wall = 2 × (L + W) × H; Net = Gross − windows − doors; Gallons = ⌈ Net ÷ 350 ⌉ (round UP)
Gross wall area = 2 × (15 + 12) × 8 = 54 × 8 = 432 sq ft
Net paintable area = 432 − 30 − 20 = 382 sq ft
Paint needed = 382 ÷ 350 = 1.09 → ⌈1.09⌉ = 2 gallons
382 sq ft — 2 gallons of paint

Key teaching point: 382 ÷ 350 ≈ 1.09, so one gallon is not enough — Math.ceil rounds up to 2 whole gallons. Inputs: wall-height, window-area, door-area. This wall surface-coverage work aligns to 6.GR.4 (nets / surface area).

Step 3  ·  Flooring & Paint Cost  ·  6.GR.1 Application

Total Makeover Cost

Floor = 180 sq ft Gallons = 2 Defaults: Carpet $3.50 · Budget $25.00
Formula: Flooring = Area × price/sq ft; Paint = Gallons × price/gallon; Total = Flooring + Paint
Flooring (Carpet $3.50): 180 × $3.50 = $630.00
Paint (Budget $25.00): 2 × $25.00 = $50.00
Total makeover cost: $630.00 + $50.00 = $680.00
$680.00 (below Low Makeover range)

The two dropdowns default to their first option — Carpet $3.50/sq ft and Budget Paint $25.00/gallon — so the live default total is $680.00, which the app labels "below the typical Low Makeover range" (Low = $1,000–$2,000). Other choices: Laminate $5.00, Hardwood $9.00, Ceramic $12.00; Standard $45.00, Premium $75.00. Example with Hardwood + Premium: flooring 180 × $9.00 = $1,620.00, paint 2 × $75.00 = $150.00, total $1,770.00 → within the Low Makeover range.

Step 4  ·  Partner Comparison  ·  6.GR.1

Compare With a Partner

Students enter a partner's floor area, paint gallons, and total cost. The page reports the difference for each and states who is larger / needs more paint / spent more.

Example: my 180 sq ft, 2 gallons, $680.00 vs partner 150 sq ft, 2 gallons, $900.00
Floor: 180 − 150 = 30 sq ft larger (yours)
Paint: 2 − 2 = same 2 gallons
Cost: $900.00 − $680.00 → partner's makeover costs $220.00 more.
Level 2  ·  Challenge Extensions (open response)

Level 2 Challenge Answers

L-shaped composite floor: main 12 × 15 = 180 sq ft plus closet extension 4 × 5 = 20 sq ft → total = 200 sq ft (split the L into two rectangles and add).

Two coats of paint: double the net paintable area (or double the gallons). With 382 sq ft one coat needs ⌈382 ÷ 350⌉ = 2 gallons; two coats need ⌈764 ÷ 350⌉ = 3 gallons.

$1,500 budget strategy & cost efficiency: open-ended — credit any material combination whose total stays under $1,500 with correct math, and any correct cost-per-sq-ft comparison (Total Cost ÷ Floor Area).
Sample Expert Response — Makeover Proposal (4 / Expert) My room's floor is 15 ft by 12 ft, so the floor area is 180 sq ft (Area = L × W). The gross wall area is 2 × (15 + 12) × 8 = 432 sq ft; after subtracting 30 sq ft of windows and 20 sq ft of doors, the net paintable area is 382 sq ft, which needs 2 whole gallons of paint because 382 ÷ 350 = 1.09 and I must round up. I chose carpet at $3.50/sq ft ($630.00) and budget paint at $25.00/gallon ($50.00), for a total makeover cost of $680.00 — a smart, low-cost makeover that stays below the typical Low Makeover range.
