Key Words
Learn these 5 math words before you begin
⚖️
Equation
A mathematical sentence stating that two expressions are equal (=).
180 + x = 500
↔️
Inequality
A statement comparing values using <, >, ≤, or ≥.
amount raised > 350
🔤
Variable
A letter or symbol representing an unknown value.
x = amount remaining
🎯
Solution
A value for the variable that makes the equation true.
x = 320

Step 1 — Fundraiser Goal & Progress
Write an addition equation to find the needed remaining funds
Your job: Enter a cause for your class fundraiser, set your total goal and current amount raised. Then write and solve an addition equation to find how much more you need.
🟢 Level 1 suggestion: Use the default values: Goal $500, Raised $180, and variable x.
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE

Choose your own unique fundraiser goal and variable name. Ensure your variable makes sense for the context!

Need a hint?

Your equation balances the money you have already raised plus the variable (needed amount) to equal your total goal: Raised + x = Goal. To solve it, subtract the raised amount from both sides.

↔️
Step 2 — Progress Milestones
Write and evaluate inequalities to represent milestones
Your job: Plan a key progress milestone (e.g., getting a stretch goal or class reward). Write an inequality using y (total amount raised) to represent reaching this milestone.
🟢 Level 1 suggestion: Write an inequality showing that the total raised (y) must be more than $350. Use symbol > and write y > 350.

🕵️ Test the Milestone (Substitution Check)

Substitute different raised amounts (values for y) to see if they satisfy your inequality milestone.

Need a hint?

An inequality uses comparison signs like > (greater than) or ≥ (greater than or equal to). An open circle on the number line means that exact value is excluded (like >), while a closed circle means it is included (like ≥).

Step 3 — Plan Product Sales
Write a multiplication equation to calculate item sales
Your job: Your class will sell fundraiser products to raise the remaining needed funds. Write and solve a multiplication equation to find the number of items (n) to sell.
