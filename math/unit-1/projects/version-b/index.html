Visual Math Notes

Learn these words first. Tap EN / ES help at the top for Spanish.

✖️Decimal Multiplication

Price × quantity, keeping the decimal places.

Multiplicación de decimales: precio × cantidad, manteniendo los decimales.

➗Multi-Digit Division

Splitting a cost into equal shares.

División de varios dígitos: dividir un costo en partes iguales.

🧰GCF — Greatest Common Factor

The biggest number that divides two counts evenly.

MCD — Máximo Común Divisor: el número más grande que divide a ambos sin sobrar.

⏱️LCM — Least Common Multiple

The first number two cycles share.

mcm — mínimo común múltiplo: el primer número que comparten dos ciclos.

Formula Bank

Line total: unit price × quantity

Fair share: total cost ÷ team members

GCF(a, b): biggest shared factor → number of equal kits

Budget left: budget − total spent

Distributive (6.NS.4): a + b = GCF × (a÷GCF + b÷GCF)

"The GCF of ___ and ___ is ___, so I can build ___ kits."

💡 Guided Steps & Help

Multiplying Decimals for Parts Order:

  1. Enter your part quantities and unit prices carefully.
  2. For each part, write the multiplication problem: unit price × quantity.
  3. Multiply as if they are whole numbers, then place the decimal point in the answer so it has 2 decimal places.
  4. Add the three line totals to find your Parts Subtotal.
💡 Example: Motor: 4 × $18.75 = $75.00. Wheel Set: 6 × $6.40 = $38.40.

💡 Guided Steps & Help

Dividing Decimals for Cost Sharing:

  1. Write down the Total Shared Cost as the dividend and the number of Teammates as the divisor.
  2. When dividing decimals by a whole number, place the decimal point in the quotient directly above the decimal point in the dividend.
  3. Divide step-by-step using long division.
  4. Multiply your answer by the teammate count to verify you get the exact total.
💡 Example: Total cost $384.00 ÷ 8 members = $48.00 each.

🧰 Organize the toolkit (GCF)

Level 1 tip: try counts that share a factor, like 24 and 36.

Level 2 extension (Distributive Property · 6.NS.4): Rewrite screws + bolts as GCF × (a + b). Example: 72 + 54 = 18 × (4 + 3). Write yours below.
Need a hint?

GCF(72, 54) = 18, so 18 kits with 4 screws and 3 bolts each.

💡 Guided Steps & Help

GCF Toolkit Packaging:

  1. List all factors of your Screw count (numbers that divide it evenly).
  2. List all factors of your Bolt count.
  3. Find the largest factor shared by both lists. This GCF is the maximum number of identical toolkits you can build.
  4. Divide your screw and bolt counts by the GCF to find the contents of each kit.
💡 Example: GCF of 72 screws and 54 bolts is 18 toolkits. Each toolkit gets 4 screws and 3 bolts.

Part 1d · Self — your real plan

💵 Are you under budget? (decimal operations)

Quick division check: A reel of 96 zip ties is shared among 6 robots. How many per robot?

💡 Guided Steps & Help

Decimal Operations for Budget Tracking:

  1. Align the decimal points vertically when writing down your expenses.
  2. Add up Parts, Travel, and Snacks to find your Total Cost.
  3. Subtract this Total Cost from your starting Budget Limit.
  4. A positive difference means you are under budget, while a negative difference means you are over budget.
💡 Example: Budget $500.00 − Expenses $425.45 = $74.55 leftover.
