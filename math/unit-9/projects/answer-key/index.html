Unit 9 Projects — Teacher Answer Key

Two-Variable Relationships  |  6.AT.11  |  y = kx, tables, graphs

For teachers only — this page shows fully worked solutions using each project's example values. Students enter their own real numbers (a channel they would run / a phone plan they model), so totals vary, but the reasoning is identical. Use these as your grading reference and model responses.

Standards: 6.AT.11 — identify independent and dependent variables, write an equation for the dependent variable in terms of the independent variable, and analyze the relationship using tables and graphs.

Project arc: Each version follows Self → Peer → World. Self = the student designs their own context, names the variables, writes the equation, builds a table, and reads a live SVG graph. Peer = a classmate interview with an auto-compare. World = Version A classifies the student's rate into a real creator-growth tier; Version B finds the break-even point against a classmate's plan. Level 1 prefills a friendly starting value; Level 2 explores the non-proportional case (a head-start b in A; a nonzero flat fee in B).

Teacher grading console: each activity has a hidden console that reads the student's live inputs. It is off by default for students — open a project with ?teacher in the URL, then press Alt + Shift + K (or triple-click the title).

Version A — Streaming Channel Growth Lab

Part 1 · Self Design YOUR Channel

Variables · Equation · Table · Graph · 6.AT.11
Topic = student's choice k = 15 subscribers/week Level 1 head start b = 0
y = 15x
Independent variable (x): time in weeks — the student controls how many weeks have passed.
Dependent variable (y): total subscribers — the total depends on how many weeks have passed.
k = 15 is the constant of proportionality (a unit rate: 15 subs per week). After x weeks, y = 15 × x. Students pick and justify their own k, so the rate will vary — the equation form y = kx (Level 1) is what matters.
Level 2: students add a head start b, giving y = 15x + b (example b = 50 → y = 15x + 50). This is non-proportional because the line starts at (0, b) instead of the origin.
Data table (y = 15x), weeks 0–5

1 15 × 1 15 (1, 15)
2 15 × 2 30 (2, 30)

4 15 × 4 60 (4, 60)

Each y increases by 15 (constant rate of change). Each row is an ordered pair (x, y) — enter with no spaces, e.g. (2,30). At Level 2 with b = 50 the column becomes 50, 65, 80, 95, 110, 125.
Read & Analyze the Graph (k = 15)
The live SVG line passes through the origin (0, 0) and rises 15 per week (at Level 2 it starts at (0, b) — e.g. (0, 50)).
Reflection prompts (accept reasoning): "At week 0…" the channel has 0 subscribers (Level 1) because a proportional line starts at the origin. "The constant rate k represents…" how many subscribers are gained each week (the steepness/slope). "To reach 1,000 subscribers…" it takes 1000 ÷ 15 ≈ 66.7 weeks.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Self
4 (Expert): Both variables named correctly; a researched k justified; equation, all six table rows, and the graph reflections correct.
3 (Proficient): Equation and table correct; reasonable graph reflections.
2 (Developing): One variable correct or some work attempted with computation errors.

Part 2 · Peer Channel Showdown

Compare Relationships · 6.AT.11
My k = 15 Classmate k = 10 App compares at week 5 & 1,000-sub goal
Your channel grows faster
You (y = 15x) at week 5: 15 × 5 = 75 subscribers
Classmate (y = 10x) at week 5: 10 × 5 = 50 subscribers
Your channel grows faster by 5 subscribers per week (15 − 10), so your line is steeper.
To reach 1,000 subscribers: yours takes 1000 ÷ 15 ≈ 66.7 weeks; the classmate's takes 1000 ÷ 10 = 100.0 weeks. (App uses (1000 − b) / k, so a Level-2 head start shortens the time.)

Part 3 · World Creator Growth Tier

Compare to Real Data · 6.AT.11
My k = 15 subs/week Tier uses monthly = k × 4.33
Growth tier for k = 15 → Rising Star ✨
The app estimates a monthly rate as k × 4.33 = 15 × 4.33 ≈ 65 subscribers/month.
It then classifies that monthly rate into a tier: Hobbyist Creator (< 50/mo), Rising Star (50–500/mo), Professional Creator (500–2,000/mo), Viral Sensation (> 2,000/mo). About 65/mo lands in Rising Star. Accept any reflection that compares the student's rate to these tiers.
Level 2 — proportional vs. non-proportional
y = kx (no head start) is proportional and passes through the origin. y = kx + b (b > 0, e.g. y = 15x + 50) is NOT proportional — it crosses the y-axis at b, not the origin. Two channels with the same k but different b run parallel and are never equal (the head start is kept forever).
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — World
4 (Expert): Correctly reads the growth tier and explains it; Level 2 correctly identifies loss of proportionality with a head start.
3 (Proficient): Reasonable comparison to the tiers.
2 (Developing): Comparison attempted.

Deliverable Growth Report — Sample Expert Response

"My channel is about skateboarding tricks. My independent variable is time in weeks (x) and my dependent variable is total subscribers (y). My equation is y = 15x — I chose k = 15 because channels that post a few times a week gain about 15 subscribers a week. In the first 5 weeks I reach 75 subscribers, and it would take about 67 weeks to reach 1,000. My channel grows faster than my classmate's (k = 10) because my k is bigger, so my line is steeper and I gain 5 more subscribers each week. A monthly rate of about 65 subscribers puts my channel in the Rising Star tier."
Version B — Phone Plan Showdown

Part 1 · Self Model YOUR Two Plans

Variables · Equation · Table · Graph · 6.AT.11
Example plan: $20 flat fee + $10 / GB Equation: C = 10g + 20 Values vary — students pick real prices
Cost equation: C = (rate) · g + (flat fee)  ·  example C = 10g + 20
Independent variable (g): gigabytes of data used — chosen by the customer.
Dependent variable (C): total monthly cost ($) — depends on the data used.
The rate ($/GB) is the slope; the flat fee is the y-intercept (the cost at 0 GB). Because the flat fee is not $0, the relationship is y = mx + c (NOT proportional). Students choose their own flat fee and per-GB rate from real carrier prices, so accept any correct equation in this form.

Cost grows by $10 for each additional GB. Each row is an ordered pair (g, C). The line starts at $20 (the flat fee), not the origin.
Graph & equation readings (example plan C = 10g + 20)
Cost for 4 GB: 10 × 4 + 20 = $60.00
Data used when the bill is $70: (70 − 20) ÷ 10 = 5 GB
Cost for 10 GB: 10 × 10 + 20 = $120.00
The line crosses the C-axis at $20 (the flat fee) and rises $10 per GB. Numbers depend on the student's chosen plan; check the method against their own rate and flat fee.

Part 2 · Peer Plan Showdown

Compare Relationships · 6.AT.11
My plan: C = 10g + 20 Partner plan: C = 8g + 25 Compare at 5 GB
Which plan wins depends on the data used
Me (C = 10g + 20) at 5 GB: 10 × 5 + 20 = $70.00
Partner (C = 8g + 25) at 5 GB: 8 × 5 + 25 = $65.00
Break-even: 10g + 20 = 8g + 25 → 2g = 5 → g = 2.5 GB. Below 2.5 GB my lower flat fee wins; above 2.5 GB the partner's lower per-GB rate wins. At 5 GB the partner is $5.00 cheaper.
Both lines are of the form y = mx + c. The plan with the smaller rate (slope) is the flatter line and wins for heavy data users. Accept any correct comparison of the two students' real plans.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Peer
4 (Expert): Correct costs at the chosen data amount; identifies the flatter line / break-even and who pays more.
3 (Proficient): Correct costs and comparison.
2 (Developing): One plan computed; comparison attempted.

Part 3 · World Real Market Prices

Compare to Real Data · 6.AT.11
My plan cost at 5 GB (example): $70 Basic $15–30 · Mid $35–48 · Unlimited $50–80
My plan's 5 GB cost vs. real market tiers
Example plan (C = 10g + 20) at 5 GB: 10 × 5 + 20 = $70.00.
The app shows three real tiers: Basic $15–30 (2–5 GB), Mid-Tier $35–48 (10–15 GB), Unlimited $50–80. $70 for only 5 GB is expensive — it costs as much as an unlimited plan while giving far less data, so this example plan is a poor deal. Accept any 2-sentence comparison that cites the student's own 5 GB cost against these tiers.
Level 2 — reading the flat fee as the y-intercept
Because C = (rate)·g + (flat fee) has a non-zero flat fee, it is NOT proportional (it does not pass through the origin). A plan with a bigger flat fee but a smaller per-GB rate starts higher yet rises more slowly — set two plans equal to find the break-even data amount (e.g., 10g + 20 = 8g + 25 → g = 2.5 GB). Below the break-even the lower flat fee wins; above it the lower rate wins.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — World
4 (Expert): Computes the plan's 5 GB cost and compares it to the real tiers; Level 2 explains the flat fee as a non-zero y-intercept / break-even.
3 (Proficient): Reasonable comparison to real prices.
2 (Developing): Comparison attempted.

Deliverable Recommendation — Sample Expert Response

"The independent variable is the data used (g, in GB) and the dependent variable is the total monthly cost (C). My plan is C = 10g + 20: a $20 flat fee plus $10 per GB. The flat fee is the y-intercept, so the relationship is C = mg + c, not proportional. For my typical 5 GB, my plan costs $70, which is more than the Basic and Mid-Tier market plans, so I would switch to a plan with a lower per-GB rate. When I compared with my partner (C = 8g + 25), the two plans cost the same at 2.5 GB; above that, their lower rate makes them cheaper, so the better plan depends on how much data I actually use."
