# Math Fluency Lab

Grades 1–8, 88 focused skills. The curriculum hub links here under Featured Learning Resources.

Students can explore three checked visual steps, complete a starting-point checkup, practice with adaptive support, revisit a personal daily mix, and repair mistakes with fresh problems. Each set generates new problems; the smallest fact domains intentionally revisit a finite set of facts.

The Game Room combines untimed fact games, model-first challenges, and four grade-appropriate puzzle worlds. The original games use the grade's fact trail: Fact Match (flip cards to pair problems with answers), Target Hunt (find every problem that equals a target), Answer Sort (sort problems by whether the answer is below a number), Spot the Mistake (find the wrong step in a solution, or decide whether a classmate's answer is right, then fix it), Make It (tap two tiles that add or multiply to the target; every board hides several pairs), Fact Family Builder (Grades 1–5: place three numbers into four related equations, in both orders), Balance It (a scale shows one side as a problem and the other as a part plus a missing number; the beam tilts toward the heavier side after each try but never gives the number), Tricky Facts (flash cards built from facts and problems the learner missed lately: type the answer, a wrong try shows steps up to but not including the answer step, a right answer retires the card), and Mystery Picture (type answers to clear fog from one of six hidden pictures). **Make 24** (Grades 3-8, from the classic 24 Game) deals four numbers; the learner combines two at a time with + − × ÷ until one number is left, and it must be 24. Every card is proven solvable by `make24-core.js`; Grades 3-5 only meet whole-number steps (a division must come out even), Grades 6-8 may pass through fractions. Hints are staged (a pair of numbers, then the move, then "Show a way"), with Undo and restart, and stars reflect how much help was used, never speed. **Class Code Challenge** (all grades, from shared-code live games) turns a six-character code into the same eight problems for everyone at the same grade, so a class can play side by side with no accounts or server; two tries per problem, steps (never the answer) after a first miss, and the result line carries the code for comparing. **Buddy shop and coins**: every game star is one coin (`rewards.coins`), spent on six extra buddy accessories (`SHOP` in `buddy-items.js`). Coins come from stars that reward thinking, never speed; spent can never exceed earned, a bought item is worn at once, and `cleanRewards` drops unknown, duplicate or unowned shop items, so a backup cannot hand them out. Coins and purchases travel in the JSON backup. **Team Relay** (all grades) puts two to four teams on one screen, one problem per turn, one answer typed after the team talks it over; the class earns the stars together, and because a team answer is not one learner's evidence it never writes to the personal fact chart or the missed-facts list. **Math Baseball** (all grades, from Funbrain's Math Baseball; one or two players on one screen) draws a diamond: the batter picks a swing (Single, Double, Triple or Home run, an easier-to-harder problem tier worth 1 to 4 bases), a right first try is a hit and every runner moves up that many bases, a first miss shows steps and allows one more try (a right second try is a single), and a second miss is an out. Three outs, or five swings, end a half-inning; a game is three innings (two in calm mode). Runners and the ball animate along the base paths unless reduced motion is on. Two-player turns never write to the personal fact chart; a solo game records first tries like the other solo games. **Guess the Number** (all grades, from Funbrain's Guess the Number) hides a whole number (1–10 in Kindergarten up to 1–1000 in Grades 6–8); a number line shades what each guess rules out, a smart-guess hint points at the middle, and stars compare the guess count with a halving plan (`guess-core.js`), never speed. **Math Race** (all grades, from MathCar Racing) moves your car one segment per right answer while the rival advances on a fixed plan after every problem (`race-core.js`: it finishes after 10, then 8 problems, against a 6-segment track), never on a clock, so a careful learner can always win; stars come from first-try answers. Every Game Room card shows a grade-band chip (K–3, Grades 3–8, All grades) derived from the game's `grades` list in `game-catalog.js`, and a filter row (All, Facts, Puzzles, 2-player, Race & sports) narrows the cards; `tools/fluency-arcade.test.mjs` pins these rules and plays all three games. **Spell Duel** (all grades, from quest-and-battle math games) is a map of four zones (Sunny Meadow, Frosty Peak, Tide Cove, Starlit Keep), each with three creatures and a boss. The learner picks a creature and a spell (Spark, Frost, Leaf or Tide; each creature is weak to one spell, which adds 1), then answers a problem from the grade's facts to cast it. A wrong answer never costs the wizard anything: steps, a second try, then the answer. A creature that reaches 0 energy is calmed and joins the zone's progress; a boss opens after its zone's creatures, and a zone opens after the previous boss. Correct answers pay gold (3 on a first try, 1 after a miss, plus a bonus for calming a creature); 25 gold hatches the next pet in a fixed order (no luck involved). The companion pet grows with every landed spell and adds 1 to each spell once fully grown. Three daily quests (the same all day, chosen by the date) pay gold. Everything is untimed, stored on the device in the rewards record (`duel`), carried by the JSON backup, and bilingual (`i18n-es-duel.js`); rules live in `duel-core.js` and content in `duel-data.js`, pinned by `tools/fluency-duel.test.mjs`. Model-first play also includes Number Quest, Array Architect, and Fraction Forge. Equation Factory models inverse operations and equations, Fraction Bridge builds exact fractional spans, Balance Station checks equations with a responsive scale, and Ratio City scales quantities in proportional models. Puzzle attempts feed the shared local evidence engine; independent attempts and supported revisions are kept separate. Games include decimals, fractions, and equations, and can draw from any of the grade's skills as well as its trail stops. Game messages are announced through one persistent screen-reader live region, focus moves to the new screen, and forced-colors mode keeps every state distinguishable. In mixed play, up to a third of each board is facts the learner missed on an earlier day. Every trail ends with a Boss round that opens when each stop has 2 stars. Teachers can print paper versions of Fact Match, Target Hunt, and Answer Sort (answer key on its own page) and pick skills by standard. The class snapshot can compare two weeks of facts reports, and a one-page family summary (stars, streak, tricky facts, things to ask and try) can be printed or saved as a file. Calm mode uses fewer cards and gives the picture a head start; read-aloud works in the games. Each trail round ends with an optional "How did you think about it?" and every day the trail shows one strategy tip. Trail rounds and games earn stars, stickers, a rank, and a practice-day streak; everything is stored on the device with the other progress, and the JSON backup now carries trail stars, the fact chart, stickers, and strategy notes. "Download my facts report" saves the tricky facts (no names); the teacher studio can open several of those files for a class facts snapshot, and an assignment link can name a game and a set of facts. The fact chart continues to fill only from first-try typed answers; puzzle-world evidence is recorded separately through the local tutoring engine.

Teacher studio creates shareable assignments for selected skills, a problem count, and a support level. The same link generates new questions each visit. Five-day printable packets optionally include a separate teacher answer key. Family progress views, CSV reports, and JSON backups work locally.

The three longer-form publisher games share the same evidence and reward records. **Math World Builder** sets a target floor area and, from Grade 3, a fence-material budget; changing row and column controls redraws the building grid and updates both calculations. **Number Puzzle Studio** presents a tile bank with multiple valid addition or multiplication pairs for each target, supports touch or keyboard selection, and explains the pair after checking. **Fluency Rally** presents an untimed route checkpoint: students choose the route matching a multi-operation expression, calculate its distance, and enter the result. All three provide hints, feedback, five challenge levels, saved completion rewards, and grade-appropriate teacher assignments.

## Math Discovery Studio

`studio/` is the interactive explanation layer. It opens beside the feedback after a missed practice problem (never inside the live region) and above "See idea" on every skill. One panel set, driven by the problem's own numbers:

- **Watch**: animated frames with Previous / Play / Next / Replay, speed, "Show all steps" and arrow keys. The lesson stops at **Predict** points (a wrong choice is explained, a right one asks for a one-sentence reason) before it shows what happens. Reduced motion removes autoplay and animation.
- **Build**: the learner makes the moves (trade ten chips for one, break a ten, cut pieces, walk the number line, apply an operation to both pans, scale both ratio numbers). Every action goes through the engine's rules; impossible moves are refused with a reason and change nothing. The moves are kept as "My thinking".
- **Try**: a fresh problem with a three-rung hint ladder (a clue, a picture, a guided first step). Any rung makes the answer "with help"; the learner is then asked for a new problem on their own. Wrong answers get the named slip and a tip when the engine recognises it.
- **Explore**: a simulator for the skill's idea (fraction equivalence, proportional graph, function machine, area and perimeter, volume, angles, dot plot).
- **Find the mistake**: the lab's own mistake bank (first wrong step, the slip, the repair), then the right way in the model and a second case.
- **Explain it differently**: simpler words, listen, another representation (number line or zero-pair chips; ratio table or double number line), another example, English / Español.

Engines (`engine-*.js`): place value (whole numbers and decimals), fraction bars (equivalent, compare, add or subtract), integers (number line and chips, absolute value), balance (one- and two-step equations), ratio tables (ratios, proportions, unit rates, conversions, scale, percent), arrays and area (facts, area model, division, rectangle area, perimeter, triangle, volume), an area grid for fraction multiplication and "a/b of N", a rounding line (nearest ten through million, halfway rounds up), place-value columns (name a digit and its value; compare numbers place by place), number-line hops (skip-counting), powers and roots (an exponent as repeated factors; a square root as the side of a square), factor and multiple lists (GCF, LCM), a coordinate grid and function machine (slope as rise over run; evaluating a linear function), counters, ten-frames, clocks, coins, rulers and patterns for the early grades, order of operations one legal step at a time, systems, inequalities, percent change, mean, median and probability, bar charts and scatter plots, decimal multiplication and division, powers of ten as a moving decimal point, long division in chunks, fraction division and unit-fraction word problems, and geometry (circle area, Pythagorean theorem, angle relationships, reflections, quadrants), integer multiplication by pattern, and factors and multiples. Every one of the 96 skill entries has a model; about 3% of generated problems have an unusual shape (negative fractions, decimal unit rates, division with a remainder) and use the worked-steps player. At a Predict point the learner sees the step before it, never the answer. `engines.js` picks an engine from the question text; a problem with no model gets the worded-steps player and says so. `docs/fluency-studio-coverage.md` (from `node tools/fluency-studio-coverage.mjs`) lists every skill.

The mission (Explore, Build, Solve, Explain, Challenge) records what the learner did and earns one quiet badge per engine; nothing is timed. `adaptive.js` keeps the last 12 attempts per skill on the device (right or wrong, with help or without, a named slip; no names) and uses fixed rules: two independent successes fade one level of support, a miss brings it back, a slip named twice is called out. It is a pattern in recent answers, never a mastery score, and it does not change the lab's mastery evidence. Spanish for the Studio's interface is in `i18n-es-studio.js`; captions, hints and feedback carry both languages inside the engines. Tests: `node tools/fluency-studio.test.mjs` (logic, every generated problem) and `npm run validate:fluency-studio-browser` (axe WCAG 2 A/AA and 2.2 AA on every tab at phone and desktop width, keyboard stepping, 44px targets, reduced motion). The Studio record travels in the JSON backup (`studio` in `play-backup.js`, cleaned on import).

## Progress rules

- Profiles are local learner slots, not student accounts or a school roster.
- First independent responses establish evidence. Hints, retries, visual lessons, and coached answers cannot inflate independent mastery.
- A skill is remembered after at least eight recent independent checks, at least 85% success, and successful practice on two days. Recent unsuccessful work removes that status.
- Review intervals grow from one to fourteen days. A short checkup recommends topics; it does not assign a grade level.
- Two fresh independent successes in a row during repair practice resolve that skill’s notebook entries.
- Every mode is untimed. Sessions save on each response and navigation, including Streak (10 correct in a row at any pace).
- Older progress or backup files that carry the retired `sprintBest` field still load; the field is ignored.

No analytics, student names, central database, or network submission is added. Share assignment links through the school’s existing classroom system. Use downloaded reports there if collection is needed.

## Español

The page header has an English / Español button. Spanish covers the page tabs, the trail (stop names, ideas, rewards, boss round, strategy tips, teach-back), the whole Game Room, stickers, the family summary, and the language layer below. Math problems are not replaced: the English stays on screen as the practice, and a "See it in Spanish" peek (plus the Spanish read-aloud button) shows the same question in Spanish. Skill titles and worked-example steps stay in English. Spanish text lives in `i18n-es.js` and `i18n-es-language.js` (English text is the key); `tools/fluency-i18n.test.mjs` fails if a learner-facing string has no Spanish entry or its placeholders differ.

## Language layer (math, language, writing)

Every learner picks a **Talk & write help** level (Starter, Building, Growing, On my own; saved per learner on the device, changeable any time). It changes how much of a sentence is given, never the math, and it is not a proficiency score.

- **Words** (`language-terms.js`): a bilingual word bank (English, Español, a plain meaning in both, a "looks alike" flag) mapped to all 88 skills. Meanings contain no digits, so a word card cannot echo an answer. The Learn tab and Starter-level practice show the skill's words with read-aloud in both languages.
- **Explain card** (`language-card.js`, `language-frames.js`): after an answer (right or wrong) a card offers four moves (notice, explain, compare, check) plus "fix it" after a slip, with sentence frames at the learner's level in English or Español, word-bank chips, joining words, and a microphone. The writing is read for four ideas (a math word, a reason, a first-then order, a way to check) and shown as a checklist, never as a mark.
- **Math journal** (`language-journal.js`): saved explanations stay on the device, print as a one-page journal, and travel in the JSON backup (`play-backup.js`). The family summary shows counts only, with a bilingual starter to try at home.
- **Academic Language** on the progress card is evidence-based: it reads "Not enough yet" until the learner has written or looked up words, then blends the four ideas in the last 20 entries, the variety of math words, and word lookups.
- **Spanish stems** (`question-es.js`, `story-problems.js`): every generated question stem has a Spanish version with the same numbers; story problems carry Spanish wording. `tools/fluency-language.test.mjs` generates thousands of questions across all 88 skills and fails on any wordy stem without a Spanish rule or a changed number.
- **Teacher**: the teacher studio's "Print the language pack" prints, for the chosen skills, the words in both languages, the frames at each level, a partner-talk task, and a teacher page that explains the levels (with WIDA ELD Standard 3 language functions, no placement).

## Math Writing Studio

The **Write** tab (and "Write about it" on every skill's Learn page) is a writing room for math. A learner picks an organizer, gets a fresh problem from any skill, writes one part at a time, and watches the paragraph build. Writing never changes practice evidence or mastery, and nothing is graded.

- **Five organizers** (`writing-organizers.js`): Math paragraph (topic sentence, steps, because, check, closing), Test-style response (restate, answer, show your work, explain), Claim-evidence-reasoning, Compare two ways, and Find and fix the mistake. Each part has one job (say it, show it, explain why, check it, wrap up) and a colour that follows the job into the finished paragraph. Colour is always paired with the job's name. Every organizer has a worked model about a different problem.
- **Four support levels** are the same Starter, Building, Growing and On my own as the explain card. Each part offers a sentence starter that fades with the level (level 4 shows the task only, with "Need a starter?"). Starters contain no numbers, so one can never state an answer. English and Español are a writing choice separate from the page language.
- **Practice like the test** turns any organizer into one ruled response box with no starters or word bank, plus the four-idea checklist and two self-check boxes.
- **Prompts** (`writing-prompts.js`) generate a new problem from the chosen skill. Claim-evidence-reasoning and find-and-fix show a classmate's answer that is either correct or a plausible slip (a slip is never labelled), so the learner decides.
- **Checklist**: the four language moves the journal reads (math word, reason, first-then order, a check). It shows ideas the words already carry; it is not a grade.
- **Folder, print, backup**: finished pieces (`writing-store.js`) stay on the device, print as a one-page piece, and travel in the JSON backup (`play-backup.js`). "Print a blank organizer" gives ruled paper for writing by hand.

Test: `node tools/fluency-writing.test.mjs`. New learner strings need Spanish in `i18n-es-language.js`.

## Explanation steps

Guided steps (Learn it, the step player, live help, and "Work it with me" in practice) share one workbook-card style in `explain.css`: one idea per card, large type, a short "why" callout, and the picture directly under the words. Step wording lives in `detail-steps.js` and the generators in `problem-bank.js`: one idea per step, short sentences, number sentences instead of prose. Two-digit by two-digit multiplication splits the first factor, matching the area model beside the problem.

The picture beside the steps follows the active step (`step-model-link.js`, pinned by `tools/fluency-step-model-link.test.mjs`). In the area model, the split step marks the split, each partial-product step lights its own part with a "Step n of N" tag, finished parts get a check, and the adding step lights the total with arrows up from the parts. Tapping any step shows it in the picture. Other pictures are left alone.

## Step lens: highlighted, tappable explanations

Every worked explanation (Learn it, "Work it with me" in guided practice, and the step player in miss feedback and live help) runs through one layer, `step-lens.js` (styles in `step-lens.css`, tests in `tools/fluency-step-lens.test.mjs`). It only decorates text a surface has already shown, so the answer-free rules (`peekCap`, "Your turn" boxes) are untouched, and it never rewrites text.

- **Highlighted parts**: numbers from the problem (blue, solid underline), numbers made along the way (amber, dotted underline) and moves (coral: the action word a step opens with, and + − × ÷ =). Colour is never the only cue, and every chip has a spoken label.
- **Tap to trace**: tapping a number says where it came from (the problem, or "made in step 2", with a button that jumps there) and lights the same number in the problem line, the picture and every other step. Tapping a move says what it means. Arrow keys walk the chips; one chip per list is a tab stop.
- **Show me**: All, Numbers or Moves dims the other kind, so a learner can read what is being DONE apart from what it is being done WITH. The choice is kept on the device.
- **Path and Play**: a dot per open step jumps between steps (the picture follows, as when a step is tapped); Play walks them one by one (hidden under reduced motion).
- **Predict the next move**: an optional toggle. "Next step" first asks which move comes next, with the right move among three. Only the move's NAME is ever stamped on the button (`data-next-move`, from `step-moves.js`), never a number or result; a wrong guess is not recorded and the step opens either way.

`tools/fluency-step-lens.test.mjs` sweeps 576 generated problems across all 96 skills: the text is unchanged, no hidden step or result reaches the page, a second pass changes nothing, the right move is always among the three choices, and every move name has Spanish.

## Kindergarten to Grade 3 explanations

Young learners get their own wording (`kid-steps.js`, applied in `problem-bank.js` before the older step writers): short sentences, no vocabulary words (addend, digit, quotient, interval, regroup), one idea per step, and number sentences wherever a picture can be drawn. `kid-learn.js` holds the "big idea" and "watch out" lines for every K-3 skill, in English and Español, from one table. Step pictures include a ten-frame ("Picture 10 boxes. 5 are full."), split bars ("Split 7 into 5 and 2."), tens-and-ones blocks, place-name boxes, and tens blocks for comparing numbers. A builder returns null for a shape it does not know and the skill's own steps stay. `tools/fluency-kid-steps.test.mjs` checks every K-3 draw for jargon, sentence length, true arithmetic, step count and answer-only-on-the-last-step.

## Fraction pictures

Fraction problems (Grades 3-8) now have a picture beside the steps (`fraction-picture.js`, pinned by `tools/fluency-fraction-picture.test.mjs`): equal-part bars for equivalent and compared fractions, renamed bars with the same-size pieces for adding and subtracting, a column-and-row grid for "a fraction of a fraction", a bar with one piece outlined for dividing fractions, a bar cut into groups for "a fraction of a number", and wholes cut into servings for dividing a whole by a unit fraction. The Learn example draws the result; the practice model stops before it. Negative fractions, mixed numbers and very large parts get no picture rather than a wrong one. The step link lights the bar a step names (or the renamed and result bars for the "common denominator" and last steps) and the grid's tops, bottoms or result.

## Buddy and weekly goal

Every learner has a buddy (no name, no photo). Six colors are free; hats, face items and back items unlock with stickers and ranks, so a buddy's look is always earned. The buddy shows on the trail strip and cheers after each game with a short message that praises thinking, never speed. A weekly goal of 2–5 practice days (Monday to Sunday) is set in the Game Room; meeting it earns a sticker. Buddy look, goal, retired flash cards, and missed-fact cards travel with the JSON backup.

## Steps that explain, and "Your turn"

Every guided step says what to do and why, in plain words. `detail-steps.js` writes number-specific steps (with reasons) for whole-number and decimal arithmetic, fractions, rounding, area/perimeter/volume, coins, equations, percent, unit rates, probability, and more; `step-whys.js` adds a one-line reason under the fact-strategy number sentences. When a step ends in a partial result (for example `34 + 50 = 84`), the learner types that number first ("Your turn"): a right entry opens the step, a wrong one gets a nudge, and two misses open it so nobody is stuck. The next step waits for the typed number, and the result is never in the page until then (`step-fill.js`, pinned by `tools/fluency-step-fill.test.mjs`).

## Models that open with the problem's own numbers

Every model opens loaded with the problem on screen (`problem-numbers.js` reads the numbers, story problems through `item.expr`): the Interactive Solver, the Math Tool Drawer (with a "Use this problem" / "Try my own numbers" bar), the "Show the model" picture and the guided step pictures. Adding and subtracting whole numbers and decimals use the stacked column method (`vertical-columns.js`): the numbers lined up by place value, empty answer boxes and empty regrouping boxes the learner fills; a box says right or try again, and two misses open it. There are no number-line jumps for adding or subtracting. While the answer is not earned, models show only what the learner builds and the givens (totals, products, net values and unit rates are masked), and a first miss or "Show a step" shows steps up to `peekCap` (`live-help.js`) instead of the worked solution. `tools/fluency-model-inputs.test.mjs` pins this for all 88 skills; the audit is in `docs/fluency-models-audit.md`.

## Comfort and keepsakes

The header has a Reading font switch (Lexend, more letter and line spacing) and an optional Sound switch (soft chimes for a right answer or a finished game; nothing plays for a wrong answer; off by default). The Progress area can print a Certificate of Achievement with the learner's rank, trail stops, stars, and stickers, in English or Spanish.

## Answer-free steps and wrong-answer notes

`step-leak.js` (`peekCap`) decides how many guided steps can show before one states the answer; "Show a step", the trail miss screen, Tricky Facts, Mystery Picture and the post-game review all use it, and `tools/fluency-audit-fixes.test.mjs` fails if any of the 88 skills leaks. A step that is only a list ("Factors of 48: 1, 2, 3…") counts as a search space, not an answer. When a problem has no safe step the hint shows instead. After a trail miss the learner sees the safe steps, then a fresh problem of the same kind returns as the "one more look". `mistake-notes.js` adds a one-line note on the likely slip (adding instead of subtracting, a reversed order, one group off, a place-value slip); it never contains a number. Mixed numbers ("1 5/12") are read as mixed numbers. A practice day counts for any finished practice set, trail round or game, and earned streak stickers stay earned (`bestStreak`).

## Family Math Expedition and in-Lab tours

`/math/fluency-lab/family-adventure/` (the URL the Lab's "Family start here" button already uses) is the **Family Math Expedition**: five bilingual stops (Welcome, Discover how math works, Learn together, Explore the Lab, Your first Family Math Night), a map for visiting stops in any order, resume and restart, optional read-aloud, and a printable quick-start guide. English, Español, or both.

- **Stop 2** has seven hands-on models (ten-frame, number line, array and area model, fraction bars, base-ten blocks with decimals, ratio tape and table, slope graph) and one challenge per grade. A child builds the model, answers, then picks the reason. Each wrong answer names the misconception it comes from; hints come one at a time; a third miss shows a worked solution; "Try a fresh one" loads a parallel version. `family-adventure/math-items.js` holds the content and `math-items.test.mjs` recomputes every answer and checks both languages carry the same numbers.
- **Stop 3** is a parent–child practice scene in three grade bands. Moving on needs the strongest response, and every other response explains why it helps less.
- **Stop 4** pictures eight real Lab tools; each card links to the page it describes (`routes.js` is the only place links are built).
- **Stop 5** builds a plan from the real Family Math games (`family/fm2`): grade, skill, and a 5-minute Quick Practice, 10-minute Math Together or 15-minute Deeper Thinking routine. "Start practicing now" opens `family/play.html` with the grade, skill, level, rounds and mode already set.
- **State** lives in `ewl-fluency-family-expedition-v2` (grade, finished stops, challenge results, plan). The first adventure's `ewl-fluency-family-adventure-v1` is read once and kept. Nothing leaves the device.
- **In-Lab tours** (`tour/`): a first-visit offer (never forced, never over practice), a "?" launcher, "Show me how", help bubbles, and separate student and family walkthroughs. `?tour=family` or `?tour=student` opens one directly.

Verify with `npm run validate:family-expedition-browser` (every stop, grades 1–8, both languages, three viewports, axe) and `node math/fluency-lab/family-adventure/math-items.test.mjs`.
