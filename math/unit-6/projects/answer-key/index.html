Game Studio Scoring Engine — Worked Solutions

Step 1  ·  Base Score Formula — Write & Evaluate  ·  6.AT.6

Base Score: (points rate)p + (bonus rate)b

Default points coefficient = 10 Default bonus coefficient = 5 Default formula: 10p + 5b
Order of operations: multiply each coefficient by its variable, then add.
Setup A — evaluate 10p + 5b at p = 50, b = 10:
10 × 50 + 5 × 10 = 500 + 50 = 550
Setup B — evaluate 10p + 5b at p = 100, b = 20:
10 × 100 + 5 × 20 = 1000 + 100 = 1100
Setup A = 550  |  Setup B = 1100

Method to grade: the value should equal (their points rate) × p + (their bonus rate) × b. Coefficient = the rate, variable = p (points) or b (bonuses). Level 1 fixes the defaults 10 and 5; Level 2 lets students pick their own coefficients and add a flat starting constant, so re-derive with their numbers. The Live Scoring Engine simulator uses whatever p and b the student enters (e.g. p = 10, b = 3 → 10 × 10 + 5 × 3 = 115). A common error is adding before multiplying.

Step 2  ·  Level Exponent Multipliers  ·  6.AT.5

Level Multiplier: Basen (Level 1 base = 2)

Level 1 multiplier base = 2 (fixed) Level 2: base 3 or 4 allowed
Evaluate 2n for each level (multiply the base by itself n times):
Level 2: 2² = 2 × 2 = 4
Level 3: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
Level 4: 2⁴ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16
Level 5: 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32
Table: 4  ·  8  ·  16  ·  32

The Total Score Calculator multiplies Base Score × Level Multiplier. With its defaults (Base Score 500, Level 3 → 2³ = 8): 500 × 8 = 4000. A common error is treating the exponent as multiplication (2 × 5 = 10 instead of 2⁵ = 32). Level 2 may choose base 3 (9/27/81/243) or base 4 (16/64/256/1024) — re-derive with the student's base.

Step 3  ·  Equivalent Expressions (evaluate at p = 3, b = 2)  ·  6.AT.7 · 6.AT.7

Three Equivalence Challenges + Level 2 Factoring

Each challenge is checked by substituting p = 3 and b = 2 into both sides.
Challenge 1: 3(2p + 4b) vs 6p + 12b
Left 3(2·3 + 4·2) = 3(6 + 8) = 3 × 14 = 42; Right 6·3 + 12·2 = 18 + 24 = 42 → Yes, equivalent

Challenge 2: 4(p + 3b) vs 4p + 3b
Left 4(3 + 3·2) = 4(3 + 6) = 4 × 9 = 36; Right 4·3 + 3·2 = 12 + 6 = 18 → No, NOT equivalent (the 3b was never distributed)

Challenge 3: 5(2p + b + 8) vs 10p + 5b + 40
Left 5(2·3 + 2 + 8) = 5(6 + 2 + 8) = 5 × 16 = 80; Right 10·3 + 5·2 + 40 = 30 + 10 + 40 = 80 → Yes, equivalent
42 / 42 / Yes  ·  36 / 18 / No  ·  80 / 80 / Yes
Level 1 — property explanation: a full-credit answer states that 2(5p + 3b) = 2·5p + 2·3b = 10p + 6b, i.e. the outside factor multiplies each inside term.
Level 2 — factor your own formula: for the default 10p + 5b, GCF(10, 5) = 5, so 10p + 5b = 5(2p + 1b). Re-derive with the student's own coefficients (the page checks GCF and the inside coefficients live).
Step 4  ·  Partner High Score Duel (p = 80, b = 15, Level 3)  ·  6.AT.6

Trade Coefficients & Compare Total Scores

You (default): 10p + 5b, base 2 Partner (default): 8p + 6b, base 2 Match: p = 80, b = 15, Level 3
Total Score = Base Score (at p = 80, b = 15) × Level 3 multiplier (2³ = 8).
You: base 10 × 80 + 5 × 15 = 800 + 75 = 875; 875 × 8 = 7000
Partner: base 8 × 80 + 6 × 15 = 640 + 90 = 730; 730 × 8 = 5840
7000 > 5840 → You win by 1160.
You 7000  |  Partner 5840 (you win by 1160)

The defaults above are the built-in partner values. Students enter their real partner's rates, so re-derive with their numbers; grade whether they multiply the base score by the correct exponent multiplier and identify who scores higher. In Level 2 the partner may use a different multiplier base.

Sample Expert Response — Design Pitch (4 / Expert) In my game Pixel Quest, my base score formula is 10p + 5b, where p is points and b is bonuses. It scores 550 for a run of 50 points and 10 bonuses (10 × 50 + 5 × 10). Levels use the multiplier 2ⁿ, so by Level 5 the score is multiplied by 32 (2⁵). I checked three equivalence challenges at p = 3, b = 2 and found 3(2p + 4b) = 6p + 12b (both 42) and 5(2p + b + 8) = 10p + 5b + 40 (both 80) are equivalent, but 4(p + 3b) ≠ 4p + 3b because the 3b was not distributed (36 vs 18). In the Level 3 duel at p = 80, b = 15 my engine scored 7000 and my partner's scored 5840, so my scoring rewards high-point runs more.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring Note 4 — Expert: Base score written and evaluated for both setups; exponent table (4/8/16/32) correct with growth explained; all three equivalence challenges correct with the non-example (Challenge 2) justified; duel total scores correct; pitch uses the exact computed numbers.
3 — Proficient: All calculations correct; duel and equivalence present; pitch uses most numbers.
2 — Developing: Exponent treated as multiplication (2 × 5 = 10), a coefficient or constant missing when evaluating, or the duel score computed without applying the exponent multiplier.

App Pricing Engine — Worked Solutions

Step 1  ·  Subscription Revenue — Write & Evaluate  ·  6.AT.6

Revenue Expression: (std price)s + (prem price)p

Default standard price = $3.99 Default premium price = $8.99 Default subs: s = 100, p = 50
Revenue = (standard price)·s + (premium price)·p. Multiply each price by its subscriber count, then add.
Expression: 3.99s + 8.99p
Evaluate at s = 100, p = 50:
3.99 × 100 + 8.99 × 50 = $399.00 + $449.50 = $848.50
Total Revenue = $848.50

Method to grade: total should equal (their standard price) × s + (their premium price) × p. Coefficients = the two prices, variables = s (standard subs) and p (premium subs). Re-derive with the student's own prices and subscriber counts. A common error is adding subscriber counts before multiplying by price.

Step 2  ·  Server Cost Exponent  ·  6.AT.5

Server Cost: C × 2m (C = base cost, m = month)

Default base cost C = $1.00 Default target month m = 5
Server cost doubles each month: C × 2m. With C = 1.00:
Month 1: 1.00 × 2¹ = $2.00
Month 2: 1.00 × 2² = $4.00
Month 3: 1.00 × 2³ = $8.00
Month 4: 1.00 × 2⁴ = $16.00
Month 5: 1.00 × 2⁵ = $32.00
Month 5 Server Cost = $32.00

2⁵ = 32, so the Month 5 cost is C × 32. With a different base cost C, multiply 32 by that C (e.g. C = 2.50 → $80.00). A common error is treating the exponent as multiplication (2 × 5 = 10 instead of 2⁵ = 32).

Step 3  ·  Net Profit & Benchmarks  ·  6.AT.6

Net Profit = Revenue − Month 5 Server Cost

Combine Step 1 and Step 2 at the default values.
Month 5 Revenue (from Step 1): $848.50
Month 5 Server Cost (from Step 2, 2⁵ = 32): $32.00
Net Profit = $848.50 − $32.00 = $816.50
Net Profit = $816.50

Method to grade: Net Profit = (their revenue) − (their Month 5 server cost). The benchmark reflection should place the profit in a reasonable tier and justify it. Re-derive from the student's own revenue and base server cost.

Step 4  ·  Compare With a Partner  ·  6.AT.6

Compare Revenue, Server Cost & Net Profit

You (default): $848.50 / $32.00 / $816.50 Partner: student-entered
The partner fields start blank — students enter a classmate's prices, subscriber counts, and base server cost, and the page recomputes the partner's revenue, Month 5 server cost, and net profit with the same formulas.
Your default figures: Revenue $848.50, Server Cost $32.00, Net Profit $816.50.
Example partner (std $4.99, prem $9.99, s = 80, p = 40, C = $1.00): 4.99 × 80 + 9.99 × 40 = $399.20 + $399.60 = $798.80 revenue; cost $32.00; net profit $766.80 → your app keeps $49.70 more.
You $816.50 vs partner (varies)

Grade whether the student applies the same three formulas to the partner's numbers and correctly states who is more profitable and by how much. The balance-scale visual tips toward the higher net profit.

Sample Expert Response — Pricing Memo (4 / Expert) Manager, my app charges $3.99 for standard and $8.99 for premium, so the revenue expression is 3.99s + 8.99p. With 100 standard and 50 premium subscribers that is $399.00 + $449.50 = $848.50 in Month 5. Server costs double each month as 1.00 × 2ᵐ, reaching $32.00 in Month 5 (2⁵ = 32). Net Profit = $848.50 − $32.00 = $816.50, which lands in a healthy tier. Compared with my partner's app my net profit is higher because my premium tier brings in more per subscriber, so I recommend keeping the premium price and growing premium sign-ups.

3 — Proficient: All calculations correct; net profit and partner comparison present; memo uses most values.
2 — Developing: Revenue found with the wrong operation order, exponent treated as multiplication (2 × 5 instead of 2⁵), or net profit computed without subtracting the server cost.
