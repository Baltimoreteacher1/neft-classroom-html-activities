Key Words
Learn these 6 math words before you begin
🌐
Coordinate Plane
A 2D grid formed by a horizontal line (x-axis) and a vertical line (y-axis) intersecting.
Perfect for layouts and maps.
📍
Ordered Pair
A pair of numbers (x, y) that names a precise location on the coordinate plane.
Example: Roller Coaster at (3, −4)
📐
Quadrant
One of the four quarters of the coordinate plane, labeled I, II, III, and IV.
Quadrant signs: I(+,+), II(-,+), III(-,-), IV(+,-)
🍕
Origin
The point where the x-axis and y-axis cross. The coordinates are always (0, 0).
Located right in the center.
↔️
x-axis
The horizontal number line on a coordinate plane. Numbers go left (-) and right (+).
The first coordinate in (x, y).
↕️
y-axis
The vertical number line on a coordinate plane. Numbers go down (-) and up (+).
The second coordinate in (x, y).

Need a hint about signs and quadrants?

Remember:
• Quadrant I: x is positive, y is positive (+, +)
• Quadrant II: x is negative, y is positive (−, +)
• Quadrant III: x is negative, y is negative (−, −)
• Quadrant IV: x is positive, y is negative (+, −)
• On an axis: if x is 0, the point is on the y-axis; if y is 0, the point is on the x-axis.

Step 2 — Attraction Distance
Find the distance between attractions sharing an axis
Your job: Pick two attractions that share an axis (same x-coordinate or same y-coordinate), and use absolute values to calculate the distance between them.
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE — Diagonal Distance (Pythagoras Preview)

Select two attractions that do not share an axis (diagonal line). Count the grid units of the horizontal and vertical legs, then estimate the straight-line diagonal distance.

Need a distance hint?

Different Quadrants: If points are on opposite sides of an axis, we ADD their distances from the axis (add their absolute values).
Example: (3, 5) and (3, −2). The shared coordinate is x = 3. The y-coordinates are 5 and −2. Distance = |5| + |−2| = 5 + 2 = 7 units.

Same Quadrant: If points are on the same side of an axis, we SUBTRACT their distances from the axis (subtract their absolute values, larger minus smaller).
Example: (3, 5) and (3, 2). Distance = |5| − |2| = 5 − 2 = 3 units.

Need a reflection hint?

Reflection Rules:
• Across the x-axis: Flip over the horizontal line. Keep the x-coordinate, negate the y-coordinate. `(x, y) → (x, −y)`
• Across the y-axis: Flip over the vertical line. Negate the x-coordinate, keep the y-coordinate. `(x, y) → (−x, y)`

Width Calculation: Distance = |Max x − Min x|. Miles = Distance × 0.1.
