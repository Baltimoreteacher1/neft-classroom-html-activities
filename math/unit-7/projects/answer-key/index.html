Unit 7 Projects — Teacher Answer Key

Integers & Coordinate Plane · Grade 6 · Assesses 6.NS.6 (coordinate plane, quadrants, reflections) & 6.NS.8 (distance on the plane), using negative integers (6.NS.5) and absolute value (6.NS.7) as the tools throughout

For teachers only — do not distribute to students. These are the shipped Unit 7 projects: Version A — Theme Park Map Designer and Version B — Submarine Mission Control. Both let students change the numbers, so answers will vary — grade the method (correct quadrant signs, |n| as distance from 0, correct reflection sign rules, valid distance reasoning). The worked examples below use each app's built-in sample values — Version A's "Use Example Coordinates" button and Version B's default checkpoint coordinates — so you have a parallel model. Every step has a live verifier that checks the student's own numbers against these same formulas. Use the "Sample Expert Response" paragraphs as 4-point anchor papers.
Version A

Theme Park Map Designer

Standards: 6.NS.6 (ordered pairs, quadrants, reflections) · 6.NS.8 (distance on the coordinate plane, using absolute value)

Sample "Use Example Coordinates" map (the button on Step 1 — Plot Attractions). All worked answers below trace these values:
🎡 Ferris Wheel (3, 5)
🎢 Roller Coaster (−6, 3)
🌊 Water Slide (−5, −4)
🚗 Bumper Cars (7, −4)
🍟 Food Court (3, −2)

Step 1 (app "Plot Attractions") — Ordered Pairs & Quadrants 6.NS.6 · Ordered Pairs & Quadrants

Ferris Wheel (3, 5): (+, +) → Quadrant I
Roller Coaster (−6, 3): (−, +) → Quadrant II
Water Slide (−5, −4): (−, −) → Quadrant III
Bumper Cars (7, −4): (+, −) → Quadrant IV
Food Court (3, −2): (+, −) → Quadrant IV
Quadrants reached: all four (I, II, III, IV) — this also satisfies the Level 2 layout requirements (≥1 attraction per quadrant; an attraction on an axis requires a 0 coordinate, e.g. moving one to (0, 4)).

Key rule: Quadrant I (+,+) · Quadrant II (−,+) · Quadrant III (−,−) · Quadrant IV (+,−). Any point with a 0 coordinate lies on an axis, not in a quadrant. Answers vary — the app's per-row badge marks ✅/❌ against the student's own signs.

Sample: I, II, III, IV, IV  →  reaches all 4 quadrants

Step 2 (app "Attraction Distance") — Distance on a Shared Axis 6.NS.8 · Distance on the Coordinate Plane

Sample pair sharing an axis: Ferris Wheel (3, 5) and Food Court (3, −2) — both have x = 3 (a vertical line).

Shared-axis check: both have x = 3, so measure along the y-values 5 and −2.
Opposite sides of the x-axis (one +, one −) → ADD the absolute values:
  |5| + |−2| = 5 + 2 = 7 units
(equivalently, distance = |5 − (−2)| = |7| = 7).

Same-side example: (3, 5) and (3, 2) are both above the x-axis → SUBTRACT, larger minus smaller: |5| − |2| = 5 − 2 = 3 units. (The app always displays larger-abs minus smaller-abs, so the shown equation is never negative.)
Sample distance = 7 units
Level 2 — Diagonal (Pythagoras preview): pick two attractions that do not share an axis, e.g. Ferris Wheel (3, 5) and Roller Coaster (−6, 3): legs |Δx| = 9 and |Δy| = 2 → √(9² + 2²) = √85 ≈ 9.22 units (the verifier accepts answers within ±0.25).

Step 3 (app "Park Reflections") — Reflections & Real Scale 6.NS.6b · Reflections · 6.NS.8 · Scale

Reflect the Ferris Wheel (3, 5):

Across the x-axis (keep x, negate y): (3, 5) → (3, −5)
Across the y-axis (negate x, keep y): (3, 5) → (−3, 5)
Across both axes (negate both = 180° rotation about the origin): (3, 5) → (−3, −5)

Real theme-park scale (1 grid unit = 0.1 miles):

Using the sample map, leftmost x = −6 (Roller Coaster), rightmost x = 7 (Bumper Cars).
Width = |7 − (−6)| = 13 grid units.
Miles = 13 × 0.1 = 1.3 miles → the app classifies this as a Major Destination Theme Park (> 0.45 mi).

Level 2 coaster drop: +52 m above ground to −13 m underground → |52| + |−13| = 52 + 13 = 65 meters total drop.
Reflections (3,−5) / (−3,5) / (−3,−5)  |  Width 13 u = 1.3 mi

Step 4 (app "Partner Park") — Compare Blueprints 6.NS.8 · Compare & reason

The app compares each park's horizontal width (max x − min x) in grid units and miles. Answers vary by partner — grade the absolute-difference reasoning.

Worked example: your sample map spans x from −6 to 7 → width 13 units. Suppose a partner's spans x from −3 to 5 → width 8 units. Difference = |13 − 8| = 5 grid units (0.5 mi); your park is more spread out.
Your 13 u vs partner 8 u → diff 5 u (0.5 mi)

Sample Expert Response (Step 5 report — 4/4 Deliverable)

Sample Park Blueprint using the "Use Example Coordinates" map (student work will use their own coordinates):
"I plotted 5 attractions across all four quadrants: the Ferris Wheel (3, 5) in Quadrant I, the Roller Coaster (−6, 3) in Quadrant II, the Water Slide (−5, −4) in Quadrant III, and the Bumper Cars (7, −4) and Food Court (3, −2) in Quadrant IV. The Ferris Wheel and Food Court share x = 3, and because they sit on opposite sides of the x-axis I added their absolute values: |5| + |−2| = 7 units apart. I reflected the Ferris Wheel (3, 5) across the x-axis to (3, −5) — the x-coordinate stayed the same while the y-coordinate changed sign. My park runs from x = −6 to x = 7, a width of 13 units = 1.3 miles, so it is closest to a major destination theme park."
Rubric (mirrors the app's 4 / 3 / 2 table):
4 — Expert: all 5 points plotted with quadrant/axis labeled and sign reasoning stated; distance shown with absolute-value work; reflection sign logic explained; scale converted to miles and compared to a real park.
3 — Proficient: 4 points correct; correct distance, reflection, and scale conversion, but may skip reasoning.
2 — Developing: 3 or fewer points correct; a reflection or distance error, or counted squares without absolute value.
Version B

Submarine Mission Control

Standards: 6.NS.6 (ordered pairs, quadrants, reflections) · 6.NS.8 (vertical distance using absolute value; negative integers as depth)

Default checkpoint coordinates (pre-filled on Step 1; 1 grid unit = 100 m). All worked answers below trace these values:
A · Starting Harbor (3, 4)
B · Twilight Trench (−5, 6)
C · Sunken Galleon (−7, −5)
D · Research Base (6, −3)

Step 1 — Plot Mission Checkpoints & Quadrants 6.NS.6 · Ordered Pairs & Quadrants

A (3, 4): (+, +) → Quadrant I
B (−5, 6): (−, +) → Quadrant II
C (−7, −5): (−, −) → Quadrant III
D (6, −3): (+, −) → Quadrant IV
The "Verify Plot & Quadrants" button checks each selection against the student's own coordinates.
A → QI · B → QII · C → QIII · D → QIV

Step 2 — Depth Distances (Absolute Value) 6.NS.8 · Absolute Value & Distance

Default selected pair: Checkpoint A (y = 4) and Checkpoint B (y = 6).

Both y-values are positive → same side of sea level (y = 0) → SUBTRACT, larger minus smaller:
  |6| − |4| = 6 − 4 = 2 units = 2 × 100 = 200 meters.

Opposite-sides example: A (y = 4) and C (y = −5) are on opposite sides of sea level → ADD: |4| + |−5| = 9 units = 900 meters. (distance = |4 − (−5)| = 9.)
A↔B = 2 units (200 m); A↔C = 9 units (900 m)

Step 3 — Reflections & Ocean Zones 6.NS.6b · Reflections

Reflect the default checkpoint B (−5, 6):

Over the x-axis (keep x, negate y): (−5, 6) → (−5, −6)
Over the y-axis (negate x, keep y): (−5, 6) → (5, 6)
Over both axes (negate both): (−5, 6) → (5, −6)

Ocean-zone classification (depth = −y × 100 m):

A (y = 4) and B (y = 6): y ≥ 0 → at or above sea level (surface).
C (y = −5): depth 500 m → Twilight Zone (200 m – 1,000 m).
D (y = −3): depth 300 m → Twilight Zone (200 m – 1,000 m).
(Sunlight Zone = 0–200 m; Twilight = 200–1,000 m; Midnight > 1,000 m.)
B refl: (−5,−6) / (5,6) / (5,−6)  |  C, D → Twilight Zone

Step 4 — Partner Mission Comparison 6.NS.8 · Compare & reason

The app compares maximum depth (deepest checkpoint, in meters) and, at Level 2, total vertical range. Answers vary by partner.

Worked example (your defaults vs the shipped partner "Navigator Sarah," y = 2, −8, −4, 7):
Your deepest checkpoint is C at y = −5 → max depth = 5 × 100 = 500 m.
Sarah's deepest is y = −8 → max depth = 8 × 100 = 800 m.
Difference = |800 − 500| = 300 m → the partner went deeper.

Level 2 vertical range (greatest height − least):
  You: (6 − (−5)) × 100 = 1,100 m
  Sarah: (7 − (−8)) × 100 = 1,500 m
  Difference in range = |1,500 − 1,100| = 400 m.
Max depth 500 m vs 800 m (diff 300) · range 1,100 m vs 1,500 m (diff 400)

Sample Expert Response (Step 5 report — 4/4 Deliverable)

Sample Navigator Log using the default checkpoints (student work will use their own coordinates):
"My submarine's four checkpoints are A (3, 4) in Quadrant I, B (−5, 6) in Quadrant II, C (−7, −5) in Quadrant III, and D (6, −3) in Quadrant IV. Checkpoints A and B are both above sea level, so the vertical distance between them is |6| − |4| = 2 units = 200 meters. I reflected Checkpoint B (−5, 6) over the x-axis to (−5, −6), over the y-axis to (5, 6), and over both axes to (5, −6). My deepest point is Checkpoint C at y = −5, which is 500 meters down in the Twilight Zone; compared with my partner Sarah's 800-meter maximum, she navigated 300 meters deeper."
Rubric (mirrors the app's 4 / 3 / 2 table):
4 — Expert: all 4 checkpoints plotted and quadrants identified with explanation; depth distance shown with absolute-value math and real-world meaning; all reflections correct with sign reasoning; ocean zones and partner max depth/range compared with the absolute difference.
3 — Proficient: all 4 plotted and identified; distance, reflections, and comparison correct but may skip reasoning.
2 — Developing: some plotting/identification errors, a distance or reflection sign error, or an incomplete comparison.
