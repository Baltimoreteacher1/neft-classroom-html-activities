Key Words
Learn these 4 math words before you begin
🏀
Ratio
A comparison of two numbers or quantities.
3 basketballs to 2 soccer balls (3:2)
⏱️
Rate
A ratio that compares two quantities with different units of measure.
24 points scored in 12 minutes
🎯
Unit Rate
A rate compared to exactly one unit of the second quantity.
2 points scored per 1 minute (2:1)
📈
Projection
Using a constant rate to predict future stats or results.
2 points/min × 10 mins = 20 points

Step 1 — Scoring Unit Rate
Find points per minute (unit rate)
Your job: Calculate how fast your athlete scores. Enter the athlete's total points and the total minutes they played, then find their scoring unit rate (points per minute).
🟢 Level 1 tip: Try friendly numbers, like 60 points in 30 minutes (2 points per minute).
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE
Use real stats from a professional or local game (e.g., 23 points in 36 minutes). Write down the division exactly.
Need a hint?

Divide the total points by the total minutes played. This gives you the unit rate (points per 1 minute).
Example: 40 points ÷ 20 minutes = 2 points per minute.

📈
Step 2 — Project Season Performance
Predict season stats over 10, 20, 40, and 80 games
Your job: How many points will your athlete score in a full season? Convert their points per minute to points per game, and then project their performance using a ratio table.
🟢 Level 1 tip: Standard youth games are 20 minutes long. Pro games are 32 minutes (or 48 minutes in the NBA). Try a 20-minute average game length.
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE — Ratio Notation (6.RP.1)

Write your scoring rate as equivalent ratios using ratio notation (points : games).

Need a hint?

First, find the Points Per Game (PPG) by multiplying the points-per-minute (from Step 1) by the minutes per game. Then, multiply the PPG by 10, 20, 40, and 80 to scale the points scored.
Example: If a player averages 1.5 points per minute and plays 20 minutes, they average 30 PPG. Over 10 games, they will score 30 × 10 = 300 points.

LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE — Statistics & Outliers (6.SP.5c)

Find the **mean** points per game for the 8 youth players above. Identify which player is an **outlier** and explain how they pull the average up.

Need a hint?

To compare two averages, divide total points by games played for each player to find their PPG. Subtract the smaller rate from the larger rate to find the difference.
Example: Player A scores 24 points in 2 games (12 PPG). Player B scores 30 points in 3 games (10 PPG). Player A averages 2 more PPG than Player B.

Compare reports with a partner using equivalent ratios
