Fraction Sense

Compare and compute with fractions with real understanding — the make-or-break skill for ratios and rates.

Learning objective

I can find equivalent fractions, write fractions in simplest form, compare fractions, add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators, and multiply and divide fractions.

🎯 Builds 6.NS.A.1 📚 4 lessons ⏱ 30 min

Core question

How can I model, solve, and explain fraction Sense so another student understands my thinking?

Evidence of mastery

Score 80% or higher, correct one missed item in Smart Review, and write a complete explanation using at least one vocabulary word.

Key vocabulary

  • Numerator — The top number; how many parts you have.
  • Denominator — The bottom number; how many equal parts in all.
  • Equivalent fractions — Different fractions that name the same amount.
  • Simplest form — A fraction with no common factor left but 1.
  • Common denominator — The same bottom number shared by two fractions.
  • Reciprocal — A fraction flipped upside down.

Worked examples

Study these three examples — easy to challenge — then head to Practice.

Example 1Warm-up

Write 6/8 in simplest form.

  1. Find a number that divides both 6 and 8. Both share the factor 2.
  2. Divide the top: 6 divided by 2 equals 3.
  3. Divide the bottom: 8 divided by 2 equals 4.
  4. Check 3/4: the only common factor of 3 and 4 is 1, so it is done.
Answer: 3/4
Example 2On level

Add 1/3 + 2/5.

  1. The denominators 3 and 5 are different, so find a common denominator. Multiply 3 x 5 = 15.
  2. Rename 1/3: multiply top and bottom by 5 to get 5/15.
  3. Rename 2/5: multiply top and bottom by 3 to get 6/15.
  4. Add the numerators: 5/15 + 6/15 = 11/15. It is already in simplest form.
Answer: 11/15
Example 3Challenge

Divide 3/4 ÷ 2/3.

  1. Keep the first fraction the same: 3/4.
  2. Change divide to multiply and flip the second fraction (its reciprocal): 2/3 becomes 3/2.
  3. Multiply across: 3 x 3 = 9 on top and 4 x 2 = 8 on the bottom, giving 9/8.
  4. Write 9/8 as a mixed number: 9 divided by 8 is 1 with remainder 1, so 1 1/8.
Answer: 1 1/8

Error clinic

Use this as a quick conference script after a missed diagnostic, a worksheet error, or a low post-quiz score.

Likely misconception

Students may compare digits instead of values, treat denominators as whole-number size, or forget that decimals and fractions can represent the same amount.

Teacher move

Listen for unit language: halves, tenths, hundredths, equal parts, and benchmark values.

Student self-check

Can I show the problem with a model, name the operation or relationship, and explain why my answer is reasonable?

Two-minute conference

  1. Ask the student to point to the exact step where the answer changed.
  2. Have the student restate the problem using one vocabulary word from this topic.
  3. Rebuild one simpler example together, then ask the student to solve a parallel problem alone.

60-second fluency drill

🍕 Fraction Sense Set B · Application

Name: ____________________ Date: __________
A cake recipe uses 3/4 cup of flour. You triple the recipe. How much flour do you need?
Jenna walked 2/3 mile to school and 1/4 mile to the library. How far did she walk in all?
A ribbon is 7/8 yard long. You use 1/2 yard. How much ribbon is left?
There are 12 cookies. Tom takes 1/3 of them. How many cookies does Tom take?
A juice jug holds 3/4 gallon. You pour it into cups that hold 1/8 gallon. How many cups can you fill?
Half of a garden is planted. Of that planted part, 1/3 is tomatoes. What fraction of the whole garden is tomatoes?
Liam read 2/5 of a book on Monday and 1/4 on Tuesday. What fraction did he read in all?
A board is 5/6 foot long. You cut off 1/3 foot. How long is the remaining piece?
A bottle has 2/3 liter of water. You drink 1/2 of what is in the bottle. How much did you drink?
A wall needs 4/5 gallon of paint per coat. How much paint is needed for 3 coats?
A trail is 9/10 mile. Rest stops are every 3/10 mile. How many 3/10-mile sections are in the trail?
★ A pitcher holds 3 1/2 cups of lemonade. Each glass holds 1/4 cup. How many full glasses can you pour, and how much lemonade is left over?

  1. 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups
  2. 11/12 mile
  3. 3/8 yard
  4. 4 cookies
  5. 6 cups
  6. 1/6
  7. 13/20
  8. 1/2 foot
  9. 1/3 liter
  10. 12/5 = 2 2/5 gallons
  11. 3 sections
  12. 14 glasses, 0 left over
