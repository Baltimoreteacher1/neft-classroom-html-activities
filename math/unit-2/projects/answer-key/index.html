Unit 2 Projects — Teacher Answer Key

Worked solutions for both versions of the Unit 2 culminating project (Fraction Division). Covers Version A: Recipe Remix Bakery and Version B: Maker Workshop Cut List.

For teachers — worked solutions using each activity's Level 1 default values. Both projects are 6-step guided wizards (Key Words → three fraction-division calculators → Partner Compare → Finish). Students who change the inputs will get different (but similarly-structured) answers, and the Partner Compare step has no fixed answer — it shows the difference between the student's computed yields and a partner's entered numbers. Every number below was verified by tracing the exact formula each calculator uses. Fraction arithmetic follows the multiply-by-reciprocal rule; all fractions are simplified to lowest terms.
Version A  ·  Design & Build

🥁 Recipe Remix Bakery

Standard: 6.NOS.1 • Fraction division in a bakery context

1 Step 1 — Portion Control (Fraction ÷ Fraction · 6.NOS.1)

Input Default Worked Solution Final Answer
Total ingredient (dividend) 3/4 cup
Rule: (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a×d) / (b×c)

(3/4) ÷ (1/8)
= (3×8) / (4×1)
= 24 / 4
= 6

Simplify: GCF(24, 4) = 4 → 24/4 = 6 (whole number)
Decimal: 6.0
Check: 6 × (1/8) = 6/8 = 3/4 ✓
6 portions
(whole number, nothing left over)
Portion serving size (divisor) 1/8 cup

2 Step 2 — Big Batch Servings (Whole Number ÷ Unit Fraction · 6.NOS.1)

Input Default Worked Solution Final Answer
Total batch size 4 cups
Rule: whole ÷ (1/d) = whole × d

4 ÷ (1/3)
= 4 × 3
= 12

Interpretation: each cup holds 3 servings of 1/3 cup,
so 4 cups × 3 = 12 servings.
Check: 12 × (1/3) = 12/3 = 4 cups ✓
12 servings
Scoop size (unit fraction 1/d) 1/3 cup

3 Step 3 — Roll Cuts (Mixed Number ÷ Fraction · 6.NOS.1)

Input Default Worked Solution Final Answer
Total roll length 2 1/2 ft
Step 1 — Convert mixed number:
2 1/2 = (2×2 + 1)/2 = 5/2

Step 2 — Divide by the slice size:
(5/2) ÷ (1/4)
= (5/2) × (4/1)
= 20/2
= 10

Remainder: 20 mod 2 = 0 → divides perfectly, no dough left over
Check: 10 × (1/4) = 10/4 = 5/2 = 2 1/2 ft ✓
10 slices
(exact; no leftover dough)
Slice size (divisor) 1/4 ft

About the "Real-World Bakery Yields" table on this step

The table below the calculator (Croissants 6–8, Cinnamon Rolls 8–10, Artisan Baguette 12–16, Sourdough Loaf 10–14) is a fixed reference — it is not computed from the student's numbers. Students write 1–2 sentences comparing their slice count to those ranges. With the default 10 slices, the closest professional match is a batch of cinnamon rolls or a sourdough loaf.

4 Step 4 — Compare With a Partner (6.NOS.1)

No single right answer. The student enters a partner's Portions, Servings, and Slices; the tool prints side-by-side values and the difference for each (their computed value − the partner's number). Grade that the differences are computed correctly and that the reflection cites real numbers and explains how a smaller divisor (serving / slice size) produces a larger quotient (more servings).

Worked example: If the student computed 10 slices and the partner reports 8, the tool shows "You cut 2.00 more slices" (10 − 8 = 2).

Sample Expert Written Menu Card (Final Deliverable)

"At the Recipe Remix Bakery, my 3/4 cup of sugar makes 6 portions because (3/4) ÷ (1/8) = (3×8)/(4×1) = 24/4 = 6 — a perfect whole number with nothing left over. My 4-cup big batch of batter makes 12 servings because dividing by 1/3 is the same as multiplying by 3: 4 × 3 = 12. My 2½-foot dough roll cuts into exactly 10 slices at 1/4 ft each (5/2 ÷ 1/4 = 5/2 × 4 = 20/2 = 10), with no dough left over. I noticed the smaller I make each serving, the more servings I get."

Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring Notes

Expert (4) responses demonstrate: (Fraction ÷ Fraction) correct answer 6, simplified fraction shown (24/4 = 6), and a decimal or check; (Whole ÷ Unit Fraction) correct count of 12 with an explanation of why multiplying by the reciprocal works ("each cup holds 3 servings of 1/3"); (Mixed Number ÷ Fraction) mixed number converted to 5/2 first and division shown giving exactly 10 slices with no leftover; Partner Compare differences computed correctly; menu card names every calculated value with correct units and connects each to the division used.

Proficient (3): All three calculator answers correct; menu card uses most numbers but may skip a reciprocal step or a leftover interpretation.

Developing (2): At least two steps attempted; error in the mixed-number conversion or a simplification error; menu card present but incomplete.

Both Versions  ·  Partner Compare, Real-World Tables & Level 2

💬 Guidance for Partner Compare, the Reference Tables & Level 2

Both projects are 6-step guided wizards: Key Words → three fraction-division calculators → Partner Compare → Finish. Every calculator recomputes live (oninput) using the multiply-by-reciprocal rule. The worked answers above use each activity's Level 1 default inputs. Level 2 hides the friendly preset selectors and lets students type their own numerators/denominators (and surfaces the scrap / remainder extensions).

C Partner Compare (both versions)

Students type a partner's yields, and the page prints each value side by side with the difference (their computed value − the partner's number). There is no single right answer — check that the differences are correct and the reflection cites real numbers and explains that a smaller divisor (serving / cut size) produces a larger quotient (more servings / pieces).

R Real-World reference tables

Version A (Step 3) shows a fixed "Real-World Bakery Yields" table (Croissants 6–8, Cinnamon Rolls 8–10, Artisan Baguette 12–16, Sourdough Loaf 10–14). It is a reference for the written comparison and is not computed from student inputs. Version B (Step 3) shows a live-computed "Commercial Supply Yield Table"; at the default inputs it reads Rope 25 ÷ 1/4 = 100 pieces (0 scrap), Fabric 15 ÷ 2/3 = 22½ swatches (1/3 yд scrap), Lumber 8 ÷ 3/4 = 10⅔ strips (10 full + 1/2 ft scrap). Those cells recompute when the student changes the rope divisor, swatch size, or strip size.

L2 Level 2 — Custom Fractions, Remainders & Scrap

Level 2 exposes numerator/denominator inputs so students divide their own fractions, and the calculators report the full-piece count plus the leftover (and, in Version B, the exact scrap in feet). Grade for a correct mixed-number conversion, the reciprocal step, and a clearly interpreted remainder.

Sample Level 2 Answers (traced from the live calculators)

Version A — Roll Cuts, 3¾ ft roll ÷ 2/3 ft slices:
3¾ = 15/4; (15/4) ÷ (2/3) = (15/4) × (3/2) = 45/8 = 5.625 → 5 full slices with 5/8 of a slice left over.

Version B — Wood Strips, 5¼ ft board ÷ 2/3 ft strips:
5¼ = 21/4; (21/4) ÷ (2/3) = (21/4) × (3/2) = 63/8 = 7.875 → 7 full strips + 7/8 of a strip; scrap = 21×3 − 7×2×4 over 4×3 = (63 − 56)/12 = 7/12 ft of scrap wood.
