Step 0 — Key Words
Learn these 4 math words before you begin
📏
Dividend — Total Material
The starting length of material that you want to divide or cut.
Total rope = 3 yards
🪚
Divisor — Piece Size
The length of each individual piece you are cutting off.
Each piece = 1/2 yard
🧱
Quotient — Total Pieces
The answer: the number of full pieces you can cut from the total.
3 ÷ 1/2 = 6 pieces
🔄
Reciprocal — Flipped Fraction
Flipping a fraction upside down to change division into multiplication.
Reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2

Step 1 — Rope Cuts
Total rope (fraction or whole) ÷ piece length (unit fraction) = number of pieces
Your job: Enter the total length of rope you have. Then select the length of each piece (a unit fraction like 1/4) to see how many pieces you can cut.
🟢 Level 1 tip: Try whole numbers like 4 feet of rope, and a piece length of 1/2 or 1/4 foot.
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE
Try entering a fraction for the total rope (like 9/4 feet) to see how the reciprocal rule divides fractions!
Need a hint?

To divide by a unit fraction (like 1/4), multiply the total length by the reciprocal (4/1 or 4). This shows how many quarters fit into the whole length.
Example: 4 ft ÷ 1/4 ft = 4 × 4 = 16 pieces.

LEVEL 2 EXTENSION — Explain the Reciprocal

Step 2 — Wood Strips
Total board (mixed number) ÷ strip length (fraction) = number of strips
Your job: Enter your total wood board size as a mixed number (e.g., 4 1/2 feet). Then enter your strip length as a fraction (e.g., 3/4 foot) to see how many strips you can yield.
🟢 Level 1 tip: Try 4 1/2 feet (4 whole, 1 numerator, 2 denominator) and cut it into 1/2 or 3/4 foot strips.
LEVEL 2 CHALLENGE
Use custom fractions that do not divide evenly (like 5 1/4 feet divided by 2/3 foot strips) to see the exact remaining scrap wood.

Need a hint?

1. Convert the mixed number total to an improper fraction.
2. Multiply by the reciprocal of the strip length fraction.
Example: 4 1/2 ft = 9/2 ft. Strip length is 3/4 ft. Reciprocal is 4/3. Multiply: 9/2 × 4/3 = 36/6 = 6 strips.
