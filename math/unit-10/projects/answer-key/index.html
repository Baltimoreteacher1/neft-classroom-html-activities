Volume & Surface Area  |  6.GR.2, 6.GR.4 (+ 6.GR.1 in Version C)  |  V = lwh, closed & open-top SA, area of 2-D figures

For teachers only — this page shows fully worked solutions using each project's default / "Use Example Numbers" input values. Students who enter their own valid measurements get different numbers but follow the same reasoning process, so grade the method. Every calculator recomputes live as students type.

Standards: 6.GR.2 — volume of rectangular prisms with whole-number and fractional edge lengths, counting unit cubes.   6.GR.4 — surface area of rectangular prisms from nets (closed and open-top).   Version C also uses 6.GR.1 (area of parallelograms, triangles, and trapezoids) and square-pyramid lateral area.

Version A — Package Design Challenge

Step 1 Calculate the Box Volume

Volume of a Rectangular Prism · 6.GR.2
Length = 8 in Width = 5 in Height = 4 in
V = l × w × h
160 cubic inches
V = 8 × 5 × 4
Step 1 — bottom layer: 8 × 5 = 40 unit cubes
Step 2 — stack layers: 40 × 4 = 160 cubic inches
Interpretation: The box holds exactly 160 one-inch cubes arranged in 4 layers of 40.
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Volume (6.GR.2)
4 (Expert): V = 160 cubic inches with correct unit label; student explains the layer-by-layer unit-cube reasoning (40 per layer, 4 layers).
3 (Proficient): V = 160 cubic inches correct.
2 (Developing): Formula V = lwh used but computation error (e.g., forgot one dimension).

Step 2 Unfold the Net & Find Surface Area

Surface Area from the Net · 6.GR.4
Length = 8 in Width = 5 in Height = 4 in
SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
184 square inches
Face Pair Dimensions One face Both faces (x2)
Top & Bottom l × w = 8 × 5 40 sq in 80 sq in
Front & Back l × h = 8 × 4 32 sq in 64 sq in
Left & Right sides w × h = 5 × 4 20 sq in 40 sq in
SA = 2(40 + 32 + 20) = 2 × 92 = 184 square inches
Alternatively: 80 + 64 + 40 = 184 square inches ✓
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Surface Area / Nets (6.GR.4)
4 (Expert): All three face pairs identified and labeled; SA = 184 sq in with correct formula and unit label.
3 (Proficient): SA = 184 sq in correct.
2 (Developing): One face pair missing (e.g., forgot sides), or computation error in one pair.

Step 4 Compare Packages (Partner)

Compare & reason · 6.GR.2 · 6.GR.4

Students enter a partner's length, width, and height; the page computes the partner's volume and surface area and the material efficiency SA ÷ V (cardboard per cubic inch) for both boxes. The lower SA ÷ V is more efficient. This is student-specific — grade that the comparison and the reflection cite the real computed numbers.

Worked example — Version A
My box 8 × 5 × 4 (V = 160, SA = 184) vs partner 6 × 5 × 4 (V = 120, SA = 148).
SA ÷ V: me 184 / 160 = 1.15; partner 148 / 120 = 1.23 → my box is more efficient (less cardboard per cubic inch).

Deliverable Package Spec Sheet — Sample Expert Response

"Our shipping box measures 8 × 5 × 4 inches. Its volume is 160 cubic inches — picture 40 unit cubes on the bottom layer stacked 4 layers high. The surface area is 184 square inches, made up of three face pairs: top/bottom (80 sq in), front/back (64 sq in), and left/right sides (40 sq in). At the default White Premium rate of $0.03 per square inch, the cardboard costs $5.52 per box. This design is a good choice because the 8-inch length gives room for the product while keeping the box compact and the cost low."
Version B — Aquarium Build Lab

Step 1 Aquarium Volume & Capacity

Volume of a Rectangular Prism · 6.GR.2
Length = 20 in Width = 10 in Height = 12 in
V = l × w × h  ·  Capacity = V ÷ 231
2,400 cubic inches (≈ 10.4 gallons)
V = 20 × 10 × 12
Bottom layer: 20 × 10 = 200 unit cubes
Stack 12 layers: 200 × 12 = 2,400 cubic inches total capacity
Capacity in gallons: 2,400 ÷ 231 ≈ 10.4 gallons (one gallon of water fills 231 cubic inches).
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Volume & Capacity (6.GR.2)
4 (Expert): V = 2,400 cubic inches with unit label and capacity ≈ 10.4 gallons (÷ 231); layer reasoning included.
3 (Proficient): V = 2,400 cubic inches correct.
2 (Developing): Formula used but arithmetic error, or gallons not converted.

Step 2 Glass Needed: Open-Top Tank

Open-Top Surface Area from the Net · 6.GR.4
Length = 20 in Width = 10 in Height = 12 in
SAopen-top = (l × w) + 2(l × h) + 2(w × h)  [5 faces — NO top]
920 square inches of glass
Face Dimensions Area
Bottom l × w = 20 × 10 200 sq in
2 long sides (front & back) 2 × (l × h) = 2 × (20 × 12) 480 sq in
2 short sides (left & right) 2 × (w × h) = 2 × (10 × 12) 240 sq in
SAopen-top = 200 + 480 + 240 = 920 square inches
Comparison: A closed box would have SA = 2(200 + 240 + 120) = 1,120 square inches. Removing the top face saves 200 square inches of glass (one l × w = 20 × 10 = 200 panel).
Sample 4 / 3 / 2 Rubric Scoring — Open-Top Surface Area (6.GR.4)
4 (Expert): 5-face net correctly identified (no top); SA = 920 sq in correct using SA = lw + 2lh + 2wh; difference from closed box (1,120 − 920 = 200) explained.
3 (Proficient): SA = 920 sq in correct; open-top formula used.
2 (Developing): Used closed-box formula (SA = 1,120) — did not subtract the top face — or one face missing.

Step 4 Compare with a Partner

Compare & reason · 6.GR.2

Students type their partner's tank capacity (gallons) and total cost directly; the page compares capacity and cost and can show cost per gallon. This is student-specific — grade that the comparison is set up correctly and the reflection cites the real numbers.

Worked example — Version B
My tank: 10.4 gal at $173.00 total. Partner's tank: 5.0 gal at $95.00 total.
Cost per gallon: me $173.00 ÷ 10.4 ≈ $16.63; partner $95.00 ÷ 5.0 = $19.00 → my larger tank costs less per gallon of capacity.

Deliverable Build Plan — Sample Expert Response

"Our aquarium measures 20 × 10 × 12 inches. Its volume is 2,400 cubic inches, which is about 10.4 gallons (2,400 ÷ 231). Because the tank is open at the top, the glass covers only 5 faces: the bottom (200 sq in) plus four side panels (480 + 240 = 720 sq in), totaling 920 square inches. A closed box would need 1,120 square inches, so the open top saves 200 square inches of glass. At $0.15 per square inch the glass costs $138.00, and with the $35.00 accessory bundle the total project cost is $173.00 — about $16.65 per gallon, a reasonable budget for a classroom tank of this size."

Level 2 Extension Features

Reasoning with volume & surface area · 6.GR.2 · 6.GR.4

Version A — decimals, open-top lid, and finish choice:

Level 2 encourages decimal edge lengths (e.g., 7.5 × 3.25 × 4). The formulas V = lwh and SA = 2(lw + lh + wh) are unchanged — they handle fractional edges directly (6.GR.2 / 6.GR.4).
The open-top tray option removes the top face: SAopen = SAclosed − (l × w). For the example box that is 184 − 40 = 144 sq in, so the cardboard cost at $0.03 drops to 144 × $0.03 = $4.32.
The finish menu changes the rate: Kraft $0.02, White Premium $0.03, Glossy $0.05. Accept any correct SA × rate for the finish the student selected.

Version B — custom glass rate and accessory cost:

Level 2 lets students set their own glass rate and accessory cost. The total is always Total = (open-top SA × rate) + accessories.
Example: if a student keeps the 920 sq in tank but chooses a premium glass rate of $0.20 and a $50 accessory bundle, the total is 920 × $0.20 + $50 = $184.00 + $50 = $234.00. Verify the student used the open-top 5-face area and added their accessory amount.
Version C — World Architect Expedition

Version C sends students to 12 landmark destinations. At each one the student must (1) identify the correct solid or 2-D shape, (2) choose the matching formula, (3) compute the target measurement, and (4) estimate a cost by multiplying the measurement by a material rate they select. The measurement answers are fixed (below); the cost varies with the chosen material, so grade the cost as measurement × the selected rate. This version reaches beyond 6.GR.2 / 6.GR.4 into 6.GR.1 (area of parallelograms, triangles, and trapezoids) and square-pyramid lateral area.
