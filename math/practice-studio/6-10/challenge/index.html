Mission control needs your math.El control de la misión necesita tus matemáticas.

Mission Control reports a solar flare, so the night crew has to re-plan every supply run with trickier numbers. Convert carefully, estimate first, and verify each answer by multiplying back.

El Control de Misión informa de una llamarada solar, así que la tripulación nocturna debe replanificar cada envío de suministros con números más difíciles. Convierte con cuidado, estima primero y verifica cada respuesta multiplicando de vuelta.

1. Climb the skill ladder

Each rung is harder than the last, and the numbers change every time.

Cada peldaño es más difícil que el anterior y los números cambian cada vez.

  1. 6 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Find the Mixed Number Error
  2. A detective has a rope that is 5 1/4 feet long. She needs to cut it into pieces that are each 3/4 foot for tying evidence tags. How many pieces can she cut? Write the equation, show each step (convert, the reciprocal shortcut, multiply, simplify), and verify your answer.
  3. Find Liam's Mistake
  4. A solar panel strip is 7 1/2 meters long. The crew cuts it into pieces that are each 5/6 meter long. How many pieces do they get?
  5. The crew uses 6 3/4 liters of water in 2 1/4 days at a steady rate. How many liters do they use each day?
    • 1/3 liter
    • 3 liters
    • 6 liters
    • 15 3/16 liters
  6. Supply pod A brings 3 1/2 kg of food and pod B brings 2 3/4 kg. Each crew meal pack uses 3/4 kg. How many FULL meal packs can the crew make from both pods?
  7. A station planner lists how long each repair job has and how long one task takes. For each row, convert the time available to an improper fraction, then find how many tasks fit.

3. Build it, move it, test it

Use the tools to build the idea. Then check it.

Usa las herramientas para construir la idea. Luego compruébala.

  1. Sort each conversion: did the cadet change the mixed number into the correct improper fraction?
  2. 2 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. Pia divided 5 1/2 ÷ 1 1/2 by splitting each mixed number into parts. Tap the step where her work goes wrong.
  2. Leo divided 3 2/3 ÷ 1/6 to find how many 1/6-hour tasks fit in 3 2/3 hours. Tap the step where his work goes wrong.
  3. Nova divided 3 1/3 ÷ 5/6. She converted correctly. Tap the step where her work goes wrong.
  4. Ravi says 7 1/2 ÷ 2 1/2 is about 3. Sana says the answer must be bigger than 7 1/2, because dividing by a fraction makes the number bigger. Who is right?
    • Ravi: 2 1/2 is greater than 1, so the quotient is smaller than 7 1/2.
    • Sana: dividing by any fraction makes the number bigger.
    • Both are right.
    • Neither: the answer is 7 1/2 − 2 1/2 = 5.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  2. What is 9 1/3 ÷ 1 1/6?
  3. Which is the best estimate for 8 1/4 ÷ 1 3/4?
    • about 14

    • less than 1
    • a little less than 5
  4. Which multiplication checks that 6 ÷ 1 1/2 = 4?
    • 4 × 1 1/2 = 6
    • 6 × 1 1/2 = 9
    • 4 × 2/3 = 2 2/3
    • 4 ÷ 1 1/2 = 2 2/3
  5. The crew has 15 3/4 kg of supplies. They set aside 3 1/4 kg for emergencies and pack the rest into boxes that each hold 1 1/4 kg. How many full boxes can they pack?
  6. Write your own Orbit Station story problem that divides one mixed number by another. Solve it, check it by multiplying, and explain why you convert the mixed numbers first.
