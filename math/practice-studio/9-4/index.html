4. Stretch your thinking

Harder problems and explanations. Say why, not just what.

Problemas más difíciles y explicaciones. Di por qué, no solo qué.

  1. Suppose the variable s counts 8-hour SHIFTS instead of hours. Which equation gives the cars produced?
    • c = 2.5s
    • c = 8s
    • c = 10.5s
    • c = 20s
  2. If the plant runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week for the full 3 weeks, how many hours is that — and can it fill the 1,000-car order?
    • 72 hours — no, that is less than 400 hours
    • 168 hours — no, that is less than 400 hours
    • 504 hours — yes, because 504 hours is more than the 400 hours needed
    • 504 hours — no, because 504 is less than 1,000
  3. The solution to one problem was 41.7 cars. Would producing or washing a PARTIAL car make sense?
    • No — cars come in whole numbers, so round up to the next whole car
    • Yes — 0.7 of a car is a real amount you can wash
    • No — so round down and accept a smaller total
    • Yes — the equation is wrong if it gives a decimal
  4. Would you recommend the student council charge $12 or $14.30 per car? Which reasoning is strongest?
    • Either can work: $12 needs at least 42 cars washed, while $14.30 needs only about 35 — so the choice depends on how many customers they expect
    • $12 is always better because a smaller number is always better
    • $14.30 is always better because more money per car is always better
    • Neither price can reach the $500 goal
