Budget review at the Robot Lab: three rental plans, two charging schedules and one tight deadline for a robot fair. Solve each equation, round the right way, and justify every recommendation with the math.

Revisión de presupuesto en el Laboratorio de Robots: tres planes de alquiler, dos horarios de carga y una fecha límite para una feria de robots. Resuelve cada ecuación, redondea en la dirección correcta y justifica cada recomendación con las matemáticas.

1. Climb the skill ladder

Each rung is harder than the last, and the numbers change every time.

Cada peldaño es más difícil que el anterior y los números cambian cada vez.

  1. 6 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Suppose the variable s counts 8-hour SHIFTS instead of hours. Which equation gives the cars produced?
    • c = 2.5s
    • c = 8s
    • c = 10.5s
    • c = 20s
  2. If the plant runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week for the full 3 weeks, how many hours is that — and can it fill the 1,000-car order?
    • 72 hours — no, that is less than 400 hours
    • 168 hours — no, that is less than 400 hours
    • 504 hours — yes, because 504 hours is more than the 400 hours needed
    • 504 hours — no, because 504 is less than 1,000
  3. The solution to one problem was 41.7 cars. Would producing or washing a PARTIAL car make sense?
    • No — cars come in whole numbers, so round up to the next whole car
    • Yes — 0.7 of a car is a real amount you can wash
    • No — so round down and accept a smaller total
    • Yes — the equation is wrong if it gives a decimal
  4. A 3D printer in the Robot Lab makes 14 gears each hour, so g = 14h, where h is the hours and g is the gears. The robotics club needs at least 200 gears. What is the fewest whole number of hours the printer must run?
  5. A robot rental costs c = 5h + 15, where h is the hours and c is the cost in dollars. The $15 is a setup fee. The club has $75. For how many hours can it rent the robot?
    • 12 hours
    • 18 hours
    • 15 hours
    • 60 hours
  6. Plan A costs c = 4h + 12 and Plan B costs c = 6h, where h is the hours and c is the cost in dollars. Fill in the cost of each plan.
  7. Plan A costs c = 4h + 12 and Plan B costs c = 6h. The club has a $90 budget and wants the plan that gives more whole hours of robot time. Which choice is correct?
    • Plan B, because it has no setup fee
    • Plan A, which allows 20 whole hours
    • Plan A, which allows 19 whole hours (Plan B allows 15)
    • Plan A, which allows 22 whole hours

3. Build it, move it, test it

Use the tools to build the idea. Then check it.

Usa las herramientas para construir la idea. Luego compruébala.

  1. A robot's battery costs c = 6h + 14 dollars to run for h hours. The club has $62. Sort each step into the stage of solving it belongs to.
  2. 2 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. A welding robot makes 18 welds every hour, so w = 18h. Sam needs at least 100 welds and finds the hours.
  2. A robot charger uses c = 4t + 6, where t is the hours and c is the cost in dollars. Tariq has $46 and finds t.
  3. A bottling robot fills 25 bottles each minute, so b = 25m. An order needs 600 bottles. Mei finds the minutes.
  4. Robot X costs c = 3h + 20 and Robot Y costs c = 5h, where h is the hours and c is the cost in dollars. For 8 hours of work, Dmitri says Robot X is cheaper because 3 is less than 5. Noor says to compute both totals first. Who is right?
    • Dmitri: the smaller hourly rate always means the lower cost.
    • Both: they use different information, so both are fine.
    • Neither: 8 hours is not enough to tell.
    • Noor: for 8 hours Robot Y costs $40 and Robot X costs $44, so Robot Y is cheaper.

5. Boss level

Tell us how sure you are, then solve. Finish with a problem of your own.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  1. A painting robot paints 7 panels each hour, so n = 7h. What does the solution of 7h = 91 tell you?
    • The robot paints 91 panels in 7 hours.
    • The robot needs 13 hours to paint 91 panels.
    • The robot paints 13 panels every hour.
    • The robot needs 98 hours to paint 91 panels.
  2. A robot battery charges by p = 12.5h, where h is the hours and p is the percent of charge. The robot needs at least 80 percent to start. What is the fewest whole number of hours it must charge?
  3. Which check shows that h = 9 is the solution of 4h + 6 = 42?
    • 4 × 9 + 6 = 36 + 6 = 42
    • 4 + 9 + 6 = 19
    • 4 × 9 = 36
    • 42 ÷ 9 = 4.67
  4. The robotics club expects to sell 26 robot kits. It wants to raise at least $400. If p is the price of one kit in dollars, then 26p must reach 400. What is the least price in whole cents, in dollars, that reaches the goal?
  5. The table shows a battery that gains the same percent of charge every hour, so b = 8h. The battery holds at most 100 percent. After how many whole hours does the equation first give a charge above 100 percent?
  6. The Robot Lab must choose between two robot rental plans: Plan A, c = 4h + 12, and Plan B, c = 6h. The lab needs the robot for 10 hours. Write a recommendation. Use the words equation and solution, show your substitution, and justify your choice.
