Keep the port running.Mantén el puerto en marcha.

A storm has delayed the fleet, and the harbor is full of ships waiting to be billed. One misplaced decimal point could overcharge a crew by hundreds of dollars, so check every product with an estimate.

Una tormenta retrasó a la flota y el puerto está lleno de barcos esperando su factura. Un punto decimal mal colocado podría cobrarle de más a una tripulación cientos de dólares, así que comprueba cada producto con una estimación.

Each rung is harder than the last, and the numbers change every time.

Cada peldaño es más difícil que el anterior y los números cambian cada vez.

  1. 6 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Find Kai's Mistake
  2. A driver buys 9.5 gallons of gas at $3.40 per gallon. Show how to find the total cost. Use estimation to check if your answer is reasonable.
  3. A harbor crew orders rope. The price list is shown in the table. What is the total cost of the order, with delivery included?
  4. Tug A burns 4.8 liters of fuel each hour and runs for 3.5 hours. Tug B burns 5.25 liters each hour and runs for 3 hours. How many more liters does Tug A burn than Tug B?
  5. Fill in each charge in dollars. Charge = amount × rate.
  6. A harbor clerk needs the cost of 7.5 m of cable at $0.46 per meter. Estimate first, then choose the exact cost.
    • $0.345, because $0.46 is less than a dollar.
    • $34.50, because 7.5 × 4.6 is about 35.
    • $3.45, because the estimate 0.5 × 7.5 = 3.75 is close.
    • $345, because 7.5 × 46 = 345.

3. Build it, move it, test it

Use the tools to build the idea. Then check it.

Usa las herramientas para construir la idea. Luego compruébala.

  1. A harbor clerk places the decimal point in six products. Before any zeros are dropped, how many decimal places does each product need? Sort each multiplication.
  2. 2 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. A clerk priced a piece of chain. One step goes wrong. Tap the first step that is wrong.
  2. A worker found how much sealant a hull patch needs. One step goes wrong. Tap the first step that is wrong.
  3. A crane operator figured out the weight of four loads. One step goes wrong. Tap the first step that is wrong.
  4. A boat stays at the dock for 0.5 hour, and the harbor charges $12 per hour. Ana says the bill is $6. Ben says the bill must be more than $12, because multiplying always makes numbers bigger. Who is right?
    • Ana: half an hour costs half of $12.
    • Ben: multiplying always gives a number greater than both factors.
    • Neither: the bill is $60, because 12 × 5 = 60.
    • Neither: the bill is $0.60, because 12 × 5 = 60 and the point moves two places.

5. Boss level

Tell us how sure you are, then solve. Finish with a problem of your own.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  1. A reflective stripe on a buoy is 0.3 m long and 0.02 m wide. What is the area of the stripe in square meters?
  2. The floor of a cargo hold is 6.8 m long and 4.5 m wide. What is the area of the floor in square meters?
  3. A clerk knows that 24 × 35 = 840. The harbor needs 2.4 m of shrink-wrap at $0.35 per meter. What is the cost?
    • $0.084
    • $0.84
    • $8.40
    • $84
  4. Which statement about the product 0.8 × 0.9 is true?
    • The product is greater than both factors, because multiplying always makes numbers bigger.
    • The product is 7.2, because 8 × 9 = 72 and the point goes after the 7.
    • The product is 0.72, which is less than both factors.
    • The product is 0.072, because there are 3 decimal places in all.
  5. A ferry burns 12.5 liters of fuel each hour. Fuel costs $1.15 per liter. What is the fuel cost of a 3.6-hour trip, in dollars?
  6. Write your own Harbor Works problem that multiplies two decimals with different numbers of decimal places. Solve it, explain where you put the decimal point and why, and use an estimate to show your answer is reasonable.
