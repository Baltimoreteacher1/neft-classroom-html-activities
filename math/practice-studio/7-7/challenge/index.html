Turno de noche en la Estación Órbita: una lluvia de meteoros soltó tres paneles. El control de la misión envía nuevas coordenadas de las esquinas, una escala en metros y un presupuesto de material muy justo. Cada longitud tiene que salir bien al primer intento.

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Maria is choosing between two store spaces on a grid where each unit is 1 foot and each grid line is 5 feet apart. Space A is a rectangle 25 ft by 40 ft; Space B is 30 ft by 30 ft. Which has more floor area?
    • Space A — 1,000 sq ft vs 900 sq ft
    • Space B — 900 sq ft vs 850 sq ft
    • They are equal
    • Space B — because a square always beats a rectangle
  2. A square has vertices (a, b) and (a, b + s) on one side. Which operation gives the side length for ANY values?
    • Add all four coordinates
    • Subtract the y-coordinates and take the absolute value
    • Multiply a × b
    • Average the coordinates
  3. The park design used width from coordinates, then length from the area, then the scale to place the missing vertices. Design your own version: pick an area and a scale, mark two vertices, and show where the other two go.
  4. Solar panel P on Orbit Station has corners at (−7, 4), (2, 4), (2, −3) and (−7, −3). Each grid unit is 2 meters. A technician runs a border strip all the way around the panel. How many meters of strip does she need?
  5. Three corners of a rectangular docking pad are at (−5, 6), (4, 6) and (4, −2). Each grid unit is 3 meters. What is the area of the pad?
    • 72 m²
    • 216 m²
    • 648 m²
    • 102 m²
  6. Solar array S has corners A(−6, 5), B(1, 5), C(1, 1), D(5, 1), E(5, −3) and F(−6, −3), joined in that order and back to A. Find each side length in units, then the perimeter.
  7. Orbit Station's cargo bay is a rectangle with a perimeter of 34 units. Two of its corners are at (−2, 3) and (6, 3), and the bay extends below that edge. Where are the other two corners?
    • (−2, −6) and (6, −6)
    • (−2, −14) and (6, −14)
    • (−2, 12) and (6, 12)
    • (−2, −23) and (6, −23)

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. Imani finds the perimeter of rectangle RSTU with vertices R(−6, 2), S(3, 2), T(3, −4) and U(−6, −4).
  2. Luca tapes the edge of panel JKLM, a rectangle with vertices J(1, −2), K(8, −2), L(8, 4) and M(1, 4). He wants to know how many units of tape go all the way around.
  3. Kenji needs the real length of side AB. Vertices A(−4, 1) and B(3, 1) are on a map where each grid unit is 5 meters.
  4. Amara and Dmitri each find the perimeter of the rectangle with vertices (−4, 1), (5, 1), (5, −6) and (−4, −6). Amara says it is 32 units. Dmitri says it is 16 units, because perimeter is the length plus the width. Who is right?
    • Dmitri: the distance around is one length plus one width.
    • Amara: every side counts, so the perimeter is 2 lengths and 2 widths.
    • Both: 16 is just half of 32, so the two answers agree.
    • Neither: the perimeter is 63 units.

5. Boss level

Tell us how sure you are, then solve. Finish with a problem of your own.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  1. A crew plots four vertices on the grid: A(1, 1), B(5, 1), C(1, 4) and D(5, 4). In which order should they join the points to draw a rectangle without any sides crossing?
    • A, B, C, D, and back to A
    • A, C, B, D, and back to A
    • A, D, B, C, and back to A
    • A, B, D, C, and back to A
  2. A support beam runs from (−3.5, 6) to (4.25, 6) on the station map. How long is the beam in units?
  3. A rectangular lab module has its top edge from (−8, 3) to (1, 3). Its area is 45 square units, and the module extends below the top edge. What is the y-coordinate of its bottom edge?
  4. Three vertices of a rectangle are P(−4, −1), Q(5, −1) and R(5, 6). What are the coordinates of the fourth vertex, S?
    • (6, −4)
    • (−4, 6)
    • (−4, −6)
    • (4, 6)
  5. Two panels are drawn on the grid. Panel X has vertices (−6, 4), (6, 4), (6, 1) and (−6, 1). Panel Y has vertices (−3, −1), (3, −1), (3, −7) and (−3, −7). Which statement is true?
    • The perimeters are the same, but X has the greater area.
    • X has the greater area and the longer perimeter.
    • The areas are the same, but X has the longer perimeter.
    • The areas and the perimeters are both the same.
  6. Create your own rectangle on the coordinate plane. Give its four vertices, and make sure at least one coordinate is negative. Find the length of each side and the perimeter, and explain how you know which coordinates to use.
