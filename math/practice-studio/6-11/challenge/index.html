Test, measure, improve.Prueba, mide, mejora.

After hours, the test robots run diagnostics that only pass if every equation is set up the right way round. Find the dividend, find the divisor, solve it, and make the robots prove the answer by multiplying back.

Después del horario, los robots de prueba hacen diagnósticos que solo se aprueban si cada ecuación está planteada en el orden correcto. Encuentra el dividendo, encuentra el divisor, resuelve y haz que los robots comprueben la respuesta multiplicando de vuelta.

1. Climb the skill ladder

Each rung is harder than the last, and the numbers change every time.

Cada peldaño es más difícil que el anterior y los números cambian cada vez.

  1. 6 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Find the Problem-Solving Error
  2. Write your own fraction division word problem where the answer is 8. Then show the equation, solve it step by step, and explain how you know your answer is reasonable.
  3. A robot's battery holds 9/10 of a full charge. One balance test uses 3/20 of a full charge. Which equation and answer tell how many tests the robot can finish?
    • 3/20 ÷ 9/10 = 1/6 of a test
    • 9/10 × 3/20 = 27/200 of a test
    • 9/10 ÷ 3/20 = 6 tests
    • 9/10 − 3/20 = 3/4 of a test
  4. A wiring spool holds 4 1/2 meters of wire. Each robot needs 5/6 meter. Technicians wire as many whole robots as they can. How many meters of wire are left on the spool?
  5. Story A: 4 liters of coolant are poured into containers that each hold 2/3 liter. How many containers? Story B: 2/3 liter of coolant is shared equally among 4 vials. How much is in each vial? Which pair of answers is correct?
    • A: 6 containers; B: 1/6 liter
    • A: 1/6 container; B: 6 liters
    • A: 2 2/3 containers; B: 2 2/3 liters
    • A: 6 containers; B: 6 liters
  6. Two lab reports list a division and its answer. For each row, find the quotient, then multiply the quotient by the divisor to check it.

3. Build it, move it, test it

Use the tools to build the idea. Then check it.

Usa las herramientas para construir la idea. Luego compruébala.

  1. Each card names one amount from a lab story. Is it the dividend (the total being split) or the divisor (the size of one group)?
  2. 2 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. A robot has 7/12 liter of hydraulic fluid. Each test uses 1/6 liter. Zane wrote an equation to find how many tests it can run. Tap the step where his work goes wrong.
  2. How many COMPLETE 3/8-meter wires can be cut from a 2-meter cable? Rosa solved it. Tap the step where her answer goes wrong.
  3. Four robots share 3/4 liter of oil equally. Ines wrote an equation to find how much oil each robot gets. Tap the step where her work goes wrong.
  4. Leila and Omar want to know how many 1/4-liter bottles can be filled from 3 liters. Leila writes 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12. Omar writes 1/4 ÷ 3 = 1/12 because "the bottle is the thing being divided." Who is right?
    • Omar: the bottle is the thing being divided.
    • Both are right: 12 and 1/12 are the same answer.
    • Neither: you should multiply 3 × 1/4.
    • Leila: the 3 liters is the total, so it is the dividend.

5. Boss level

Tell us how sure you are, then solve. Finish with a problem of your own.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  1. Which story can be solved with 3/4 ÷ 3?
    • How many 3/4-liter bottles can be filled from 3 liters?
    • Three robots share 3/4 liter of oil equally. How much does each get?
    • What is 3/4 of 3 liters of oil?
    • Three robots each use 3/4 liter of oil. How much do they use in all?
  2. A robot arm needs 5/6-meter cable pieces. How many pieces can be cut from a 10-meter cable?
  3. A student solves 3 1/2 ÷ 7/8. Which answer is reasonable?
    • 3 1/16
    • 2 5/8
    • 1/4
    • 4
  4. A student found 3/8 ÷ 3/16 = 2. Which multiplication checks the answer?
    • 2 × 3/8 = 3/4
    • 2 × 3/16 = 3/8
    • 3/8 × 3/16 = 9/128
    • 2 × 16/3 = 10 2/3
  5. A robot cuts 3/8-meter ribbons from 5 1/4 meters of ribbon. How many ribbons can it cut?
  6. Write your own Robot Lab story problem where the answer tells how much is in ONE group (a total shared equally). Write the equation, solve it, check it by multiplying, and explain why your answer is reasonable.
