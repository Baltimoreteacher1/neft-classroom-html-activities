The stadium lights are on and the equipment crew has a stack of order forms with tricky totals. The head coach wants every total checked two different ways before anything ships.

Las luces del estadio están encendidas y el equipo de utilería tiene una pila de pedidos con totales difíciles. El entrenador quiere cada total comprobado de dos maneras diferentes antes de enviar nada.

1. Climb the skill ladder

Each rung is harder than the last, and the numbers change every time.

Cada peldaño es más difícil que el anterior y los números cambian cada vez.

  1. 6 fresh problems with new numbers each time (interactive only).

2. Multi-step missions

Real situations that need more than one step: plan, solve, then check that your answer makes sense.

Situaciones reales que necesitan más de un paso: planea, resuelve y revisa que tu respuesta tenga sentido.

  1. Find the Distributive Error
  2. Use the distributive property to explain why 6 × 98 can be calculated as 6 × 100 − 6 × 2 = 588. Then create your own example of using the distributive property to make multiplication easier.
  3. Fill in the table
  4. The stadium shop prices a bundle with three different rules. Test each rule at three values of x and fill in the empty cells.
  5. A booster club buys 47 team water bottles at $8 each. Split 47 into 50 − 3 and use the distributive property to find the total cost.
  6. The bleachers have 6 equal sections. Each section has x reserved seats and 8 general seats, as shown in the diagram. Which expression gives the total number of seats?
    • 6x + 48
    • 6x + 8
    • x + 48
    • 6x + 14
  7. The team has 24 jerseys and 36 pairs of shorts to pack into identical kits. Every kit gets the same number of jerseys and the same number of shorts, and nothing is left over. Each expression below equals 24 + 36. Which one shows the greatest possible number of kits?
    • 6(4 + 6)
    • 4(6 + 9)
    • 12(2 + 3)
    • 3(8 + 12)

3. Build it, move it, test it

Use the tools to build the idea. Then check it.

Usa las herramientas para construir la idea. Luego compruébala.

  1. Is each expression equivalent to 8(w + 5)? Sort every card into Equivalent or Not equivalent.

4. Detective work

Somebody made a mistake. Find the step, then decide who is right.

Alguien cometió un error. Encuentra el paso y decide quién tiene razón.

  1. Noor expanded 7(w − 3). Find the step where her work first goes wrong.
  2. Tomás wants to factor 24x + 36 completely. Find the step where his reasoning first goes wrong.
  3. Imani expanded 4(3p + 2). Find the step where her work first goes wrong.
  4. Aiden and Bea test whether 5(x + 4) and 5x + 4 are equivalent. They both try x = 2. Aiden says, "Both come out to 14, so they are equivalent." Bea says, "5(x + 4) comes out to 30 and 5x + 4 comes out to 14, so they are not equivalent." Who is right?
    • Aiden. Both expressions give 14.
    • Bea. The two expressions give different values at x = 2.
    • Both. Testing one number can never show anything.
    • Neither. Equivalent expressions must look exactly the same.

5. Boss level

Tell us how sure you are, then solve. Finish with a problem of your own.

Dinos qué tan seguro estás y luego resuelve. Termina con un problema tuyo.

  1. Which expression is equivalent to 4(3x − 5)?
    • 12x − 20
    • 12x − 5
    • 7x − 9
    • 12x + 20
  2. Each of the 15 players on the bench gets a shirt that costs $9 and a hat that costs h dollars, so the total cost is 15(9 + h). If each hat costs $4, what is the total cost in dollars?
  3. Which expression is 28a + 42 written with its greatest common factor pulled out?
    • 7(4a + 6)
    • 2(14a + 21)
    • 14(2a + 3)
    • 14(2a + 42)
  4. Fill in the blank so both sides are equal: 6(m + ___) = 6m + 54. What number goes in the blank?
  5. The table shows the value of two expressions at x = 1, x = 2 and x = 3. The two expressions are equivalent. Which pair of expressions produced the table?
    • 6(x + 3) and 6x + 18
    • 8(x + 2) and 8x + 16
    • 8(x + 2) and 8x + 2
    • 10x + 14 and 8(x + 2)
  6. Create your own problem. Think of two items a team might buy for every player, such as shirts and socks. Write a situation that fits a(b + c) with your own numbers. Find the total two ways, by adding inside first and by distributing first, and explain why both ways match.
