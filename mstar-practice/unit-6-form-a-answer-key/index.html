16.NOS.16-1 · Division Expressions with Fractions and Whole Numbers

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the expression 4 ÷ 1/3.

A, B, C, E. The whole number 4 can be written as the fraction 4/1. · Keep, change, flip rewrites the expression as 4/1 × 3/1. · The reciprocal of 1/3 is 3/1. · The quotient is 12.

Write 4 as 4/1, keep it, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/3 to 3/1. Then 4/1 × 3/1 = 12/1 = 12. The quotient 4/3 comes from multiplying by 1/3 instead of dividing, and dividing by a number smaller than 1 makes the quotient larger, not smaller.

26.NOS.16-2 · Division Expressions with Fractions and Mixed Numbers

Written response — 2 points

Marisol divided 5/6 ÷ 2/3. Her work was: 6/5 × 2/3 = 12/15 = 4/5. She wrote that the quotient is 4/5.

Explain Marisol's misconception and give the correct quotient.

Score 2: Student explains that keep, change, flip flips only the divisor, not the dividend, so 5/6 stays as it is and 2/3 becomes 3/2, and gives the quotient 5/4 or 1 1/4.

Score 1: Student gives the quotient 5/4 or 1 1/4 but does not explain that only the second fraction is flipped.

Score 0: Student agrees with Marisol that the quotient is 4/5 or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Marisol flipped the dividend instead of the divisor. Keep the first fraction as it is, change ÷ to ×, and flip only the second fraction: 5/6 × 3/2 = 15/12 = 5/4 = 1 1/4.

36.AT.56-3 · Explore Numerical Expressions with Exponents

Part A

Which value is equal to the power 3⁴?

D. 81

The base is 3 and the exponent is 4, so 3 is used as a factor four times: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

C. The exponent 4 tells how many times to use the base 3 as a factor: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81.

An exponent counts repeated factors of the base. It does not tell you to multiply, swap, or add the two numbers.

46.AT.6c6-4 · Write and Evaluate Numerical Expressions with Exponents

Part A

Evaluate the numerical expression 3 × 4² − 8.

C. 40

Following the order of operations, evaluate the power first: 4² = 16. Then multiply: 3 × 16 = 48. Then subtract: 48 − 8 = 40.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Evaluate the power first: 4² = 16. Then multiply: 3 × 16 = 48. Then subtract: 48 − 8 = 40.

The order of operations puts exponents before multiplication, and multiplication before subtraction. Each other path breaks that order.

56.AT.6a6-5 · Write and Evaluate Algebraic Expressions

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the algebraic expression 7n + 4.

A, B, C, D. The variable is n. · The coefficient of the variable term is 7. · The constant is 4. · When n = 3, the expression has a value of 25.

In 7n + 4 the variable is n, its coefficient is 7, and 4 is the constant. Substituting n = 3 gives 7 × 3 + 4 = 21 + 4 = 25. A value of 33 comes from combining 7 and 4 into 11 first, which is not allowed because 7n and 4 are not like terms. The term 7n means 7 times n.

66.AT.76-6 · Identify Equivalent Algebraic Expressions

Written response — 2 points

Priya claimed that 2x + x and 2x² are equivalent expressions. Her only check was x = 0. She wrote: 2(0) + 0 = 0 and 2(0)² = 0. Because both results were 0, she said the expressions are equivalent.

Explain Priya's misconception and give the correct simplified form of 2x + x.

Score 2: Student explains that one test value, especially 0, is not enough and shows a second value where the expressions differ, and gives 3x as the simplified form of 2x + x.

Score 1: Student gives 3x as the simplified form but does not explain why a single test value is not enough.

Score 0: Student agrees with Priya that the expressions are equivalent or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Substituting 0 makes many different expressions come out to 0, so one test value proves nothing. Test a second value: at x = 2, 2x + x = 2(2) + 2 = 6 but 2x² = 2(2)² = 8. The values differ, so the expressions are not equivalent. Since 2x and x are like terms, 2x + x simplifies to 3x.

76.NOS.46-7 · Find Factors and Multiples

Part A

What is the greatest common factor of 18 and 24?

C. 6

The factors of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 18. The factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24. The shared factors are 1, 2, 3, and 6, and the largest is 6.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

A. List the factors of each number, find the ones they share, which are 1, 2, 3, and 6, and take the largest shared factor, 6.

The greatest common factor is the largest factor in both factor lists. The number 72 is the least common multiple, and stopping at 3 or 2 skips the larger shared factor 6.

86.AT.76-8 · Generate Equivalent Expressions

Part A

Which property of operations is shown by the equation (7 + 4) + 6 = 7 + (4 + 6)?

D. Associative Property of Addition

The addends stay in the order 7, 4, 6 on both sides. Only the grouping changes, which is the associative property. Both sides have a value of 17.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

D. The addends appear in the same order on both sides, but the parentheses move. Changing the grouping without changing the sum is the associative property, and both sides equal 17.

Grouping changed and order did not, so the equation shows the associative property of addition.

96.NOS.16-9 · Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the expression 10 ÷ 2/5.

A, B, C, E. The reciprocal of the divisor 2/5 is 5/2. · The expression can be rewritten as 10/1 × 5/2. · The quotient is 25. · The quotient is greater than 10 because the divisor is less than 1.

Write 10 as 10/1 and multiply by the reciprocal 5/2: 10/1 × 5/2 = 50/2 = 25. A quotient of 4 comes from multiplying by 2/5 instead of its reciprocal. Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a quotient larger than the dividend.

106.NOS.16-10 · Divide Mixed Numbers

Written response — 2 points

Tyrone divided 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4. His work was: keep 2 1/2, change ÷ to ×, flip 1/4 to 4/1. Then he multiplied only the whole number, 2 × 4 = 8, and carried the 1/2 along, so he answered 8 1/2.

Explain Tyrone's misconception and give the correct quotient.

Score 2: Student explains that 2 1/2 must be converted to the improper fraction 5/2 before multiplying so that the half is included, and gives the quotient 10.

Score 1: Student gives the quotient 10 but does not explain the missing conversion to an improper fraction.

Score 0: Student agrees with Tyrone that the quotient is 8 1/2 or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Tyrone never converted the mixed number, so the 1/2 was never multiplied by 4. Convert first: 2 1/2 = 5/2. Then keep, change, flip: 5/2 × 4/1 = 20/2 = 10. The quotient is 10.

116.NOS.16-11 · Fraction Division Problem Solving

Part A

A ribbon is 4 1/2 feet long. Marta cuts the whole ribbon into pieces that are each 3/4 foot long. How many pieces does she get?

D. 6 pieces

The total amount 4 1/2 feet is the dividend and the size of one piece, 3/4 foot, is the divisor: 9/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 × 4/3 = 36/6 = 6 pieces.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

C. The total amount is the dividend and the size of each group is the divisor, so total ÷ size = number of groups: 9/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 × 4/3 = 36/6 = 6.

Asking how many pieces fit means dividing the total by the group size, in that order. Reversing the order or choosing another operation changes what the answer counts.

126.NOS.46-12 · Least Common Multiple

Part A

What is the least common multiple of 6 and 8?

C. 24

Multiples of 8 are 8, 16, 24, 32, and multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24. The first multiple that appears in both lists is 24.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

D. Skip count starting with the larger number: 8, 16, 24. Then skip count by 6: 6, 12, 18, 24. The first non-zero multiple in both lists is 24.

The least common multiple is the first shared multiple in the two skip-counting lists. The product 48 is a common multiple but not the least one, and 2 is the greatest common factor.

136.NOS.46-13 · Prime Factorization

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the number 45.

A, B, E. 45 is a composite number. · The prime factorization of 45 is 3² × 5. · The only prime factors of 45 are 3 and 5.

45 has the factors 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, and 45, so it is composite, not prime. Its factor tree ends in the primes 3, 3, and 5, giving 3² × 5 = 9 × 5 = 45. The expression 5 × 9 is not a prime factorization because 9 is composite, and 1 is never a prime factor because a prime has exactly two factors.

146.AT.76-14 · The Distributive Property

Written response — 2 points

Elena was asked to factor 18m + 24 completely. She wrote 18m + 24 = 2(9m + 12) and said she was finished because 2 divides both terms.

Explain Elena's misconception and give the completely factored expression.

Score 2: Student explains that 2 is a common factor but not the greatest common factor of 18 and 24, so the expression is not completely factored, and gives 6(3m + 4).

Score 1: Student gives 6(3m + 4) but does not explain that Elena used a common factor instead of the greatest common factor.

Score 0: Student agrees that 2(9m + 12) is completely factored or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Elena pulled out a common factor instead of the greatest common factor. Her expression is equivalent to the original, but 9m and 12 still share the factor 3. The GCF of 18 and 24 is 6, so 18m + 24 = 6(3m + 4). Checking by expanding: 6 · 3m = 18m and 6 · 4 = 24.

156.AT.76-15 · Simplify Algebraic Expressions

Part A

Simplify the expression 8x + 3 + 2x − 1.

B. 10x + 2

The terms 8x and 2x are like terms, so add their coefficients: 8 + 2 = 10, giving 10x. The constants 3 and −1 combine to 2. The simplified expression is 10x + 2.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

A. 8x and 2x share the same variable part, so add their coefficients to get 10x. The constants 3 and −1 combine separately to 2, giving 10x + 2.

Only terms with the same variable part can be combined, and constants combine with constants. The constant 1 is being subtracted, so 3 − 1 = 2, and the x terms are added, not subtracted.
