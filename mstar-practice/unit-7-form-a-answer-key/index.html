16.NOS.67-1 · Explore Integers and Their Opposites

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about integers and opposites:

A, B, C, E. Zero is an integer. · The opposite of a positive integer is negative. · Opposites are the same distance from zero on a number line. · Zero is its own opposite.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — opposites are by definition the SAME distance from zero, just on opposite sides.

26.NOS.67-2 · Represent Rational Numbers and Their Opposites on the Number Line

Written response — 2 points

Ines plotted −2¼ on a number line. She found −2, then counted one quarter to the RIGHT, landing between −2 and −1.

Explain Ines's misconception and describe where −2¼ actually belongs.

Score 2: Student explains that on the negative side, larger magnitude means moving LEFT, so −2¼ sits a quarter unit left of −2, between −3 and −2.

Score 1: Student says the point belongs between −3 and −2 without explaining the direction.

Score 0: Student agrees with Ines or gives an unrelated response.

Model: −2¼ lies a quarter of a unit to the LEFT of −2, between −3 and −2. Moving right from −2 goes toward zero and gives −1¾, a different number.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 3, 2

Reasoning words (any 2): moving, left, quarter, unit, between

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

36.NOS.87-3 · Understand Absolute Value of Rational Numbers

Part A

What is the value of |−12|?

C. 12

Absolute value is distance from zero, and distance is never negative. −12 sits 12 units from zero, so |−12| = 12.

Part B

Why can an absolute value never be negative?

D. It measures distance from zero, and a distance is never negative.

|a| is defined as how FAR a is from zero, regardless of direction. Distance has no direction, so it is always zero or positive.

46.NOS.87-4 · Compare and Order Integers and Rational Numbers

Part A

Order these numbers from LEAST to GREATEST: −2, 1.5, −4.5, 0, 3.

C. −4.5, −2, 0, 1.5, 3

Reading a number line from left to right gives −4.5, −2, 0, 1.5, 3.

Part B

Which statement correctly compares −4.5 and −2?

B. −4.5 < −2, because −4.5 lies further left on the number line.

Left is less. −4.5 sits to the left of −2, so −4.5 is the smaller number even though its absolute value is larger.

66.NOS.97-6 · Determine Distance on the Coordinate Plane

Written response — 2 points

Priya found the distance between (−3, 2) and (4, 2). She wrote: 'Just subtract: 4 − 3 = 1, so they are 1 unit apart.'

Explain Priya's misconception and give the correct distance.

Score 2: Student identifies that Priya dropped the negative sign on −3, sets up |4 − (−3)| = |4 + 3| = 7, and confirms by counting grid units from −3 to 4.

Score 1: Student gives 7 units without explaining the dropped negative sign.

Score 0: Student agrees with Priya or gives an unrelated response.

Model: |4 − (−3)| = |4 + 3| = 7 units. Priya treated −3 as 3. Counting on the number line from −3 to 0 is 3 units and 0 to 4 is 4 more, confirming 7.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 7

Reasoning words (any 2): priya, counting, units

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

76.NOS.97-7 · Represent Polygons on the Coordinate Plane

Part A

A rectangle has vertices at (1, 1), (7, 1), (7, 5), and (1, 5). What is its perimeter?

C. 20 units

The horizontal sides are |7 − 1| = 6 units and the vertical sides are |5 − 1| = 4 units. Perimeter = 2(6) + 2(4) = 20 units.

Part B

How can the side lengths be found from the coordinates alone?

B. Each side is horizontal or vertical, so its length is the difference of the coordinates that change.

Every side of this rectangle runs along a grid line, so one coordinate stays fixed and the length is the absolute difference of the other.

86.NOS.77-8 · Ordered Pairs in All Four Quadrants

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the four quadrants:

A, B, C, E. Quadrant I contains points with both coordinates positive. · Quadrant III contains points with both coordinates negative. · A point on an axis belongs to no quadrant. · Quadrant II contains points of the form (−, +).

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — the quadrants are numbered COUNTERCLOCKWISE from the upper right: I, II, III, IV.

96.NOS.77-9 · Reflect Points Across Axes

Part A

Reflect the point (5, −2) across the x-axis. What are the coordinates of the image?

B. (5, 2)

Reflecting across the x-axis changes the sign of the y-coordinate only: (x, y) → (x, −y), so (5, −2) → (5, 2).

Part B

Why does reflecting across the x-axis change only the y-coordinate?

B. The x-axis is horizontal, so the point flips vertically while its horizontal position stays fixed.

A reflection across a line flips the point to the other side of that line at the same distance. Across the horizontal x-axis, only the vertical position changes.
