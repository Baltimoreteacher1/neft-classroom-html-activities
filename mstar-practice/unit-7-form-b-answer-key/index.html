16.NOS.67-1 · Explore Integers and Their Opposites

Part A

Badwater Basin sits 86 meters below sea level, where sea level is defined as 0. Which integer represents this elevation?

C. −86

Below sea level means less than zero, so the elevation is −86 meters. Writing 86 would place it 86 meters ABOVE sea level.

Part B

What is the opposite of −86, and what does it represent here?

C. 86, an elevation 86 meters ABOVE sea level

Opposites are the same distance from zero in opposite directions. The opposite of −86 is 86, which is 86 meters above sea level.

26.NOS.67-2 · Represent Rational Numbers and Their Opposites on the Number Line

Part A

Between which two consecutive integers does −3.5 fall on a number line?

C. −4 and −3

−3.5 is half a unit to the left of −3, which places it between −4 and −3.

Part B

A student says −3.5 is greater than −3 because 3.5 is greater than 3. Why is this reasoning wrong?

B. On a number line, the further left a number is, the smaller it is, and −3.5 is left of −3.

For negative numbers, a larger absolute value means a position further LEFT, which means a smaller value. −3.5 < −3.

36.NOS.87-3 · Understand Absolute Value of Rational Numbers

Written response — 2 points

Owen compared −8 and −3. He wrote: 'Since |−8| = 8 and |−3| = 3, and 8 > 3, then −8 > −3.'

Explain Owen's misconception and state the correct comparison.

Score 2: Student explains that absolute value measures distance and ignores direction, that on the number line −8 lies further LEFT than −3, and concludes −8 < −3.

Score 1: Student states −8 < −3 without explaining why absolute value cannot be used to compare.

Score 0: Student agrees with Owen or gives an unrelated response.

Model: −8 < −3. Absolute value tells you how far a number is from zero, not which is greater. A negative number with a larger absolute value sits further left, so it is SMALLER. Owing $8 is worse than owing $3.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 8, 3

Reasoning words (any 2): absolute, value, number, further, left

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

Select all that apply

Select ALL comparisons that are TRUE:

A, B, C, E. −7 < −2 · 0 > −1 · −0.5 > −1.5 · 2.5 > −9

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — −10 lies further left than −3, so −10 < −3.

56.NOS.67-5 · Represent Rational Numbers on the Coordinate Plane

Part A

Starting at the origin, which point is located by moving 4 units LEFT and 3 units UP?

C. (−4, 3)

An ordered pair is (x, y). Moving left makes x negative, so x = −4; moving up makes y positive, so y = 3. The point is (−4, 3).

Part B

Why do (−4, 3) and (3, −4) name different points?

B. The order matters: the first number is always the horizontal move, the second the vertical.

An ordered pair is ordered, and that is not a play on words. (−4, 3) is 4 left and 3 up; (3, −4) is 3 right and 4 down — different quadrants entirely.

66.NOS.97-6 · Determine Distance on the Coordinate Plane

Part A

A chest sits at (−3, 2) and a cave at (4, 2). How far apart are they?

D. 7 units

Both points share the same y-value, so the distance is horizontal: |−3 − 4| = 7 units.

Part B

Why can this distance be found by subtraction alone?

A. The two points share the same y-coordinate, so the segment between them is horizontal.

When two points share a coordinate, the segment runs straight along a grid line and its length is the difference of the coordinates that DO change. A slanted segment needs more than subtraction.

76.NOS.97-7 · Represent Polygons on the Coordinate Plane

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about polygons on the coordinate plane:

A, B, C, E. Vertices are connected in the order given to form the polygon. · A horizontal side's length is the absolute difference of the x-coordinates. · A vertical side's length is the absolute difference of the y-coordinates. · The area of a rectangle on the grid is the product of its side lengths.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — a slanted side changes in BOTH directions, so subtracting one pair of coordinates does not give its length.

86.NOS.77-8 · Ordered Pairs in All Four Quadrants

Part A

In which quadrant does the point (−4, 3) lie?

A. Quadrant II

A negative x with a positive y is the upper-left region, which is Quadrant II.

Part B

What sign pattern identifies Quadrant IV?

C. (+, −)

Quadrant IV is the lower-right region: x is positive (right of the y-axis) and y is negative (below the x-axis).

96.NOS.77-9 · Reflect Points Across Axes

Written response — 2 points

Dante reflected (3, 7) across the y-axis. He wrote: 'Change the sign of y, so the image is (3, −7).'

Explain Dante's misconception and give the correct image point.

Score 2: Student identifies that a reflection across the y-axis changes the sign of the x-coordinate, gives (−3, 7), and explains that Dante applied the x-axis rule instead.

Score 1: Student gives (−3, 7) without explaining which rule Dante used.

Score 0: Student agrees with Dante or gives an unrelated response.

Model: (−3, 7). Reflecting across the y-axis (a vertical line) flips the point horizontally: (x, y) → (−x, y). Dante used the x-axis rule, (x, y) → (x, −y), which reflects across the horizontal axis instead.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 3, 7

Reasoning words (any 2): across, y-axis, dante, x-axis, rule, instead

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.
