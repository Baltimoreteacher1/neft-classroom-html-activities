Answer Key — Unit 5 Practice Test, Form B

Solve Area, Surface Area, and Volume Problems · Teacher copy: answers, explanations, and scoring rubrics.

These are MSTAR-style questions written for this curriculum to rehearse the format. They are not official Maryland assessment items. MSTAR — Maryland's new state test, first given in spring 2027 — uses the same kinds of questions you see here: selected response, select-all, two-part evidence questions, and written responses.
16.GR.15-1 · Determine the Area of Parallelograms and Rhombuses

Part A

A parallelogram-shaped patio has a base of 16 feet and a perpendicular height of 9 feet. What is its area?

D. 144 square feet

Area = base × height = 16 × 9 = 144 square feet.

Part B

The slanted side of that patio measures 11 feet. Why is 11 feet NOT used in the area calculation?

D. Because the height must be perpendicular to the base, and the slanted side is not.

Area = b · h requires the PERPENDICULAR height. The slanted side is longer than the perpendicular distance between the parallel bases, so using it would overstate the area.

26.GR.15-2 · Determine the Area of Triangles

Part A

A triangular garden bed has a base of 14 meters and a perpendicular height of 6 meters. What is its area?

C. 42 square meters

Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 14 × 6 = 42 square meters.

Part B

Which statement explains why the area formula for a triangle includes the factor ½?

A. A triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram with the same base and height.

Two identical triangles fit together to form a parallelogram with the same base and height, so one triangle is half of b · h.

36.GR.15-3 · Determine the Area of Trapezoids

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about trapezoids and their area:

A, B, D, E. A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides called bases. · The height is measured perpendicular to both parallel bases. · A trapezoid can be split into two triangles to find its area. · Area = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h.

A, B, D, and E are true. C is false — if both bases were equal the figure would be a parallelogram.

46.GR.15-4 · Apply Area Concepts to Solve Problems

Written response — 2 points

Nia split a composite figure into a 10 by 4 rectangle and a triangle with base 10 and height 4. She added 40 + 20 = 60 square units — but the triangle sat ON TOP of part of the rectangle she had already counted.

Explain the error in Nia's method and state what she must check before adding areas.

Score 2: Student explains that decomposed pieces must not overlap, identifies that the overlapping region was counted twice, and states the total is therefore too large.

Score 1: Student says the answer is wrong but does not explain the overlap.

Score 0: Student agrees with Nia or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Decomposition only works when the pieces do not overlap. Nia counted the shared region twice, so 60 square units is too large. She must verify the pieces cover the figure exactly once before adding.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 60

Reasoning words (any 2): pieces, overlap, region, counted, twice, large

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

56.GR.25-5 · Determine the Volume of Rectangular Prisms

Part A

A rectangular time capsule container measures 8 inches long, 5 inches wide, and 3 inches tall. What is its volume?

C. 120 cubic inches

V = l × w × h = 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cubic inches.

Part B

Which expression shows the same volume written as base area times height?

B. (8 × 5) × 3

The base is the 8 by 5 rectangle, so B = 40 square inches, and V = B · h = 40 × 3 = 120 cubic inches.

66.GR.45-6 · Represent Three-Dimensional Figures in Two Dimensions

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about nets of three-dimensional figures:

A, B, C, E. A net shows every face of the solid laid flat. · A triangular prism net has 2 triangles and 3 rectangles. · The area of a net equals the surface area of the solid. · One solid can have more than one correct net.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — overlapping faces mean the pattern does not fold into the solid, so it is not a net.

76.GR.45-7 · Determine Surface Area of Prisms

Part A

A rectangular prism measures 6 cm long, 4 cm wide, and 2 cm tall. What is its surface area?

D. 88 square centimeters

2(6×4) + 2(6×2) + 2(4×2) = 48 + 24 + 16 = 88 square centimeters.

Part B

Why is surface area measured in square units while volume is measured in cubic units?

A. Surface area covers the outside faces, which are flat regions; volume fills the inside space.

Surface area is the total of flat face areas, so it uses square units. Volume measures how much space the solid fills, so it uses cubic units.

86.GR.45-8 · Determine Surface Area of Pyramids

Part A

A square pyramid has a base measuring 6 m by 6 m and four triangular faces, each with a base of 6 m and a slant height of 5 m. What is its surface area?

C. 96 square meters

Base area = 6 × 6 = 36. Each triangle = ½ × 6 × 5 = 15, and 4 × 15 = 60. Total = 36 + 60 = 96 square meters.

Part B

Why does a pyramid's surface area use the slant height rather than the vertical height?

A. The slant height is the height of each triangular face, measured along the face itself.

Each lateral face is a triangle lying on the surface, and its height is the slant height. The vertical height runs through the inside of the solid and is not a face measurement.

96.GR.15-9 · Area of Regular Polygons

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the area of a regular polygon:

A, B, D, E. It equals the number of sides times the area of one center triangle. · Each center triangle has the same height for a regular polygon. · A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. · The height of each center triangle is measured perpendicular to a side.

A, B, D, and E are true. C is false — a regular pentagon has 5 sides and decomposes into 5 congruent triangles just as well.

106.GR.25-10 · Volume of Rectangular Prisms

Written response — 2 points

Omar found the volume of a box measuring 2½ ft by 4 ft by 2 ft. He wrote: 'V = 2 × 4 × 2 = 16 cubic feet' and said the half 'was too small to matter.'

Explain Omar's misconception and give the correct volume.

Score 2: Student converts 2½ to 5/2, computes 5/2 × 4 × 2 = 20 cubic feet, and explains that dropping the half discards a real part of the length.

Score 1: Student gives 20 cubic feet without explaining the conversion.

Score 0: Student agrees with Omar or gives an unrelated response.

Model: V = 5/2 × 4 × 2 = 20 cubic feet. The half foot is a real part of the edge; dropping it undercounts the volume by 4 cubic feet.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 20

Reasoning words (any 2): cubic, feet, dropping, half, real, part

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.
