16.AT.88-1 · Understand Equations and Their Solutions

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about equations and their solutions.

A, B, C. An equation is a math sentence that uses an equal sign to show two expressions have the same value. · You can test a value by substituting it for the variable and checking whether both sides are equal. · The value n = 6 is a solution of n + 9 = 15 because 6 + 9 = 15.

The first three statements are true. The fourth is false because 4y + 2 has no equal sign, so it is an expression, not an equation. The fifth is false because 15 − 4 = 11, not 9; the value that makes x − 4 = 9 true is x = 13, since 13 − 4 = 9.

26.AT.88-2 · Write and Solve Equations Using Addition or Subtraction

Written response — 2 points

Darius solved the equation x + 23 = 40. He wrote: 'x + 23 = 40, so x = 40 + 23, which means x = 63.'

Explain Darius's misconception and give the correct value of x.

Score 2: Student explains that adding 23 is undone by subtracting 23 from both sides, not by adding again, and gives x = 17.

Score 1: Student gives x = 17 but does not explain why subtraction is the inverse operation needed.

Score 0: Student agrees with Darius or gives an unrelated response.

Model: x = 17. The 23 is being added to x, so the inverse operation is subtraction: subtract 23 from both sides to get x = 40 − 23 = 17. Check by substituting: 17 + 23 = 40. Darius's answer fails the same check, because 63 + 23 = 86, not 40.

36.AT.88-3 · Write and Solve Equations Using Multiplication or Division

Part A

What is the solution of 6m = 54?

A. m = 9

In 6m the coefficient 6 is multiplied by the variable, so divide both sides by 6: m = 54 ÷ 6 = 9. Check by substituting: 6 × 9 = 54.

Part B

Which reasoning justifies the step used to find that solution?

A. Division undoes multiplication, so dividing both sides by 6 isolates the variable.

In 6m the variable is multiplied by 6, so the inverse operation is division. Dividing both sides by 6 keeps the equation balanced and gives m = 9. Subtracting 6 would give 48, and 6 × 48 is not 54.

46.AT.98-4 · Write and Represent Inequalities

Part A

A fair ride requires each rider to be at least 48 inches tall. Let h be a rider's height in inches. Which inequality represents the heights that are allowed to ride?

A. h ≥ 48

The phrase 'at least 48 inches' means 48 inches is allowed and any greater height is allowed too. That is greater than or equal to, so the inequality is h ≥ 48.

Part B

Which explanation justifies the symbol chosen above?

C. The words 'at least' include the boundary value, so a rider who is exactly 48 inches tall may ride.

'At least' sets a minimum that is part of the group, which is why the symbol carries the bar underneath. A rider who measures exactly 48 inches meets the rule, so h ≥ 48 rather than h > 48.

56.AT.98-5 · Understand Inequalities and Their Solutions

Select all that apply

Select ALL numbers that belong to the solution set of y ≤ 12.

A, B, D. 12 · 11.5 · 0

The symbol ≤ includes the boundary point, so 12 is a solution and its circle would be filled in. Numbers below 12, such as 11.5 and 0, are solutions as well. The values 12.5 and 20 are greater than 12, so they are outside the solution set.

66.AT.88-6 · Solve and Graph Inequalities

Written response — 2 points

Tomas solved 3x ≤ 18. He wrote: '3x ≤ 18, so x ≤ 18 − 3, which gives x ≤ 15.' He then drew a closed circle at 15 and shaded to the left.

Explain Tomas's misconception and give the correct solution and graph.

Score 2: Student explains that 3 is multiplied by the variable, so both sides must be divided by 3 instead of having 3 subtracted, gives x ≤ 6, and describes a closed circle at 6 with shading to the left.

Score 1: Student gives x ≤ 6 but does not explain why division is the inverse operation that is needed.

Score 0: Student agrees with Tomas or gives an unrelated response.

Model: x ≤ 6, graphed with a closed circle at 6 and shading to the left. In 3x the variable is multiplied by 3, so the inverse operation is division: divide both sides by 3 to get x ≤ 18 ÷ 3, which is x ≤ 6. The symbol ≤ includes the boundary point, so the circle at 6 is filled in. Tomas's answer fails a check, because 3 × 15 = 45, and 45 is not less than or equal to 18. His circle and shading were right in style but sat at the wrong boundary number.

76.AT.88-7 · Equations and Inequalities Problem Solving

Part A

A school van can carry at most 14 passengers. There are already 9 passengers on board. Let p be the number of additional passengers who can get on. Which inequality models this situation?

A. 9 + p ≤ 14

The 9 passengers already on board plus the p additional passengers make up the total. The phrase 'at most 14' means that total may reach 14 but cannot pass it, so the model is 9 + p ≤ 14.

Part B

Which reasoning justifies that model and gives the greatest number of additional passengers?

C. The total cannot pass the limit of 14, so 9 + p ≤ 14 and at most 5 more passengers can get on.

'At most 14' sets a maximum the total may reach but not pass, so 9 + p ≤ 14. Subtracting 9 from both sides gives p ≤ 5. Checking in context confirms it: 9 + 5 = 14 fits the van, while 9 + 6 = 15 is over the limit. The situation does not require the van to be full, so an equation would leave out the smaller totals that are also allowed.
