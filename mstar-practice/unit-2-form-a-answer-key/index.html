26.DS.52-2 · Represent and Describe Data in a Histogram

Part A

A histogram uses intervals 0–9, 10–19, 20–29, and 30–39. A data value of 19 belongs in which interval?

A. 10–19

19 falls between 10 and 19 inclusive, so it belongs in the 10–19 bar. Intervals must not overlap, so every value lands in exactly one.

Part B

Why are the bars of a histogram drawn with no gaps between them?

B. The intervals are continuous, so the bars show one unbroken range of values.

A histogram displays numerical data over continuous intervals. Touching bars show that the number line runs without breaks. A bar graph of categories DOES have gaps.

56.DS.32-5 · Describe Data by Range and Interquartile Range

Part A

Two classes have the same median score of 81. Class A scores range from 78 to 84; Class B scores range from 60 to 99. What does this tell you?

B. Class B has greater variability, even though the centers are the same.

Class A's range is 84 − 78 = 6; Class B's is 99 − 60 = 39. Equal centers can hide very different spreads, which is what measures of variability reveal.

Part B

Why is the IQR often preferred over the range as a measure of spread?

D. The range depends on only two values and is strongly affected by an outlier.

The range uses only the minimum and the maximum, so one unusual value changes it completely. The IQR describes the middle 50% and is not moved by an extreme value.

66.NOS.22-6 · Divide Multi-Digit Numbers Using an Algorithm

Andre computed 618 ÷ 3. He wrote: '6 ÷ 3 = 2, then 1 ÷ 3 does not go so I skip it, then 8 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 2. My answer is 22 remainder 2.'

Explain Andre's misconception and give the correct quotient.

Score 2: Student explains that when a digit is too small the quotient gets a 0 in that place and the digit is carried to the next step (1 becomes 18), and gives 206.

76.NOS.32-7 · Divide Decimals Using an Algorithm

Part A

Find 18.9 ÷ 6.3 using the decimal division algorithm.

C. 3

Slide both decimal points one place right: 189 ÷ 63 = 3. Multiplying both numbers by 10 leaves the quotient unchanged.

Part B

Why does sliding BOTH decimal points the same number of places leave the quotient unchanged?

D. Multiplying the dividend and the divisor by the same power of ten produces an equivalent division.

18.9 ÷ 6.3 and 189 ÷ 63 are equivalent because both numbers were multiplied by 10 — the same scaling on both terms of a ratio keeps its value.

86.DS.42-8 · Describe Data Using the Mean

Part A

Find the mean of this data set: 6, 10, 14, 8, 12.

B. 10

Sum = 6 + 10 + 14 + 8 + 12 = 50. Mean = 50 ÷ 5 = 10.

Part B

The mean is often described as a 'fair share' or a balance point. Why?

D. If the total were redistributed equally among all the values, each would receive the mean.

Adding all values and dividing by the count answers: if this total were shared out evenly, how much would each get? That is the fair-share meaning of the mean.

96.DS.6c2-9 · Describe Data by Mean Absolute Deviation

Written response — 2 points

Sofia found the MAD of 3, 5, 9, 11 (mean 7). She used the signed differences −4, −2, 2, 4, added them to get 0, and concluded 'the MAD is 0, so there is no variability.'

Explain Sofia's misconception and give the correct MAD.

Score 2: Student explains that signed differences always sum to zero because the mean is the balance point, that absolute distances must be used, and computes (4 + 2 + 2 + 4) ÷ 4 = 3.

Score 1: Student gives 3 without explaining why the signed differences cancel.

Score 0: Student agrees with Sofia or gives an unrelated response.

Model: MAD = (4 + 2 + 2 + 4) ÷ 4 = 3. The signed differences sum to zero for EVERY data set, because the mean balances the values above and below it. Taking absolute distances is what makes the measure meaningful.

Value: 3

Reasoning words (any 2): signed, differences, zero, mean, absolute, distances

A, B, D, E. Skewed data with outliers is better summarized by the median and IQR. · Symmetric data with no outliers is well summarized by the mean and MAD. · An outlier affects the range more than it affects the IQR. · The shape of the distribution should influence which measures you report.

116.NOS.32-11 · Add and Subtract Decimals

Part A

Find 12.4 + 3.75.

C. 16.15

Line up the decimal points and use a placeholder zero: 12.40 + 3.75 = 16.15.

Part B

Why is a placeholder zero written as 12.40 rather than leaving 12.4?

C. It makes both numbers have the same number of decimal places so each column holds the same place value.

12.4 and 12.40 are equal in value. The zero simply fills the hundredths column so digits with the same place value line up in the same column.

126.NOS.32-12 · Multiply Decimals

Part A

Find 2.4 × 0.5.

B. 1.2

Multiply as whole numbers: 24 × 5 = 120. The factors have 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places in total, so the product is 1.20, which is 1.2.

Part B

How is the number of decimal places in the product determined?

C. Add the number of decimal places in the two factors.

2.4 has 1 decimal place and 0.5 has 1, so the product has 1 + 1 = 2 decimal places before trailing zeros are dropped.
