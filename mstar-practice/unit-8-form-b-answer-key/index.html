16.AT.88-1 · Understand Equations and Their Solutions

Part A

Marisol tests values in the equation 7 + m = 23. Which value of m makes the equation true?

B. m = 16

Substitute each value for the variable. When m = 16, the left expression is 7 + 16 = 23 and the right expression is 23, so both sides balance. That makes 16 the solution.

Part B

Which reasoning best justifies that answer?

C. Substituting 16 for m gives 7 + 16 = 23, so both sides of the equation have the same value.

A value is a solution only when substituting it makes the left expression equal the right expression. Substituting 16 gives 23 = 23, so the equation is true. Adding 7 and 23 gives 30, and 7 + 30 = 37, which does not balance.

26.AT.88-2 · Write and Solve Equations Using Addition or Subtraction

Part A

Which value of n is the solution of n − 18 = 27?

D. n = 45

The variable has 18 subtracted from it, so add 18 to both sides to isolate n: n = 27 + 18 = 45. Check by substituting: 45 − 18 = 27.

Part B

Which reasoning justifies the step used to find that answer?

A. Because 18 is subtracted from n, adding 18 to both sides is the inverse operation that leaves n by itself.

Subtraction is undone by addition. Adding 18 to both sides keeps the equation balanced and isolates the variable, giving n = 45. Subtracting 18 from both sides instead would give 27 − 18 = 9, and 9 − 18 does not equal 27.

36.AT.88-3 · Write and Solve Equations Using Multiplication or Division

Select all that apply

Select ALL equations that have x = 8 as their solution.

A, B, E. 4x = 32 · x ÷ 2 = 4 · 56 = 7x

Substitute 8 for the variable in each equation. 4 × 8 = 32 is true, 8 ÷ 2 = 4 is true, and 56 = 7 × 8 is true. The third is false because 3 × 8 = 24, not 21; its solution is x = 7. The fourth is false because 8 ÷ 4 = 2, not 32; its solution is x = 128.

46.AT.98-4 · Write and Represent Inequalities

Written response — 2 points

Priya read this situation: 'A backpack can hold no more than 15 pounds.' She let w stand for the number of pounds in the backpack and wrote the inequality w ≥ 15.

Explain Priya's misconception and give the correct inequality.

Score 2: Student explains that 'no more than' sets a maximum the weight cannot pass, so the symbol must be less than or equal to, and writes w ≤ 15.

Score 1: Student writes w ≤ 15 but does not explain why 'no more than' sets a maximum.

Score 0: Student agrees with Priya or gives an unrelated response.

Model: w ≤ 15. 'No more than 15 pounds' means the weight may be 15 pounds or anything below 15 pounds, so the correct inequality is w ≤ 15. Priya's inequality w ≥ 15 describes weights of 15 pounds and heavier, which is the opposite group. She kept the boundary number but pointed the symbol the wrong way.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 15

Reasoning words (any 2): weight, symbol

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

56.AT.98-5 · Understand Inequalities and Their Solutions

Part A

A number line shows an open circle at 7, and the shading covers the numbers to the right of 7. Which inequality does this graph represent?

A. x > 7

Shading to the right means the solution set holds the numbers greater than 7. The open circle means the boundary point 7 is left out, so the inequality is x > 7.

Part B

Why is the circle at 7 open instead of closed?

C. The symbol > does not include 7, so 7 is not in the solution set and the boundary point stays unfilled.

An open circle marks a boundary point that is not a solution. Substituting the boundary gives 7 > 7, which is false, so 7 is excluded and the circle is left unfilled. A closed circle would be used for ≥, where the boundary is a solution.

66.AT.88-6 · Solve and Graph Inequalities

Part A

Solve n − 6 > 9. Which number line shows the solution set?

B. An open circle at 15 with shading to the right

Add 6 to both sides to isolate the variable: n > 9 + 6, so n > 15. The symbol > uses an open circle at 15, and the solutions are the numbers greater than 15, so the shading goes to the right.

Part B

Which reasoning supports that graph?

A. Adding 6 to both sides gives n > 15, and > leaves the boundary point unfilled with shading toward the larger numbers.

The inverse of subtracting 6 is adding 6, which gives n > 15. Substituting the boundary gives 15 − 6 = 9, and 9 is not greater than 9, so 15 is not a solution and the circle stays open. Substituting 16 gives 16 − 6 = 10, which is greater than 9, so the shading covers the numbers above 15.

76.AT.88-7 · Equations and Inequalities Problem Solving

Select all that apply

A club has already sold 46 tickets and must sell at least 120 tickets in all. Let t be the number of additional tickets the club sells. Select ALL statements that are true about this situation.

A, B, C. The inequality 46 + t ≥ 120 models the situation. · The club must sell at least 74 more tickets to reach its goal. · Selling exactly 74 more tickets reaches the goal.

The tickets already sold plus the additional tickets must reach at least 120, so the model is 46 + t ≥ 120. Subtracting 46 from both sides gives t ≥ 74. Selling exactly 74 more works because 46 + 74 = 120. The fourth statement points the symbol the wrong way, since 'at least' sets a minimum, not a maximum. The fifth is false because 46 + 70 = 116, which is short of 120.
