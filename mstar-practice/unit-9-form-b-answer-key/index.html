16.AT.119-1 · Explore Relationships Between Two Variables

Part A

Devon adds a different number of scoops of plant food to each tray of seedlings. Two weeks later he counts the new leaves on every tray. Which pair correctly names the variables?

B. Independent: scoops of plant food; Dependent: new leaves counted

Devon chooses how many scoops of plant food a tray receives, so the scoops are subject to choice and are the independent variable. The new leaves respond to that choice, so the leaf count is the dependent variable. The sentence check works only one way: the number of new leaves depends on the scoops of plant food.

Part B

Which sentence shows why the pair chosen in Part A is correct?

C. The number of new leaves depends on the scoops of plant food, because Devon chooses the scoops and the leaves respond.

The sentence check reads in only one direction. The leaves respond to the plant food, so the leaf count depends on the scoops, and the quantity that is depended on, the scoops, is the independent variable. The order in which Devon measures, the size of the numbers, and what the question asks about do not decide which variable is independent.

26.AT.119-2 · Analyze Graphs of Relationships Between Two Variables

Part A

A stadium shuttle carries fans from the parking lot. The table pairs the number of trips with the total fans carried: 2 trips with 96 fans, 5 trips with 240 fans, and 8 trips with 384 fans. On a graph with trips on the horizontal axis and fans on the vertical axis, which ordered pair belongs to 3 trips?

B. (3, 144)

Every row of the table is 48 times the number of trips: 96 ÷ 2 = 48, 240 ÷ 5 = 48, and 384 ÷ 8 = 48. The independent variable, trips, is written first, and 3 × 48 = 144, so the ordered pair is (3, 144).

Part B

Which explanation justifies the ordered pair chosen in Part A?

B. The shuttle carries 48 fans on each trip, and trips come first in the ordered pair, so 3 trips pair with 3 × 48 = 144 fans.

The ordered pair always lists the independent variable first, so trips come before fans. The relationship is a rate of 48 fans per trip, so the second coordinate is 3 × 48 = 144. Reversing the coordinates gives (144, 3), which reads as 144 trips carrying 3 fans.

36.AT.119-3 · Write Equations to Represent Relationships Between Two Variables

Select all that apply

A community pool charges $9 for each hour of lane rental. Let h be the number of hours rented and let c be the total cost in dollars. Select ALL statements that correctly represent this relationship.

A, C, E. c = 9h · The total cost is 9 times the number of hours rented. · In the equation, h stands for the number of hours rented and c stands for the total cost in dollars.

The $9 is charged for every hour, so the total cost is the rate times the hours: c = 9h, which also checks against 4 hours at 9 × 4 = $36. Writing h = 9c makes the hours depend on the cost, and c = 9 + h charges the fee only once. Defining the variables means saying what each letter counts: h counts hours and c counts dollars of total cost, while the 9 is the fixed rate and is not a variable.

46.AT.119-4 · Apply Two-Variable Relationships to Solve Problems

Written response — 2 points

The paint booth coats 6 car doors every hour, so d = 6h, where h is the hours the booth runs and d is the doors coated. Marisol needs 100 doors coated. She writes 6h = 100, divides to get 100 ÷ 6 = 16.66..., says the booth needs about 16 hours, and schedules 16 hours.

Explain Marisol's misconception and give the correct number of whole hours the booth must run.

Score 2: Student explains that 16 hours coats only 96 doors, so the leftover part of an hour cannot be dropped when the goal must still be met, and gives 17 hours.

Score 1: Student gives 17 hours but does not explain why rounding down leaves the goal unmet.

Score 0: Student agrees with Marisol or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Marisol rounded down. Substituting h = 16 into d = 6h gives 6 × 16 = 96 doors, which is fewer than the 100 doors needed. Because the booth must coat at least 100 doors, the leftover part of an hour still has to be run, so the answer rounds up to 17 hours. Substituting h = 17 gives 6 × 17 = 102 doors, which meets the goal.
