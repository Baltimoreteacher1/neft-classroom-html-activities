16.NOS.16-1 · Division Expressions with Fractions and Whole Numbers

Part A

What is the value of 6 ÷ 1/5?

D. 30

Put a 1 under the whole number so it becomes 6/1. Keep 6/1, change ÷ to ×, and flip 1/5 to its reciprocal 5/1. Multiply across: 6/1 × 5/1 = 30/1 = 30.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Write 6 as 6/1. Keep 6/1, change ÷ to ×, flip 1/5 to 5/1, then multiply across: 6/1 × 5/1 = 30.

Keep, change, flip works only after the whole number is written as a fraction over 1, and only the second fraction gets flipped. That gives 6/1 × 5/1 = 30.

26.NOS.16-2 · Division Expressions with Fractions and Mixed Numbers

Part A

What is the value of 2 1/4 ÷ 3/8?

C. 6

Rewrite 2 1/4 as the improper fraction 9/4. Keep 9/4, change ÷ to ×, flip 3/8 to 8/3: 9/4 × 8/3 = 72/12 = 6.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Change 2 1/4 to 9/4. Keep 9/4, change ÷ to ×, flip 3/8 to 8/3: 9/4 × 8/3 = 72/12 = 6.

A mixed number has to become an improper fraction before keep, change, flip, and only the divisor gets flipped. That gives 9/4 × 8/3 = 72/12 = 6.

36.AT.56-3 · Explore Numerical Expressions with Exponents

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the power 2⁵.

A, B, C, E. The base is 2. · The exponent is 5. · It means 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. · Its value is 32.

In 2⁵ the base 2 is used as a factor 5 times: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. A value of 10 comes from multiplying 2 × 5, and 5 × 5 swaps the base and the exponent.

46.AT.6c6-4 · Write and Evaluate Numerical Expressions with Exponents

Written response — 2 points

Devon evaluated 20 − 2 × 3². His work was: 3² = 9, then 20 − 2 = 18, then 18 × 9 = 162. He wrote that the value of the expression is 162.

Explain Devon's misconception and give the correct value of the expression.

Score 2: Student explains that the order of operations requires multiplying 2 × 9 before subtracting from 20, and gives the value 2.

Score 1: Student gives the value 2 but does not explain that multiplication comes before subtraction.

Score 0: Student agrees with Devon that the value is 162 or gives an unrelated response.

Model: Devon evaluated the power correctly but then subtracted before multiplying. In PEMDAS, multiplication comes before subtraction: 3² = 9, then 2 × 9 = 18, then 20 − 18 = 2. The value is 2.

56.AT.6a6-5 · Write and Evaluate Algebraic Expressions

Part A

A concert ticket costs 8 dollars. The website also charges one 5 dollar service fee for the whole order. Let t represent the number of tickets bought in one order. Which expression gives the total cost in dollars?

A. 8t + 5

The variable t is the number of tickets. Each ticket costs 8 dollars, so the ticket cost is 8t. The 5 dollar fee is charged once, so it is a constant added on: 8t + 5.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. The 8 is the coefficient because it is paid for each ticket, and the 5 is a constant because it is paid one time. This gives 8t + 5, so an order of 6 tickets costs 8 × 6 + 5 = 53 dollars.

A per-item price becomes the coefficient of the variable and a one-time charge becomes a constant. Substituting t = 6 into 8t + 5 gives 53 dollars.

66.AT.76-6 · Identify Equivalent Algebraic Expressions

Part A

Which expression is equivalent to 4(x + 3)?

A. 4x + 12

Use the distributive property to multiply 4 by each term inside the parentheses: 4 · x = 4x and 4 · 3 = 12, so the expression is 4x + 12.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

A. Multiply 4 by each term inside the parentheses: 4 · x + 4 · 3 = 4x + 12. Testing x = 2 gives 4(2 + 3) = 20 and 4(2) + 12 = 20, so the two expressions match.

Equivalent expressions give the same value for every substitution. Distributing 4 to both terms produces 4x + 12, and a test value confirms the match.

76.NOS.46-7 · Find Factors and Multiples

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the numbers 12 and 20.

A, B, C, E. 6 is a factor of 12. · The greatest common factor of 12 and 20 is 4. · The least common multiple of 12 and 20 is 60. · 40 is a multiple of 20.

The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12, so 6 is a factor and 5 is not. The factors of 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, and 20, so the greatest common factor is 4. Multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 and multiples of 20 are 20, 40, 60, so the least common multiple is 60. Since 20 × 2 = 40, the number 40 is a multiple of 20.

86.AT.76-8 · Generate Equivalent Expressions

Written response — 2 points

Jamal wrote (20 − 8) − 5 = 20 − (8 − 5). He said the associative property lets him move the parentheses anywhere, so both sides must have the same value of 7.

Explain Jamal's misconception and give the correct value of each side.

Score 2: Student explains that the associative property applies to addition and multiplication but not to subtraction, and shows that the left side equals 7 while the right side equals 17.

Score 1: Student computes both sides as 7 and 17 but does not explain that the associative property does not apply to subtraction.

Score 0: Student agrees with Jamal that both sides equal 7 or gives an unrelated response.

Model: The associative property holds for addition and multiplication only. Moving the parentheses in a subtraction changes the value: (20 − 8) − 5 = 12 − 5 = 7, but 20 − (8 − 5) = 20 − 3 = 17. The two sides are not equal, so the equation is false.

96.NOS.16-9 · Divide Whole Numbers by Fractions

Part A

What is the value of 6 ÷ 3/4?

D. 8

Write 6 as 6/1 and multiply by the reciprocal of 3/4: 6/1 × 4/3. Cross-cancel the 6 and the 3 to get 2/1 × 4/1 = 8.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Write 6 as 6/1 and multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor: 6/1 × 4/3. Cross-cancel the common factor 3 to get 2/1 × 4/1 = 8.

Division by a fraction is rewritten as multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor. Cross-cancelling the shared factor 3 before multiplying gives 8.

106.NOS.16-10 · Divide Mixed Numbers

Part A

What is the value of 3 3/4 ÷ 1 1/2?

B. 2 1/2

Convert both mixed numbers to improper fractions: 3 3/4 = 15/4 and 1 1/2 = 3/2. Then keep, change, flip: 15/4 × 2/3 = 30/12 = 5/2 = 2 1/2.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

C. Convert to improper fractions, 15/4 and 3/2, then keep, change, flip: 15/4 × 2/3 = 30/12 = 5/2 = 2 1/2.

Converting 3 3/4 requires 3 × 4 + 3 = 15 in the numerator, giving 15/4. After keep, change, flip the product is 30/12, which simplifies to 2 1/2.

116.NOS.16-11 · Fraction Division Problem Solving

Select all that apply

A jug holds 3/4 gallon of juice. Each cup holds 1/8 gallon. Select ALL statements that correctly describe this situation.

A, B, C, D. The dividend is 3/4 gallon, the total amount of juice in the jug. · The divisor is 1/8 gallon, the size of one group. · The equation that models the situation is 3/4 ÷ 1/8. · The jug fills 6 cups.

The total 3/4 gallon is the dividend and the cup size 1/8 gallon is the divisor, so the model is 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 cups. The value 3/32 comes from multiplying instead of dividing, and dividing by a number smaller than the dividend gives a quotient larger than 1 here.

126.NOS.46-12 · Least Common Multiple

Written response — 2 points

Sofia found the least common multiple of 4 and 10 by multiplying the two numbers: 4 × 10 = 40. She wrote that the least common multiple of 4 and 10 is 40.

Explain Sofia's misconception and give the correct least common multiple.

Score 2: Student explains that the product is a common multiple but not always the least one because 4 and 10 share the factor 2, and gives 20 as the least common multiple.

Score 1: Student gives 20 as the least common multiple but does not explain why the product is too large.

Score 0: Student agrees with Sofia that the least common multiple is 40 or gives an unrelated response.

Model: The product 40 is a common multiple, but it is only the least one when the two numbers share no factor other than 1. Here 4 and 10 both have the factor 2. Skip count from the larger number: 10, 20. Skip count by 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. The first multiple in both lists is 20, so the least common multiple is 20.

136.NOS.46-13 · Prime Factorization

Part A

What is the prime factorization of 72?

A. 2³ × 3²

A factor tree for 72 ends in the primes 2, 2, 2, 3, and 3. Written with exponents in ascending order, that is 2³ × 3², and 8 × 9 = 72 confirms the value.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Every branch of the factor tree for 72 ends in a prime: 2, 2, 2, 3, 3. Grouping the repeated primes with exponents gives 2³ × 3², and 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72.

A prime factorization must have every branch ending in a prime number. The number 12 is composite, and 8 and 9 are both composite, so those splits are unfinished. Counting the primes gives three 2s and two 3s.

146.AT.76-14 · The Distributive Property

Part A

Which expression is equivalent to 6(2x + 5)?

B. 12x + 30

The distributive property says a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the outside factor by each term inside: 6 · 2x = 12x and 6 · 5 = 30, so the expression is 12x + 30.

Part B

Which reasoning shows why your answer to Part A is correct?

B. Multiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses: 6 · 2x = 12x and 6 · 5 = 30, giving 12x + 30.

Expanding requires the outside factor to reach every term inside the parentheses. Skipping a term or adding instead of multiplying produces an expression that is not equivalent.

156.AT.76-15 · Simplify Algebraic Expressions

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the expression 5y + 7 + 3y + y.

A, B, C, E. 5y, 3y, and y are like terms. · The coefficient of the term y is 1. · The simplified expression is 9y + 7. · 7 is a constant and cannot be combined with the y terms.

All three variable terms have the same variable part y, so they are like terms. A term written as y has an understood coefficient of 1, so 5 + 3 + 1 = 9, giving 9y. The constant 7 has no variable and stays separate, so the simplified expression is 9y + 7. The answer 8y + 7 leaves out the coefficient 1, and 16y wrongly folds the constant into the variable terms.
