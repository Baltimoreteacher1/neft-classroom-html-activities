16.AT.13-1 · Understand Ratios

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about ratios:

A, B, C, E. The order of the quantities in a ratio matters. · A ratio can compare a part to a whole or a part to another part. · 3 : 4 and 6 : 8 describe the same relationship. · A ratio can be written as 3 : 4, 3 to 4, or 3/4.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — a rate is a ratio comparing quantities with DIFFERENT units, such as miles per hour.

26.AT.23-2 · Understand Rates and Unit Rates

Written response — 2 points

Marisol wanted the cost per pound of apples. The sign read '4 pounds for $6.' She wrote: 'Unit rate = 4 ÷ 6 = about $0.67 per pound.'

Explain Marisol's misconception and give the correct unit rate.

Score 2: Student identifies that 'cost per pound' means dollars divided by pounds, computes 6 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per pound, and explains that 4 ÷ 6 gives pounds per dollar instead.

Score 1: Student gives $1.50 per pound without explaining which quantity is divided by which.

Score 0: Student agrees with Marisol or gives an unrelated response.

Model: 6 ÷ 4 = $1.50 per pound. The words 'per pound' say what to divide by: dollars ÷ pounds. Marisol's 4 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.67 is pounds per dollar, a different rate that answers a different question.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 1.50

Reasoning words (any 2): pound, dollars, pounds, dollar

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

36.AT.3a3-3 · Determine Equivalent Ratios Using Tables

Part A

A ratio table pairs 3 cups of flour with 2 cups of water. If the recipe is scaled to use 12 cups of flour, how much water is needed?

B. 8 cups

12 ÷ 3 = 4, so the scale factor is 4. Both terms scale: 2 × 4 = 8 cups of water.

Part B

Why is 11 cups an incorrect answer for Part A?

C. It adds 9 to both terms instead of multiplying both by the same factor.

Adding the same amount to both terms changes the relationship. Equivalent ratios require multiplying or dividing BOTH terms by the same non-zero factor.

56.AT.33-5 · Compare Ratio Relationships

Part A

Car A travels 180 miles on 6 gallons. Car B travels 225 miles on 9 gallons. Which car gets better gas mileage?

D. Car A, at 30 miles per gallon

Car A: 180 ÷ 6 = 30 miles per gallon. Car B: 225 ÷ 9 = 25 miles per gallon. 30 > 25, so Car A gets better mileage.

Part B

Why is comparing total miles alone NOT enough to decide which car is more efficient?

C. The cars used different amounts of gas, so the totals are not comparable until each is expressed per gallon.

Car B traveled farther but also used more gas. Converting each to a unit rate puts both on the same per-gallon basis, which is what makes them comparable.

66.AT.33-6 · Ratio Reasoning: Convert Measurements within the Same System

Written response — 2 points

Tariq converted 240 centimeters to meters. Knowing 1 meter = 100 centimeters, he wrote: '240 × 100 = 24,000 meters.'

Explain Tariq's misconception and give the correct conversion.

Score 2: Student identifies that a meter is LARGER than a centimeter so the number of meters must be smaller, computes 240 ÷ 100 = 2.4 meters, and explains why division is correct here.

Score 1: Student gives 2.4 meters without explaining the size relationship.

Score 0: Student agrees with Tariq or gives an unrelated response.

Model: 240 ÷ 100 = 2.4 meters. A meter is larger than a centimeter, so fewer of them cover the same length — the number must get smaller, which means dividing, not multiplying. 24,000 meters is 24 kilometers.

Instant scoring looks for (2 pts = value + reasoning; 1 pt = one of them):

Value: 2.4

Reasoning words (any 2): meter, larger, centimeter, number, meters, smaller

Word matching cannot judge an argument. A student who reasons correctly in other words will score low here; re-score from the rubric above.

76.AT.33-7 · Ratio Reasoning: Convert Measurements Between Systems

Part A

A backpack weighs 5 kilograms. Using 1 kilogram ≈ 2.2 pounds, about how much does it weigh in pounds?

D. 11 pounds

5 kilograms × 2.2 pounds per kilogram = 11 pounds.

Part B

Why is the symbol ≈ used in the conversion 1 kilogram ≈ 2.2 pounds?

C. The conversion between these systems is approximate, not exact.

1 kilogram is about 2.2046 pounds. Between-system conversions are usually rounded, so results are approximations rather than exact values.

86.AT.23-8 · Solve Problems with Unit Rates

Part A

A store sells 8 notebooks for $20. At the same unit price, what is the cost of 14 notebooks?

C. $35.00

Unit price = 20 ÷ 8 = $2.50 per notebook. Then 14 × 2.50 = $35.00.

Part B

Which sequence of steps solves Part A?

C. Divide the total cost by the quantity to find the unit price, then multiply by the new quantity.

Finding the unit price first gives the cost of one item, which can then be scaled to any quantity.

96.AT.33-9 · Equivalent Ratios

Select all that apply

Select ALL pairs of ratios that are equivalent:

A, B, D. 2/3 and 8/12 · 5/8 and 10/16 · 6/9 and 2/3

A (2×12 = 24 = 3×8), B (5×16 = 80 = 8×10), and D (6×3 = 18 = 9×2) are equivalent. C fails: 3×5 = 15 but 4×4 = 16. E fails: 7×21 = 147 but 10×14 = 140.

106.AT.3c3-10 · Convert Measurement Units

Part A

Convert 3 hours to seconds using 1 hour = 60 minutes and 1 minute = 60 seconds.

D. 10,800 seconds

3 hours × 60 minutes per hour = 180 minutes. Then 180 × 60 seconds per minute = 10,800 seconds.

Part B

In dimensional analysis, how are the conversion ratios arranged?

D. So the unwanted units appear in both a numerator and a denominator and cancel.

Writing 3 hr × (60 min / 1 hr) × (60 s / 1 min) puts 'hr' and 'min' in both a numerator and a denominator, so they cancel and only seconds remain.
