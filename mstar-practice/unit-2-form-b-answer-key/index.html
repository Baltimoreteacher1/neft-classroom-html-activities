16.DS.12-1 · Understand Statistical Questions

Part A

Which question is a STATISTICAL question?

D. How many minutes did each student in our class sleep last night?

A statistical question anticipates answers that VARY. Different students slept different amounts, so the data will vary. The other three each have exactly one fixed answer.

Part B

What makes the other three questions non-statistical?

C. Each has a single fixed answer, so there is no variability to describe.

Variability is the whole point. A question with one fixed answer produces one value, and there is no distribution to summarize.

26.DS.52-2 · Represent and Describe Data in a Histogram

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about histograms:

A, B, C, E. The intervals must be equal in width. · The height of each bar is the frequency of values in that interval. · Bars touch because the intervals are continuous. · Intervals must not overlap.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — a histogram shows how many values fall in each interval, but the individual values are lost inside the bar.

36.DS.42-3 · Describe the Data Using the Median

Part A

Find the median of this data set: 12, 7, 15, 9, 20, 11, 8.

B. 11

Ordered: 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 20. There are 7 values, so the median is the 4th one: 11.

Part B

Why must the data be put in order before finding the median?

C. The median is the middle value by position in the ordered list, not by where it was written.

The median is defined by position in an ordered list. Taking the middle of an unordered list picks an arbitrary value with no meaning.

46.DS.52-4 · Represent and Describe Data in a Box Plot

Part A

A box plot has a 5-number summary of minimum 4, Q1 = 8, median 12, Q3 = 18, maximum 24. What is the interquartile range?

B. 10

IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 18 − 8 = 10.

Part B

What does the box itself represent in a box plot?

C. The middle 50% of the data, from Q1 to Q3.

Q1 marks the 25th percentile and Q3 the 75th, so the box spans the middle half of the data. The whiskers reach out to the minimum and maximum.

56.DS.32-5 · Describe Data by Range and Interquartile Range

Written response — 2 points

Lena compared two data sets. Both had a median of 50. She concluded: 'The two sets are the same because their medians match.'

Explain why Lena's conclusion may be wrong, and name a measure that would tell the sets apart.

Score 2: Student explains that the median describes CENTER only, that two sets with the same center can have very different spreads, and names range, IQR, or MAD as a measure of variability.

Score 1: Student says the sets may differ but does not name a measure of spread.

Score 0: Student agrees with Lena or gives an unrelated response.

Model: The median describes the center only. One set could be 48, 49, 50, 51, 52 and another 10, 30, 50, 70, 90 — same median, very different spread. Range, IQR, or MAD would distinguish them.

66.NOS.22-6 · Divide Multi-Digit Numbers Using an Algorithm

Part A

Use the long division algorithm to find 936 ÷ 4.

C. 234

9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1; bring down 3 to make 13. 13 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 1; bring down 6 to make 16. 16 ÷ 4 = 4. The quotient is 234.

Part B

In the D-M-S-B cycle, what does the SUBTRACT step tell you?

B. How much of the current value is left over before the next digit is brought down.

Subtracting the product from the current partial dividend leaves the remainder at that place, which the next digit is appended to.

76.NOS.32-7 · Divide Decimals Using an Algorithm

Written response — 2 points

Bianca computed 7.5 ÷ 0.5. She slid the divisor's point to make it 5, but left the dividend as 7.5, and wrote '7.5 ÷ 5 = 1.5.'

Explain Bianca's misconception and give the correct quotient.

Score 2: Student explains that both decimal points must slide the same number of places to keep the division equivalent, computes 75 ÷ 5 = 15, and notes that 1.5 is ten times too small.

Score 1: Student gives 15 without explaining why both points must move.

Score 0: Student agrees with Bianca or gives an unrelated response.

Model: 75 ÷ 5 = 15. Bianca multiplied only the divisor by 10, which changes the problem. Both numbers must be scaled by the same factor, so 7.5 becomes 75. A check confirms it: 0.5 × 15 = 7.5.

86.DS.42-8 · Describe Data Using the Mean

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about the mean:

A, B, D, E. Mean = sum of all values ÷ number of values. · Adding one very large value pulls the mean upward. · The mean can be a decimal even when every value is a whole number. · The mean uses every value in the data set.

A, B, D, and E are true. C is false — the mean of 2 and 5 is 3.5, which is not in the set.

96.DS.6c2-9 · Describe Data by Mean Absolute Deviation

Part A

A data set is 3, 5, 9, 11 with a mean of 7. What is the mean absolute deviation?

B. 3

Distances from the mean of 7 are 4, 2, 2, and 4. Their sum is 12, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

Part B

Why are the DISTANCES from the mean used rather than the signed differences?

C. The signed differences always add to zero, so they would give a MAD of 0 for every data set.

The mean is the balance point, so values above and below cancel exactly. Using absolute distances measures how far values sit from the center regardless of direction.

106.DS.6d2-10 · Choose Appropriate Measures

Part A

A data set of house prices contains one mansion worth far more than every other home. Which pair of measures best describes this data?

A. Median and IQR

The mansion is an outlier that pulls the mean upward and inflates the range. The median and IQR describe the middle of the data and are resistant to a single extreme value.

Part B

When are the mean and MAD the better choice?

D. When the data is roughly symmetric with no outliers.

With symmetric data and no extreme values, the mean sits at the center and uses every value, so the mean and MAD summarize the set well.

116.NOS.32-11 · Add and Subtract Decimals

Written response — 2 points

Malik computed 9.6 + 0.45 by lining up the RIGHT-hand edges of the digits, as if adding whole numbers. He got 1.41.

Explain Malik's misconception and give the correct sum.

Score 2: Student explains that decimals are aligned by DECIMAL POINT so that tenths add to tenths and hundredths to hundredths, and computes 9.60 + 0.45 = 10.05.

Score 1: Student gives 10.05 without explaining place-value alignment.

Score 0: Student agrees with Malik or gives an unrelated response.

Model: 9.60 + 0.45 = 10.05. Decimals are lined up by the decimal point, not by the right edge, so that each column holds one place value. Estimation confirms it: 9.6 is nearly 10, so the sum must be just over 10.

126.NOS.32-12 · Multiply Decimals

Select all that apply

Select ALL statements that are true about multiplying decimals:

A, B, C, E. Multiply the factors as if they were whole numbers first. · The total number of decimal places in the factors gives the number in the product. · Multiplying by a number less than 1 gives a product smaller than the other factor. · Estimating first is a good way to check the placement of the decimal point.

A, B, C, and E are true. D is false — lining up decimal points is required for ADDING and subtracting, not for multiplying.
